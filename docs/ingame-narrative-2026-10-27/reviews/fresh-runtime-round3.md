## Findings and verified corrections

**P2 — A trace can become an apparent encounter without the story acknowledging it.** In `game/src/data/narrative-relationships.js:7`, Priya's closer text says, “A square mark shows on the gecko resting behind it.” At line 10, Samir's closer text shows a tricolor guinea pig at a low window, including its identifying eye patch. `game/src/ui/story.js`, the `A small trace` insertion, offers these to an unknown pet's Kid in any current region. `reunionUnits` in `state/narrative.js` does not register the pet as found until the Heart reunion. A child can therefore see the recognizable pet in the Foyer and then continue a six-wing search without being told why they cannot approach or how that room relates to the Heart. This is not a demand to make visual recognition equal rescue: the missing element is an understandable distance or barrier. **Fix:** keep these two early observations indirect, or establish an unmistakably remote view whose route cannot yet be reached. Reserve the unqualified physical sighting for reunion. Preserve the animal's identifying detail in the later scene.

**P2, small — Cat Flap reward copy promises the wrong relationship between rewards.** In `game/src/data/events.js`, `the-cat-flap` → `look`, the label reads “Clues; possibly healing instead.” Both outcomes grant Clues. The sunny-patch outcome additionally heals 8; the close-inspection outcome loses 10 Courage. Healing never replaces Clues. **Fix:** “Clues, with possible healing” or “Clues; possibly healing too.” This is a copy correction, not a combat-balance request.

**Both P2 findings are now resolved in the actual source.** I reread the changed files: Pixel's closer text now follows toe-prints through a bedding gap; Bean's trace and closer text lead to a grille, speaking tube, and carrying pocket, with no visible pet. The Cat Flap reward now reads “Clues, with possible healing.” These changes address the findings without changing outcome weights or requiring additional rescue state. The original findings are retained above as an audit trail, not as outstanding defects.

**P3 — Aftermath participation needs more dramatic variety.** `narrative-aftercare.js` repeatedly offers an object adjustment, a prompt to notice it, and waiting. All are legitimate player actions, and the selected branch is honored. Nevertheless, after several wings the player can predict that either pointing or doing nothing will make the guardian independently discover the same lesson in the next paragraph. The kitchen's three spoon outcomes at lines 89–105 are the clearest example. **Improve selectively:** let one choice uncover a different personal detail, ask the resident rather than the guardian a question, or produce a different comic complication before the shared resolution. Keep the common regional promise and existing mechanics. The Graveyard's “What were they like?” already demonstrates a useful alternative.

**P3 — The Ballroom choice presupposes a wave not yet shown.** `narrative-aftercare.js:232` says “Return the guest's goodbye wave,” but the setup has only lowered the plate and reached for a coat; this branch begins with the player waving and the phantom answering. **Fix:** “Wave goodbye to the guest,” or show the guest's initial wave in the setup. It is a tiny continuity issue rather than an unselected-branch leak.

**P3 — A few interactions spend a click on an action that barely changes the visible situation.** Kitchen setup already places the plain spoon beside the soup, then `AFTER-kitchens-cellars-spoon` asks to put it beside the bowl. **Fix:** place it partly trapped beneath the Recipe Board in the setup, or offer to slide it into the Confectioner's reach. This gives the child's contribution a readable physical result.

No P0/P1 issue was established. I found no need for a mandatory new combat mechanic, invented founder/owner biography, present pet death, forced pet departure, or all-three-outcomes rendering defect in the reviewed narrative.
