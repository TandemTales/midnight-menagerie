# Fresh editorial review of the executable narrative

Independent assessment, 28 October 2026. **82/100.** This is a good, unusually considerate children's game narrative with memorable animal comedy. It is not yet an exceptional playable mystery: its strongest emotional developments and solutions are still mostly things the player reads after winning fights.

I reviewed the current executable data and consumers, not a proposed manuscript. I did not read previous score sheets or defenses. I read the supplied runtime result JSONs and the tutorial runtime-round2-response only as verification evidence. I did not run a browser or edit game source. This score is editorial judgment from the executable scripts and reported verification, not a claim to have tested comprehension with children or independently played every route.

## Architecture and what the game actually asks the player to do

`state/run.js:172–187` defines a six-wing expedition: the initial Foyer, four chosen wings, then the Heart, with additional entry flexibility unlocked through play. This is not a seventeen-chapter linear story. I have not deducted for a child missing a particular optional wing or required a fixed revelation order.

`state/narrative.js` routes arrivals, real boss introductions, post-boss scenes, reunions, and the finale around existing scene boundaries. `scenes/narrative.js` supplies full/short versions and commits each completed unit. `ui/story.js` places optional observations in Discoveries and relationship scenes at rest. `ui/investigation.js` supports optional/changeable ideas, two physical checks in either order, and a saved comparison. Foyer, Attic, and Lampworks each have an authored response from the guardian after that comparison. These are real implemented interactions and deserve credit.

The three investigations do not alter combat or offer an alternate route around a boss. That is permissible. The limitation is dramatic: after a child has proved an adult figure wrong, the dominant next action is still a Scuffle, followed by that figure changing its behavior on its own. Fourteen regional packages offer observations but no comparable experiment. A small number of different, playable repair moments would add more than another seventeen explanations of the theme.

Clue currency from Curiosities and named Discoveries are separate systems. The text generally respects that distinction; collecting generic Clues is not falsely treated as proving a specific reunion. Curiosities have meaningful resource choices and disclosed costs. However, a child can reasonably expect the words “follow the clues” to mean a stronger causal role for evidence than the current narrative provides.

## Specific findings and fixes

**P2 — An opinion about identity has a wrong-answer damage charge.** `data/events.js:879–907`, The Mended Thing: “STILL THE SAME RABBIT?” followed by Write YES (no risk, maximum Courage and Snack) versus Write NO (7 Courage, frightened by the lamp, then asked “WHAT MAKES IT THE SAME?”). The NO option also grants useful rewards, so this is not a mechanical claim that YES strictly dominates. It is an emotional mismatch: disagreeing with the apparent lesson turns the light off and hurts. The investigations elsewhere explicitly welcome an unsure or revised answer. Make the lamp invite a second look without damage for either identity answer, or replace the binary judgment with two equally safe ways of examining the rabbit. Keep tactical costs on a physical bargain rather than on assent to the theme.

**P2 — The central mystery is largely answered before it has developed.** `narrative-regions.js:48`, Foyer “Two answers”: the House recorded HAS A HOME and crossed out going home under KEEP SAFE. That is unusually conclusive first-wing evidence. The bells then demonstrate the same act. Library's unattached HOME CONFIRMED, Kennels' postponed return, Bathhouse's moving standard, and the Heart's blocked gate mostly restate it. This makes a strong thematic pattern but a modest mystery. Keep a useful early suspicion while reserving one unresolved question that later evidence can answer in any route: who can authorize a return, what the House thinks will happen, or why a previous departure worked. Do not simply hide every clue until late; give later clues a new job.

**P2 — Specific disappearance promises do not receive specific payoffs.** `scenes/tutorial.js` KID_OPENINGS: Pepper said “Open the little door,” and Mateo wants to know whose voice he copied; Samir's cousin saw a red-coated carrier; Amina/Priya find unusual moss. The reunion/home entries in `narrative-relationships.js:3–10` beautifully restore contact but do not return to those causal hooks. The general House explanation does not identify the copied voice or carrier. Add a brief, evidenced recognition to relevant trace/closer/reunion lines, or soften the opening's explicit detective question. Do not invent an unproved identity in a recap.

**P2 — Several conversions have no participatory hinge.** `narrative-regions.js:149–154` Confectioner finds the spoon, tries it, and puts down icing; `:444–449` Gardener removes its mushroom and patches its own glove; `:554–559` King changes the label. These are lovely miniature scenes, but all player contribution is upstream combat victory. For two or three distinct wings, let the player offer the plain spoon, hold the pot, or turn the direction marker, and let the resident decide whether to use it. A tiny optional interaction would make the change feel helped rather than awarded. This need not be a moral quiz, alternate boss system, or new animation.

**P2 — The first resource lesson is dense for the lower age target.** `ui/coach.js:623–625`, TUT-002, combines pointing/keyboard selection, Trick reading, Nerve cost comparison, Courage, and Guard duration while highlighting the hand. A seven-year-old can click Got it without knowing which number to use. Keep this beat about reading one Trick and comparing its cost to available Nerve. Introduce Courage/Guard when the visible next attack makes them relevant, or add a clearly optional focused explanation. Preserve the five-step pacing; do not replace this with a longer mandatory lecture.

**P3 — The safe-room Companion beat repeats verbatim.** `ui/story.js:105–108` always offers the stage-one “Sit with” button and writes its seen flag without consulting that flag before offering it again. Wisp's lunchbox, Taffy's spoon, and Crinkle's dry boat are excellent once; repeating them at every rest makes a relationship feel stuck. Offer a short return beat after first viewing, or label the old scene as something to reread. This does not require a full branching relationship system.

**P3 — The naming reveal is preempted.** `ui/coach.js:177` displays Marmalade by name during practice, while `scenes/tutorial.js` OPN-070 later makes discovering her tag a naming moment and deliberately withholds the earlier story nameplate. Use “Your ghost-cat partner” in the coach until the tag, or make the later beat about Marmalade offering the tag rather than discovering her name. Combat HUD may also need checking if retaining a strict reveal.

**P3 — Guardian voice is an interface task rather than comic timing.** `ui/story.js:145–151` unlocks a button after an enemy turn, then the player opens a modal for one bark. That keeps flavor from blocking tactics, but weakens lines that would be funny as a brief visible reaction. Consider a quiet, nonblocking caption with a replay button, while respecting reading speed and visual accessibility. This is polish, not a blocker.

## Children, pets, and relationships

All eight opening voices, pet traces/closer looks, reunions/home choices, and two solo check-ins were reviewed. Maya/Orbit has practical lists, the reserved window, and a lovely crutch bump; Mateo/Pepper has reciprocal altered whistles rather than generic chatter; Amina/Mochi waits and lets the rabbit approach; Eli/Sprocket's sniff/paw/bite inspection makes affection funny; Priya/Pixel admits her packing error and preserves the exact stone; Jordan/Scout interrupts self-blame with the actual dog, then learns to follow its pause; Lena/Mooncake's museum travels rather than being tidied away; Samir/Bean's broken-up hello is warmer than a perfect speech.

The pair scenes also have distinct dynamics: Maya/Mateo keeps the question pocket; Amina/Jordan shares quiet and a blanket corner; Eli/Priya turns disagreement into collaborative making; Lena/Samir permits a whispered museum introduction; Maya/Lena learns to wait while listening; Mateo/Eli needs a second experiment; Amina/Samir uses the squeaky backpack as a joke about a quiet entrance; Priya/Jordan makes room for the rest of the sentence. Only these eight pairs have special material, not all twenty-eight combinations. Solo check-ins mean that is not a solo-character exclusion. Pair material remains optional, so a child can miss substantial character development without obvious prompting.

All sixteen Companion triptychs were read. Marmalade's interrupted paw wash, Wisp's sandwich outline, Crumbula's trapped cape, Boggle's cushion shade, Bones considering one throwing arm, Pipkin's seed staring contest, Taffy's spoon rescue, Truffle's considered fourth leaf, Hush's lagging shadow, Mopsy choosing the lumpy cushion, Drizzle's overshooting bucket, Pudding's blanket-shaking snore, Wink's added curtain, Crinkle's waterless boat, Mossbit's unhurried crumb, and Brambleboo turning away from too-close light are specific animal behaviors. Sanctuary beats preserve their unusual bodies and preferences. They do not turn them into humans delivering lessons. This is some of the strongest writing in the game.

The main individuality limitation lies in regional Kid quips: several children sound like equally polished commentators on administrative absurdity. Their personal/pair material is more differentiated. Keep more of those concrete habits in regional reactions rather than giving every available speaker a perfectly compressed punchline.

## Tutorial, skip, state, and replay checks

Opening has a selected child's personal reason, shared preparation, the impossible return hallway, ghost cat, actual practice Scuffle, retry/skip, and a fresh expedition. It does not claim practice damage persists. Skip sends the user to selection with any chosen Kid retained; the narrative router supplies the search and Companion-versus-pet distinction on the actual run. It is not necessary to read a manuscript to learn the objective.

Coach waits on actual card play/end-turn events, remembers actions done early, can be dismissed, and offers Help. Help now opens with three topic groups and allows direct access to Nerve, Haunt, keyboard, and piles. Supplied tutorial verification reports real-browser pointer/keyboard/controller-navigation checks, focus/input isolation, fitting footer controls, and no page errors. That is credible functional evidence; it does not establish how well a seven-year-old understands Courage versus Nerve after TUT-002.

`petState`/`markPet` preserve unknown → found → home. A lost run does not revoke found; a returning found pet has its own scene rather than an impossible first reunion. Home pets are not rescued again. Pets are not sent into the Keeper fight, and no ending requires curing, normalizing, sacrificing, or permanently losing them. Companion bodies remain supernatural. Quiet sanctuary visits let other children find and take home pets after the gate opens. Memory-run framing is persisted and explains why later expeditions can revisit an earlier closed House.

Supplied runtime tests cover migration, future-save refusal, seventeen-region packages, unique units, skipped-opening recap, absent-Kid filtering, no narrative RNG consumption, real run snapshot/resume, boss routing, repeat scenes, failed-run reunion retention, home-state monotonicity, refused/backend writes, pending finale resume, peaceful routes, and all-home milestone. The additional investigation tests cover optional guesses, either test order, reopening, focus, revised ideas, earned guardian response, and historical records without remote physical checks. I found no contrary pet-state claim in the code inspected. Network play deliberately avoids reading-speed-gated local story scenes and retains finale/context; this is a different narrative experience and should not be described as identical to local play.

The score reflects the work as inspected. The main path to a higher score is stronger evidence progression and a few moments where the player physically helps make a better arrangement. More eloquent statements of the existing lesson would not solve those limitations.
