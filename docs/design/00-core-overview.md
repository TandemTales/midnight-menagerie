# Core Overview: premise, loop, kids, backpack, floors, bosses, progression

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 1-691_

Midnight Menagerie is a solo and cooperative deckbuilding roguelike about a group of neighborhood kids whose pets have disappeared. After hearing generations of rumors that animals vanish around an abandoned mansion, they enter to search for them and discover a living supernatural house filled with strange animal companions. These creatures were once lost pets themselves, transformed after spending too long inside. Choose a persistent Kid with customizable Backpack equipment, pair them with one rescued Menagerie Companion whose unique card pool defines your deck, and repeatedly explore a mansion that rearranges itself with every expedition. Rescue the sixteen Menagerie Companions, uncover the path to the Heart of the House, and find your missing pets before the mansion changes them forever.
Midnight Menagerie is about a group of neighborhood kids whose pets have gone missing. Each disappearance happened under different circumstances, but all of the kids have heard the same local rumor:
Animals that wander near the old abandoned mansion sometimes never come back.
None of them knows whether the rumor is true. None has proof that their own pet went there.
But after comparing stories, the kids realize that too many neighborhood pets have disappeared near the mansion for it to be coincidence.
So they get together, gather whatever equipment they can carry, and enter the mansion looking for them.
What they discover is much stranger than they expected.
The mansion really does collect lost animals.
And if an animal remains inside for long enough, the house gradually changes it into something supernatural.
Those transformed animals are the Menagerie Companions.
That discovery completely changes the children's mission.
They are no longer merely trying to find their pets.
They are racing to find them before the mansion changes them permanently.
1. The central gameplay structureEvery expedition is built around:
## Kid + Menagerie Companion
The player chooses one Kid and one unlocked Companion before entering the mansion.
The Kid is the persistent RPG character.
The Companion determines the player's deck and combat strategy for that expedition.
The Kid answers:
What am I bringing into the mansion?
The Companion answers:
How am I going to survive it?
With 8 Kids and 16 Companions, the base game already provides 128 Kid and Companion combinations before considering Backpack equipment, cards, Keepsakes, difficulty settings, and other systems.
2. Why the kids enter the mansionThe eight playable kids live in the same neighborhood or surrounding area.
Over the past several months, each has lost a pet.
A dog slipped its leash.
A cat disappeared overnight.
A rabbit escaped its enclosure.
A parrot flew away.
Another pet simply vanished from a backyard.
Individually, none of these incidents seems supernatural.
Then the kids begin talking.
Each has heard some version of the same neighborhood story:
Animals disappear around the old mansion.
Some older residents remember pets disappearing there when they were children.
There are stories of barking coming from behind boarded windows.
People claim to have seen cats watching from upstairs rooms even though nobody has lived there for years.
Children tell stories about strange animals appearing around the property at night.
Adults mostly dismiss it as local folklore.
The kids do not.
They compare when and where their pets disappeared and conclude that the mansion is their best lead.
That is why they enter.
They are not thrill seekers.
They are not investigating because it is Halloween.
They have a concrete reason to take the risk.
They want their pets back.
3. The first expeditionAt the beginning of the game, the children still believe they are entering an abandoned mansion.
They bring flashlights, backpacks, snacks, tools, first aid supplies, and whatever else they think might help them search.
They expect:
Broken floors.
Dark rooms.
Possibly squatters.
Maybe wild animals.
They do not expect the mansion to be alive.
Soon after entering, the doors close.
Hallways shift.
Rooms appear where they should not exist.
The group encounters one of the starting Menagerie Companions.
Perhaps Marmalade is the first.
The kids initially assume Marmalade is simply a ghost cat.
Eventually they discover something much more important.
Marmalade was once an ordinary cat.
4. What the Menagerie actually areThe Menagerie Companions are animals that have spent so long inside the mansion that the supernatural influence of the house has transformed them.
The transformation is different for every animal.
A cat might become a ghost.
A chinchilla might become a tiny vampire.
A dog might become a skeleton puppy.
A frog might become a living pumpkin creature.
A hedgehog might become a harmless zombie.
A turtle might become partially fused with an ancient gravestone.
The mansion seems to transform each animal according to some combination of:
Its personality.
The rooms where it spends its time.
Objects it becomes attached to.
Its fears.
Its habits.
The supernatural forces surrounding it.
The result is not necessarily horrible.
Many of the Menagerie are adorable, friendly, intelligent creatures.
But they are also trapped.
And after enough time has passed, they cannot simply turn back into ordinary animals.
5. The central urgencyThis gives the story a clear ticking clock.
The kids' missing pets might still be normal.
They might be somewhere inside the mansion right now.
But the longer they remain there, the greater the chance that the mansion will begin changing them.
At first the children do not know how long transformation takes.
They encounter evidence of different stages.
A normal dog behaving strangely.
A cat whose paws occasionally become translucent.
An animal beginning to understand human speech.
A creature developing a supernatural ability.
Eventually, full transformation produces a Menagerie Companion.
The kids therefore have two related objectives:
Rescue the existing Menagerie.
Find their own pets before they become part of it.
6. Why the mansion takes animalsThe house should not simply be an evil animal stealing machine.
Its motivation is more interesting if it originally believed it was helping them.
The mansion has spent decades taking in:
Lost animals.
Abandoned pets.
Injured strays.
Animals escaping abuse.
Animals separated from their families.
From the house's perspective, it is providing them with shelter.
Inside the mansion:
They never starve.
They never grow old normally.
Predators cannot reach them.
Bad owners cannot hurt them.
The outside world cannot take them away.
The problem is that the mansion has lost the ability to distinguish protection from possession.
Once an animal enters its care, the house does not want to let it leave.
Over time, its supernatural influence changes the animal so that it can exist permanently inside the mansion.
The Menagerie are therefore the house's idea of animals that have been made "safe forever."
7. Why the Companions help the kidsThe transformed animals have different opinions about the mansion.
Some have been there for years.
Some have been there for decades.
Some barely remember being ordinary animals.
But many understand that the newly arrived pets still have a chance to escape.
The Companions therefore help the children explore.
Mechanically, this is why each Kid enters an expedition with one Menagerie Companion.
Narratively, the Companion knows things the Kid does not.
Hidden passages.
Dangerous rooms.
Mansion creatures.
Supernatural rules.
Ways of manipulating the house.
The Kid provides determination and equipment.
The Companion provides supernatural abilities.
They need each other.
8. Companion rulesThere are 16 Menagerie Companions in the base game.
The player chooses exactly one Companion at the beginning of an expedition.
That Companion remains with the player for the entire run.
Companions cannot normally be switched during an expedition.
Multiple Companions do not accompany a single player simultaneously.
The Companion is effectively the player's combat archetype.
Each has:
A unique starting deck.
A unique card pool.
One or more exclusive mechanics.
Several possible deckbuilding strategies.
Unique dialogue.
Personal history.
A relationship with the mansion.

10. Companion decksChoosing Marmalade does not immediately give the player every Marmalade card.
Instead, every Companion has a starting deck.
For example, Marmalade might begin with approximately 10 cards consisting of basic attacks, defensive cards and several cards demonstrating Marmalade's core mechanics.
The rest of Marmalade's cards belong to the larger Marmalade Card Pool.
Cards are acquired during the expedition.
As a result, two Marmalade runs can develop very differently.
One might emphasize Nine Lives.
Another might become an Evasion deck.
Another might rely on spectral counterattacks.
Another might focus on rapidly cycling cards.
The same principle applies to every Companion.
11. Unlocking CompanionsThe player does not initially have access to all sixteen.
Approximately four are available when beginning a new save.
The remaining Companions appear as dark silhouettes on the Menagerie selection screen.
They must be discovered inside the mansion.
Finding one does not allow the player to replace their current Companion during the expedition.
Instead:
Rescuing a Companion permanently unlocks that Companion for future expeditions.
This makes Menagerie rescue part of the game's campaign progression without undermining the deckbuilding structure.
12. What rescuing a Companion meansThe word "rescue" does not necessarily mean returning it to normal.
Some Menagerie have been transformed for far too long.
Instead, the children might release them from whatever part of the mansion currently controls or imprisons them.
A Companion might be:
Trapped inside a mirror.
Bound to a nursery.
Locked inside a supernatural cage.
Unable to cross a certain doorway.
Controlled by one of the mansion's guardians.
After being freed, the Companion can reach the children's safe area outside the mansion and accompany them on later expeditions.
This makes rescued Companions part of the growing cast.

The others are silhouettes.

14. Why finding Menagerie matters to the missing petsEvery Companion knows something.
One remembers a section of the mansion.
Another knows how certain doors work.
Another remembers seeing ordinary animals deeper inside.
Another knows what happens during transformation.
Another remembers the creature or force responsible for preventing animals from leaving.
Collectively, the sixteen Menagerie hold enough knowledge to reach somewhere the children cannot access at the beginning.
## The Heart of the House
This is where the mansion keeps the newest animals.
Including, potentially, the children's missing pets.
The Menagerie are therefore not arbitrary collectibles.
Each rescue brings the children closer to their actual goal.
15. The KidsThere are approximately eight playable Kids.
Every Kid has lost a pet.
Their circumstances should be different enough that they feel like individual stories rather than interchangeable protagonists.
For example:
One lost a dog that escaped through a broken gate.
One's indoor cat somehow vanished despite every door being closed.
One lost a rabbit while moving houses.
One's elderly dog disappeared during a storm.
One's bird flew toward the mansion and never returned.
One's pet disappeared months earlier and the family has stopped believing it will come home.
One saw their pet near the mansion after being told it had probably been stolen.
One recently lost a pet and convinced the others that they should finally investigate together.
The kids discover one another through missing pet posters, school conversations, neighborhood social media, parents talking, or simply recognizing that several families have been searching for animals.
16. Each Kid has a specific missing petThe player's chosen Kid has a specific animal they are trying to rescue.
This should be visible throughout the game.
The Kid's character screen might include:
A photograph.
Name.
Species.
Description.
Date missing.
Where they were last seen.
A handwritten note from the Kid.
For example:
## Lucy
Missing Pet: Baxter
Golden retriever.
Last seen three weeks ago near the eastern woods behind the mansion.
Lucy keeps Baxter's old collar in her Backpack.
This immediately gives the character a personal objective.
17. Kid progressionUnlike Companion decks, Kids persist between expeditions.
A Kid retains:
Experience.
Level.
Unlocked skills.
Backpack capacity.
Equipment.
Cosmetics.
Personal story progression.
Mansion discoveries.
Potential upgrades.
The Kid therefore represents the long term RPG progression layer.
The Companion represents the roguelike deckbuilding layer.

Backpack items persist between runs once discovered or unlocked.
Examples:

Glow sticks.
Compass.
Dog whistle.
Notebook.

Blanket.
Thermos.
Spare batteries.
Different equipment supports different Companion strategies and exploration approaches.
# 19. Backpack limitations
The Backpack has limited capacity.
A Kid cannot bring everything.
For example:
Small item: 1 slot.
Medium item: 2 slots.
Large item: 3 slots.
A new Kid might have 5 total slots.
Progression might eventually increase this to 8.
This creates loadout decisions before every expedition.
20. Backpack items can interact with the storyBecause the kids are actually searching for missing animals, mundane equipment can have meaningful uses.
Examples:
### Dog Whistle
Can reveal hidden canine creatures.
May provide special dialogue with Bones or Pudding.
### Pet Treats
Can calm frightened ordinary animals.
May produce alternate Curiosity outcomes.
### Camera
Can photograph supernatural clues.
May reveal things invisible to the naked eye.
### Familiar Toy
A Kid can bring something belonging to their missing pet.
In certain rooms it might cause the pet to leave clues behind.
### Collar or Tag
May interact with supernatural tracking systems.
This makes Backpack customization directly relevant to the story rather than simply providing combat bonuses.
21. The investigation boardThe children's persistent hub should emphasize that they are actively solving a mystery.
Their base could be:
A garage.
A basement.
A backyard shed.
A treehouse.
An unused room above someone's family shop.
The exact location can be decided later.
Inside is an enormous homemade investigation board.
It contains:
Missing pet photographs.
Maps.
Newspaper clippings.
Drawings of Companions.
Room sketches.
Polaroids.
Strings connecting clues.
Notes about mansion behavior.
Dates of disappearances.
Names of previous missing animals.
Every successful expedition visibly adds information.
22. Why repeated expeditions make senseThe kids cannot simply walk through the mansion once and search every room.
The mansion rearranges itself.
Every time they enter:
Hallways change.
Rooms move.
Entire wings disappear.
Previously locked doors open.
Staircases lead somewhere new.
The house reacts differently to different Kids and Companions.
This explains the procedural map generation.
The children are repeatedly exploring the same supernatural mansion, but the structure is never quite the same.
23. Modular room generationIndividual rooms are authored separately.
At the beginning of each floor, the game constructs a randomized branching map from them.
Room categories include:
Combat Rooms.
Big Scares.
Curiosities.
Shops.
Blanket Forts or Safe Rooms.
Treasure Rooms.
Companion Encounters.
Clue Rooms.
Secret Rooms.
Boss Rooms.
All routes eventually converge on the floor boss.
24. Safe RoomsBecause Halloween is no longer part of the premise, I would retain the Blanket Fort concept but make it something the kids deliberately construct during exploration.
The kids find a defensible room, barricade the door, drag furniture together, put blankets over tables, and create a temporary shelter.
The contrast remains strong:
Dangerous supernatural mansion outside.
Warm flashlight lit fort inside.
At a Blanket Fort the player might:
Rest.
Upgrade a card.
Repair Backpack equipment.
Bond with their Companion.
Help another player.
Review clues.
Use special Kid abilities.
25. ShopsThe Midnight Market can still exist because it does not require Halloween.
It becomes one of the strange supernatural institutions inside the mansion.
The merchant may accept something other than Halloween Candy as currency.
A better general currency could be:
## Lost Things
The mansion is filled with buttons, coins, keys, marbles, charms, bottle caps, jewelry, game pieces, and other small objects lost by generations of occupants.
Mr. Moth considers these incredibly valuable.
Alternatively, if you want to preserve the playful simplicity of Candy, it could simply be Treats or Snacks the kids bring and find rather than specifically Halloween candy.
I would lean toward Lost Things because it connects directly to the mansion's theme.

These can now lean more strongly into the missing animal mystery.
Examples:
## Scratching Behind the Wall

Leave food.
Walk away.
The creature might be harmless, hostile, a normal animal, or a future Companion.
The CollarA pet collar lies in the middle of an empty room.
Pick it up.

Leave it.

27. Stages of transformationThe game can gradually introduce a recognizable progression.
### Stage 1
The animal appears completely normal.
### Stage 2
Minor supernatural behavior.
Unusual eyes.
Strange shadows.
Understanding speech.
Unexplained longevity.
### Stage 3
Physical supernatural traits emerge.
Transparency.
Unusual coloration.
Magical abilities.
### Stage 4
The animal becomes a Menagerie creature.
### Stage 5
Long term inhabitants may begin forgetting their original lives.
This makes old Companions potentially poignant.
Some remember their owners clearly.
Others remember only fragments.
Some no longer want to return to being ordinary animals.
28. An important moral distinctionThe objective should probably not be to turn every Menagerie Companion back into a normal pet.
Many have spent decades or generations transformed.
Their supernatural form is now part of who they are.
Instead, rescuing them means giving them freedom.
The children learn to distinguish between:
Saving an animal from transformation
and
Treating an existing Companion as if there is something wrong with what it has become.
That distinction can give the story much more emotional depth.
Their own recently missing pets can still be rescued before the transformation becomes permanent.
29. Floor progressionA campaign could gradually open deeper mansion regions.
### The Foyer
The first evidence that the mansion is alive.
### The Crooked Upstairs
The kids discover that animals have lived here for generations.
### The Nursery
Old toys and transformed pets reveal the mansion's strange affection for its residents.
### The Conservatory
Plant based transformations and Brambleboo related lore.
### The Cellar
Evidence of older supernatural experiments or mechanisms.
### The Attic
Records of animals that disappeared decades ago.
### The Servants' Passages
Secret routes used by the Menagerie.
### The Clock Tower
Evidence that time inside the mansion behaves differently.
### The Heart of the House
Where the newest captured animals are protected.
30. BossesEach major boss should represent some aspect of the mansion's attempt to keep its animals safe.
For example:
## The Butler
Stops unauthorized visitors.
## The Governess
Attempts to return children and animals to their assigned rooms.
## The Groundskeeper
Prevents animals from crossing the property boundary.
## The Veterinarian
A disturbing but ultimately protective supernatural figure obsessed with keeping every creature "healthy."
## The Collector
Catalogues every animal the mansion has ever taken.
These enemies do not necessarily believe they are evil.
They believe the children are trying to remove animals from the safest place they could possibly be.
31. The sixteen Companion objectiveThe Menagerie selection screen begins mostly dark.
Perhaps only four Companions are initially playable.
As the children explore, they free more.
Eventually:
16 / 16 Companions Found
At this point something significant happens.
Each Companion possesses some piece of knowledge about the house.
When all sixteen are brought together, they collectively reveal how to access:
# The Heart of the House
The mansion has hidden this region even from most of its supernatural residents.
That is where recently acquired animals are kept.
32. The final levelThe Heart of the House becomes effectively the game's seventeenth major area.
It is unlocked by completing the base Menagerie collection and associated story progression.
Here the children finally find evidence of their own pets.
Some may actually appear during the level.
The player's selected Kid sees their missing pet for the first time since entering the mansion.
But reaching it requires confronting the intelligence controlling the house.
33. The mansion's argumentThe final antagonist should have a coherent philosophy.
The mansion essentially tells the Kid:
Your pet was lost.
I found it.
Your world loses animals every day.
People abandon them.
Cars hit them.
They become sick.
They grow old.
They die.
Inside me, none of that has to happen.
I can keep them safe.
Forever.
The mansion genuinely believes transformation is a gift.
From its perspective, the Menagerie are proof that its system works.
The children must demonstrate that:
Protection without freedom is still imprisonment.
That gives the final confrontation a thematic core beyond defeating a giant monster.
34. Rescuing the Kid's petCompleting the main story with a Kid allows that Kid to successfully rescue their pet before its transformation becomes permanent.
This should be an actual narrative reward.
The player sees the reunion.
The rescued animal appears at the hub afterward.
The Kid's dialogue changes.
Their personal investigation board changes.
The pet might occasionally react to particular Menagerie Companions it met inside.
The game acknowledges that the objective has genuinely been achieved.
35. Why that Kid can continue playingRescuing one Kid's pet does not end their involvement.
They still have seven friends whose pets may be trapped.
There are still Menagerie creatures inside.
There are unresolved secrets.
And the mansion continues attracting lost animals.
After their personal rescue, the Kid's motivation changes from:
I need to find my pet.
to:
Someone helped me bring mine home. Now I'm going back to help everyone else.
That is a natural reason to keep using a favorite character after finishing their personal story.
36. Multiple Kid campaignsThe eight Kids effectively provide eight overlapping personal campaigns.
A player could eventually rescue:
8 / 8 Missing Pets.
That can become another major long term progression goal.
The Menagerie objective and Kid objective are related but separate:
16 / 16 Menagerie Companions Freed
8 / 8 Missing Pets Rescued
Completing both could unlock the true campaign finale.
37. The final campaign endpointOnce every Kid has rescued their pet and all sixteen base Companions have been freed, the mansion's relationship with the neighborhood permanently changes.
Perhaps the final true objective is not destroying the mansion.
Instead, the children teach it another way to fulfill its purpose.
The house can become a genuine sanctuary for creatures that need one.
Animals that actually need protection can remain.
Animals with homes can leave.
The Menagerie can come and go freely.
The house becomes strange, haunted, dangerous, and occasionally troublesome, but no longer a prison.
That provides a satisfying conclusion without destroying the location that the entire franchise depends upon.
38. Higher difficulty progressionAfter completing a successful expedition, the player unlocks higher Haunt Levels.
These make subsequent runs more difficult.
Examples:
Haunt 1: Standard.
Haunt 2: Stronger enemies.
Haunt 3: Harder Curiosities.
Haunt 4: Additional boss abilities.
Haunt 5: More dangerous room combinations.
Higher levels continue escalating.
However, the basic story ending should not require completing the maximum difficulty.
Normal campaign completion should allow players to rescue pets.
Extreme Haunt Levels can instead unlock:
Special Companion variants.
Secret lore.
Alternate bosses.
Cosmetics.
Prestige rewards.
A hidden ending.

42. The emotional arcThe story begins with:
"Maybe our pets are inside that creepy old house."
Then becomes:
"Why are there supernatural animals living here?"
Then:
"These creatures used to be pets."
Then:
"Our pets could become like this if we don't find them."
Then:
"The mansion isn't trying to hurt them. It thinks it's saving them."
And finally:
"We have to save our pets, free the Menagerie, and teach the house that keeping someone safe doesn't mean keeping them forever."
the companions are effectively the living keys to the deepest part of the mansion.
When the house transforms an animal into a Menagerie Companion, it binds that creature to itself. Each Companion becomes connected to a particular part of the mansion, almost like the house has distributed pieces of its own identity among them.
The kids eventually discover that their missing pets are being kept in a protected inner region called something like the Heart of the House, where newly arrived animals are held while the transformation process begins.
The Heart cannot be reached through an ordinary hallway. The house deliberately hides it by constantly rearranging itself.
The sixteen existing Companions are the only creatures who have been inside long enough to understand different pieces of the house.
For example, Marmalade remembers doors that only appear to ghosts. Wink can see passages hidden behind walls. Mossbit remembers rooms that existed decades ago. Crinkle knows how the house changes its floor plan. Hush can travel through certain shadows. Brambleboo is connected to roots growing through the mansion itself.
Individually, none of them knows how to reach the Heart.
Together, they do.
Once all sixteen have been freed, their supernatural connections resonate with one another and effectively force the mansion to reveal its complete shape for the first time.
That gives you a very clean progression:
4 / 16 Companions rescued: The kids realize the Menagerie know things about the mansion.
8 / 16: They discover that each Companion is connected to a different part of the house.
12 / 16: They learn that their pets are probably being kept in a hidden central area.
15 / 16: They almost have enough information to locate it, but one section of the route is still impossible.
16 / 16: The complete route appears.
Then you unlock:
# THE HEART OF THE HOUSE
There is also a good story reason the kids cannot simply ignore some of them. The mansion has deliberately separated the Companions because it learned that when enough transformed animals gather together, they can resist its control.
So the house keeps them scattered.
Some are imprisoned.
Some are bound to particular rooms.
Some have forgotten that escape is possible.
Some are afraid of what will happen if they leave.
The kids slowly reunite them.
That makes rescuing all sixteen more than collecting characters. You are rebuilding a group the mansion intentionally broke apart.
I would also make one additional revelation important: the kids cannot rescue their own pets without rescuing the existing Companions first because abandoning those creatures would make them hypocrites. Once they understand what the Menagerie really are, their mission changes from:
"Find our pets."
to:
"Get every animal out that wants to leave."
Their pets are simply the newest members of a much older problem.
Tricks.
That gives you natural game language such as “Choose a Trick,” “Add a Trick to your deck,” “Upgrade a Trick,” “Marmalade’s Tricks,” and “Your Companion determines which Tricks you can discover.”
For internal card types, I would keep Attack, Skill, and Power for now because they communicate function clearly. Those labels can be renamed later if desired.
As of the current Slay the Spire 2 card structure, each of its five playable characters has 80 regular cards: 20 Common, 35 Uncommon, and 25 Rare. Multiplayer adds another five character specific cards, currently following a three Uncommon and two Rare structure.
So I would use exactly that structure for every Midnight Menagerie Companion.
