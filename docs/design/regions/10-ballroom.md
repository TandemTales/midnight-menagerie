# The Ballroom and Velvet Suites
Companion: Count Crumbula Boss: The Master of Revels Region identity: Temptation, bargains, Courage exchange, invitations, escalating rewards with costs, social pressure, and deciding when to accept power that may become dangerous
This region should feel seductive rather than immediately hostile.
The Ballroom is beautiful.
Music plays without musicians.
Tables are covered with cakes, fruit, crystal goblets, and silver trays.
Masked guests bow politely.
Velvet suites promise warmth and rest.
The danger is that almost everything here offers the player something desirable.
The central combat lesson is:
A good deal can still be a trap if the cost compounds.
That makes this the natural home for Count Crumbula, whose identity already revolves around feeding, temptation, Courage manipulation, vampirism, and risk versus reward.

# 2. Regional mechanic: Invitations
Several Ballroom enemies offer Invitations.
An Invitation is a temporary player choice.
The player may:
Accept
Gain an immediate benefit.
Also accept the listed cost.
Or:
Decline
Receive no benefit and no cost, unless the enemy specifically reacts to rejection.
Invitations should always show the complete terms before the player chooses.
No hidden consequences.
The region is about temptation, not tricking the player with missing information.
# 3. Dancing Shoe
Role: Escalating tempo enemy Courage: 25
A pair of elegant dancing shoes tap around the battlefield with no visible wearer.
### Core mechanic: Tempo
Dancing Shoe begins with 0 Tempo.
Maximum 3.
Whenever it attacks without taking damage during the previous player turn:
Gain 1 Tempo.
Each Tempo increases its attack damage by 2.
### Moves
Quickstep
Deal 5 damage plus 2 per Tempo.
Spin
Deal 3 damage twice plus 1 per Tempo on each hit.
Pause for Applause
Gain 8 Guard.
Lose 1 Tempo.
Minimum 0.
### Behavior
Quickstep.
Spin.
Quickstep.
Pause for Applause.
Repeat.
### Combat lesson
A small amount of pressure can prevent an enemy from snowballing.
This is a familiar concept presented in a more rhythmic form.
# 4. Masquerade Mask
Role: Buff copying enemy Courage: 31
A porcelain party mask floats through the room trailing ribbons.
It changes expression to imitate whoever currently looks strongest.
### Core mechanic: Mimicry
Whenever another enemy gains a positive effect:
Masquerade Mask copies a reduced version once per enemy turn.
Examples:
If another enemy gains Guard:
Mask gains half as much Guard.
If another enemy gains temporary attack damage:
Mask gains half, rounded down.
If another enemy gains a named non stackable buff:
Mask instead gains 5 Guard.
It cannot copy boss unique mechanics or permanent transformation states.
### Moves
Borrowed Smile
Deal 7 damage.
If Mimicry triggered this turn, deal 3 additional damage.
Change Face
Gain 9 Guard.
The next positive effect another enemy gains is copied at full value instead of half.
Mocking Bow
Deal 5 damage.
Another enemy gains 5 Guard.
This can intentionally trigger Mimicry.
### Pattern
Mocking Bow.
Borrowed Smile.
Change Face.
Borrowed Smile.
Repeat.
### Strategic effect
Enemy buffs can cascade.
The Mask often becomes more dangerous because of what its allies are doing.
# 5. Velvet Curtain
Role: Protector and target manipulation enemy Courage: 36
A huge red velvet curtain slides across the battlefield on an invisible rail.
### Core mechanic: Draw the Curtain
Velvet Curtain can protect one other enemy.
While protected:
That enemy receives 50 percent less damage from Attack Tricks.
Velvet Curtain receives the prevented damage.
The protected enemy remains fully targetable.
### Moves
Draw the Curtain
Protect one ally.
Gain 6 Guard.
Heavy Drape
Deal 8 damage.
Close the Show
Gain 12 Guard.
If currently protecting an enemy, that enemy gains 6 Guard.
### Breaking protection
If Velvet Curtain loses at least 14 Courage during one player turn:
Protection immediately ends.
### Pattern
Draw the Curtain.
Heavy Drape.
Close the Show.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The player is not forbidden from attacking the protected target.
They are choosing where the damage effectively goes.

### Sparkling Punch

No effect.
### Encore
Accept:
Draw 2 Tricks.
Party Phantom gains 1 Indulgence.
Decline:
No effect.
### Indulgence

### Moves

# 12. Encounter generation rules
Party Phantom should appear alone before being paired with Waltzing Armor.
Masquerade Mask cannot appear alone in normal Scuffles.
Two Party Phantoms cannot appear together at Haunt 0.
Two Velvet Curtains cannot protect each other.
Waltzing Armor cannot apply Encore to another Waltzing Armor at baseline difficulty.
Goblet Geist cannot offer Take a Sip when the player has 5 or less Courage.
Scuffle 14 belongs only to the advanced pool.
# 13. Big Scare: The Grand Masque
Courage: 143
Role: Changing identity and bargain adaptation
A giant floating mask rotates through several elaborate faces.
Each face represents a different social role.
### Core mechanic: Persona
The Grand Masque cycles between four Personas.
Host
Dancer
Critic
Guest
Each Persona changes its behavior.
## Host
Whenever the player accepts an Invitation:
Grand Masque gains 5 Guard.
### Host move
Welcome In
Offer:
Accept:
Draw 1 Trick and gain 1 Nerve this turn.
Then Grand Masque gains 1 Favor.
Decline:
No effect.
## Dancer
Whenever the player plays a fourth Trick in one turn:
Grand Masque gains 1 Tempo.
Each Tempo gives 2 attack damage.
Maximum 3.
### Dancer move
Perfect Step
Deal 8 damage plus 2 per Tempo.
## Critic
The first Trick type the player repeats consecutively each turn causes Grand Masque to gain 6 Guard.
Once per turn.
### Critic move
Harsh Review
Deal 11 damage.
If its defensive reaction triggered last turn, deal 4 additional damage.
## Guest
The Grand Masque becomes less threatening.
It takes 20 percent additional damage.
But it makes the most tempting offer.
### Guest move
Your Turn
Accept:
Recover 6 Courage and draw 2 Tricks.
Grand Masque gains 2 Favor.
Decline:
Grand Masque loses 5 Courage.
### Favor
Each Favor gives Grand Masque 1 attack damage.
Maximum 6.
Favor persists across Personas.
### Persona change
After two actions in one Persona:
Use Change Mask.
Gain 6 Guard.
Move to the next Persona.
### Strategic identity
The player must continually reassess whether the current bargain is worth strengthening a future Persona.
# 14. Big Scare: The Eternal Dance
Role: Paired enemy coordination
This encounter consists of two enemies.
### The Lead
Courage: 74
### The Follow
Courage: 74
They dance together and share a rhythm.
### Core mechanic: Rhythm
A three beat cycle is always visible.
Beat 1.
Beat 2.
Beat 3.
Then repeat.
### Lead actions
Beat 1:
Opening Step
Deal 8 damage.
Beat 2:
Turn
Deal 5 damage twice.
Beat 3:
Finale
Deal 14 damage.
### Follow actions
Beat 1:
Support
Lead gains 7 Guard.
Beat 2:
Mirror
Repeat half the damage of Lead’s current action, rounded down.
Beat 3:
Catch
If Lead is alive, Lead gains 10 Guard.
If Lead is defeated, Follow instead deals 10 damage.
### Breaking Rhythm
If either dancer loses at least 18 Courage during one player turn:
The Rhythm becomes Broken for one enemy turn.
While Broken:
The other dancer’s support component does not function.
Examples:
Mirror fails.
Catch fails.
Guard support fails.
### When one dancer is defeated
The survivor becomes Solo.
Solo gains 3 attack damage.
But loses all cooperative effects.
### Strategic question
Focus one dancer?
Alternate burst damage to keep Rhythm Broken?
Spread damage and finish both near the same time?
# 15. Big Scare: The Velvet Host
Courage: 134
Role: Escalating bargain encounter
A tall faceless host in a deep velvet coat presides over a private suite.
Its entire fight is built around increasingly strong offers.
### Core mechanic: Hospitality
The Velvet Host has a meter called Hospitality.
Begins at 0.
Maximum 5.
Whenever the player accepts an offer:
Gain 1 Hospitality.
Offers become stronger as Hospitality rises.
So do the costs.
### Offer 1: Appetizer
At Hospitality 0 or 1.
Accept:
Recover 5 Courage.
Host gains 1 Hospitality.
Decline:
No effect.
### Offer 2: Fine Drink
At Hospitality 2.
Accept:
Gain 2 Nerve this turn.
Lose 4 Courage.
Host gains 1 Hospitality.
### Offer 3: Private Performance
At Hospitality 3.
Accept:
Draw 3 Tricks.
All Tricks drawn this way cost 1 additional Nerve this turn.
Host gains 1 Hospitality.
### Offer 4: Anything You Want
At Hospitality 4.
Choose one:
Gain 12 Guard.
Or draw 3 Tricks.
Or gain 2 Nerve.
Then:
Lose 7 Courage.
Host reaches Hospitality 5.
### Hospitality 5
The Host stops offering bargains.
It gains:
Overindulged
Attack damage plus 5.
At the start of its turn, gain 8 Guard.
### Other moves
Velvet Cane
Deal 10 damage.
Toast the Guest
Deal 6 damage.
If Hospitality is 3 or more, deal 5 additional damage.
Another Round
Present the appropriate offer.
### Pattern
Another Round.
Velvet Cane.
Toast the Guest.
Repeat.
### Important strategic possibility
The player can simply refuse every offer.
But doing so means giving up substantial value.
A skilled player may accept exactly two or three bargains and stop before the Host reaches its most dangerous state.
# 16. The Master of Revels
Boss Courage: 375
The Master of Revels is an elegant supernatural host wearing a crimson tailcoat and a porcelain smile.
Its face periodically changes beneath the mask, but never enough to reveal what is underneath.
It carries a silver tray in one hand and a conductor’s baton in the other.
Music responds to its gestures.
Tables refill themselves when it looks at them.
It considers the mansion’s captives honored guests.
Its philosophy is:
If someone is comfortable enough, why would they ever want to leave?
That makes it a direct expression of the mansion’s central flaw.
# 17. Core boss mechanic: The Banquet
Beside the Master of Revels are three serving stations:
The Goblet
The Plate
The Dance Card
At the beginning of certain player turns, one becomes available.
The player may accept or decline the offer.
Accepting gives immediate power.
It also gives the boss Revelry.
Maximum 8 Revelry.
## The Goblet
Accept:
Lose 4 Courage.
Gain 2 Nerve this turn.
Master gains 1 Revelry.
## The Plate
Accept:
Recover 6 Courage.
Master gains 1 Revelry.
## The Dance Card
Accept:
Draw 2 Tricks.
One of them costs 0 Nerve this turn.
Master gains 1 Revelry.
Declining any offer:
No immediate effect.
# 18. Revelry
Revelry represents how much the player has participated in the party.
At specific thresholds, the Master gains new bonuses.
### 2 Revelry
Gain 5 Guard whenever an offer is accepted.
### 4 Revelry
Damaging moves deal 2 additional damage.
### 6 Revelry
At the beginning of the enemy turn, gain 6 Guard.
### 8 Revelry
The Master becomes:
Perfect Host
Its next action becomes Never Leave.
# 19. Never Leave
Deal 18 damage.
Apply Comfortable.
### Comfortable
The first Trick the player plays next turn costs 1 less Nerve.
The third Trick played that turn costs 1 more Nerve.
Both modifiers disappear after triggering.
Then:
Reduce Revelry from 8 to 5.
### Why this works
Even the boss’s punishment still contains a small benefit.
That makes the whole region feel seductively strange rather than purely punitive.
# 20. Reducing Revelry
The player can actively lower Revelry.
Whenever the Master loses at least 25 Courage during one player turn:
Reduce Revelry by 1.
Once per turn.
This is called:
Spoiling the Mood
The Master has a visible reaction.
Music skips.
Candles flicker.
Guests vanish momentarily.
### Strategic result
A high damage deck can accept more bargains because it can periodically reduce Revelry.
A low damage scaling deck may want to refuse more offers.

### Grand Waltz

### More for Everyone

# 27. Phase two bargain pressure
Phase one bargains occur in predictable places.
Phase two introduces offers at awkward times.
The player may be low on Courage and desperately want Plate.
But accepting could push Revelry to 8 and trigger Never Leave.
That is the intended tension.
# 28. Breaking the party
Whenever the player destroys The Admirer or The Chaperone:
The Master loses 5 Courage.
Revelry decreases by 1.
This represents the party atmosphere collapsing.
If both are destroyed:
The music audibly deteriorates.
The boss becomes visibly less composed.
# 29. Final escalation
At 80 Courage or less:
The Master gains:
Last Dance
Offers stop appearing.
Revelry becomes locked at its current value.
The fight becomes a straightforward finale.
### Last Dance effects
For every 2 current Revelry:
The Master gains 1 attack damage.
Maximum 4.
Whenever the Master loses 20 or more Courage during one player turn:
It loses an additional 3 Courage.
Once per turn.
### Why stop the bargain system?
The final stretch should resolve the choices already made rather than introduce more accounting.
The player now lives with how much Revelry they accumulated.
# 30. Crumbula home region synergy
Count Crumbula should feel especially expressive here.
### Courage as resource
Goblet offers are immediately legible to a player already accustomed to spending Courage for value.
### Feeding
Crumbula may be able to recover Courage more effectively, making certain bargains more attractive.
### Temptation
A Crumbula deck may already contain self imposed offers or risk mechanics.
The player is therefore comfortable evaluating costs.
### Vampirism
The ability to regain Courage through offense may let Crumbula sustain a higher Revelry strategy than other Companions.
### Risk and reward
The region should not become easier for Crumbula.
It should become more interesting.
# 31. Boss narrative outcome
### Count Crumbula not yet rescued
The Master of Revels adores Crumbula.
Crumbula is treated like a permanent honored guest.
Tiny velvet cushions.
Endless snacks.
A little formal cape.
Special invitations.
Everything Crumbula could want.
Except permission to leave.
The Master cannot understand why that is a problem.
Defeating it allows Crumbula to choose when the party is over.
### Crumbula already rescued and currently active
This becomes Crumbula’s Legacy homecoming.
The thematic statement is:
Being given everything you want is not freedom if you are not allowed to say no.
### Another Companion active
Normal boss reward.
No Crumbula Legacy advancement.
# 32. Multiplayer scaling
Use the established baseline:
2 players: 160 percent Courage
3 players: 210 percent
4 players: 255 percent
The Ballroom should be one of the most socially interactive multiplayer regions because different players can make different bargain decisions.
# 33. Dancing Shoe multiplayer
Tempo checks whether any player damaged it during the team round.
If nobody did:
Gain Tempo.
Quickstep targets one player.
At 3 Tempo:
Spin distributes hits among two players if possible.
# 34. Masquerade Mask multiplayer
Mimicry remains global.
It can copy buffs gained by any allied enemy.
Its attacks target one player.
# 35. Velvet Curtain multiplayer
The protection threshold scales with party size.
Breaking protection requires total team damage:
2 players: 22
3 players: 29
4 players: 36
# 36. Party Phantom multiplayer
Each Invitation is offered to one specific player.
Targets rotate when possible.
Only that player’s acceptance grants Indulgence.
This prevents one bargain loving player from making every decision for the team.
# 37. Goblet Geist multiplayer
Take a Sip is offered to one player.
If accepted:
Only that player loses Courage and gains Exhilarated.
# 38. Waltzing Armor multiplayer
Encore marks the next enemy attack and its intended target.
Players therefore know who may need protection.
# 39. Grand Masque multiplayer
Offers rotate among players.
Favor remains global.
Persona reactions use team behavior where appropriate.
For example, Dancer checks total team Trick count.
Critic checks each player’s own consecutive Trick types separately.
# 40. Eternal Dance multiplayer
Lead and Follow attack different players when possible.
Rhythm breaking uses team damage to either dancer.
Thresholds:
2 players: 28
3 players: 37
4 players: 45
# 41. Velvet Host multiplayer
Each player has their own Hospitality meter.
This is important.
One player may accept several offers while another refuses everything.
The Host’s global attack bonus uses the highest Hospitality among all players.
At Hospitality 5, Overindulged triggers only for that player’s relationship with the Host.
This creates genuine team discussion about who is taking the risky bargains.
# 42. Master of Revels multiplayer Banquet
Each player has their own Revelry meter.
The boss also has a separate global Party Mood.
### Personal Revelry
Tracks each player’s accepted offers.
Threshold effects that affect that player use their personal Revelry.
### Party Mood
Gain 1 whenever any player accepts an offer.
Maximum:
2 players: 10
3 players: 13
4 players: 16
At Party Mood milestones, the Master gains global bonuses.
This prevents one player’s decisions from being completely isolated from the party.
# 43. Multiplayer Party Mood thresholds
At approximately 25 percent maximum:
Master gains 5 Guard whenever any player accepts.
At 50 percent:
Boss attacks gain 2 damage.
At 75 percent:
Boss gains 7 Guard at the start of its turn.
At maximum:
Use Everyone Stay
Deal moderate damage to all players.
Then reduce Party Mood by approximately one third.
# 44. Multiplayer cooperation opportunities
One player may take risky Goblet offers because their Companion can restore Courage.
Another may deliberately refuse invitations and focus The Chaperone.
A defensive player can help protect someone whose high personal Revelry makes them a priority target.
The party may intentionally feed offers to one build that benefits most from them.
A burst damage player can Spoil the Mood after teammates accept several bargains.
The Ballroom should produce legitimate conversations like:
“Take the Nerve. I can knock Revelry back down.”
That is exactly the cooperative texture the region wants.
# 45. Haunt Level progression
### Haunt 1
Ordinary enemies gain approximately 8 percent Courage.
Big Scares and boss gain approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced Ballroom formations appear earlier.
### Haunt 3
Dancing Shoe begins advanced encounters with 1 Tempo.
### Haunt 4
Masquerade Mask copies 60 percent of numerical buffs instead of half.
Round down.
### Haunt 5
Velvet Curtain’s protection break threshold increases from 14 to 17.
### Haunt 6
Party Phantom can reach 5 Indulgence.
### Haunt 7
Goblet Geist’s Take a Sip offer becomes:
Lose 5 Courage.
Gain Exhilarated plus draw 1 Trick.
This makes the temptation stronger rather than merely increasing the penalty.
### Haunt 8
Waltzing Armor’s Encore grants 6 additional damage instead of 4.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares gain upgrades.
Grand Masque begins with 1 Favor.
Eternal Dance begins on Beat 2.
Velvet Host begins at 1 Hospitality.
### Haunt 10
The Master of Revels begins combat with 1 Revelry already accumulated.
The opening Plate offer can therefore push the player immediately to the first threshold.
# 46. Higher Haunt behavior upgrades
Potential later upgrades include:
Dancing Shoe can transfer Tempo to another Dancing Shoe.
Masquerade Mask can copy one player buff in simplified form.
Velvet Curtain can move protection between allies.
Party Phantom can offer a choice between two bargains with different costs.
Goblet Geist can offer increasingly powerful Sips.
Waltzing Armor can switch the target of an ally’s attack.
Grand Masque can reorder Persona transitions.
Velvet Host may offer a rare bargain that modifies the rest of the encounter.
Master of Revels may offer one personalized bargain based on the active Companion’s mechanics.
That last concept should only appear at high Haunt because bespoke Companion interactions are substantially more complex.
# 47. Ballroom themed run reward concepts
### Silver Dance Card
The first time each combat you play exactly 3 Tricks in a turn, draw 1 Trick.
### Empty Goblet
The first time each combat you lose Courage due to one of your own effects, gain 4 Guard.
### Velvet Ribbon
Whenever you accept an explicit Curiosity bargain with a stated cost, improve its positive reward slightly.
This would need global event rules.
### Masquerade Pin
The first temporary enemy debuff applied to you each combat lasts one fewer turn, minimum 1 turn.
### Party Favor
After winning a Scuffle without using a consumable, gain a small amount of extra run currency.
### Ballroom Key
Velvet suites, private lounges, locked party rooms, dressing rooms, balconies, and servant entrances become easier to access.
# 48. Ballroom Curiosity hooks
### The Endless Banquet
Eat.
Pack food for later.
Inspect the table.
Refuse everything.
Feed something to a Companion.

Enemy offers can create genuine advantages.

The correct answer to a bargain is not always refusal.
Courage can be deliberately exchanged for tempo or damage.

Damage redirection can make apparently obvious targets inefficient.
Player controlled escalation creates a different kind of difficulty from enemy controlled escalation.
Powerful rewards become dangerous when repeatedly accepted without an exit plan.
Consent requires the ability to refuse and the ability to leave.
Comfort and freedom are not the same thing.
