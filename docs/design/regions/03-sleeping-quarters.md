# The Sleeping Quarters
Companion: Boggle Boss: The Bedframe Beast Region identity: Hiding, ambushes, Fear, target uncertainty, delayed scares, and manipulating who can be attacked
The Sleeping Quarters should feel very different from the Nursery.
The Nursery is full of visible systems the player can understand and manipulate.
The Sleeping Quarters is about not being entirely sure what is safe.
Beds have things underneath them.
Wardrobes breathe.
Blankets move when nobody touches them.
Doors open into bedrooms that were not there a moment ago.
Enemies disappear, hide behind objects, prepare enormous attacks, and sometimes change what they intend to do based on the player's actions.
The central combat lesson is:
Not every threat should be attacked immediately, and not every visible target is the real threat.
This region is Boggle's natural home because Boggle understands hiding, Fear, ambushes, and dramatic scares better than anything else in the mansion.

# 2. Pillow Puff
Role: Introductory nuisance enemy Courage: 22
A floating pillow whose stuffing leaks into the air like sleepy fog.
It looks almost harmless.
That is intentional.
### Core mechanic: Drowsy
Pillow Puff can add Drowsy to the player's draw pile.
Drowsy:
Cost 1 Nerve.
Gain 4 Guard.
Remove this Trick from combat after playing it.
Drowsy is not completely negative.
It is inefficient, but it still does something useful.
This establishes an important design principle for Midnight Menagerie status Tricks.
Some enemy generated Tricks should create awkward choices rather than simply being dead cards.
### Moves
Puff
Deal 5 damage.
Feather Cloud
Add 1 Drowsy to the player's discard pile.
Gain 5 Guard.
Pillow Bonk
Deal 8 damage.
### Pattern
Feather Cloud.
Puff.
Pillow Bonk.
Repeat.
### Combat purpose
Pillow Puff introduces the region gently.
The player learns that Sleeping Quarters enemies can affect future turns without immediately threatening enormous damage.
# 3. Slipper Skitter
Role: Fast harassment enemy Courage: 24
A pair of slippers scuttles across the floor like two nervous mice.
### Core mechanic: Scurry
After Slipper Skitter attacks, it gains Scurry.
While it has Scurry:
The first Attack Trick targeting it during the next player turn deals 50 percent damage.
Rounded up.
After that Attack resolves, Scurry ends.
Skill and Power effects are unaffected unless they specifically deal targeted damage.
### Moves
Toe Stomp
Deal 4 damage twice.
Gain Scurry.
Heel Kick
Deal 7 damage.
Gain Scurry.
Hide Under the Bed
Gain 9 Guard.
Remove Scurry.
### Pattern
Toe Stomp.
Heel Kick.
Hide Under the Bed.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The player can still attack it.
It is simply inefficient to lead with the strongest single Attack.
Multi hit, indirect damage, setup, or simply using a weaker Attack first may be preferable.
This begins teaching target timing.
# 4. Wardrobe Guest
Role: Hiding enemy Courage: 36
Something lives inside the wardrobe.
The player sees only a long arm, an eye between the doors, or a coat moving when there is no breeze.
### Core mechanic: Hidden
Wardrobe Guest alternates between Exposed and Hidden.
While Hidden:
It cannot be targeted by Attack Tricks.
It can still be affected by:
Area damage
Random target effects
Statuses already attached to it
Effects specifically capable of reaching Hidden enemies
It can also still be targeted by non damaging effects if those effects explicitly allow it.
### Moves while Exposed
Long Fingers
Deal 9 damage.
Back Inside
Gain 7 Guard.
Become Hidden.
### Moves while Hidden
Rustle
Gain 5 Guard.
Its next attack deals 3 additional damage.
Peek Out
Become Exposed.
Deal 5 damage.
### Pattern
Long Fingers.
Back Inside.
Rustle.
Peek Out.
Repeat.
### Important design rule
Hidden is not invulnerability.
It changes what kinds of interaction are available.
This distinction will become important for Hush's region later.
# 5. Blanket Creeper
Role: Defensive threat Courage: 39
A large blanket crawls across the floor by folding itself over and over.
Several lumps move beneath it.
### Core mechanic: Layers
Blanket Creeper begins combat with 3 Layers.
Each Layer grants:
4 Guard at the start of its turn.
When Blanket Creeper takes at least 10 damage during one player turn:
Remove 1 Layer.
Only one Layer can be removed per turn.
### Moves
Wrap Up
Gain 6 Guard.
Blanket Lash
Deal 8 damage.
Smother
Deal 5 damage.
The player draws 1 fewer Trick next turn.
Minimum draw remains 3.
### Behavior
If it has 2 or more Layers:
Wrap Up.
Blanket Lash.
Smother.
Repeat.
If it has 1 or fewer Layers:
Blanket Lash.
Smother.
Blanket Lash.
Repeat.
### Final Layer effect
When the final Layer is removed:
Blanket Creeper becomes Uncovered.
It permanently loses all automatic Layer Guard.
Its attacks deal 2 additional damage.
### Design purpose
Like Porcelain Doll, damaging this enemy changes it.
Unlike Porcelain Doll, the player is stripping away protection and revealing something more aggressive.
# 6. Nightlight Snuffer
Role: Support enemy Courage: 27
A tiny soot covered creature with a long brass snuffer for a nose.
It hates light.
### Core mechanic: Darkness
Nightlight Snuffer can place the battlefield into Darkness.
Darkness lasts until the beginning of Nightlight Snuffer's next turn.
While Darkness is active:
All other enemies deal 2 additional attack damage.
Hidden enemies gain 4 Guard when they become Hidden.
Nightlight Snuffer itself does not gain these benefits.
### Moves
Snuff
Activate Darkness.
Hot Wick
Deal 6 damage.
Flicker Out
Gain 8 Guard.
If Darkness is active, another enemy gains 6 Guard.
### Behavior
Snuff.
Hot Wick.
Flicker Out.
Hot Wick.
Repeat.
### Combat purpose
This enemy is a clean priority target.
However, the player may sometimes decide that another enemy's immediate telegraphed attack is more important.
# 7. Thing Beneath
Role: Major telegraphed threat Courage: 45
The player never sees the whole creature.
Only claws beneath a bed.
A pair of eyes.
A hand disappearing behind a dust ruffle.
### Core mechanic: Scare
Thing Beneath accumulates Scare.
Maximum 3.
Its most dangerous attack becomes stronger based on Scare.
### Moves
Scratch Scratch
Gain 1 Scare.
Gain 7 Guard.
Grab an Ankle
Deal 6 damage.
Gain 1 Scare.
UNDER THE BED
Deal 8 damage plus 6 per Scare.
Remove all Scare.
### Maximum attack
At 3 Scare:
26 damage.
### Interrupting Scare
If Thing Beneath loses at least 15 Courage during a single player turn:
Remove 1 Scare.
This can occur once per turn.
### Pattern
Scratch Scratch.
Grab an Ankle.
Scratch Scratch.
UNDER THE BED.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
This is the region's clearest slow building threat.
The player has several turns to respond.
The correct response may be:
Attack it early.
Save Guard.
Apply weakening effects.
Burst it before the scare.
Or deliberately accept the attack while setting up something stronger.
# 8. Fear as a regional enemy mechanic
Because Boggle uses Fear as part of its identity, the Sleeping Quarters should establish the broader world concept without making every enemy apply the exact same status.
I recommend a general combat status:
Frightened
When a player becomes Frightened:
The next Attack Trick they play deals 25 percent less damage.
Then Frightened is removed.
Frightened does not stack.
Some Sleeping Quarters enemies and Big Scares can apply it.
Boggle can interact with Fear much more extensively in its own Trick pool.
The region therefore introduces the concept without making it universal.

# 12. Encounter generation rules
Thing Beneath cannot appear before the player's second Sleeping Quarters Scuffle.
Nightlight Snuffer cannot appear in the first Scuffle.
Wardrobe Guest must be encountered in a simpler formation before it can appear alongside Nightlight Snuffer.
Scuffle 14 belongs only to the advanced pool.
Two Thing Beneath enemies cannot appear together at baseline difficulty.
Two Nightlight Snuffers cannot appear together.
Pillow Puff should become less common later in the region as more threatening enemies enter the pool.
# 13. Big Scare: The Night Terror
Courage: 104
Role: Intent uncertainty and reaction
The Night Terror changes shape depending on what the player expects it to be.
Long arms.
No face.
Too many teeth.
Then suddenly nothing except a shadow on the wall.
### Core mechanic: Uncertain Intent
The Night Terror sometimes displays two possible intents.
For example:
Possible intent A:
Deal 14 damage.
Possible intent B:
Gain 16 Guard and apply Frightened.
The player knows both possibilities.
The actual choice is determined after the player performs a specific trigger action.
### Move: Watching You
Displays two possible intents.
The first Trick the player plays determines which intent occurs.
If the first Trick is an Attack:
The Night Terror prepares Recoil.
Gain 14 Guard.
Apply Frightened.
If the first Trick is a Skill or Power:
The Night Terror prepares Lunge.
Deal 14 damage.
The actual intent updates immediately after that first Trick.
### Other moves
Long Shadow
Deal 8 damage twice.
Bedroom Corner
Gain 12 Guard.
Become Hidden until the beginning of its next turn.
Sudden Face
Deal 10 damage.
Apply Frightened.
### Pattern
Watching You.
Long Shadow.
Bedroom Corner.
Sudden Face.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The player is not dealing with pure randomness.
They control which branch occurs.
That is critical.
Uncertainty should create decisions, not coin flips.
# 14. Big Scare: The Blanket Hydra
Courage: 118
Role: Multi target transformation encounter
A giant pile of blankets rises from several beds at once.
Three covered heads emerge from beneath the fabric.
### Core mechanic: Blanket Heads
The Hydra begins with three Heads:
Snoring Head
Biting Head
Crying Head
Each Head has 24 Courage.
The Hydra's main body has 118 Courage.
Heads are separately targetable.
Damage to Heads does not damage the body.
### Snoring Head
Every second turn:
Add 1 Drowsy to the player's discard pile.
### Biting Head
Every turn:
Deal 5 damage.
### Crying Head
Every second turn:
The Hydra body gains 8 Guard.
### Regrowth
Whenever a Head is defeated:
The Hydra body loses 10 Courage.
After two enemy turns, that Head regrows with 12 Courage unless the Hydra body has fallen below 50 percent Courage.
Once below 50 percent:
Defeated Heads remain defeated.
### Body moves
Blanket Crush
Deal 10 damage.
Roll Over
Gain 12 Guard.
Tangle
Apply Frightened.
Gain 6 Guard.
### Strategic options
Destroy Heads repeatedly for body damage.
Ignore Heads and race the body.
Remove only the most troublesome Head.
Push the body below half Courage to stop regeneration.
This gives many deck styles valid approaches.
# 15. Big Scare: The Wardrobe
Courage: 132
Role: Hidden threat and encounter composition
This is not merely a creature inside a wardrobe.
The Wardrobe itself is alive.
Its doors lead somewhere much larger than the piece of furniture should contain.
### Core mechanic: Doors
The Wardrobe has two Doors.
Each Door has 26 Courage.
The Wardrobe body cannot be directly damaged while both Doors are closed.
### Door states
Closed
The Door is targetable.
The Wardrobe body is protected.
Open
The Door cannot be attacked.
The Wardrobe may release something from inside.
### Breaking a Door
When a Door reaches 0 Courage:
It breaks permanently.
Once at least one Door is broken:
The Wardrobe body becomes targetable.
If both Doors break:
The Wardrobe becomes fully exposed and takes 20 percent additional damage for the rest of combat.
### Moves
Creak Open
Open one unbroken Door.
Summon one temporary creature:
Slipper Skitter at 50 percent Courage.
Pillow Puff at 50 percent Courage.
Wardrobe Guest at 40 percent Courage.
Maximum two summoned creatures.
Slam Shut
Close all unbroken Doors.
Deal 8 damage.
Something Reaches Out
Deal 13 damage.
Empty Hangers
Apply Frightened.
Gain 10 Guard.
### Behavior
Creak Open.
Something Reaches Out.
Slam Shut.
Empty Hangers.
Repeat.
### Important rule
Summoned enemies disappear when The Wardrobe is defeated.
The player can therefore decide whether to clear them or focus the main threat.
# 16. The Bedframe Beast
Boss Courage: 295
At first, the boss room appears to be an enormous antique bedroom.
There is no obvious creature.
Then the four poster bed moves.
Its wooden legs unfold into long jointed limbs.
The mattress splits into a mouth.
The curtains become wings.
Something beneath the bed laughs.
The entire bed is the monster.
# 17. Bedframe Beast combat thesis
The fight is about:
Hiding.
Revealing.
Choosing when to attack.
Surviving enormous scares.
Manipulating where the boss is exposed.
Its central mechanic is:
Under the Bed
The Bedframe Beast periodically retreats into a defensive state and prepares a major ambush.
The player can either disrupt that preparation or use the time to prepare.
# 18. Boss mechanic: Exposure
The Bedframe Beast has three states:
Standing
Covered
Underneath
Each state changes how it behaves.
# 19. Standing
This is its normal combat state.
The Bedframe Beast is fully targetable.
### Standing moves
Bedpost Swipe
Deal 11 damage.
Spring Snap
Deal 5 damage twice.
Rattle the Frame
Gain 8 Guard.
Gain 1 Scare.
Maximum 3 Scare.
# 20. Covered
The Beast pulls its blankets around itself.
While Covered:
The first 12 damage it would take each player turn is reduced by 50 percent.
After that threshold is passed, damage returns to normal.
Covered lasts until its next action.
### Covered move
Pull the Covers Up
Gain 10 Guard.
Become Covered.
Prepare Retreat Underneath.
# 21. Underneath
The Beast disappears partially beneath the bed itself.
Yes, the bed goes underneath itself.
The architecture is impossible.
While Underneath:
Attack Tricks cannot target the Beast.
Area effects and effects that explicitly reach Hidden enemies may still affect it.
The Bedframe Beast gains Scare each turn while Underneath.
### Underneath moves
Scratching Below
Gain 1 Scare.
Add 1 Drowsy to the player's discard pile.
Footsteps Around the Bed
Gain 1 Scare.
Apply Frightened.
BOO
Exit Underneath.
Deal 9 damage plus 7 per Scare.
Remove all Scare.
# 22. Maximum BOO
At 3 Scare:
30 damage.
That should be among the largest single attacks the player has seen at this point in a baseline campaign.
However, the player gets substantial warning.
# 23. Pulling the Beast out early
Whenever the Bedframe Beast is Underneath:
If the player causes at least 18 indirect damage to it during one player turn through area effects, Haunt effects, retaliation, effects that can target Hidden enemies, or similar mechanics:
It is Dragged Out.
It immediately returns to Standing.
Lose 1 Scare.
Its next action becomes:
Disoriented
Gain 8 Guard.
Deal no damage.
This is intentionally difficult for some decks.
It is an optional answer, not a required mechanic.
Other decks simply prepare for BOO.
# 24. Phase one
From 295 through 161 Courage.
### Opening sequence
Bedpost Swipe.
Rattle the Frame.
Spring Snap.
Pull the Covers Up.
Retreat Underneath.
### Underneath sequence
Scratching Below.
Footsteps Around the Bed.
BOO.
Then return to Standing.
### Subsequent sequence
Spring Snap.
Bedpost Swipe.
Pull the Covers Up.
Underneath cycle.
Repeat.
# 25. Important interaction
The Scare accumulated by Rattle the Frame carries into the next Underneath cycle.
That means the player can see the dangerous ambush developing before the boss even hides.
If the Beast already has 1 Scare before retreating:
The player knows the coming BOO could reach maximum intensity quickly.
# 26. Phase transition
At 160 Courage:
The Bedframe Beast uses:
NO MORE HIDING
The mattress tears.
The frame grows larger.
The room's other beds drag themselves into the boss and merge with it.
Remove all existing Scare.
Gain 15 Guard.
Phase two begins.
# 27. Phase two mechanic: Moving Beds
Three smaller haunted beds appear around the room as abstract Bed Positions.
They are not separately targetable enemies.
They represent where the Beast can emerge.
The three positions are:
Left Bed
Center Bed
Right Bed
At the beginning of each player turn while the Beast is Underneath, one position is marked:
Rattling
The Beast will emerge there.
Each position corresponds to a different BOO variation.
# 28. Left Bed: Claw Ambush
When the Beast emerges:
Deal 7 damage three times.
This is dangerous against defenses that excel against one large hit.
# 29. Center Bed: Giant Scare
When the Beast emerges:
Deal 13 damage plus 5 per Scare.
This is the largest single hit version.
# 30. Right Bed: Grab and Drag
When the Beast emerges:
Deal 10 damage.
Add 2 Drowsy to the discard pile.
Apply Frightened.
This attacks future turn quality rather than raw Courage.
# 31. Manipulating emergence
During the player turn before emergence:
Playing the third Trick of the turn moves the Rattling position one step clockwise.
Playing the sixth Trick moves it one additional step.
The order is:
Left.
Center.
Right.
Left.
This creates a subtle sequencing puzzle.
Fast decks can deliberately reposition the ambush.
Slow decks simply prepare for the current one.
Boggle naturally has interesting interactions because many Boggle builds care about timing, Fear, and playing around enemy expectations.
# 32. Phase two moves
When Standing:
Splinter Swipe
Deal 13 damage.
Mattress Maw
Deal 6 damage twice.
Apply Frightened if both hits deal Courage damage.
Shake the Room
Gain 10 Guard.
Gain 1 Scare.
Dive Under
Become Underneath.
### Phase two sequence
Splinter Swipe.
Shake the Room.
Mattress Maw.
Dive Under.
Resolve Underneath cycle.
Repeat.
# 33. Underneath phase two duration
The Beast remains Underneath for only one full player turn in phase two.
At the start of its next action:
It immediately performs the ambush associated with the current Rattling position.
This makes phase two faster and more aggressive than phase one.
# 34. Why Boggle feels good here
Boggle should have several natural advantages without receiving arbitrary numerical bonuses.
### Hiding
Boggle understands hidden states better than other Companions.
Some Boggle Tricks may benefit from enemies being Hidden or from Boggle hiding itself.
### Fear
Frightened and Scare reinforce Boggle's thematic language.
A Boggle deck focused on Fear may turn the boss's own scary behavior into opportunities.
### Ambush
Boggle can potentially exploit the predictable moments when the Bedframe Beast emerges.
### Enemy targeting manipulation
If Boggle can manipulate enemy targeting or intentions, the phase two ambush choices become especially interesting.
### Large scare attacks
Defensive Boggle builds may prefer disappearing or redirecting rather than simply stacking Guard.
That can make the boss feel especially personal.
# 35. Boss narrative outcome
### Boggle not yet rescued
The Bedframe Beast has been keeping Boggle trapped beneath a bed because the mansion considers Boggle one of the dangerous things children need protection from.
This creates a nice reversal.
Boggle looks like the monster under the bed.
But the actual monster keeping Boggle imprisoned is the bed itself.
Defeating the boss frees Boggle.
### Boggle already rescued and currently active
This becomes Boggle's Legacy homecoming.
Boggle returns to defeat the thing that taught it that hiding was the only way to stay safe.
### Another Companion active
Normal regional boss reward.
No Boggle Legacy advancement.
# 36. Multiplayer scaling
Use the established baseline:
2 players:
160 percent Courage.
3 players:
210 percent.
4 players:
255 percent.
Damage values are adjusted selectively rather than universally.
# 37. Pillow Puff in multiplayer
Feather Cloud targets one player.
Prefer the player with the largest draw pile.
This makes Drowsy more likely to meaningfully affect that player's future cycle.
# 38. Slipper Skitter in multiplayer
Scurry applies separately to each player.
The first Attack from each player is reduced by 50 percent.
One player cannot remove Scurry for the entire team.
This keeps the mechanic relevant in larger groups.
# 39. Wardrobe Guest in multiplayer
Hidden remains global.
When Hidden, targeted Attack Tricks from all players fail to select it.
Area and special effects still work normally.
# 40. Blanket Creeper in multiplayer
Layer removal threshold becomes:
10 damage per player across the team round.
All players contribute.
Only one Layer can still be removed per round at baseline difficulty.
# 41. Nightlight Snuffer in multiplayer
Darkness increases enemy damage by 2 regardless of player count.
Its support effect remains globally dangerous.
# 42. Thing Beneath in multiplayer
UNDER THE BED targets all players.
Multiplayer damage:
6 plus 4 per Scare to each player.
Maximum at 3 Scare:
18 to each player.
The target is therefore the whole team.
# 43. Night Terror multiplayer
Watching You tracks the first Trick played by the entire team round.
Whichever player acts first determines the branch.
This creates communication.
However, that player is visually marked as:
Being Watched
so the team understands who will determine the outcome.
# 44. Blanket Hydra multiplayer
Heads scale Courage normally.
Each Head can choose separate players.
Snoring Head puts Drowsy into one player's discard pile.
Biting Head attacks the player with the lowest Guard.
Crying Head remains a global defensive support unit.
# 45. Wardrobe multiplayer
Maximum summons:
2 with one or two players.
3 with three players.
4 with four players.
The Wardrobe cannot summon duplicate Wardrobe Guests simultaneously.
# 46. Bedframe Beast multiplayer
The boss's hiding states remain global.
### Phase one BOO
Instead of one giant hit to everyone, the Beast selects one player as:
The One Looking Under the Bed
That player receives the full BOO.
All other players receive:
4 plus 3 per Scare damage.
The targeted player is shown one full round in advance.
This encourages teammates to protect the threatened player.
### Phase two Bed Positions
Each Bed Position affects players differently.
Left Bed:
Three hits distributed randomly among players, with at least one hit targeting the marked player.
Center Bed:
One large hit against the marked player.
Right Bed:
All players receive 1 Drowsy.
The marked player also becomes Frightened.
### Position manipulation
The entire team contributes to Trick count.
The third total Trick moves the Rattling position.
The sixth moves it again.
This creates genuine cooperative sequencing.
# 47. Haunt Level progression
### Haunt 1
Ordinary enemy Courage increases approximately 8 percent.
Big Scares and boss approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced encounter formations appear earlier.
### Haunt 3
Slipper Skitter begins advanced Scuffles with Scurry already active.
### Haunt 4
Pillow Puff's first Feather Cloud adds 2 Drowsy instead of 1.
Later uses remain normal.
### Haunt 5
Wardrobe Guest gains 8 Guard rather than 5 when Rustling while Hidden.
### Haunt 6
Blanket Creeper begins with 4 Layers instead of 3.
Only advanced encounters receive this change.
### Haunt 7
Nightlight Snuffer's Darkness grants 3 additional attack damage instead of 2.
### Haunt 8
Thing Beneath can hold 4 Scare.
Maximum UNDER THE BED becomes:
32 damage.
The player receives an additional visual warning when Scare reaches 4.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares gain upgraded mechanics.
Night Terror's Watching You may also react to Power as a separate branch.
Blanket Hydra regrows Heads one turn faster.
Wardrobe begins with one Door already open.
### Haunt 10
The Bedframe Beast begins combat with 1 Scare.
Its first retreat therefore becomes significantly more threatening.
# 48. Higher Haunt behavior upgrades
At higher mastery levels, behavior changes should include possibilities such as:
Wardrobe Guest may hide another enemy with it.
Nightlight Snuffer may extinguish a positive player battlefield effect.
Thing Beneath may delay UNDER THE BED for one turn, creating uncertainty about when the scare arrives.
Blanket Creeper may restore a Layer if completely ignored for several turns.
Night Terror may display three possible intents.
The Wardrobe may hide one of its summons inside itself and release it later.
The Bedframe Beast may occasionally choose its phase two Bed Position based on the player's previous behavior.
# 49. Sleeping Quarters themed run rewards
These should emphasize preparation, hiding, uncertainty, and surviving frightening turns.
### Underbed Flashlight
The first time each combat an enemy becomes Hidden, draw 1 Trick.
### Nightlight Bulb
At the beginning of the enemy turn, if you have no Guard, gain 4 Guard.
### Pillowcase Pocket
At the start of combat, draw 1 additional Trick.
Choose 1 Trick from your hand to place on the bottom of your draw pile.
### Monster Spray
The first time each combat an enemy prepares an attack of 15 or more damage, apply a small temporary damage reduction to that enemy.
The exact global weakening terminology can be established later.
### Bedroom Key
Secrets connected to bedrooms, wardrobes, closets, and sleeping areas become easier to discover during exploration.
### Favorite Pillow
The first Drowsy or similar enemy generated status Trick added to your deck each combat is automatically removed instead.
# 50. Sleeping Quarters Curiosity hooks
### The Bed With Someone's Name

The most recent carving is only days old.
### The Moving Wardrobe
Open it.
Barricade it.
Mark its location on the blueprint.
Climb inside.
### The Nightlight
It only turns on when nobody is looking directly at it.
### The Empty Pet Bed
The blanket still smells like one Kid's missing animal.
This can become a significant Clue room.
### The Sleepwalker
A ghostly child walks through the corridor every night carrying an empty leash.

### Under the Mattress
The player can discover:
Currency.
A Trick.
A clue.
A handwritten note from an earlier child investigator.
Or something that should absolutely not be under a mattress.
# 51. Region architecture interactions
The Sleeping Quarters is also a good region for environmental route gimmicks.
Possible connectors include:
Wardrobes leading into other bedrooms.
Beds that slide aside to reveal crawlspaces.
Laundry chutes.
Servant corridors.
Hidden doors behind headboards.
Mirrors connecting bedrooms.
A staircase that only appears after the lights go out.
A bedroom that changes occupants between expeditions.
This helps reinforce the theme outside combat.
# 52. What the Sleeping Quarters teaches
By the time the player reaches The Bedframe Beast, the region has taught:
Enemies can temporarily become difficult to target.
Status Tricks can be inconvenient without being purely useless.
Large attacks may build over several turns.
Player actions can influence enemy intentions.
Some enemies should be controlled rather than immediately killed.
Hidden states create timing windows.
Support enemies can amplify already dangerous threats.
Multi stage defensive enemies require different damage patterns.
Uncertainty should still contain meaningful player agency.
The most frightening enemy is not always the one currently attacking.
The Sleeping Quarters therefore gives Boggle a home region that feels mechanically connected to its identity without requiring Boggle to succeed.
