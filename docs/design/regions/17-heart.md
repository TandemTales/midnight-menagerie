# The Heart of the House
Region 17 Companion: None Final Boss: The Keeper Region identity: Memory, protection, containment, house rules, identity, investigation, rearrangement, and the mansion’s refusal to let go
The Heart should not feel like another themed wing.
Every previous region represented one distorted expression of care, control, memory, growth, safety, or preservation.
The Heart is where the player discovers that these were not isolated eccentricities.
They were all manifestations of the same underlying belief:
If something might be lost, keep it forever.
The Heart is cleaner than most of the mansion.
Quieter.
Warmer.
Almost peaceful.
That should make it more unsettling.
The house believes this is the safest place in the world.
And in some respects, it may be.
The problem is that every exit locks from the outside.
# 1. Heart combat thesis
The Heart should test concepts the player has already learned rather than introducing another large standalone subsystem.
Its enemies combine ideas such as:
Visible consequences.
Protection and redirection.
Future information.
Temporary Trick movement.
Offers with costs.
Delayed effects.
Adaptive behavior.
Battlefield objects.
Temporary restrictions that can be deliberately violated.
Choosing when a threat is worth tolerating.
But Heart enemies should synthesize these ideas rather than simply reuse old enemies.
The central combat lesson is:
A system can protect you and control you at the same time.

# 4. Housekeeper
Role: Restoration support Courage: 42
A tall shape made from folded sheets, dusting cloths, old aprons, and polished wooden hands.
Its only goal is to put everything back where it belongs.
### Core mechanic: Tidy
Housekeeper remembers the most recently defeated ordinary enemy.
If that enemy left any temporary battlefield objects or effects behind:
Housekeeper can remove them.
In return:
Housekeeper gains Order.
Maximum 3 Order.
Each Order grants 2 Guard whenever Housekeeper gains Guard.
### Moves
Tidy Up
Remove one enemy generated battlefield object or expired enemy remnant if one exists.
Gain 1 Order.
If nothing exists:
Gain 8 Guard.
Put That Back
Reveal the most recently discarded Trick.
Place it on the bottom of the draw pile.
Then draw 1 Trick.
This is intentionally not always harmful.
Feather Duster
Deal 8 damage.
Everything in Its Place
Gain 9 Guard plus 2 per Order.
Another enemy gains 5 Guard.
### Pattern
Tidy Up.
Feather Duster.
Put That Back.
Everything in Its Place.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The Housekeeper attacks disorder itself.
Sometimes its card movement improves the player’s hand.
That is appropriate.
The house’s systems are not malicious in every interaction.

# 6. Sanctuary Warden
Role: Protective control Courage: 48
A white wooden figure with padded arms and a small gate built into its chest.
### Core mechanic: Sanctuary
Sanctuary Warden protects one other enemy.

### Difference from earlier protectors
Whenever redirected damage occurs:
The protected enemy becomes Calmed.

### Moves

### Design purpose

That contradiction is the Heart in miniature.

# 15. Heart encounter generation rules
Perfect Keeper cannot appear in the first Heart Scuffle.
Two Perfect Keepers cannot appear together.

# 30. Final Boss: The Keeper
Courage: 540
The Keeper is the mansion's protective instinct given physical form.
It should not look like a demon.
It should look strangely reassuring.
A large animal like silhouette made from:
Warm wooden doors.
Blankets.
Brass keys.
Window glass.
Old wallpaper.
Pet beds.
Picture frames.
House beams.
Soft blue supernatural light.
Its face is simple.
Almost friendly.
Its body contains small doors and windows that open onto different parts of the mansion.
The Keeper is not a separate villain secretly controlling the house.
The Keeper is the house trying to speak in a shape the Kids can understand.
# 31. The Keeper's belief
Its philosophy is the culmination of the entire game:
Outside, things get lost.
Outside, animals get hurt.
People leave doors open.
Collars break.
Families move away.
Cars are dangerous.
Storms happen.
People fail.
Here, I can keep them safe.
Everything the mansion has done follows logically from that premise.
The error is not that the outside world is perfectly safe.
It obviously is not.
The error is:
The Keeper believes eliminating risk is more important than preserving freedom.

Therefore phase two always begins at 4 Panic.
The player chooses only:

And which benefits are worth claiming first.

# 40. Phase one strategic identity
The player has several valid approaches.
Break Shelter first because boss defense is the main problem.
Break Routine because the deck relies on long turns.
Ignore Observation because the deck naturally plays varied Trick types.
Leave Return intact because the deck usually spends all Nerve anyway.
Or ignore Locks almost entirely and race the boss.
The fight should adapt naturally to the deck without explicit Companion counters.

# 47. Rejecting an Argument
The player can permanently reject the currently active Argument.
During that player turn, satisfy any two different Independence conditions.
### Act
Play at least 4 Tricks.
### Stand
Gain at least 12 Guard.
### Push
Deal at least 18 Courage damage to The Keeper.
### Choose
Spend all available Nerve.
At the end of the turn:
If at least two different conditions were satisfied:
The active Argument becomes Rejected.
Remove it from the cycle.
The Keeper loses 7 Courage.
# 48. Why there are four different conditions
No Companion should require one specific build.
A slow defensive deck might satisfy:
Stand plus Choose.
A combo deck:
Act plus Choose.
A burst deck:
Push plus Act.
A balanced deck:
Push plus Stand.
The fight asks the player to demonstrate agency through whatever their deck is actually good at.
# 49. Rejected Arguments
Once Rejected:
That Argument never becomes active again.
The remaining cycle becomes shorter.
This has an interesting consequence.
If the player rejects the easiest Argument first:
The more difficult ones appear more frequently.
So even removing boss mechanics changes the rhythm.

# 59. Missing pet transformation state
The pet's exact appearance depends on campaign progression.
An early rescue might show:
Very subtle supernatural traits.
A later rescue might show:
Obvious Stage 2 or Stage 3 changes.
A particularly deep narrative route might find a pet approaching Stage 4.

Rescue means freedom, not restoring someone to a previous state against their will.

# 62. Multiplayer House Arguments
Each Argument applies to the entire team.
To Reject an Argument:
The team must satisfy three different Independence conditions during the team round rather than two.
Conditions are normalized for party size.
### Act
Team plays approximately 3 Tricks per player.
### Stand
Team gains approximately 10 Guard per player.
### Push
Team deals a scaled Courage threshold.
2 players: 30
3 players: 40
4 players: 49
### Choose
At least half the players end with no unspent Nerve.
This encourages different players to contribute different proofs of independence.
# 63. Multiplayer phase three
Open Heart remains global.
The bonus crack damage threshold scales:
2 players: 40 team Courage damage
3 players: 52
4 players: 64
Maximum one Heart crack per team round.
The Keeper's multi hit actions spread across the party where possible.

# 64. Multiplayer narrative
The Keeper should acknowledge that the Kids and Companions have chosen each other.
It may understand individual loyalty.
What it fails to understand is why a group would willingly leave a guaranteed sanctuary and accept responsibility for one another outside.
That makes multiplayer especially appropriate for the final thematic argument.
The party's answer is not:
Outside is safe.
The answer is:
We will help each other when it isn't.
# 65. Haunt Level progression
### Haunt 1
Ordinary Heart enemies gain approximately 8 percent Courage.
Big Scares and Keeper gain approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced Heart formations appear earlier.
### Haunt 3
Housekeeper begins advanced encounters with 1 Order.
### Haunt 4
Memory Animal can hold 4 Memory rather than 3.
### Haunt 5
Sanctuary Warden redirects the first 8 Attack damage instead of 6.
### Haunt 6
Namekeeper's Recognized attack bonus increases from 4 to 6.
### Haunt 7
Quiet Room generates Noise on the fourth and fifth Tricks rather than fourth and sixth.
Maximum remains 2.
### Haunt 8
House Pulse's Systole grants 3 attack damage instead of 2.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares gain signature upgrades.
Hall of Names begins with one Countdown reduced by 1.
House Remembers draws five Memory Patterns rather than four.
Perfect Sanctuary begins with one System carrying 8 Guard.
Door That Says Stay begins with 1 Stayed and 1 Refusal.
### Haunt 10
The Keeper begins combat with:
1 Panic.
Shelter Lock and Routine Lock each have 5 additional Integrity.
Phase one still caps at 4 Panic.
# 66. Higher Haunt behavior upgrades
At very high Haunt Levels:
Housekeeper can restore one destroyed minor enemy object.
Memory Animal can remember both first and last Trick type.
Sanctuary Warden can transfer Sanctuary.
Namekeeper can maintain two Named Tricks.
Quiet Room can alter its Noise thresholds visibly.
House Pulse can delay a Heartbeat to align with an ally's strong turn.

Perfect Keeper can assess two player behaviors and choose between two visible responses.
House Remembers can include a fifth or sixth Memory Pattern.

Not memory of undocumented exceptions.

# 82. Final campaign revelation
The strongest version of the House's history is that it was once a genuinely good sanctuary.
Lost animals arrived.
They were fed.
Treated.
Protected.
Families found some of them.
Animals without homes stayed.
The supernatural mansion gradually developed a consciousness from decades of:
Care.
Loss.
Fear.
Memory.
And responsibility.
Then something happened that convinced the House that releasing animals was dangerous.
The exact historical event can be developed separately.
Afterward, its logic shifted.
From:
Keep them safe until they can go home.
To:
If they leave, I cannot keep them safe.
And eventually:
Therefore they should never leave.
That transformation mirrors the Menagerie Companions themselves.
The mansion changed too.
# 83. First major ending

The Kid stays playable.

This preserves the cooperative campaign structure.
# 84. Eight pet completion
Rescuing all eight Kids' missing pets should be a major campaign completion state.
It proves that the House has been forced to recognize repeatedly that:
Animals can leave.
They can remain loved.
They can remain safe enough.
People can come back for them.
# 85. Sixteen Companion completion
Rescuing all sixteen Menagerie Companions is a parallel objective.
Those animals may not have conventional homes to return to anymore.
Their resolution is therefore about freedom rather than restoration.
Some may:
Stay with the Kids.
Live around the neighborhood.
Return voluntarily to parts of the mansion.
Travel.
Choose entirely new homes.
# 86. Deep resolution
Completing both:
16 of 16 Companions
and
8 of 8 missing pets
can unlock the strongest version of the ending.
The House does not need to be destroyed.
Instead, it can become what it originally wanted to be:
A genuine supernatural sanctuary.
Lost animals can enter.
Injured animals can recover.
Animals without homes can stay.
Animals with homes can leave.
The crucial new rule is simple:
The doors open from both sides.
That is the resolution of Midnight Menagerie’s central theme.
