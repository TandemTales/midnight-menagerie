# The Moonlit Attic and Observatory
Companion: Wink Boss: The Watcher in the Rafters Region identity: Prediction, enemy intent manipulation, future information, webs, traps, delayed setup, false forecasts, and celestial cycles
The Moonlit Attic and Observatory should feel like the mansion has become obsessed with what happens next.
Telescopes turn on their own.
Star charts redraw themselves.
Cobwebs spell out warnings.
Dust patterns resemble footprints that have not happened yet.
The player is repeatedly given glimpses of future actions, but those glimpses are not always fixed. Some can be altered. Some are conditional. Some are deliberately misleading.
The central combat lesson is:
Knowing the future is useful only if you understand what can still change it.
Wink belongs here because prediction is not passive for Wink. Wink reads intent, manipulates it, prepares around it, and creates advantages from knowing what will happen before it happens.

## 2. Regional mechanic: Forecast
Several enemies use Forecasts.
A Forecast shows an action that may happen in a future enemy turn.
Forecasts have three possible states:
Fixed
The action will happen unless the enemy is defeated or the effect is explicitly canceled.
Conditional
The action changes depending on what the player does.
Uncertain
Several possible actions are displayed, and the final one is resolved later according to a known rule.
Forecasts must always communicate their state clearly.
The region should never rely on hidden coin flips disguised as prediction.
## 3. Rafter Peeker
Role: Introductory reactive intent enemy Courage: 25
A pale little creature hangs upside down from attic beams with enormous eyes.
### Core mechanic: Flinch
Rafter Peeker changes its next intent when attacked.
At the beginning of its turn it displays:
Watching
If the player does not damage it:
It uses Drop Down.
Deal 9 damage.
If the player damages it at least once:
Its intent changes immediately to Scramble Away.
Gain 9 Guard.
Deal 4 damage.
This can change only once per turn.
### Other move
Peek Again
Gain 5 Guard.
Return to Watching next turn.
### Pattern
Watching.
Resolve Drop Down or Scramble Away.
Peek Again.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The player directly manipulates enemy intent through a simple action.
This is the region's cleanest introduction to the concept.
## 4. Star Chart
Role: Support forecast enemy Courage: 28
A brass framed celestial chart floats in the air.
Stars rearrange themselves around silhouettes of the other enemies.
### Core mechanic: Auspicious
Star Chart predicts one allied enemy's next damaging action after its current one.
That future action becomes Auspicious.
An Auspicious attack deals 4 additional damage when it eventually occurs.
The affected future intent is visibly marked.
### Moves
Read the Stars
Make one allied future attack Auspicious.
Prefer the highest base damage forecast available.
Falling Star
Deal 6 damage.
Recalculate
Gain 8 Guard.
Move Auspicious from one eligible future attack to another.
### Behavior
Read the Stars.
Falling Star.
Recalculate.
Falling Star.
Repeat.
### On defeat
Existing Auspicious marks disappear.
### Combat lesson
Sometimes the most dangerous enemy is the one making another enemy's future turn worse.

## 8. Orrery Imp
Role: Intent manipulation support enemy Courage: 31
A tiny brass creature runs inside a miniature mechanical solar system.
### Core mechanic: Rotate Intent
Orrery Imp can move allied planned actions forward or backward.
It cannot change what the action is.
It changes when it happens.
### Move: Speed Up
Choose another enemy with a future forecast.
Move that forecast one enemy turn closer.
Cannot cause an action to occur immediately on the current enemy turn.
### Move: Slow Down
Choose another enemy's future forecast.
Move it one turn later.
Why would it ever slow something down?
Because delaying a moderate attack may allow it to combine with another enemy's major attack.
### Other moves
Brass Kick
Deal 6 damage.
Wind the Mechanism
Gain 8 Guard.
Its next Speed Up or Slow Down affects two different forecasts if possible.
### Behavior
If a useful forecast exists:
Alternate Speed Up and Slow Down.
Otherwise:
Brass Kick.
Wind the Mechanism.
### Design purpose
Orrery Imp does not increase damage directly.
It attacks the player's timeline.

## 13. Big Scare: The Great Orrery
Courage: 142
Role: Multi turn celestial scheduling
The Great Orrery fills an entire observatory chamber.
Brass planets orbit a miniature moon while gears extend through the walls.
### Core mechanic: Celestial Cycle
The Great Orrery has four celestial positions.
Each lasts one enemy turn.
Dawn
Zenith
Eclipse
Midnight
The entire four turn cycle is shown at all times.
### Dawn
The Orrery gains 12 Guard.
### Zenith
Its damaging attack deals 4 additional damage.
### Eclipse
The player draws 1 fewer Trick next turn.
Minimum 3.
The Orrery takes 20 percent additional damage.
### Midnight
The Orrery uses its strongest attack.
Then cycle returns to Dawn.
### Moves by position
Dawn: First Light
Gain 12 Guard.
Deal 5 damage.
Zenith: Solar Flare
Deal 13 damage.
Eclipse: Shadow Transit
Deal 7 damage.
Apply the draw reduction.
Midnight: Falling Stars
Deal 5 damage three times.
### Core interaction: Nudge the Heavens
Whenever the player deals at least 20 total damage in one turn:
They may choose after the damage resolves:
Advance the Celestial Cycle one position.
Or move it backward one position.
Once per turn.
This is optional.
### Strategic possibilities
Move toward Eclipse to exploit vulnerability.
Delay Midnight.
Force Midnight early while heavily defended.
Skip Dawn protection.
The player gets meaningful control over the cycle.
## 14. Big Scare: The Rafter Seer
Courage: 128
Role: Uncertain but controllable forecasts
A long limbed creature crawls across the ceiling.
Its face consists of several closed eyes.
It speaks predictions before opening them.
### Core mechanic: Three Futures
At the beginning of certain turns, Rafter Seer displays three future outcomes:
Claw
Deal 15 damage.
Hide
Gain 17 Guard.
Hex
Deal 7 damage and Web one Trick in hand.
One outcome is currently Favored.
The player can change which future becomes Favored.
### Changing the future
The first Attack played moves Favored one position clockwise.
The first Skill played moves it one position counterclockwise.
The first Power played locks the current Favored outcome.
Each trigger can happen once per turn.
At end of player turn:
The current Favored outcome becomes the actual intent.
### Other moves
See Further
Display the Three Futures.
Gain 5 Guard.
Many Eyes
Deal 4 damage twice.
The next Three Futures begins with Hide Favored.
False Prophecy
Deal 8 damage.
The next Three Futures starts with a random Favored outcome, but all three options remain visible.
### Pattern
See Further.
Resolve chosen future.
Many Eyes.
See Further.
Resolve.
False Prophecy.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
This is prediction as a small tactical control puzzle.

### Moon Lens moves

## 16. The Watcher in the Rafters
Boss Courage: 355
The Watcher hangs high above the observatory.
At first only dozens of eyes are visible between beams.
Then a huge spiderlike body slowly unfolds from the darkness.
Its abdomen is patterned like a star map.
Long strands of silver web connect it to telescopes, clocks, charts, and every corner of the room.
It does not merely see what the player is doing.
It believes everything dangerous can be prevented if it sees far enough ahead.
Its philosophy is:
If every possible future is known, nothing has to be risked.
That makes it a distorted mirror of Wink.
Wink predicts so it can act.
The Watcher predicts so it can control.
## 17. Core boss mechanic: The Future Line
Beside the Watcher is a visible timeline containing its next three planned actions.
Example:
Current enemy turn:
Rafter Strike
Next:
Web the Hand
Then:
Great Descent
The player can inspect all three.
This is more information than most bosses provide.
The fight is balanced around the fact that the timeline can change.
## 18. Watcher phase one actions
From 355 through 201 Courage.
Its action pool is:
### Rafter Strike
Deal 12 damage.
### Web the Hand
Web two random Tricks in the player's hand.
If fewer than two valid Tricks exist:
Web the top Trick of the draw pile instead.
### Skitter Above
Gain 13 Guard.
The next damaging action in the Future Line deals 3 additional damage.
### Drop Behind You
Deal 6 damage twice.
### Readjust
Swap the second and third actions in the Future Line.
Gain 6 Guard.
The Watcher does not select actions randomly every turn.
At the beginning of combat:
It creates a three action Future Line.
Each time an action resolves:
A new action is added to the far end according to controlled boss logic.
## 19. Future Line generation rules
The Watcher cannot schedule:
The same damaging action twice consecutively.
More than one Web the Hand within three slots.
More than one Skitter Above within three slots.
At baseline difficulty.
This produces readable but varied timelines.
## 20. Player interaction: Tug the Web
The Future Line is connected to three visible web strands.
Whenever the player reaches specific Trick counts during one turn:
They can manipulate it.
### Third Trick
Swap positions 2 and 3.
### Fifth Trick
Move position 3 to position 2 and position 2 to position 3 again, effectively allowing a second swap if desired.
More simply in UI terms:
At Tricks 3 and 5, the player receives one Tug.
Each Tug can swap the second and third future actions.
Unused Tugs disappear at end of turn.
### Important limitation
The current next enemy action cannot be moved during phase one.
Only the farther future can be manipulated.
This keeps immediate intents reliable.
## 21. Why swapping matters
Suppose the line is:
Rafter Strike
Skitter Above
Great Descent later in the fight
Swapping can prevent Skitter Above from immediately empowering the dangerous attack behind it.
Or the player may intentionally move setup forward because they are not ready for the other option.
This creates future sequencing decisions every turn.
## 22. Phase one special: Prediction Marks
Every third Watcher turn:
It uses:
I Saw That Coming
The Watcher predicts one player behavior for the next turn.
Possible predictions:
You will play 3 or more Attacks.
You will gain at least 15 Guard.
You will play 5 or more Tricks.
You will end with 0 Nerve.
The prediction is visible before the player acts.
### If the prediction is correct
The Watcher gains Certainty.
### If incorrect
The Watcher becomes Doubtful.
## 23. Certainty
Each Certainty gives:
2 attack damage.
Maximum 3.
Certainty lasts until phase transition.
### Doubtful
The Watcher loses 8 Guard if it has any.
Its next newly generated Future Line action is revealed before insertion, and the player chooses whether it enters position 2 or 3.
Doubtful then ends.
### Design purpose
Sometimes the player may deliberately behave suboptimally to prove a prediction wrong.
Sometimes doing exactly what was predicted is still worth it.

Choose the first option.

Choose the second.

This temporarily makes timeline manipulation harder.

### Skitter Above

## 30. Phase two Tug rules
The player earns Tugs at:
Third Trick.
Fifth Trick.
Seventh Trick.
Maximum three Tugs per turn.
Each Tug can swap any two adjacent future positions except the current action.
For example:
Swap slots 2 and 3.
Or 3 and 4.
This creates much deeper manipulation without letting the player simply delete boss actions.
## 31. Timeline lock
Certain actions display a small lock icon.
Great Descent becomes Locked once it reaches position 2.
Locked actions cannot be moved.
This prevents infinite postponement of the boss's signature attack.
False Future is also Locked once resolved into its actual action.
## 32. Final escalation
At 75 Courage or less:
The Watcher gains:
Every Thread Leads Here
Whenever a Future Line action is moved by the player:
The Watcher loses 2 Courage.
Maximum 6 Courage lost this way per player turn.
The player is now damaging the boss by disrupting its perfect prediction network.
At the same time:
Rafter Strike gains 2 damage.
Drop Behind You gains 1 damage per hit.
This creates a faster final race.
## 33. Why Wink feels especially good here
### Intent reading
Wink already cares about enemy intentions, so the expanded Future Line naturally plays into its strengths.
### Prediction
Wink can potentially gain value from knowing several turns ahead rather than merely the next enemy intent.
### Webs
Wink's own web mechanics can create thematic symmetry with the Watcher's control system.
### Preparation
A preparation focused Wink deck can plan powerful turns around Grounded.
### Intent manipulation
If Wink has Tricks capable of modifying enemy intent, those should interact cleanly with the Future Line.
The key rule should be:
Intent changing effects modify the current actionable intent unless they explicitly mention future actions.
Wink specific Tricks can be designed later that interact with future slots more directly.
## 34. Boss narrative outcome
### Wink not yet rescued
The Watcher has kept Wink in the Observatory because it views Wink's many eyes and supernatural perception as uniquely valuable.
It has spent years forcing Wink to watch:
Doors.
Hallways.
Animals.
The night sky.
Possible futures.
Wink was treated as part pet, part telescope, part security system.
Defeating The Watcher allows Wink to choose what it wants to look at.
### Wink already rescued and currently active
This becomes Wink's Legacy homecoming.
The thematic statement is:
Seeing danger does not mean you must spend your life afraid of it.
### Another Companion active
Normal boss reward.
No Wink Legacy advancement.

## 36. Rafter Peeker multiplayer
It tracks damage from any player.
If any player damages it during the team round:
Scramble Away replaces Drop Down.
Drop Down targets one player if not disrupted.
The target is shown before the round begins.
## 37. Star Chart multiplayer
Auspicious can mark an attack against any player.
The intended target is shown.
This allows teammates to protect the threatened player.
## 38. Telescope Eye multiplayer
Observations use team averages or totals.
### Observe Attacks
Triggers if the team plays at least 2 Attacks per player on average.
### Observe Guard
Triggers if the team collectively gains at least 12 Guard per player.
### Observe Nerve
Triggers if at least half the team ends with unused Nerve.
These thresholds should be tuned later by playtesting.
## 39. Cobweb Bundle multiplayer
Web Ahead targets one player's future Trick.
It rotates targets whenever possible.
Sticky Strand prefers a player currently without another Webbed Trick.
## 40. Moon Moth multiplayer
Its phase remains shared.
Full Moon Moonflash hits one selected player.
The target is announced one full round early.
This makes Full Moon a cooperative protection opportunity.
## 41. Orrery Imp multiplayer
Forecast manipulation remains global.
Speed Up and Slow Down can affect future attacks targeting any teammate.
This makes it a high priority support enemy.
## 42. Great Orrery multiplayer
Celestial Cycle is shared.
Nudge the Heavens uses team damage thresholds:
2 players: 32 damage
3 players: 42
4 players: 51
The team gets at most one Nudge per round.
Midnight Falling Stars distributes hits among the party.
## 43. Rafter Seer multiplayer
Three Futures are shared.
The first qualifying Attack, Skill, and Power played by the team move or lock the forecast.
Players therefore need to coordinate sequencing.
The resolved damaging future targets one player unless otherwise specified.
## 44. Moon Lens multiplayer
The third total Trick played by the team swaps intended Focus.
The sixth allows another swap.
The ninth allows a third in four player games.
Moon Echo attacks one player.
The Moon Lens's direct attacks may target another.
This creates two parallel threat channels.
## 45. Watcher multiplayer Future Line

### Tug thresholds

4th, 7th, and 10th team Tricks.
3 players:
5th, 9th, and 13th.
4 players:
6th, 11th, and 16th.

### Great Descent multiplayer

## 47. Prediction Marks in multiplayer

## 48. Multiplayer cooperative opportunities
One player can intentionally poke Rafter Peeker to alter its attack.
A teammate can prepare to block a Full Moon attack known a full round early.
Players can coordinate who triggers the Rafter Seer's first Attack or Skill movement.
The team can save burst damage for Great Orrery cycle manipulation.
One player can deliberately play the Trick that generates the final Watcher Tug.
Another then decides how to reorder the Future Line.
A protective Companion can see a major targeted attack several turns away and prepare specifically for it.
This region should make cooperative combat feel unusually deliberate.

### Cracked Telescope Lens

## 55. Blueprint investigation progression
Early notes:
"Attic moves when moon moves?"
Later:
"Spider thing predicts routes."
Later:
"It was watching the neighborhood."
Eventually:
"House knew which pets were coming BEFORE they vanished."
That should be a major escalation in the investigation.
At this point, the kids have reason to question whether the mansion merely receives lost animals or sometimes actively engineers their arrival.
That mystery can remain unresolved until much deeper in the campaign.
## 56. What the Moonlit Attic and Observatory teaches
By the time the player reaches The Watcher in the Rafters, the region has taught:
Enemy intents can change after the player acts.
Future actions can be useful information without being immutable.
Enemy support effects can strengthen attacks that have not happened yet.
Player behavior can determine future enemy outcomes.
Card interference is more interesting when shown before the affected Trick arrives.
Cyclical enemy states create predictable offensive and defensive windows.
Timeline manipulation can be as important as damage.
Uncertain forecasts remain fair when the player understands what determines the outcome.
Future information can create stronger enemy mechanics without creating unfairness.
Prediction is most powerful when paired with the ability to act on what you know.
