# 2. Button Baby
Role: Support enemy Courage: 21
A tiny stuffed doll covered in mismatched buttons.
It spends most of combat improving other toys rather than attacking directly.
### Core mechanic: Buttons
Button Baby can sew one Button onto another enemy.
An enemy can have only one Button at a time.
There are three possible Buttons.
### Brass Button
That enemy's damaging attacks deal 2 additional damage.
### Pillow Button
The enemy gains 4 Guard at the beginning of its turn.
### Spring Button
The enemy's next attack occurs twice at 60 percent damage each.
Once that attack occurs, Spring Button falls off.
### Removing Buttons
If a Buttoned enemy takes at least 12 damage during one player turn, its Button breaks.
The Button Baby itself cannot be Buttoned.
### Moves
Sew On
Attach a Button to another enemy without one.
Priority:
Spring Button if the target is preparing an attack.
Pillow Button if the target is below half Courage.
Otherwise Brass Button.
Button Toss
Deal 5 damage.
Gather Thread
Gain 7 Guard.
Its next Sew On also gives the target 5 Guard.
### Behavior
If an ally lacks a Button:
Sew On.
Otherwise:
Button Toss.
Gather Thread.
Button Toss.
Then check again for an available Button target.
### Combat lesson
Support enemies can alter how another enemy should be evaluated.
A weak Rocking Horse with a Spring Button may suddenly become the most dangerous enemy in the room.
# 3. Jack in the Box
Role: Telegraph enemy Courage: 32
The box rattles violently while something inside tries to escape.
### Core mechanic: Wound Up
Jack in the Box can accumulate up to 2 Wound Up.
### Moves
Turn the Handle
Gain 1 Wound Up.
Gain 6 Guard.
POP!
Deal:
7 damage at 0 Wound Up.
12 damage at 1 Wound Up.
17 damage at 2 Wound Up.
Remove all Wound Up.
### Jammed
Whenever the player removes all Guard granted by Turn the Handle during the same player turn, Jack in the Box becomes Jammed.
Jammed removes 1 Wound Up.
Minimum 0.
### Secondary move: Box Bite
Deal 7 damage.
### Pattern
Turn the Handle.
Turn the Handle.
POP!
Box Bite.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
This is another telegraphed threat, but unlike Red Carpet Runner, the player is dealing with a creature that mechanically becomes more tightly wound.
The concept fits the Nursery while reinforcing a familiar combat language.
# 4. Patchwork Soldier
Role: Repair enemy Courage: 38
A stuffed toy soldier with a wooden sword, one cloth arm, one metal leg, and far too many repaired seams.
### Core move: Patch Up
Choose another damaged enemy.
That enemy recovers 9 Courage.
Patchwork Soldier loses 7 Courage.
Patch Up cannot reduce Patchwork Soldier below 1 Courage.
### Other moves
Wooden Saber
Deal 9 damage.
Stand Guard
Gain 8 Guard.
Another enemy gains 5 Guard.
### Behavior
If another enemy is missing at least 12 Courage:
Patch Up.
Patch Up cannot occur twice consecutively.
Otherwise alternate:
Wooden Saber.
Stand Guard.
### Strategic effect
The player can exploit Patchwork Soldier's repair behavior.
Damaging another enemy may cause the Soldier to hurt itself repairing them.
Sometimes the correct strategy is deliberately letting it continue doing so.
That makes healing an interactive mechanic rather than simply frustrating recovery.
# 5. Rocking Horse
Role: Interaction bruiser Courage: 42
A wooden rocking horse whose eyes glow whenever the rest of the Nursery becomes more animated.
### Core mechanic: Excitement
Rocking Horse gains 1 Excitement whenever another enemy:
Gains Guard from another enemy.
Recovers Courage.
Receives a Button.
Becomes Covered.
Maximum 3 Excitement.
### Moves
Rock
Deal 6 damage.
Gain 5 Guard.
Gallop
Deal 7 damage plus 4 per Excitement.
Remove all Excitement.
Happy Clatter
Gain 1 Excitement.
Give another enemy 4 Guard.
### Behavior
If at 2 or more Excitement:
Gallop.
Otherwise:
Rock.
Happy Clatter.
Rock.
### Maximum Gallop
At 3 Excitement:
19 damage
### Combat lesson
Enemy support actions can create secondary consequences elsewhere.
The player begins reading the entire enemy formation as a system.
# 6. Blanket Blob
Role: Protector Courage: 34
Several nursery blankets have crawled together into one shambling pile.
Occasionally a stuffed foot sticks out.
Nobody is sure whether it belongs to the Blob.
### Core mechanic: Cover
Blanket Blob can Cover one other enemy.
While Covered:
The first 8 damage that enemy would take each player turn is dealt to Blanket Blob instead.
Any remaining damage hits the intended enemy normally.
Blanket Blob cannot Cover another Blanket Blob.
### Moves
Tuck In
Cover another enemy.
Gain 5 Guard.
Blanket Snap
Deal 8 damage.
Smother
Deal 5 damage.
The player draws 1 fewer Trick next turn.
This cannot reduce the player's draw below 3.
### Behavior
If no ally is Covered:
Tuck In.
Otherwise:
Blanket Snap.
Smother.
Blanket Snap.
### When Blanket Blob is defeated
Cover immediately ends.
### Design purpose
This is the Nursery's clearest introduction to transferred damage.
It also creates interesting interactions with multi hit and high damage Tricks.
# 7. Porcelain Doll
Role: Transformation enemy Courage: 36
A beautiful porcelain doll sitting perfectly still.
Until it cracks.
### Core mechanic: Cracked
Porcelain Doll has three states based on remaining Courage.
### Pristine
Above 24 Courage.
Attacks deal normal damage.
Gains 4 Guard at the beginning of its turn.
### Cracked
24 through 13 Courage.
No automatic Guard.
Attacks deal 3 additional damage.
### Shattered
12 Courage or less.
Attacks deal 6 additional damage.
After attacking, Porcelain Doll takes 3 damage from its own instability.
### Moves
Tea Cup Tap
Deal 7 damage.
Perfect Posture
Gain 10 Guard.
Sharp Little Hands
Deal 4 damage twice.
### Pattern
Tea Cup Tap.
Perfect Posture.
Sharp Little Hands.
Repeat.
### Shattered example
Sharp Little Hands becomes:
10 damage twice.
Then Porcelain Doll loses 3 Courage.
### Strategic problem
Damaging it makes it more dangerous.
But once sufficiently damaged, it also starts destroying itself.
The player must decide whether to:
Leave it mostly intact.
Burst through the dangerous middle.
Or intentionally push it into Shattered and let its own attacks finish the job.

# 11. Nursery encounter generation rules
The procedural encounter system should enforce several restrictions.
Rocking Horse cannot appear during the first two Nursery Scuffles.
Blanket Blob cannot appear during the first Scuffle.
The player must encounter either Jack in the Box or Porcelain Doll before encountering the triple enemy formations.
Button Baby should appear alone only in the earliest encounter pool.
Patchwork Soldier cannot appear alongside another Patchwork Soldier.
Blanket Blob cannot Cover another Blanket Blob.
Scuffles 12 and 14 belong exclusively to the advanced pool.
# 12. Big Scare: The Toy Chest
Courage: 110
Role: Battlefield management
An enormous wooden toy chest shakes violently.
Different hands, paws, wheels, and springs occasionally protrude through the lid.
### Core mechanic: Contents
The Toy Chest begins combat with 6 Contents.
Contents represent animated toys trapped inside.
### Move: Spill Toys
Remove 1 Content.
Summon one Nursery Toy.
Possible summons:
Button Baby at 50 percent Courage.
Jack in the Box at 50 percent Courage.
Patchwork Soldier at 45 percent Courage.
Maximum two summoned enemies at once.
### Core interaction: Slam the Lid
Whenever The Toy Chest takes at least 16 damage during one player turn, its lid becomes Slammed.
While Slammed:
It cannot Spill Toys during its next turn.
If its planned move was Spill Toys, that move becomes:
Rattle Angrily
Gain 9 Guard.
### Other moves
Lid Slam
Deal 13 damage.
Toy Barrage
Deal 4 damage three times.
Tidy Up
Return one summoned enemy to the chest.
Recover 12 Courage.
Add 1 Content.
The returned enemy disappears.
### Behavior
Spill Toys.
Lid Slam.
Spill Toys.
Toy Barrage.
Tidy Up.
Repeat.
### Strategic question
Do you continually spend damage keeping the lid shut?
Do you allow toys out and kill them?
Or do you race the Toy Chest itself?
Different decks have legitimate answers.
# 13. Big Scare: The Patchwork Giant
Courage: 126
Role: Progressive transformation test
An enormous stuffed creature made from several different toys.
Rabbit ears.
Bear arms.
A crocodile tail.
A doll's face.
Three different kinds of stuffing.
### Core mechanic: Patches
The Patchwork Giant begins with three attached Patches.
Each Patch provides an ability.
### Bear Patch
Damaging attacks deal 3 additional damage.
### Pillow Patch
Gain 6 Guard at the beginning of each turn.
### Spring Patch
The first attack each cycle occurs twice at 60 percent damage.
### Tearing off Patches
At:
90 Courage
60 Courage
30 Courage
One Patch tears away permanently.
The next Patch removed is always the one currently providing the most immediate benefit.
### But losing a Patch has a consequence
Each time a Patch tears away:
The Giant gains Loose Stuffing.
Each Loose Stuffing increases its attack damage by 1.
Loose Stuffing remains for the rest of combat.
### Moves
Stuffed Fist
Deal 11 damage.
Sit Down
Gain 13 Guard.
Wild Flail
Deal 5 damage three times.
Coming Apart
Gain 8 Guard.
Its next attack gains 4 additional damage.
### Design identity
The Patchwork Giant becomes less mechanically versatile as it falls apart, but increasingly aggressive.
The player feels like they are literally dismantling it.
# 14. Big Scare: The Porcelain Twins
Courage: 68 each
Role: Effect transfer puzzle
The Twins are named:
Prim
and
Proper
They sit beside one another holding hands.
Their heads rotate simultaneously toward the player.
### Core mechanic: Joined
While both Twins are active, they are Joined.
Whenever one Twin gains Guard:
The other gains half that amount, rounded down.
Whenever one Twin receives a negative status:
The other receives 1 stack of that status if the status is stackable.
Otherwise the effect is copied at half duration where meaningful.
### Cracking
Each Twin has the same Pristine, Cracked, and Shattered Courage thresholds proportionally as the ordinary Porcelain Doll.
But their states interact.
If one Twin is Shattered while the other remains Pristine:
The Pristine Twin immediately becomes Distressed.
Distressed causes attacks to deal 4 additional damage.
### Move coordination
Prim specializes in offense.
Proper specializes in protection.
### Prim
Pointed Finger
Deal 10 damage.
Little Slap
Deal 5 damage twice.
### Proper
Good Posture
Both Twins gain 8 Guard.
Hush Now
Prim's next attack deals 4 additional damage.
Proper gains 6 Guard.
### Shared move
Every fourth turn:
Tea Party
Each Twin recovers 5 Courage.
If either Twin is defeated, Tea Party no longer occurs.
### When one Twin is defeated
The survivor loses Joined.
The survivor gains:
Alone
Deal 3 additional damage permanently.
### Strategic question
Do you distribute damage?
Focus one Twin?
Exploit copied negative effects?
Push both into Shattered simultaneously?
This is intentionally one of the Nursery's more sophisticated combats.
# 15. The Governess
Boss Courage: 280
The Governess appears at first to be human.
She is not.
Her dress is made from nursery curtains.
Her fingers taper into silver sewing needles.
A measuring tape moves around her neck like a snake.
She does not believe she is cruel.
She believes everything damaged should be repaired.
Everything messy should be corrected.
Everything frightened should be kept somewhere safe.
Forever, if necessary.
Her central belief is:
Nothing properly cared for should ever be allowed to come apart.
# 16. The Governess's companion construct
The Governess begins combat beside:
Her Favorite Doll
Favorite Doll Courage:
50
Favorite Doll does not act independently.
Instead, it functions as part of The Governess's boss mechanics.
# 17. Core mechanic: Stitched Together
While Favorite Doll is active, The Governess and the Doll are connected by a supernatural thread.
At the beginning of every player turn:
The first 10 damage that would be dealt to The Governess that turn is redirected to Favorite Doll.
Example:
The player attacks The Governess for 14 damage.
Favorite Doll takes 10.
The Governess takes 4.
After that, further attacks against The Governess that turn behave normally.
### When Favorite Doll reaches 0 Courage
It becomes:
Torn
It remains visibly on the battlefield but stops redirecting damage.
It cannot be targeted while Torn.
The Governess can repair it.
This creates a recurring choice.
Attack the Doll directly.
Use redirected damage to tear it naturally.
Or ignore it and simply overpower the protection.
# 18. Favorite Doll's Patches
Favorite Doll begins with one randomly selected Patch.
The selected Patch is visible before the first player turn.
### Stuffed Patch
Favorite Doll has 10 additional maximum Courage.
### Button Patch
Whenever damage is redirected into Favorite Doll, The Governess gains 2 Guard.
Maximum 6 Guard per player turn.
### Lace Patch
Whenever The Governess repairs Favorite Doll, the Doll returns with 6 additional Courage.
This introduces slight variation between Governess fights without radically changing the encounter.
# 19. Governess phase one
From 280 through 151 Courage.
### Move: Inspect the Nursery
Gain 10 Guard.
Favorite Doll gains 8 Guard if it is not Torn.
### Move: Sharp Correction
Deal 11 damage.
### Move: Mind Your Seams
Deal 5 damage twice.
The next time the player gains Guard, they gain 3 less.
This effect expires after triggering once.
### Move: Mend My Darling
Used if Favorite Doll is Torn.
Favorite Doll returns with 28 Courage.
The Governess gains 6 Guard.
This move deals no damage.
Mend My Darling cannot be used immediately on the turn after the Doll becomes Torn.
The player therefore always receives at least one turn of vulnerability.
### Standard pattern
Inspect the Nursery.
Sharp Correction.
Mind Your Seams.
Sharp Correction.
Then repeat.
If Favorite Doll is Torn and eligible for repair:
Mend My Darling replaces the next Inspect the Nursery.
# 20. Core tactical loop
The Governess fight therefore naturally creates a rhythm.
Favorite Doll protects her.
The player tears the Doll.
The Governess becomes exposed.
The player gets an offensive window.
The Governess sacrifices tempo to repair the Doll.
The cycle begins again.
But the player is never required to interact with it exactly this way.
A sufficiently aggressive deck can simply attack The Governess through the redirection.
A scaling deck may use repair turns to set up.
A defensive deck may use the predictable rhythm to prepare.
# 21. Governess phase transition
At 150 Courage:
The Governess immediately uses:
Look What You've Done
Favorite Doll becomes permanently Torn.
It can no longer be repaired.
The Governess removes the supernatural thread.
She takes the Doll's remaining stuffing, buttons, cloth, and thread and begins patching herself.
Phase two begins.
# 22. Governess phase two mechanic: Self Repair
The Governess gains three Repair Patches.
Only one Patch is active at a time.
The active Patch is clearly displayed.
The Patch changes after each Governess turn.
### Reinforced Patch
The first damaging Trick played against The Governess each turn deals 6 less damage.
Minimum 0.
### Stuffed Patch
At the end of the player's turn, if The Governess lost fewer than 12 Courage that turn, she recovers 5 Courage.
### Buttoned Patch
Whenever The Governess gains Guard, she gains 4 additional Guard.
The Patch cycle is always:
Reinforced.
Stuffed.
Buttoned.
Repeat.
The player therefore knows exactly what is coming.
# 23. Tearing a Repair Patch
If The Governess takes at least 20 damage during one player turn, the current Patch tears.
Its ability immediately stops functioning.
The next Patch still arrives after The Governess acts.
### Why this matters
Again, the player has agency.
They can adapt to each Patch.
Or simply hit hard enough to remove it.
The system rewards both sequencing and burst damage.
# 24. Governess phase two moves
### Needle Point
Deal 13 damage.
### Tighten the Stitch
Gain 13 Guard.
The next Needle Point deals 5 additional damage.
### Snip Snip
Deal 4 damage three times.
### Emergency Repair
Recover 10 Courage.
Gain 8 Guard.
Emergency Repair cannot occur more than twice during the entire combat.
It cannot occur on consecutive cycles.
### Pattern
Needle Point.
Tighten the Stitch.
Snip Snip.
Needle Point.
Then check Emergency Repair.
Repeat.
# 25. Why Mopsy feels especially good here
Mopsy should have many elegant interactions without receiving arbitrary home region bonuses.
### Repair strategies
Mopsy understands attrition better than most Companions.
Turns where The Governess spends actions repairing Favorite Doll may become excellent engine building opportunities.
### Effect transfer
Many Mopsy builds already care about transferring Guard, statuses, Trick modifications, or other effects.
The Governess fight visually reinforces the same conceptual language through Stitched Together.
### Patches
Mopsy's own Patch system makes the region immediately legible.
The player understands that attached modifications can fundamentally change how something behaves.
### Stuffing
A stuffing oriented Mopsy build may favor surviving the Governess's repeated attacks and exploiting repair windows rather than racing her.
### Modified Tricks
If Mopsy has modified high impact Tricks, the player can deliberately save them for the turn Favorite Doll becomes Torn or for a vulnerable Patch cycle.
That makes the homecoming feel mechanically personal without altering the encounter rules.
# 26. Boss narrative outcomes
### Mopsy not yet rescued
The Governess has been repeatedly repairing Mopsy because she considers every tear or loose seam evidence that Mopsy is unsafe.
Defeating The Governess allows Mopsy to decide for herself whether she wants repairs.
Mopsy is freed and becomes permanently available.
### Mopsy already rescued and currently active
Defeating The Governess advances Mopsy's next Legacy Rank.
The emotional meaning changes.
Mopsy is returning to the room where someone spent years insisting that being loved meant being continuously altered into what they thought Mopsy should be.
### Mopsy rescued but another Companion is active
Normal boss reward.
No Mopsy Legacy advancement.
# 27. Multiplayer scaling
Use the same baseline established for the Foyer.
2 players:
160 percent enemy Courage.
3 players:
210 percent.
4 players:
255 percent.
Individual enemy attack damage generally remains close to solo values.
Mechanics change to account for multiple players.
# 28. Button Baby in multiplayer
Buttons remain attached to enemies rather than players.
The damage required to break a Button becomes:
12 times number of players, measured across the entire player round.
Everyone can contribute.
This creates focus fire opportunities.
# 29. Jack in the Box in multiplayer
Jammed checks total team damage against its Turn the Handle Guard.
POP! targets one player.
At 2 Wound Up, it prefers the player with the lowest percentage Courage.
The target is shown clearly before players act.
# 30. Patchwork Soldier in multiplayer
Patch Up functions normally.
No major scaling change is needed because its Courage already increases with player count.
# 31. Rocking Horse in multiplayer
Excitement remains shared.
Gallop targets one player at 0 or 1 Excitement.
At 2 or 3 Excitement:
Gallop hits all players.
Multiplayer damage becomes:
5 plus 3 per Excitement to each player.
Maximum:
14 to each player.
This turns enemy support actions into a shared threat.
# 32. Blanket Blob in multiplayer
Cover redirects the first:
6 damage per player
from attacks against the Covered enemy each round.
Each player therefore has an individual Cover allowance.
This prevents one player from trivially clearing the entire protection mechanic for everyone else.
# 33. Porcelain Doll in multiplayer
Its state remains global.
Attacks choose a single target.
When Shattered, Sharp Little Hands can target two different players.
If only one player remains available, both hits target that player.
# 34. Big Scare multiplayer changes
### Toy Chest
Slam the Lid threshold:
16 damage per player.
All team damage during the round contributes.
Maximum summoned enemies becomes:
2 with one or two players.
3 with three or four players.
### Patchwork Giant
Courage thresholds remain proportional to maximum Courage.
Players therefore tear Patches at:
75 percent.
50 percent.
25 percent.
This keeps the mechanic stable regardless of party size.
### Porcelain Twins
Both Twins scale Courage normally.
Joined Guard transfer remains unchanged.
Copied negative effects do not multiply based on the number of players applying them.
This avoids runaway status scaling.
# 35. The Governess in multiplayer
Favorite Doll scales to:
2 players: 80 Courage.
3 players: 105 Courage.
4 players: 128 Courage.
### Stitched Together
The first damage redirected from each player every round is:
So with four players, each player can independently trigger the thread.
This prevents player order from determining who gets punished by the mechanic.
### Repair windows
When Favorite Doll becomes Torn, it remains Torn until every player has completed at least one player turn before The Governess can use Mend My Darling.
Every player therefore receives access to the vulnerability window.
### Phase two Patch tearing
Threshold becomes:
20 damage per player
across the entire team round.
All players can contribute.
# 36. Cooperative opportunities
The Nursery should be especially good for coordinated multiplayer play.
Examples:
One player breaks Blanket Blob's protection.
Another delivers the major attack.
One player damages Porcelain Doll into Shattered.
Another prepares defense for the resulting dangerous attack.
One player tears Favorite Doll.
Another uses their strongest modified Trick against The Governess.
One player deliberately leaves an enemy damaged so Patchwork Soldier hurts itself repairing it.
The region should reward communication without requiring it.
# 37. Haunt Level progression
Using the same framework as the Foyer:
### Haunt 1
Ordinary enemies gain approximately 8 percent Courage.
Big Scares and The Governess gain approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced Nursery formations enter the encounter pool earlier.
### Haunt 3
Button Baby begins advanced Scuffles with one Button already attached to an ally.
### Haunt 4
Jack in the Box begins advanced encounters with 1 Wound Up.
### Haunt 5
Patchwork Soldier's Patch Up restores 11 Courage instead of 9.
It still loses 7 Courage.
This actually makes exploiting its repair behavior more important.
### Haunt 6
Blanket Blob redirects the first 10 damage instead of 8.
### Haunt 7
Rocking Horse can hold 4 Excitement.
Its Gallop still gains 4 damage per stack.
Maximum Gallop becomes 23.
### Haunt 8
Porcelain Doll enters Shattered at 14 Courage rather than 12.
The dangerous final state lasts slightly longer.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares receive signature upgrades.
Toy Chest begins with 7 Contents.
Patchwork Giant gains 2 Loose Stuffing whenever a Patch tears.
Porcelain Twins gain 6 Guard each the first time one enters a new Crack state.
### Haunt 10
The Governess begins combat with Favorite Doll carrying two Patches instead of one.
Both function simultaneously.
# 38. Higher Haunt behavior options
Beyond Haunt 10, behavioral upgrades should dominate.
Potential modifiers include:
### Button Baby
Can occasionally replace an existing Button with a stronger one.
### Patchwork Soldier
Can repair itself by dismantling another enemy that is nearly defeated.
### Blanket Blob
Can move Cover between enemies.
### Porcelain Doll
May deliberately crack itself before attacking.
### Toy Chest
Can retrieve a nearly defeated toy and return it to Contents.
### The Governess
May change the order of her phase two Patches.
At very high Haunt Levels, she could even sew one of Favorite Doll's Patches onto herself during the phase transition.
# 39. Nursery themed run rewards
The final universal terminology for this reward category can remain undecided.
For now, these are region specific passive reward concepts.
### Spare Button
The first time each combat one of your Tricks is upgraded or temporarily modified, draw 1 Trick.
### Safety Scissors
The first time each combat you remove a negative effect from yourself, gain 5 Guard.
### Favorite Blanket
At the end of your first turn each combat, gain 7 Guard.
### Sewing Kit
The first time each combat a Trick is Exhausted, discarded by an effect, transformed, or otherwise removed from its current zone by one of your own effects, gain 1 Nerve next turn.
The exact qualifying language would need to match the final global card zone terminology.
### Music Box Key
Every fourth turn, draw 1 additional Trick.
### Wooden Button
Whenever you end a Scuffle at full Courage, gain a small additional run currency reward.
This one may need adjustment depending on how easy Courage restoration eventually becomes.

# 41. What the Nursery teaches
By the time the player reaches The Governess, the region has taught:
Enemies can improve each other.
Buffs attached to enemies may be removable.
Healing can sometimes be exploited.
Damage can be redirected.
Enemy Courage thresholds can change behavior.
Weakening something may temporarily make it more dangerous.
Enemy formations can function as interconnected machines.
Breaking a protector can create a temporary offensive window.
Burst damage and controlled damage can both be correct in different situations.
Repair is not inherently beneficial.
That last concept is especially important narratively.
The Forgotten Nursery reinforces one of Midnight Menagerie's broader themes in miniature:
Fixing something without asking what it wants can become another form of control.
That makes The Governess an effective Mopsy boss while also advancing the mansion's larger philosophy.
