# The Kennels and Animal Ward
Companion: Pudding Boss: The Kennelmaster Region identity: Protection, containment, restraint, barriers, vulnerable noncombatants, defensive support, loyalty, rescue, and the difference between keeping someone safe and refusing to let them leave
The Kennels and Animal Ward should be one of the most important regions in Midnight Menagerie narratively.
Unlike most regions, the animals here are not strange monsters guarding rooms.
They are the reason the rooms exist.
Some are ordinary animals that arrived recently.
Some show early stages of Menagerie transformation.
Some are older supernatural animals that never became playable Companions.
The house considers this wing one of its safest places.
Warm beds.
Food.
Medicine.
Clean water.
Blankets.
Locked gates.
Collars.
Leashes.
Constant supervision.
The region's central combat lesson is:
Protection can become another form of control when the protected individual cannot leave.
Pudding belongs here because Pudding's own identity should represent the positive version of protection.
Loyalty.
Standing between danger and a friend.
Fetching help.
Defending someone because they need it.
And most importantly, letting them decide where they want to go afterward.

# 2. Regional mechanic: Ward Animals
Some Kennels Scuffles contain a neutral Ward Animal.
Ward Animals are not enemies.
They are never directly attacked.
They cannot die during combat.
They do not require the player to win an escort mission before they are allowed to progress.
Instead, they create optional rescue objectives inside certain fights.
Each Ward Animal has a Fright meter.
Fright ranges from 0 to 3.
Most begin at 0.
Certain containment enemies can increase Fright.
At 3 Fright:
The animal hides somewhere safe.
It leaves the combat.
The player simply loses the opportunity to free it during that encounter.
There is no permanent injury.
# 3. Freeing a Ward Animal
A Ward Animal may begin with one or more Restraints.
Examples include:
A cage latch.
A collar.
A leash.
A locked pen.
A blanket restraint.
The appropriate enemy or battlefield object explains how that Restraint can be removed.
When every Restraint is gone:
The animal is Freed.
It leaves the battlefield safely.
The player gains:
Encouraged
Gain 5 Guard.
Draw 1 additional Trick at the start of the next turn.
This happens once per Ward Animal.
### Important principle
Freeing Ward Animals is advantageous.
It is not mandatory.
A struggling player can focus on surviving the Scuffle.
A strong player can take the additional challenge for a small reward.

# 13. Encounter generation rules
A Ward Animal appears in approximately one third of normal Kennels Scuffles at Haunt 0.
The first Walking Cage encounter should always contain a Ward Animal.
Ward Animals cannot be directly damaged.
No ordinary Scuffle can contain more than one Ward Animal at baseline difficulty.
Feeding Cart and Ward Orderly can reduce Fright but cannot reduce it below 0.
Fright 3 never kills or permanently harms an animal.
Two Ward Orderlies cannot appear together at Haunt 0.
Two Comfort Blankets cannot Cover each other.
Scuffle 14 belongs only to the advanced pool.

# 17. The Kennelmaster
Boss Courage: 440
The Kennelmaster is a large supernatural caretaker in an old animal ward uniform.
It is not cruel looking.
That is important.
It carries:
Keys.
Treats.
Medical supplies.
Leads.
Towels.
A clipboard.
Its coat pockets are full of little toys.
Animals do not universally fear it.
Some even like it.
The Kennelmaster knows how to feed frightened animals.
How to approach without startling them.
How to clean wounds.
How to sit quietly beside an animal that does not trust people yet.
And it has completely lost sight of the fact that safety does not give it ownership.
Its philosophy is:
If I cannot guarantee that you will be safe outside, then I cannot responsibly let you leave.
That should make the Kennelmaster one of the clearest expressions of the mansion's central moral failure.

# 25. Important boss principle
The Kennelmaster should never attack a Ward Animal.
No boss mechanic should involve threatening to injure one if the player fails.
The conflict is:
You are trying to free them.
The Kennelmaster is trying to:
Keep the gates locked.
Calm them.
Feed them.
Restrain them.
Return them to their pens.
This distinction is central to the region.
# 26. What happens if the player ignores the animals
Nothing catastrophic.
The boss remains stronger because its protocols remain active.
At the phase transition:
Any remaining Ward Animals are moved through open side doors into safe holding rooms.
They leave the combat.
The player simply receives no Trust for animals they did not personally free.
The game never punishes an overwhelmed player with animal harm.

Remove 1 Restraint.

Remove 1 Restraint.

# 36. Why Pudding feels especially good here
### Protection
Pudding should understand defensive support better than almost any other Companion.
The region repeatedly asks:
Who actually needs protecting?
And what does useful protection look like?
### Loyalty
Pudding's loyalty should be voluntary and active.
Pudding stands beside someone because Pudding wants to.
That directly contrasts with collars, leashes, and pens.
### Fetching
Pudding may have Tricks capable of retrieving resources, objects, or support effects.
That creates thematic resonance with keys, treats, and ward supplies.
### Digging
A Pudding deck may interact naturally with buried or hidden utility concepts elsewhere, and the Kennels can reinforce the idea that Pudding always finds another way out.
### Multiplayer support
This region should make Pudding's cooperative identity particularly obvious.
Protecting another player is fundamentally different from restricting what that player may do.
That distinction should appear mechanically across Pudding's Trick pool.
# 37. Boss narrative outcome
### Pudding not yet rescued
The Kennelmaster loves Pudding.
That is important.
Pudding was fed.
Brushed.
Given toys.
Given a warm bed.
Comforted during storms.
Praised for being good.
And never allowed to decide whether to leave.
Pudding may even initially hesitate when the Kennelmaster is defeated.
This place was safe.
But Pudding ultimately chooses to follow the Kid.
That choice is what matters.
### Pudding already rescued and currently active
This becomes Pudding's Legacy homecoming.
The thematic statement is:
Loyalty means staying because you choose someone, not because they locked the gate behind you.
### Another Companion active
Normal regional boss reward.
No Pudding Legacy advancement.

# 39. Ward Animals in multiplayer
A Ward Animal is shared by the team.
Any player can damage its Restraints.
Fright is global.
Encouraged affects the player who destroys the final Restraint:
Gain 5 Guard.
Draw 1 additional Trick next turn.
In important encounters, the team may choose which player receives that benefit.
# 40. Walking Cage multiplayer
Latch Integrity:
2 players: 16
3 players: 20
4 players: 24
If no Ward Animal exists, Enclose protects one enemy against each player's first Attack separately.
# 41. Collar Keeper multiplayer
Tight Collar is player specific.
Collar Keeper rotates targets where possible.
One player's fourth Trick does not trigger another player's Collar.
# 42. Leash Hand multiplayer
Enemy Tether is shared.
Any player can contribute toward damaging the Leash Hand.
If tethering a Ward Animal, the Leash object remains a shared target.
# 43. Feeding Cart multiplayer
Meal targeting remains enemy side.
Spilling a Meal gives Found Treat to the player who crosses the damage threshold.
The Cart can reduce shared Ward Animal Fright regardless of player count.
# 44. Ward Orderly multiplayer
Stable remains a single enemy effect.
Any player can trigger it.
The whole team can contribute to pressuring the Orderly, but the break threshold is checked per player's turn rather than combined team damage.
This preserves tactical targeting.
# 45. Comfort Blanket multiplayer
Cover redirects the first 6 Attack damage from each player each round to the Blanket.
This keeps the mechanic relevant across the entire party.
# 46. Collar Collector multiplayer
Collars are shared battlefield objects.
However, player facing restrictions apply individually.
For example:
Short Collar affects each player's fourth Trick independently.
Heavy Collar reacts to each player's Guard gain separately.
Bell Collar reacts to each player's extra draw separately.

# 67. What the Kennels and Animal Ward teaches
By the time the player reaches The Kennelmaster, the region has taught:
Not every creature present during combat is an enemy.
Rescue objectives can coexist with normal victory conditions without becoming mandatory escort missions.
Protection can be mechanically beneficial while still serving an oppressive system.
Some support enemies genuinely help neutral animals.
Enemy healing and defense are not automatically the highest priority threats.
Restraint should create consequences rather than simply disabling the player.
Optional side objectives can permanently weaken a boss during the current encounter.
Defensive support can express both care and control depending on who has agency.
The mansion's systems were originally capable of helping and releasing animals.
Safety without the ability to leave is still confinement.
The thematic distinction between Pudding and The Kennelmaster should be extremely clear:
The Kennelmaster protects because it does not trust anyone else to choose correctly.
Pudding protects because someone Pudding cares about needs help, and then follows their choice.
