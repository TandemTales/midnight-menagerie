# The Mansion Graveyard
Companion: Mossbit Boss: The Groundskeeper of Names Region identity: Patience, delayed retaliation, countdowns, Epitaphs, memory, defensive timing, and consequences scheduled several turns in advance
The Mansion Graveyard should feel mechanically slower than the Impossible Greenhouse.
The Greenhouse constantly asks:
What must I stop before it grows?
The Graveyard asks:
What will happen two turns from now, and what do I need to do before then?
Most enemies here are unusually predictable.
That does not make them easy.
The danger comes from several clearly announced future events overlapping until the player has to solve a timeline rather than a single enemy turn.
This is Mossbit’s natural environment.
## 1. Graveyard enemy roster
The six ordinary enemies are:
Grave Moth
Headstone Hopper
Mourning Candle
Name Gnawer
Forget Me Not
Epitaph Spirit
The three Big Scares are:
The Mourning Angel
The Epitaph Choir
The Mausoleum Mouth
Boss:
The Groundskeeper of Names
## 2. Regional mechanic: Countdown
Several Graveyard effects use Countdown.
A Countdown is always displayed clearly.
Example:
Mournful Mark: 2
At the end of each enemy turn:
Reduce the Countdown by 1.
When it reaches 0:
Resolve the listed effect.
The player can inspect exactly what will happen.
The Graveyard should almost never surprise the player with untelegraphed delayed damage.
Its difficulty comes from overlapping forecasts.
## 3. Grave Moth
Role: Introductory delayed effect enemy Courage: 24
A pale moth large enough to cover a gravestone.
Names and dates flicker across its wings.
### Core mechanic: Mournful Mark
Grave Moth can place Mournful Mark on the player.
Mournful Mark begins at Countdown 2.
When it resolves:
Deal 8 damage.
Then remove the Mark.
Only one Mournful Mark from each Grave Moth can exist at once.
### Moves
Dust the Name
Apply Mournful Mark with Countdown 2.
Wing Brush
Deal 6 damage.
Circle the Stone
Gain 7 Guard.
If Mournful Mark is active, increase its eventual damage by 2.
Maximum 12.
### Pattern
Dust the Name.
Wing Brush.
Circle the Stone.
Wing Brush.
Repeat.
### On Grave Moth defeat
Mournful Mark remains.
This establishes the Graveyard rule:
Defeating the source does not necessarily cancel something already set in motion.
## 4. Headstone Hopper
Role: Delayed retaliation enemy Courage: 39
A gravestone that pulls itself from the soil and hops around on two stone feet.
### Core mechanic: Epitaph Retaliation
Whenever Headstone Hopper takes damage from an Attack Trick:
It records 3 Retaliation.
Maximum 15 stored Retaliation.
It does not retaliate immediately.
Instead, every third enemy turn it uses:
Epitaph Comes Due
Deal damage equal to stored Retaliation.
Then remove all stored Retaliation.
### Moves
Stone Bump
Deal 8 damage.
Sink Down
Gain 12 Guard.
Epitaph Comes Due
Deal stored Retaliation.
If no Retaliation is stored:
Gain 8 Guard instead.
### Pattern
Stone Bump.
Sink Down.
Epitaph Comes Due.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
Unlike Glassvine, Headstone Hopper does not punish the player immediately.
The player can attack freely now.
The cost arrives later.
This creates much more interesting planning.
The player may deliberately accumulate 12 Retaliation because they know they can defend it three turns from now.
## 5. Mourning Candle
Role: Support and death sequencing enemy Courage: 26
A black candle floating above a small memorial pedestal.
Its flame burns blue whenever another creature is damaged.
### Core mechanic: Mourning
Whenever another enemy is defeated:
Mourning Candle gains 1 Mourning.
Maximum 3.
Each Mourning changes its next Funeral Flame.
### Moves
Funeral Flame
Deal 5 damage plus 3 per Mourning.
Remove all Mourning.
Vigil
Another enemy gains 7 Guard.
Mourning Candle gains 5 Guard.
Remember Them
If an enemy has been defeated during this combat:
Gain 1 Mourning.
Otherwise:
Gain 8 Guard.
### Behavior
Vigil.
Funeral Flame.
Remember Them.
Repeat.
### Strategic effect
The Candle becomes stronger when the player clears its allies.
This makes kill order matter.
Sometimes the player should kill the Candle early.
Sometimes it is worth letting it remain because its baseline threat is low.
## 6. Name Gnawer
Role: Player resource interference Courage: 31
A small gray creature with oversized teeth.
It gnaws letters directly out of gravestones.
### Core mechanic: Forgotten
Name Gnawer places Forgotten on one Trick.
A Forgotten Trick remains fully playable.
However:
The first time that Trick is played while Forgotten, it costs 1 additional Nerve.
Then Forgotten disappears.
The affected Trick is shown clearly.
### Moves
Nibble the Name
Mark one random Trick in the player's hand as Forgotten.
Prefer a Trick costing at least 1 Nerve.
Gravestone Bite
Deal 8 damage.
Chew the Date
Mark the top Trick of the draw pile as Forgotten.
Its identity is revealed to the player.
### Pattern
Nibble the Name.
Gravestone Bite.
Chew the Date.
Gravestone Bite.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
This is not random cost inflation.
The player knows exactly which future Trick has been altered.
They can plan around it.
That preserves the region's prediction identity.
## 7. Forget Me Not
Role: Recurring enemy Courage: 27
A cluster of tiny blue flowers growing from a little mound of earth.
A pair of glowing eyes peers through the petals.
### Core mechanic: Remembered
The first time Forget Me Not reaches 0 Courage:
It becomes Forgotten instead of being defeated.
It disappears from the battlefield.
A Countdown 2 marker appears.
When the Countdown reaches 0:
Forget Me Not returns with 13 Courage.
The second time it reaches 0 Courage:
It is defeated permanently.
### Preventing the return
If combat ends before the Countdown finishes, it remains defeated.
Some Companion effects may later be able to remove or seal returning enemies, but baseline decks do not require such tools.
### Moves
Little Scratch
Deal 6 damage.
Bloom Again
Recover 5 Courage.
Gain 4 Guard.
Remember Me
Its next Little Scratch deals 5 additional damage.
### Pattern
Little Scratch.
Remember Me.
Little Scratch.
Bloom Again.
Repeat.
### Strategic question
Kill it early and deal with the return later?
Leave it alive while solving another threat?
Time its first defeat so the combat ends before it comes back?
## 8. Epitaph Spirit
Role: Future intent enemy Courage: 35
A translucent figure rises from behind a grave marker.
Words appear on the stone before the Spirit moves.
### Core mechanic: Epitaph
Epitaph Spirit always shows its next two actions instead of only its next action.
Example:
Current:
Cold Hand: 7 damage
Next:
Final Line: 14 damage
This gives the player unusually strong information.
The Spirit is balanced around that advantage.
### Moves
Cold Hand
Deal 7 damage.
Restless
Gain 9 Guard.
Its next damaging attack deals 3 additional damage.
Final Line
Deal 14 damage.
Fade
Gain 5 Guard.
The next Attack Trick targeting Epitaph Spirit deals 5 less damage.
Then Fade ends.
### Fixed sequence
Cold Hand.
Restless.
Final Line.
Fade.
Repeat.
The player always sees the current and following action.
### Design purpose
This enemy directly teaches the player to plan beyond the visible enemy turn.

## 12. Encounter generation rules
Grave Moth should appear before any three enemy Graveyard formation.
Two Headstone Hoppers cannot appear together at Haunt 0.
Mourning Candle should never appear alone in a normal Scuffle.
Forget Me Not cannot appear in two consecutive encounters.
Epitaph Spirit should appear alone before appearing with Headstone Hopper.
Name Gnawer cannot mark more than two Tricks simultaneously at baseline difficulty.
Scuffle 14 belongs only to the advanced pool.
## 13. Big Scare: The Mourning Angel
Courage: 127
Role: Patience and burst timing
A stone angel stands beside an empty grave.
It does not move while watched.
Its hands cover its face.
Every few turns, those hands shift slightly.
### Core mechanic: Stillness
The Mourning Angel alternates between:
Still
and
Awakened
### While Still
The Angel gains:
8 Guard at the start of its turn.
It does not attack.
Damage dealt to it creates Grief.
For every 10 Courage lost while Still:
Gain 1 Grief.
Maximum 5.
### Awakening
After two Still turns:
The Mourning Angel becomes Awakened.
Its next attack is:
Grief Unbound
Deal 5 damage plus 4 per Grief.
Then remove all Grief.
### Example
At 5 Grief:
25 damage.
### After Grief Unbound
The Angel uses:
Return to Stone
Gain 12 Guard.
Become Still again.
### Additional Still move
Silent Vigil
Gain 9 Guard.
Its next Grief Unbound gains 2 additional damage.
### Combat puzzle
Damage while Still advances the fight but powers the next attack.
The player can choose:
Attack aggressively and prepare a large defense.
Use Still turns to set up.
Deal moderate damage across cycles.
Or burst the Angel during an Awakened vulnerability window.
### Awakened vulnerability
During the player turn immediately after Grief Unbound:
The Mourning Angel takes 25 percent additional damage.
That creates a repeating rhythm:
Prepare.
Endure.
Punish.
## 14. Big Scare: The Epitaph Choir
Role: Timeline management Structure: Four gravestones rather than one shared Courage pool
The Epitaph Choir consists of four named stones.
Each has:
Courage: 32
They do not make ordinary attacks.
Instead, each Gravestone schedules an Epitaph.
### Stone of Rain
Countdown 2:
Deal 8 damage.
### Stone of Silence
Countdown 3:
Add 1 Hush status Trick to the discard pile.
Hush costs 1 Nerve.
When played:
Gain 3 Guard.
Remove it from combat afterward.
### Stone of Weight
Countdown 2:
The next Trick played after this resolves costs 1 additional Nerve.
### Stone of Sorrow
Countdown 4:
Deal 14 damage.
### Reinscription
After an Epitaph resolves:
That Gravestone waits one enemy turn.
Then inscribes the same Epitaph again at its original Countdown.
### Destroying a Gravestone
Permanently removes that timeline.
### Choir effect
As long as all four Gravestones remain:
At the end of every enemy turn:
The Gravestone with the highest current Countdown reduces its Countdown by one additional step.
This causes the timelines to gradually converge.
### Design purpose
The player can see every future event.
The challenge is deciding which timeline to erase first.
This is one of the clearest expressions of the region's identity.
## 15. Big Scare: The Mausoleum Mouth
Courage: 143
Role: Growing countdown threat
An old family mausoleum has developed teeth around its doorway.
The doors slowly open wider throughout combat.
### Core mechanic: Opening
The Mausoleum Mouth begins at Opening 0.
Maximum 4.
Each Opening level changes the encounter.
### Opening 0
No special effect.
### Opening 1
Attacks deal 2 additional damage.
### Opening 2
Gain 5 Guard at the start of every turn.
### Opening 3
Its bite attacks hit twice.
### Opening 4
Its next action becomes:
THE DOORS OPEN
### Increasing Opening
At the end of every third player turn:
Gain 1 Opening.
The timer is displayed permanently.
### Reducing Opening
If the Mausoleum Mouth takes at least 20 damage during one player turn:
Reduce Opening by 1.
Once per turn.
### Moves
Stone Teeth
Deal 10 damage.
Crypt Breath
Deal 6 damage.
Apply Forgotten to one Trick in hand.
Shut Tight
Gain 15 Guard.
The next threshold required to reduce Opening this turn becomes 16 instead of 20.
### The Doors Open
At Opening 4:
Deal 22 damage.
Summon one Epitaph Spirit with 18 Courage.
Set Opening to 1.
### Strategic identity
This encounter contains a visible long term clock.
The player can repeatedly push the clock backward through offense, or accept a Doors Open cycle and plan around it.
## 16. The Groundskeeper of Names
Boss Courage: 330
The Groundskeeper is a tall figure wrapped in a heavy cemetery coat.
A ring containing hundreds of grave markers hangs from its belt instead of keys.
Its face resembles weathered stone.
Names constantly appear and disappear across its skin.
It does not think of itself as a jailer.
It believes that being remembered is the only way something truly survives.
Its tragedy is that it has confused:
Remembering someone
with
refusing to let them leave.
## 17. Core boss mechanic: The Ledger
Beside The Groundskeeper is a supernatural cemetery ledger.
The Ledger contains three visible spaces called:
Entries
The Groundskeeper writes future events into these Entries.
Each Entry has a Countdown.
The player can always inspect:
What will happen.
Who it affects.
How many turns remain.
This is the most important rule of the encounter.
Nothing on the Ledger should be hidden.
## 18. Phase one Ledger Entries
The Groundskeeper uses four possible Epitaph Entries.
### Here Lies Courage
Countdown 2.
When resolved:
Deal 12 damage.
### Here Lies Shelter
Countdown 2.
When resolved:
The Groundskeeper gains 16 Guard.
### Here Lies Certainty
Countdown 3.
When resolved:
Mark 2 random Tricks in the player's draw pile as Forgotten.
The player is shown which Tricks were marked.
### Here Lies Haste
Countdown 2.
When resolved:
The next two Tricks the player plays cost 1 additional Nerve.
Each modifier disappears after affecting one Trick.
## 19. Altering the Ledger
The player can interact with the Ledger through combat.
Whenever The Groundskeeper loses at least 22 Courage during one player turn:
The Entry with the lowest Countdown becomes Smudged.
A Smudged Entry is delayed by 1 turn.
This can trigger once per player turn.
### Example
Here Lies Courage is at Countdown 1.
The player deals 22 damage.
It returns to Countdown 2.
This is deliberately not deletion.
The future consequence still exists.
The player has bought time.
## 20. Groundskeeper phase one moves
From 330 through 191 Courage.
### Record the Name
Add one random Ledger Entry.
Cannot duplicate an Entry already present.
Maximum three active Entries.
Gain 5 Guard.
### Cemetery Spade
Deal 12 damage.
### Mark the Stone
Deal 6 damage.
The next Ledger Entry to resolve gains an additional effect:
The Groundskeeper gains 6 Guard.
### Turn the Page
Reduce the Countdown of the Entry with the highest Countdown by 1.
If no Entries exist:
Use Record the Name instead.
### Opening sequence
Record the Name.
Cemetery Spade.
Record the Name.
Mark the Stone.
Turn the Page.
Repeat.
## 21. Ledger capacity
The Ledger holds a maximum of three Entries.
If The Groundskeeper attempts to Record the Name while all three spaces are filled:
Use:
Review the Records
Gain 10 Guard.
Reduce one random Entry Countdown by 1.
This means a crowded Ledger becomes dangerous even without adding new effects.
## 22. Phase one strategic identity
The player is constantly deciding between:
Damaging The Groundskeeper.
Reaching the 22 damage threshold to delay an Entry.
Preparing for an Entry rather than delaying it.
Allowing one manageable Entry to resolve so the Ledger frees space.
Sometimes delaying everything is actually bad because the Ledger remains full and Review the Records accelerates existing threats.
That is exactly the kind of decision this boss should create.
## 23. Mossbit connection: Patience
A Mossbit player should immediately understand that not every Countdown needs to be prevented.
If Here Lies Courage will resolve in two turns but the player can comfortably defend it:
Let it happen.
Use the current turn to establish a stronger position.
The fight rewards patience without ever requiring Mossbit specifically.
## 24. Phase transition
At 190 Courage:
The Groundskeeper uses:
Names Should Not Be Forgotten
Resolve no current Entries.
Instead:
Freeze every active Entry at its current Countdown.
Set the Ledger aside.
Three large gravestones rise from the ground.
They bear:
The player's name.
The active Companion's name.
A name the player does not recognize.
Phase two begins.
## 25. Phase two core mechanic: Memorial Stones
Three Memorial Stones become targetable battlefield objects.
Each has:
Courage: 24
Each Stone changes the battle.
### Player Stone
At the end of every second enemy turn:
Deal 6 damage to the player.
### Companion Stone
At the end of every second enemy turn:
The Groundskeeper gains 7 Guard.
### Unknown Stone
At the end of every third enemy turn:
The Groundskeeper gains 1 Memory.
Each Memory increases attack damage by 2.
Maximum 5.
### Destroying Stones
A destroyed Memorial Stone is removed permanently.
The player chooses which long term pressure to eliminate.
## 26. The unknown name
The Unknown Stone should have narrative significance.
Early in the campaign, it displays an unreadable or unfamiliar name.
As investigation progresses, its identity can change.
Possible later revelations include:
A previous child who entered the mansion.
One of the earliest animals transformed by the house.
A former caretaker.
A name connected directly to the mansion's origin.
This lets the same boss encounter reveal new story information over repeated expeditions.
## 27. Phase two moves
### Gravekeeper's Swing
Deal 15 damage.
### Dirt Over Everything
Deal 6 damage twice.
Gain 8 Guard.
### Remember Them
Gain 1 Memory.
Each surviving Memorial Stone gains 5 Guard.
### Rewrite the Stone
If at least one Memorial Stone has been destroyed:
Restore one destroyed Stone with 12 Courage.
Can occur only twice during phase two.
A restored Stone returns with its Countdown reset.
### Pattern
Gravekeeper's Swing.
Remember Them.
Dirt Over Everything.
Gravekeeper's Swing.
Then check Rewrite the Stone.
Repeat.
## 28. What happens to the old Ledger
The frozen phase one Ledger Entries remain visible during phase two.
When a Memorial Stone is destroyed:
Choose one frozen Ledger Entry.
That Entry is erased permanently.
If no Ledger Entries remain:
Destroying a Stone instead causes The Groundskeeper to lose 6 Courage.
This creates a satisfying connection between phases.
The player is dismantling the system built during phase one.
## 29. Final escalation
At 70 Courage or less:
The Groundskeeper gains:
Nothing Leaves Unremembered
Whenever a Memorial Stone is destroyed:
The Groundskeeper immediately loses 8 Courage.
Rewrite the Stone is disabled.
Memory can no longer increase.
The battle accelerates toward conclusion instead of adding another phase.
## 30. Why Mossbit feels especially good here
### Delayed effects
Mossbit's entire combat identity already asks the player to think in future turns.
The Ledger feels natural rather than overwhelming.
### Tombstone defense
Memorial Stones visually and mechanically echo Mossbit's own tombstone identity.
### Delayed retaliation
Headstone Hopper demonstrates a hostile version of the same broad idea Mossbit may use offensively.
### Inevitability
Mossbit builds can often allow a future threat to exist because their own delayed engine is even more powerful.
### Patience
The Groundskeeper punishes panic more than slowness.
That makes the region a natural homecoming for Mossbit.
## 31. Boss narrative outcome
### Mossbit not yet rescued
The Groundskeeper has kept Mossbit near the graveyard because Mossbit carries an old tombstone as part of its transformed body.
The Groundskeeper believes Mossbit belongs among the names of the dead.
But Mossbit is not dead.
Mossbit is still very much alive.
Defeating The Groundskeeper allows Mossbit to leave the graveyard and decide what its tombstone means for itself.
### Mossbit already rescued and currently active
This becomes Mossbit's Legacy homecoming.
The thematic statement is:
Remembering where you came from does not mean you have to stay there.
### Another Companion active
Normal regional boss reward.
No Mossbit Legacy advancement.
## 32. Multiplayer scaling
Use the established baseline:
2 players:
160 percent Courage.
3 players:
210 percent.
4 players:
255 percent.
The Graveyard's main multiplayer challenge should come from several personal timelines running simultaneously.
## 33. Grave Moth multiplayer
Mournful Mark targets one player.
Different Grave Moths can mark different players.
The target is clearly shown.
Circle the Stone strengthens only that Moth's current Mark.
## 34. Headstone Hopper multiplayer
Stored Retaliation tracks damage from the whole team.
Epitaph Comes Due targets the player who contributed the most Attack damage to the stored Retaliation.
The intended target is updated live during the round.
This creates an interesting cooperative choice.
A heavily defensive player can deliberately become the primary attacker against the Hopper and absorb the future retaliation.
## 35. Mourning Candle multiplayer
Funeral Flame targets one player at 0 or 1 Mourning.
At 2 or 3 Mourning:
It hits all players.
Recommended team damage:
4 plus 2 per Mourning to each player.
## 36. Name Gnawer multiplayer
Nibble the Name targets one player's hand.
It prefers the player with the most available Nerve.
Chew the Date reveals and marks one future Trick belonging to one player.
## 37. Forget Me Not multiplayer
Its return Countdown remains global.
It does not gain additional resurrection cycles with player count.
## 38. Epitaph Spirit multiplayer
Its future intents include the intended target.
Players therefore have two turns of information about who will be attacked.
This gives protective Companions and multiplayer Tricks meaningful opportunities.
## 39. Mourning Angel multiplayer
Grief accumulates from all team damage.
Grief Unbound targets all players.
Recommended multiplayer damage:
3 plus 3 per Grief to each player.
Maximum at 5 Grief:
18 to each player.
The Awakened vulnerability window lasts through one full team round.
## 40. Epitaph Choir multiplayer
Some Stones become player specific.
Rain:
Targets one player.
Silence:
Targets a different player when possible.
Weight:
Targets the player with the highest current Nerve.
Sorrow:
Hits all players for reduced damage.
This produces multiple parallel countdowns that teammates can discuss.
## 41. Mausoleum Mouth multiplayer
The Opening meter remains shared.
At Opening 4:
The Doors Open damages all players.
Recommended damage:
2 players: 17 each
3 players: 15 each
4 players: 14 each
The summoned Epitaph Spirit remains one enemy regardless of party size.
## 42. The Groundskeeper multiplayer
The Ledger remains shared.
However, Entries can target specific players.
### Here Lies Courage
Targets one player.
### Here Lies Shelter
Still benefits The Groundskeeper.
### Here Lies Certainty
Marks one Trick in two different players' draw piles when possible.
### Here Lies Haste
Affects the next Trick played by two separate players.
### Smudge threshold
Use team damage during the round.
2 players:
34 damage.
3 players:
45 damage.
4 players:
55 damage.
Only one Entry can be Smudged per team round.
## 43. Multiplayer Memorial Stones
Phase two gains one additional Memorial Stone at larger party sizes.
1 player:
3 Stones.
2 players:
3 Stones.
3 players:
4 Stones.
4 players:
5 Stones.
Additional Stones can include:
### Friendship Stone
Every third enemy turn:
All players lose 3 Guard at the end of their turns for one round.
### Promise Stone
Every third enemy turn:
The Groundskeeper gains 8 Guard.
The player team therefore chooses which persistent problems to erase first.
## 44. Multiplayer cooperative opportunities
The Graveyard should be excellent for coordination.
One player may volunteer to absorb Headstone Hopper retaliation.
Another can save Guard for a known Epitaph Spirit attack.
The team can decide which Epitaph Choir timeline is unacceptable.
A burst oriented player can hold damage for the Groundskeeper's Smudge threshold.
A defensive player may tell the group to allow Here Lies Courage to resolve because they can protect the targeted teammate.
The region turns combat discussion into:
What happens next?
rather than merely:
Who do we kill first?
## 45. Haunt Level progression
### Haunt 1
Ordinary enemies gain approximately 8 percent Courage.
Big Scares and The Groundskeeper gain approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced Graveyard formations appear earlier.
### Haunt 3
Grave Moth begins advanced encounters with Mournful Mark already at Countdown 2.
### Haunt 4
Headstone Hopper can store up to 18 Retaliation instead of 15.
### Haunt 5
Mourning Candle's maximum Mourning increases to 4.
### Haunt 6
Name Gnawer can maintain up to 3 Forgotten Tricks simultaneously.
### Haunt 7
Forget Me Not returns after Countdown 1 rather than 2 in advanced encounters.
### Haunt 8
Epitaph Spirit displays its next three actions rather than two.
This sounds beneficial, but its sequence also becomes more dangerous:
Cold Hand.
Restless.
Final Line.
Final Line.
Fade.
The extra information allows the stronger pattern to remain fair.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares receive upgrades.
Mourning Angel begins with 1 Grief.
Epitaph Choir begins with one Countdown already reduced by 1.
Mausoleum Mouth begins at Opening 1.
### Haunt 10
The Groundskeeper begins combat with one Ledger Entry already written at Countdown 3.
The Entry is visible before the player takes their first turn.
## 46. Higher Haunt behavior upgrades
At higher mastery levels, potential changes include:
Grave Moth may transfer its Mournful Mark to another target.
Headstone Hopper may split stored Retaliation across two future turns.
Mourning Candle may delay Funeral Flame deliberately to accumulate more Mourning.
Name Gnawer may move Forgotten from one Trick to another.
Forget Me Not may return in a different bloom state.
Epitaph Spirit may rewrite one future action after the player commits to a particular behavior.
Epitaph Choir may contain one randomly selected fifth Gravestone.
The Groundskeeper may move an Entry from Countdown 3 directly to Countdown 1 by sacrificing Guard.
These should remain clearly telegraphed.
Even at the highest Haunt Levels, the Graveyard should be difficult because of planning complexity, not hidden information.
## 47. Graveyard themed run reward concepts
### Smooth Pebble
The first time each combat you would take delayed damage from a Countdown effect, reduce it by 4.
### Graveyard Candle
Every third turn, gain 5 Guard.
### Weathered Epitaph
At the start of combat, reveal the enemy's next action after its current intent whenever that enemy supports future intent information.
For enemies without future intent systems, this does nothing.
This reward may need broadening during global relic design.
### Forget Me Not Flower
The first time each combat your Courage would fall below 25 percent, gain 8 Guard.
### Old Gate Key
Graveyard gates, crypt doors, mausoleums, memorial passages, and burial tunnels are easier to open during exploration.
### Mourning Ribbon
The first time an enemy is defeated each combat, draw 1 additional Trick at the start of your next turn.
## 48. Graveyard Curiosity hooks
### The Fresh Grave
The dirt is newly disturbed.
No name appears on the stone.
Possible choices include:
Dig.
Read the ground with a clue item.
Leave a personal object.
Mark it on the blueprint.
### The Pet Cemetery
Many ordinary pet names appear on tiny stones.
Some date back more than a century.
Several names match current Menagerie Companions.
But the dates do not make sense.
### The Headstone Without a Death Date
It displays one Kid's full name.
Their birth date is correct.
The death date is blank.
This should be unsettling without becoming graphic horror.

## 51. Blueprint investigation progression
Early map notes might read:
"Graveyard is INSIDE the house?"
Later:
"Sometimes outside. Sometimes not."
Later still:
"Pet graves older than mansion?"
And eventually:
"Names show up before animals disappear."
That last discovery could be an important campaign clue.
It suggests the mansion may somehow anticipate which animals will eventually enter it.
Whether that is prophecy, supernatural observation, or something else can remain unresolved for a long time.
## 52. What the Mansion Graveyard teaches
By the time the player reaches The Groundskeeper of Names, the region has taught:
A threat can be dangerous even when it is several turns away.
Killing an enemy does not always cancel effects it already created.
Retaliation does not have to be immediate.
Enemy death order can alter future turns.
Temporary Trick penalties are easier to manage when revealed in advance.
Some enemies return after apparent defeat.
More information can support more complex enemy behavior without making combat unfair.
Offensive pressure can delay future consequences rather than simply dealing damage.
Sometimes the correct response to an inevitable effect is to prepare for it rather than prevent it.
Patience is an active strategy, not passivity.
That makes the Graveyard the first region where Midnight Menagerie can strongly establish future state planning as a major part of its combat identity.
