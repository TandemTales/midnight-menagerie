# The Impossible Greenhouse
Companion: Brambleboo Boss: The Head Gardener Region identity: Roots, vines, seeds, propagation, battlefield growth, delayed hazards, territorial control, and escalating ecosystems
The Impossible Greenhouse should feel like the first mansion region where the player is not merely fighting individual creatures.
They are fighting an ecosystem.
The greenhouse is impossibly large inside. Entire groves grow beneath glass ceilings. Vines pass through walls and emerge several rooms away. Plants grow in seconds. Flower beds migrate. Roots buckle stone floors.
The central combat lesson is:
Small problems become large problems if allowed to take root.
Brambleboo naturally understands these systems because its own Trick pool revolves around propagation, Roots, Vines, growth, and controlling an increasingly crowded battlefield.

# 2. Regional mechanic: Growth
Several Greenhouse enemies use Growth.
Growth represents a plant becoming larger, more established, or more dangerous.
Growth is not one universal buff with identical effects.
Instead, each enemy explains what Growth does for that creature.
This is important because Brambleboo can use Growth as a much deeper Companion mechanic without requiring every Greenhouse enemy to share exactly the same rules.
The region teaches the language:
If something is growing, pay attention to what happens when it matures.
# 3. Potling
Role: Introductory propagation enemy Courage: 23
A ceramic flowerpot waddles around on little root legs.
A nervous seedling sticks out of the top.
### Core mechanic: Seeds
Potling can plant Seedlings.
Seedlings are minor battlefield objects.
They have:
Courage: 6
Seedlings do not attack.
At the beginning of the third enemy turn after being planted, a surviving Seedling becomes a new Potling with 14 Courage.
Maximum two Seedlings can exist from one Potling.
### Moves
Plant Seed
Create 1 Seedling.
Pot Bash
Deal 6 damage.
Water Itself
Gain 6 Guard.
If a Seedling exists, reduce its maturation timer by 1 turn.
### Pattern
Plant Seed.
Pot Bash.
Water Itself.
Pot Bash.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
This is the region's simplest lesson.
The Seedling is harmless now.
It will not remain harmless.
The player chooses whether the Seedling deserves immediate attention.

### Core mechanic: Bloom

Closed
Opening
Full Bloom
### Closed

### Opening
No automatic Guard.
### Full Bloom

### Moves

Advance one Bloom stage.
Gain 5 Guard.
Snap
Deal 7 damage.
If Full Bloom:
Deal 17 instead.
After Snap, return to Closed.
Pollen Shake
Deal 4 damage.

### Pollen

### Pattern

Pollen Shake.
Unfurl.
Snap.
Repeat.
### Interrupting Bloom
If Snapping Blossom loses at least 14 Courage during one player turn while Full Bloom:
Return it to Opening.

This gives the player a chance to prune the threat before it fully resolves.
# 5. Creeping Ivy
Role: Attachment support enemy Courage: 29
A bundle of vines creeps toward other creatures and wraps around them.
### Core mechanic: Entwine
Creeping Ivy can attach itself to one other enemy.
While Entwined:
Creeping Ivy cannot use its normal attacks.
The host gains:
5 Guard at the beginning of its turn.
2 additional attack damage.
Creeping Ivy receives 50 percent of damage dealt to the host, rounded down.
The host still receives full damage.
### Breaking Entwine
If Creeping Ivy takes 12 total damage through Entwine:
Entwine breaks.
Creeping Ivy becomes separately targetable again.
### Moves while unattached
Creep
Become Entwined with another enemy.
Vine Lash
Deal 7 damage.
Take Root
Gain 9 Guard.
### Behavior
If a valid ally exists:
Creep.
Otherwise:
Vine Lash.
Take Root.
Repeat.
### Combat lesson
Some support enemies become harder to remove after attaching themselves, but attacking the host can solve both problems at once.

When the timer reaches 0:
Deal 7 damage.

### Moves

Create 1 Spore Cloud.
Puff Up
Gain 8 Guard.

Soft Bonk

### Pattern
Release Spores.
Soft Bonk.
Puff Up.
Soft Bonk.
Repeat.
### On defeat

This is crucial.
Killing Spore Puff does not erase consequences it already created.
### Design purpose
The player must account for future damage already scheduled on the battlefield.

# 13. Big Scare: The Compost Colossus
Courage: 132
Role: Regeneration and removable growths
A gigantic creature made from soil, rotting leaves, broken flowerpots, pruning scraps, worms, and discarded roots.
It constantly regrows damaged pieces.
### Core mechanic: Regrowth Nodes
The Compost Colossus begins with three Regrowth Nodes.
Each Node has:
Courage: 16
Nodes are separately targetable.
At the end of the Colossus's turn:
Each surviving Node restores 4 Courage to the Colossus.
Maximum 12 per turn.
### Node regrowth
When a Node is destroyed:
It begins a three turn regrowth timer.
If the Colossus is above 50 percent Courage when that timer expires:
The Node returns with 8 Courage.
If the Colossus is below 50 percent:
Destroyed Nodes no longer regrow.
### Colossus moves
Compost Fist
Deal 12 damage.
Heap Up
Gain 12 Guard.
Reduce all Node regrowth timers by 1.
Root Slam
Deal 6 damage twice.
Create one Regrowth Node if fewer than three exist and no destroyed Node is already regrowing.
Mulch
Lose 6 Courage.
All surviving Nodes recover to full Courage.
### Strategic choices
Destroy Nodes early.
Race the main body.
Push below half Courage before investing in Node removal.
Use wide damage to pressure everything.
The fight supports multiple approaches.
# 14. Big Scare: The Carnivorous Conservatory
Courage: 145**
The extra asterisk above is not part of a new mechanic. Its Courage target is simply 145.
Role: Battlefield takeover
This Big Scare is not one plant.
It is an entire greenhouse room that has become predatory.
Vines seal the doors.
Flowers turn toward the player.
Roots move beneath the floor.
### Core mechanic: Overgrowth
The Conservatory begins at 0 Overgrowth.
Maximum 6.
Several moves create Overgrowth.
Each Overgrowth changes the fight.
At 2:
The Conservatory gains 4 Guard at the start of every turn.
At 4:
Its attacks deal 3 additional damage.
At 6:
It gains access to Room Consumed.
### Removing Overgrowth
Three Growth Patches appear around the battlefield.
Each Patch has 12 Courage.
Destroying a Patch:
Remove 1 Overgrowth.
The Patch regrows after two enemy turns.
Only two Patches can be active at baseline difficulty.
### Moves

Gain 1 Overgrowth.
Gain 8 Guard.
Hungry Flowers
Deal 5 damage twice.
Root Burst
Deal 10 damage.
Gain 1 Overgrowth.
Seed Everywhere

If maximum Patches already exist, gain 1 Overgrowth instead.
### Room Consumed
Available only at 6 Overgrowth.

### Pattern
Vine Across the Door.
Hungry Flowers.
Root Burst.
Seed Everywhere.
Repeat.

### Combat identity

# 16. The Head Gardener
Boss Courage: 320
The Head Gardener is enormous but still recognizably humanoid.
Its clothes are stitched from old gardening aprons.
Its face is hidden behind a cracked porcelain gardening mask.
Roots have grown through its boots and into the greenhouse floor.
One arm ends in pruning shears.
The other has become a watering can fused directly into its wrist.
The Head Gardener believes every living thing has a correct place and correct shape.
Its philosophy is:
Anything can thrive if properly cultivated.
The problem is that it decides what thriving means.
# 17. The Head Gardener's core mechanic: Garden Beds
Three Garden Beds appear beside the boss.
They are named:
Left Bed
Center Bed
Right Bed
Each Garden Bed can contain one plant.
Garden Beds are not enemies themselves.
The Head Gardener plants Seeds into them.
Seeds mature into different supernatural plants.
The player can attack Seeds before they mature.

### Water the Beds

### Sow

### Pressure curve
The player can choose to prune Seeds constantly.
Or allow selected plants to mature because their effects are manageable.
A defensive deck may tolerate Thorn Bush.
A cheap Trick deck may strongly dislike Binding Vine.
A slow scaling deck may care most about Moon Bloom.
There should not be one universally correct plant priority.
# 21. Core interaction: Uproot
Whenever a Seed or mature plant is destroyed:
The Garden Bed becomes Uprooted until the end of the Head Gardener's next turn.
The Head Gardener cannot Sow into an Uprooted Bed.
This gives removing plants additional value.
The player does not merely destroy the current problem.
They temporarily deny space for future growth.
# 22. Head Gardener special mechanic: Pruning
Every fourth Head Gardener action:
Use Prune the Weak.
Prune the Weak checks all plants.
If a Seed exists:
Destroy the Seed closest to maturity.
The Head Gardener gains Cultivated Growth.
If only mature plants exist:
Destroy the lowest Courage mature plant instead.
### Cultivated Growth
Each stack grants:
2 permanent attack damage.
Maximum 4.
This creates an important twist.
The boss can consume its own garden for power.
The player cannot simply assume every enemy planted object should remain alive because the boss wants it alive.
Sometimes the player may destroy a plant before The Head Gardener can convert it into Growth.
# 23. Phase transition
At 180 Courage:
The Head Gardener uses:
Let Nature Take Its Course
Destroy all Seeds.
All mature plants remain.
Fill every empty Garden Bed with a Wild Seed.
Wild Seeds mature immediately.
The greenhouse becomes uncontrolled.
Phase two begins.
# 24. Wild Growth
Each Wild Seed becomes one randomly selected plant.
The same plant can now appear more than once.
This is the major difference from phase one.
The player can face:
Two Thorn Bushes.
Two Binding Vines.
Two Moon Blooms.
Or three copies of one plant at higher Haunt Levels.
The Head Gardener no longer insists on proper arrangement.
Its controlled garden is collapsing into overgrowth.
# 25. Phase two core mechanic: Overgrown
At the end of each Head Gardener turn:
If all three Garden Beds contain mature plants:
Gain 1 Overgrown.
Maximum 3.
Each Overgrown gives:
2 attack damage.
At 3 Overgrown:
The Head Gardener's next action becomes:
The Greenhouse Takes You
Deal 22 damage.
Each mature plant triggers its effect one additional time.
Then remove all Overgrown.
### Preventing Overgrown
Destroying even one plant before the end of the enemy turn prevents that turn's Overgrown gain.
This creates a clear pressure objective.

### Root Sweep

### Water Everything

### Scatter Seed

# 27. Garden collapse at low Courage
At 70 Courage or less:
The Head Gardener gains:
Desperate Cultivation
Whenever the player destroys a mature plant:
The Head Gardener loses 4 Courage.
The supernatural garden is now physically connected to the boss.
This makes the final phase faster rather than adding another layer of mechanics.
It also creates a satisfying finish.
Destroying the garden now directly damages the person controlling it.
# 28. Why Brambleboo feels especially good here
Brambleboo should have natural, elegant interactions with nearly every combat concept.
### Propagation
Brambleboo players already think about what should be allowed to grow.
Seed timers are therefore intuitively readable.
### Roots and Vines
Enemy rooting and attachment mechanics mirror Brambleboo's own systems in altered forms.
### Battlefield control
A Brambleboo deck may be unusually comfortable managing multiple minor targets and persistent battlefield objects.
### Overgrowth
Brambleboo may sometimes actively benefit from crowded or overgrown battlefield states.
That creates interesting moments where a mechanic intended as enemy pressure becomes an opportunity.
### Delayed effects
Growth based Brambleboo builds may be willing to tolerate Seeds longer because they are comfortable planning several turns ahead.
Again, none of these should be explicit home region bonuses.
# 29. Boss narrative outcome
### Brambleboo not yet rescued
The Head Gardener considers Brambleboo its masterpiece.
It has spent years pruning, repotting, grafting, propagating, and controlling Brambleboo's supernatural growth.
The Head Gardener believes this is why Brambleboo became extraordinary.
The reality is that Brambleboo became extraordinary despite never being allowed to grow freely.
Defeating The Head Gardener frees Brambleboo from its assigned Garden Bed.
### Brambleboo already rescued and currently active
This becomes Brambleboo's Legacy homecoming.
The emotional theme is:
Growth is not the same thing as obedience.
### Another Companion active
Normal region boss reward.
No Brambleboo Legacy advancement.

# 40. The Head Gardener multiplayer
Garden Beds remain shared.
Three Beds are used with one or two players.
Four Beds with three players.
Five Beds with four players.
This is one of the first bosses where increasing player count should alter battlefield structure rather than only numbers.
### Multiplayer Overgrown requirement
Overgrown is gained only if every Garden Bed is occupied at the end of the enemy turn.
More players therefore create more Garden Beds but also more player actions available to control them.
### Garden Bed targeting
Some effects can explicitly target a plant threatening a particular player.
Binding Vine chooses one player when it matures.
That player's marked Trick cost is affected.
Thorn Bush retaliates against the player who triggers it.
Moon Bloom remains global.
# 41. Multiplayer cooperative opportunities
One player can function as the team's primary pruner.
One can concentrate damage on The Head Gardener.
A Companion with broad damage can control Seedlings.
A defensive player may deliberately accept a Binding Vine effect because their deck is less sensitive to Nerve cost changes.
Players can coordinate which Seed gets allowed to mature.
One player can finish a mature plant just before Overgrown would trigger.
This region should make team role differentiation feel particularly natural.

### Haunt 2

### Haunt 3

### Haunt 4

### Haunt 5
Creeping Ivy grants its host 7 starting Guard per turn instead of 5.
### Haunt 6
Spore Puff's first Spore Cloud deals 9 damage instead of 7.
### Haunt 7

It can become Stag Form, gaining 1 Growth.
### Haunt 8

### Haunt 9
Big Scares gain signature upgrades.
Compost Colossus begins with one Node already partially regrowing after destruction.

Ancient Topiary begins with 1 Growth.
### Haunt 10

# 43. Higher Haunt behavior upgrades
At higher mastery levels, potential changes include:
Potling can plant different Seedling species.
Creeping Ivy can move from one host to another.
Spore Clouds may affect the battlefield rather than only deal damage.
Topiary Beast may deliberately enter a form based on the player's strongest recent behavior.
Glassvine may spread a temporary Glass Thorn effect to another enemy.
Compost Colossus may transplant a Regrowth Node.
Carnivorous Conservatory may permanently consume one Growth Patch and replace it elsewhere.
The Head Gardener may graft one plant's effect onto another.
These should be reserved for high Haunt Levels because the baseline region already contains considerable battlefield state tracking.

# 48. What the Impossible Greenhouse teaches
By the time the player reaches The Head Gardener, the region has taught:
Minor battlefield objects can mature into real threats.
Not every dangerous effect comes from an enemy attack.
Enemies can attach themselves to other enemies.
Delayed effects may survive after their creator dies.
Enemy forms can react to player behavior.
Retaliation can sometimes be removed through concentrated offense.
Battlefield population is itself a resource.
Killing every minor object immediately is not always optimal.
Some threats should be pruned before they become established.
Growth is only beneficial when the thing growing has room and freedom to become what it actually is.
