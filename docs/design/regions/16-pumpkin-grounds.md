# The Moon Courtyard and Pumpkin Grounds
Companion: Pipkin Boss: The Harvest King Region identity: Seeds, staged growth, jumping, ripening, harvesting, pumpkin transformations, timing windows, and choosing whether something should be destroyed now or allowed to mature first
The Moon Courtyard should feel very different from the Impossible Greenhouse.
The Greenhouse is about uncontrolled propagation.
The Pumpkin Grounds are about cultivated timing.
Everything here has a season.
Seeds become sprouts.
Sprouts become fruit.
Fruit ripens.
Ripe things are harvested.
Anything left too long becomes overripe and turns into something stranger.
Moonlight acts like sunlight.
Pumpkins grow in seconds.
Scarecrows sprout directly from soil.
Frogs leap between enormous gourds.
The central combat lesson is:
The safest time to destroy something is not always the most valuable time to destroy it.
Pipkin belongs here because Pipkin's own identity should involve planting, jumping between stages of development, harvesting created resources, and deciding when growth has reached the form the player actually wants.
# 1. Regional mechanic: Ripeness
Several Pumpkin Grounds enemies and objects use four visible stages.
Seed
Growing
Ripe
Overripe
Not every enemy uses all four stages.
The broad regional rules are:
Seed and Growing forms are generally safer.
Ripe forms are more dangerous but often more vulnerable or valuable.
Overripe forms create consequences if the player waits too long.
A Ripe target that is defeated or destroyed may trigger a beneficial Harvest effect.
This creates a crucial distinction from the Greenhouse.
In the Greenhouse, allowing growth is usually something the player tolerates.
Here, the player may actively want something to reach maturity.
# 2. Harvest
Harvest is a keyword used by certain regional enemies and battlefield objects.
If something says:
Harvest: Gain 5 Guard
that effect occurs only if the target is destroyed while Ripe.
Destroying it earlier still removes the threat.
The player simply forfeits the Harvest reward.
Harvest effects should remain modest.
The player should never feel forced to let dangerous enemies mature.
# 3. Pumpkin Pip
Role: Introductory Ripeness enemy Courage: 26
A tiny pumpkin with frog legs.
It begins barely larger than an apple.
### Core mechanic: Ripen
Pumpkin Pip begins Growing.
After each of its actions:
Advance one stage.
Growing becomes Ripe.
Ripe becomes Overripe.
### Growing
Attack damage reduced by 2.
### Ripe
Take 20 percent additional damage.
Attacks deal 2 additional damage.
### Overripe
Attack damage gains another 3.
At the end of its own turn:
Lose 3 Courage.
### Moves
Pumpkin Bump
Deal 7 damage.
### Little Hop
Deal 5 damage.
Gain 5 Guard.
### Squishy Slam
Used while Overripe.
Deal 11 damage.
Lose 2 Courage afterward.
### Harvest
If defeated while Ripe:
Gain 5 Guard.
### Pattern
Pumpkin Bump.
Little Hop.
Pumpkin Bump.
Repeat.
Its state changes the numbers.
### Design purpose
This is the cleanest possible introduction to the region.
Kill it early for safety.
Or wait one turn, accept a stronger enemy, and receive a small reward for harvesting it at the correct moment.

# 12. Encounter generation rules
Pumpkin Pip should appear before Harvest Hopper.
Moonseed should first appear without Harvest Hopper.
Two Harvest Hoppers cannot appear together at Haunt 0.
Two Gourd Guards cannot appear together at Haunt 0.
Harvest rewards trigger only once per enemy.
An enemy cannot become Ripe and Overripe during the same resolution unless a special effect explicitly says so.
Harvest Hopper cannot reduce a Tasty enemy below 1 Courage.
Scuffle 14 belongs only to the advanced pool.

# 16. The Harvest King
Boss Courage: 445
The Harvest King is a huge supernatural figure with a carved pumpkin head crowned by twisting antlers made from dead vines.
Its body is a mixture of old gardening clothes, straw, roots, and moonlit leaves.
It carries a crooked harvesting sickle.
Tiny pumpkins grow along its cloak.
The Harvest King is not obsessed with growing things forever.
It understands endings.
It understands seasons.
Its flaw is different.
It believes everything has a correct moment when it should be harvested.
And it assumes it gets to decide when that moment has arrived.
Its philosophy is:
Nothing should be picked before its time, and nothing should remain after it is ready.
That is a dangerous philosophy when applied to living beings.

Gain 8 Guard.
## Spark Pumpkin
### If the King harvests it Ripe

### If the player harvests it Ripe
Gain 1 Nerve next turn.
## Moon Squash
### If the King harvests it Ripe
Recover 10 Courage.

Recover 4 Courage.

# 19. Crop growth
At the end of every Harvest King turn:
Every occupied Plot advances one stage.
Seed becomes Growing.
Growing becomes Ripe.
Ripe becomes Overripe.
### Overripe
If a Crop would become Overripe:
The Harvest King immediately receives the weakened version of its Harvest benefit.
Guard Gourd:
Gain 8 Guard.
Spark Pumpkin:
Next attack gains 4 damage.
Moon Squash:
Recover 5 Courage.
Then the Crop rots and the Plot becomes empty.
### Why weaken the Overripe reward?
The King's ideal outcome is harvesting at exactly the correct time.
Failing to harvest still provides some value, but less.
This reinforces its theme.
# 20. Player Harvest
The player can attack Crops.
Destroying a Crop while Ripe:
Triggers its player Harvest effect.
Destroying it during Seed or Growing:
Removes it with no reward.
This creates a constant timing problem.
A Ripe Moon Squash is effectively a small healing opportunity.
But waiting for it allowed the King several turns to act.
# 21. Player interaction: Encourage Growth
Whenever the player plays their fourth Trick during one turn:
They may choose one Crop and advance it one stage.
Once per turn.
This is optional.
### Why would the player do this?
A Growing Spark Pumpkin can be advanced to Ripe and immediately harvested before the King gets another turn.
A Seed can be rushed toward usefulness.
A Ripe crop should usually not be advanced because that would force it Overripe.
This gives every deck a baseline way to manipulate maturation.
Pipkin's own Tricks can go much deeper.
# 22. Player interaction: Bruise
Whenever a Crop takes at least 8 damage but survives:
It becomes Bruised.
A Bruised Crop does not advance at the end of the next enemy turn.
Then Bruised disappears.
This can occur once per Crop per stage.
### Why Bruise matters
The player can intentionally delay a Ripe crop they are not ready to harvest.
It also gives low damage decks another way to manipulate timing.

Check the Crop.
Seed Spit.

# 25. Harvest priority
If several Crops are Ripe:
The King chooses according to current need.
If below 50 percent Courage:
Prefer Moon Squash.
If incoming attack is already strong:
Prefer Guard Gourd.
Otherwise:
Prefer Spark Pumpkin.
The intended Harvest target is shown before the player acts.
This gives the player a clear opportunity to steal that Harvest first.
# 26. Spoiled Harvest
If the player destroys the Crop the King has marked for Harvest:
The King becomes Disappointed.
Its planned Harvest changes to:
Inspect the Empty Vine
Gain 7 Guard.
Deal no damage.
Then continue the normal sequence.
### Design purpose
Stealing a Harvest gives both the Crop reward and a tempo advantage.
That should feel excellent.
# 27. The King's Harvest tally
Whenever the Harvest King successfully Harvests a Ripe Crop:
Gain 1 Bounty.
Maximum 4.
Each Bounty gives:
1 attack damage.
At 4 Bounty:
The next Sickle Sweep becomes:
Royal Reaping
Deal 18 damage.
Then reduce Bounty to 2.
### Player incentive
Preventing Harvests controls long term boss scaling.
But letting a low danger Guard Gourd through may occasionally be worth it if the player can use the turn more efficiently elsewhere.
# 28. Phase transition
At 255 Courage:
The Harvest King uses:
The Moon Is High
Resolve no current Crop Harvests.
All existing Crops advance immediately to Ripe.
Any already Ripe Crop remains Ripe.
Remove Bruised.
Increase the Royal Patch from three Plots to four Plots.

Phase two begins.
# 29. Phase transition opportunity
The player begins the next turn facing several Ripe Crops.
This should feel exciting rather than purely threatening.
The boss is effectively saying:
Everything is ready.
The player gets first access to the harvest.
A strong player may turn the transition into a huge resource turn.

# 31. Phase two mechanic: Moon Ripening

### Important consequence
The player now has more ability to accelerate crops toward Ripe.

The phase is about managing abundance rather than scarcity.

# 33. Double Harvest threat
Harvest Feast is the primary danger of phase two.
The King marks its intended Crop targets before the player acts.
The player can:
Destroy one early.
Harvest one Ripe Crop themselves.
Bruise a Growing Crop so it does not become a second valid target.
Use Encourage Growth on a different Crop to create a more attractive but less dangerous Harvest target.
The battlefield should feel highly manipulable.
# 34. Player stealing a Harvest Feast target
For each marked Crop the player Harvests before Harvest Feast:
The King loses 4 Courage.

Harvest Feast becomes:
Empty Basket
Gain 8 Guard.
Reduce Bounty by 1.
Deal no damage.
This is one of the strongest mastery rewards in the fight.
# 35. Final escalation
At 95 Courage or less:
The Harvest King gains:
Last Harvest
Crops no longer become Overripe.
They remain Ripe until harvested or destroyed.
The King no longer gains Bounty.
Whenever the player Harvests a Crop:
The Harvest King loses 3 Courage.
Whenever the King Harvests one:
It loses 2 Courage after gaining the normal effect.
### Thematic reason
The King's supernatural season is ending.
Every remaining Harvest now brings the fight closer to completion regardless of who takes it.
# 36. Why Pipkin feels especially good here
### Seeds
Pipkin naturally understands the difference between placing a Seed and receiving its eventual payoff.
### Growth
Pipkin's deck should include several ways to accelerate or manipulate growth.
The Royal Patch therefore becomes especially expressive.
### Harvest
A Pipkin player should constantly evaluate whether a created object is more valuable now or later.
That is exactly what the region asks.
### Jumping
Pipkin's jumping identity can allow Tricks to change timing, skip stages, trigger effects again, or interact with created objects.
### Transformation
Pipkin should feel comfortable with things that change form repeatedly during combat.
The region reinforces that mindset without granting an automatic numerical advantage.
# 37. Boss narrative outcome
### Pipkin not yet rescued
The Harvest King considers Pipkin a particularly confusing crop.
Pipkin is a frog.
Pipkin is also somehow pumpkin like.
Pipkin plants Seeds.
Pipkin jumps out of carefully prepared garden plots.
Pipkin refuses to ripen according to schedule.
The Harvest King has repeatedly tried to determine Pipkin's proper season.
Pipkin keeps leaving the garden bed.
The King sees this as terrible agricultural discipline.
Defeating the Harvest King lets Pipkin decide whether Pipkin is a frog, a pumpkin, a plant, a ghostly garden creature, or all of those at once.
### Pipkin already rescued and currently active
This becomes Pipkin's Legacy homecoming.
The thematic statement is:
Growing into something does not mean someone else gets to decide when you are finished.
### Another Companion active
Normal region boss reward.
No Pipkin Legacy advancement.
# 38. Multiplayer scaling
Use the established baseline.
2 players:
160 percent Courage.
3 players:
210 percent.
4 players:
255 percent.
The Pumpkin Grounds should create cooperative planning around who controls growth and who claims Harvest rewards.

2 players: 22

# 44. Harvest Hopper multiplayer

# 49. Multiplayer Encourage Growth
Each player may trigger Encourage Growth on their own fourth Trick.
However:
Each Crop can be voluntarily advanced only once per team round.
This prevents four players from instantly pushing one Seed through every stage.
# 50. Multiplayer Bruise
Bruise is shared.
Any player can create it.
Once a Crop is Bruised:
Additional damage does not extend the delay.
# 51. Multiplayer Harvest allocation
The player who destroys a Ripe Crop receives its benefit unless the benefit is explicitly global.
Guard Gourd:
Final hitter gains Guard.
Spark Pumpkin:
Final hitter gains 1 Nerve next turn.
Moon Squash:
Final hitter recovers Courage.
Jumping Gourd:
Final hitter gains the Jumping Trick effect.
This makes crop assignment a legitimate cooperative decision.
# 52. Multiplayer cooperative opportunities
A fast deck can accelerate a Growing Crop.
A burst deck can claim it immediately afterward.
A defensive player can Harvest Guard Gourd.
A player running low on Courage can be given Moon Squash.
A combo deck can take Spark Pumpkin or Jumping Gourd.
The party can Bruise a dangerous Crop while focusing the boss.
Players can intentionally leave an unimportant Guard Gourd available for the King while stealing a much more dangerous Spark Pumpkin.
The region should create table talk such as:
"Leave the Moon Squash. I can ripen it and you take the heal."
# 53. Haunt Level progression
### Haunt 1
Ordinary enemies gain approximately 8 percent Courage.
Big Scares and the Harvest King gain approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced Pumpkin Grounds formations appear earlier.
### Haunt 3
Pumpkin Pip begins advanced encounters Ripe.
This creates an immediate Harvest opportunity paired with immediate danger.
### Haunt 4
Moonseed begins Growing rather than Seed in advanced encounters.
### Haunt 5
Scarecrow Sprout needs 17 damage rather than 14 to delay growth.
### Haunt 6
Gourd Guard begins at Firm rather than Soft.
### Haunt 7
Vine Lantern begins with 1 Glow.
### Haunt 8
Harvest Hopper gains 4 Guard whenever another enemy becomes Ripe.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares gain signature upgrades.
Moon Scarecrow begins at Waxing Moon.
Gourd Knight gains 8 Guard on its first Shell Burst.
Great Root begins with one Growing Crop.
### Haunt 10
The Harvest King begins combat with:
One Growing Crop.
One Seed.
One empty Plot.
The Crop identities are revealed before the first player turn.
# 54. Higher Haunt behavior upgrades
At higher mastery levels:
Pumpkin Pip may briefly split into two smaller pumpkins when Overripe.
Moonseed may produce different Moon Bloom types.
Scarecrow Sprout may regrow a lost limb.
Gourd Guard may deliberately soften its shell to prepare a different attack.
Vine Lantern may transfer Glow to another enemy.
Harvest Hopper may choose among several Tasty targets according to the strongest possible Harvest.
Moon Scarecrow may include a rare Eclipse stage.
Gourd Knight may contain a fourth hidden seed core.
Great Root may move Crops between Plots.
The Harvest King may deliberately let a Crop become Overripe if its weaker effect better fits the current situation.
All of this should remain visible.
The region is about timing, not surprise.
# 55. Pumpkin Grounds themed run reward concepts
### Seed Pouch
The first time each combat you create a temporary battlefield object or generated Trick, gain 4 Guard.
### Tiny Watering Can
The first delayed effect you create each combat resolves one step sooner if doing so is beneficial and legally defined.
This needs careful global rules during item design.
### Pumpkin Candy
The first time each combat you defeat an enemy during one of its enhanced or mature states, recover 2 Courage.
### Bent Sickle
Whenever you defeat an enemy with at least 10 excess damage, gain 4 Guard.
Once per turn.
### Frog Charm
The first time each combat you play four Tricks in one turn, draw 1 Trick.
### Harvest Key
Garden gates, pumpkin sheds, root cellars, moon gardens, crop storage, and courtyard passages become easier to access during exploration.
# 56. Pumpkin Grounds Curiosity hooks
### The Pumpkin With a Name
A pumpkin has a missing pet's name carved into it.
The carving is growing naturally with the fruit.
The kids definitely did not make it.
### The Seed Catalogue
Seed packets are labeled with impossible categories.
Cat, Ghost
Dog, Skeleton
Frog, Pumpkin
Crow, Paper
The implication is not necessarily that the mansion literally planted the Companions.
It may be cataloguing transformation possibilities using garden language.
### The Frog Pond
Normal frogs live here.
Pipkin becomes extremely interested.
One frog may be carrying a tiny key.
### The Harvest Basket
It contains:
Old collars.
Shed supernatural pieces.
Seeds.
Pet toys.
Photographs.
A familiar item belonging to a missing pet.
The King considers all of these things "harvests."
### The Moon Dial
It indicates a lunar cycle that does not match the real moon outside.
Changing it may alter nearby Curiosity outcomes or open a Secret route.
### The Unpicked Pumpkin
Records indicate it has been Ripe for decades.
The Harvest King refuses to touch it.
Something inside is still moving.
# 57. Important campaign clue
The Pumpkin Grounds can provide further evidence that transformation does not occur at the same rate for every animal.
Some records may describe:
Rapid Ripening
Slow Ripening
Stalled
Reverted
Seasonal
Unstable
This implies that the five transformation stages are not a perfectly fixed timer.
An animal's:
Personality.
Age.
Time in the house.
Emotional state.
Region.
Relationship to the mansion.
May influence how quickly transformation progresses.
That makes rescuing the Kids' recent pets urgent without requiring an exact universal countdown.
# 58. Pipkin specific story detail
Pipkin's old patch should still exist.
It is a tiny garden bed surrounded by an absurdly elaborate fence.
The fence is not tall.
Pipkin could easily jump over it.
That is exactly what Pipkin used to do.
The Harvest King kept making the fence more complicated rather than accepting the obvious conclusion:
Pipkin did not want to stay in the assigned plot.
This can provide some much needed humor in a story heavy region.

# 61. Blueprint investigation progression
Early notes:
"Pumpkins grow REALLY fast."
Later:
"Ripe doesn't always mean dangerous."
Later:
"House tracks transformation like crops."
Later still:
"Pets change at different speeds."
Eventually:
"WE DON'T KNOW HOW LONG THEY HAVE."
That is the important emotional consequence.
The kids now understand that their pets may not all be on the same timeline.
One may still be almost ordinary.
Another may already be changing.
That increases urgency without creating a literal campaign timer that could make the game punitive.
# 62. What the Moon Courtyard and Pumpkin Grounds teaches
By the time the player reaches The Harvest King, the region has taught:
Growth can create opportunities as well as threats.
Defeating an enemy immediately is not always the most valuable choice.
Mature states can combine greater danger with greater vulnerability.
The player can manipulate maturation speed.
Damage can delay growth without necessarily destroying the target.
Enemy support creatures can compete with the player for valuable resources.
Precise timing around thresholds can generate rewards.
Created battlefield objects can function as resources for both sides.
There may be no universally correct moment to harvest something.
Becoming ready for something does not give another person the right to decide what happens next.
The distinction between Pipkin and the Harvest King is:
Pipkin grows things because it is fun to see what they become.
The Harvest King grows things because it believes everything eventually belongs in its basket.
