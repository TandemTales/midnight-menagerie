# The Lampworks
Companion: Wisp Boss: The Lamplighter Region identity: Stored supernatural energy, charge, ignition, delayed detonations, transferring flame, illumination states, darkness, and choosing when dangerous power should be released
The Lampworks should feel like the mansion’s electrical system never learned the difference between light, fire, memory, and spirit.
Rows of hanging lamps burn with blue flame.
Gas pipes carry whispers instead of fuel.
Candles relight themselves.
Huge chandeliers draw energy from nearby rooms.
Some lamps contain tiny ghostly shapes that may once have been animals.
The central combat lesson is:
Power that has not been used yet can be more dangerous than power being used now.
Wisp fits naturally because Wisp builds supernatural energy, stores it, delays it, and eventually converts that stored potential into powerful effects.

## 2. Regional mechanic: Charge
Several Lampworks enemies use Charge.
Charge represents supernatural power that has been stored but not yet released.
Charge itself does nothing unless the enemy has an effect that uses it.
Each enemy clearly states what its Charge does.
Charge should usually create a recognizable rhythm:
Build.
Build.
Release.
Reset.
The player can therefore decide whether to interrupt the buildup, prepare for the release, or deliberately allow it.
## 3. Waxling
Role: Introductory state change enemy Courage: 25
A little creature made from melted candle wax.
Its flame gets larger as its body melts.
### Core mechanic: Melting
Waxling begins with 3 Wax.
Whenever Waxling finishes an enemy turn:
Lose 1 Wax.
Wax cannot fall below 0.
### At 3 Wax
Gain 5 Guard at the start of its turn.
Attack damage is normal.
### At 2 Wax
No automatic Guard.
Attacks deal 2 additional damage.
### At 1 Wax
Attacks deal 4 additional damage.
Waxling takes 20 percent additional damage.
### At 0 Wax
Waxling becomes Puddle Form.
It cannot attack.
At the beginning of its next turn:
Use Relight.
Recover 8 Courage.
Return to 2 Wax.
### Moves
Wax Slap
Deal 6 damage.
Drip
Deal 4 damage.
Gain 5 Guard.
Flare
Used only at 1 Wax.
Deal 11 damage.
### Pattern
At 3 or 2 Wax:
Wax Slap.
Drip.
Repeat.
At 1 Wax:
Flare.
Then Wax reaches 0 normally.
### Design purpose
Waxling teaches that an enemy can become both more vulnerable and more dangerous over time.
## 4. Lamp Moth
Role: Charge stealing support enemy Courage: 27
A pale moth with translucent wings that circle any bright source of supernatural energy.
### Core mechanic: Feed on Light
Lamp Moth can remove Charge from another enemy.
For each Charge removed:
Lamp Moth gains 1 Glow.
Maximum 3 Glow.
Each Glow gives 2 attack damage.
### Moves
Drink the Flame
Remove 1 Charge from another enemy.
Gain 1 Glow.
If no enemy has Charge:
Gain 6 Guard instead.
Wing Spark
Deal 5 damage plus 2 per Glow.
Dust the Bulb
Another enemy gains 5 Guard.
Lamp Moth gains 4 Guard.
### Behavior
If another enemy has Charge:
Drink the Flame.
Otherwise:
Wing Spark.
Dust the Bulb.
Wing Spark.
### Design purpose
Charge is not always something the player wants removed.
Lamp Moth can convert an ally’s stored future threat into its own immediate threat.
That creates interesting target order problems.
## 5. Spark Sprite
Role: Basic Charge enemy Courage: 30
A bouncing ball of blue flame with tiny arms and enormous eyes.
### Core mechanic: Charge
Spark Sprite can hold up to 3 Charge.
### Moves
Gather Spark
Gain 1 Charge.
Gain 5 Guard.
Static Nip
Deal 6 damage.
Discharge
Deal 7 damage plus 5 per Charge.
Remove all Charge.
### Behavior
Gather Spark.
Static Nip.
Gather Spark.
Discharge.
Repeat.
### Maximum Discharge
At 2 Charge in its normal pattern:
17 damage.
At higher difficulties or with support effects it can reach 3 Charge:
22 damage.
### Interrupting Charge
If Spark Sprite loses at least 12 Courage during one player turn:
Remove 1 Charge.
Once per turn.
### Design purpose
This is the region’s cleanest Charge tutorial.
## 6. Gaslight Ghost
Role: Illumination state enemy Courage: 36
A translucent figure made from flickering gas flame.
Its face appears only when properly illuminated.
### Core mechanic: Lit and Dim
Gaslight Ghost alternates between:
Lit
and
Dim
### Lit
Fully targetable.
Takes normal damage.
Its attacks deal 2 additional damage.
### Dim
The first Attack Trick targeting it each player turn deals 50 percent damage.
Its attacks deal normal damage.
### Moves
Brighten
Become Lit.
Gain 5 Guard.
Gas Burn
Deal 9 damage.
Fade
Become Dim.
Gain 8 Guard.
Cold Flame
Deal 6 damage.
### Pattern
Brighten.
Gas Burn.
Fade.
Cold Flame.
Repeat.
### Strategic effect
The player can attack whenever they want, but Lit creates the better offensive window.
## 7. Candle Cluster
Role: Multi charge enemy Courage: 40
A bundle of seven candles melted together into a single crawling creature.
Each flame behaves almost like an independent mind.
### Core mechanic: Flames
Candle Cluster begins combat with 3 Flames.
Maximum 5.
Each Flame gives:
1 additional attack damage.
### Growing Flames
Relight Another
Gain 1 Flame.
Gain 4 Guard.
### Extinguishing Flames
Whenever Candle Cluster takes at least 10 total damage during one player turn:
Lose 1 Flame.
Once per turn.
Minimum 1 Flame.
### Moves
Hot Wax
Deal 5 damage plus 1 per Flame.
Many Little Fires
Deal 2 damage once per Flame.
Maximum five hits.
Relight Another
Gain 1 Flame.
Gain 4 Guard.
### Pattern
Relight Another.
Hot Wax.
Many Little Fires.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The player can continually keep the Cluster small, or allow it to build toward a dangerous multi hit turn.
## 8. Blackout Blob
Role: Battlefield support enemy Courage: 35
A crawling patch of absolute darkness that leaks from broken lanterns.
### Core mechanic: Blackout
Blackout Blob can place the battlefield into Blackout.
Blackout lasts until the beginning of Blackout Blob’s next turn.
While Blackout is active:
Enemies do not lose Charge when damaged unless an effect specifically says it removes Charge.
Enemies that gain Guard gain 2 additional Guard.
Gaslight Ghost automatically becomes Dim.
### Moves
Kill the Lights
Activate Blackout.
Dark Bump
Deal 7 damage.
Swallow the Glow
Remove 1 beneficial temporary player battlefield effect if such effects exist.
Otherwise:
Gain 10 Guard.
This move cannot remove Companion core resources unless explicitly designed to do so.
### Pattern
Kill the Lights.
Dark Bump.
Swallow the Glow.
Dark Bump.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
Blackout Blob does not directly deal huge damage.
It makes the rest of the encounter harder to control.

## 12. Encounter generation rules
Spark Sprite should appear before Blackout Blob.
Lamp Moth should not appear alone in ordinary Scuffles.
Two Blackout Blobs cannot appear together.
Two Candle Clusters cannot appear together at baseline difficulty.
Gaslight Ghost should appear alone before appearing with Blackout Blob.
Lamp Moth cannot remove more than 1 Charge per turn at baseline.
Scuffle 14 belongs only to the advanced pool.
## 13. Big Scare: The Chandelier
Courage: 144
Role: Multiple stored energy sources
A huge chandelier tears itself from the ceiling and crawls across the room on chains.
Six supernatural flames burn along its arms.
### Core mechanic: Lights
The Chandelier begins with 6 Lights.
Each Light can be:
Unlit
Lit
Overcharged
### Lighting
The Chandelier’s actions progressively change Lights.
### Lit Light
Adds 1 damage to damaging moves.
### Overcharged Light
Adds 2 damage instead.
If an Overcharged Light remains Overcharged through the Chandelier’s next turn:
It explodes.
### Light Explosion
Deal 4 damage to the player.
The Light becomes Unlit.
Multiple Overcharged Lights can explode together.
### Player interaction
Whenever the Chandelier loses at least 12 Courage during one player turn:
Extinguish one Overcharged Light first.
Otherwise extinguish one Lit Light.
Once per turn.
### Moves
Light the Room
Turn two Unlit Lights into Lit Lights.
Overcharge
Turn up to two Lit Lights into Overcharged Lights.
Chain Sweep
Deal 7 damage plus 1 per Lit Light and 2 per Overcharged Light.
Crystal Rain
Deal 3 damage three times.
Gain 5 Guard per Overcharged Light.
### Pattern
Light the Room.
Chain Sweep.
Overcharge.
Crystal Rain.
Repeat.
### Combat identity
The Chandelier creates several visible timers at once.
The player can constantly manage Lights or allow a dangerous burst and prepare for it.
## 14. Big Scare: The Blackout Beast
Courage: 137
Role: Light versus darkness state management
A huge quadrupedal shadow runs between lamps.
Its body exists only where light does not.
### Core mechanic: Illumination Meter
The battlefield has a shared meter from 0 to 4.
It begins at 2.
0 means:
Total Darkness
4 means:
Blazing Light
The Beast’s abilities change according to current Illumination.
### At 0 or 1
The Blackout Beast deals 5 additional attack damage.
It takes 20 percent less Attack damage.
### At 2
No modifier.
### At 3 or 4
The Beast takes 20 percent additional damage.
Its attacks deal 2 less damage.
But at 4 it gains access to Snuff Everything.
### Changing Illumination
The Beast itself changes the meter.
The player can also manipulate it through damage thresholds.
Whenever the player deals at least 18 damage in one turn:
Increase Illumination by 1.
Once per turn.
Maximum 4.
### Moves
Devour Light
Reduce Illumination by 1.
Gain 9 Guard.
Shadow Pounce
Deal 11 damage.
Run Between Lamps
Deal 5 damage twice.
Reduce Illumination by 1 if both hits deal Courage damage.
Snuff Everything
Used only at Illumination 4.
Set Illumination to 1.
Deal 13 damage.
Gain 12 Guard.
### Strategic puzzle
The player wants high Illumination because the Beast becomes vulnerable.
But pushing it all the way to 4 enables Snuff Everything.
Sometimes holding at 3 is ideal.
## 15. Big Scare: The Great Lantern
Courage: 150
Role: Massive stored power countdown
A floor standing lantern taller than a person walks on iron legs.
Inside it burns something bright enough to cast shadows through walls.
### Core mechanic: Reservoir
The Great Lantern has a Reservoir.
Maximum 6 Charge.
It begins at 1.
### Moves
Store Flame
Gain 2 Charge.
Gain 8 Guard.
Lantern Bash
Deal 10 damage.
Gain 1 Charge.
Vent Heat
Deal 5 damage twice.
Lose 1 Charge.
Open the Lantern
Available at 5 or more Charge.
Deal 8 damage per Charge.
Then lose all Charge.
### Maximum baseline release
At 6 Charge:
48 damage.
That is deliberately terrifying.
### But the release is highly telegraphed
The Great Lantern clearly displays:
DANGEROUS PRESSURE
at 4 Charge.
At 5 or 6:
Open the Lantern becomes visible as its next possible action.
### Player interaction: Bleed Charge
Whenever the Great Lantern takes at least 15 damage during one player turn:
Remove 1 Charge.
Once per turn.
If the player deals at least 30 damage during one player turn:
Remove 2 Charge instead.
### Overventing
If Charge is reduced to 0 by player effects:
The Great Lantern becomes Dimmed.
Its next action becomes:
Relight
Gain 10 Guard.
Gain 1 Charge.
Deal no damage.
### Strategic question
The player can:
Constantly suppress Reservoir.
Race it.
Intentionally reduce it all the way to 0 for a free turn.
Or prepare to survive one enormous release while spending several turns scaling.
## 16. The Lamplighter
Boss Courage: 365
The Lamplighter is a tall figure wrapped in an old mansion maintenance coat.
Its head is an antique lantern.
Inside the glass burns a small blue flame shaped vaguely like a face.
It carries an impossibly long lighting pole.
Hundreds of tiny lamps hang from chains behind it.
The Lamplighter believes darkness is inherently unsafe.
Everything should be illuminated.
Everything should be visible.
Everything should remain where it can be watched.
Its philosophy is:
Nothing bad can happen if the lights never go out.
That makes it another manifestation of the mansion’s controlling form of protection.
## 17. The Lamplighter’s core mechanic: Lamp Row
Five hanging Lamps appear beside The Lamplighter.
Each Lamp can hold up to 2 Charge.
They begin empty.
The Lamplighter uses moves that place Charge into individual Lamps.
### Lamp states
0 Charge:
Dark
1 Charge:
Lit
2 Charge:
Bright
A Bright Lamp is at maximum.
## 18. Player interaction with Lamps
The Lamps are targetable battlefield objects.
Each has:
Courage: 10
Destroying a Lamp:
Removes all Charge inside it.
The Lamp becomes Broken for the rest of phase one.
The Lamplighter can repair Broken Lamps later.
### Important rule
Damage dealt to Lamps does not damage The Lamplighter.
The player is deciding whether to spend offense controlling the energy network.
## 19. Charged Lamp effects
At the beginning of The Lamplighter’s turn:
Each Lit Lamp gives it 1 Light.
Each Bright Lamp gives 2 Light.
Light lasts only for that enemy turn.
### Light effects
The Lamplighter’s moves consume or scale with Light.
Maximum possible Light in phase one:
10.
## 20. Phase one moves
From 365 through 211 Courage.
### Light Two Lamps
Add 1 Charge to two different non Broken Lamps.
Prefer Lamps with 0 Charge.
Gain 5 Guard.
### Lamp Pole
Deal 10 damage plus 1 per Light.
### Sweep the Hall
Deal 4 damage twice.
If Light is 5 or more:
Deal one additional 4 damage hit.
### Tend the Flame
Add 1 Charge to the Lamp with the highest Charge that is not already Bright.
If all available Lamps are Bright:
Gain 12 Guard instead.
### Extinguish You
Deal 7 damage.
If Light is 6 or more:
Apply Dimmed to the player.
## 21. Player status: Dimmed
Dimmed lasts through the next player turn.
The first time the player gains Guard during that turn:
Gain 4 less Guard.
Then Dimmed ends.
Minimum Guard gained is 0.
Dimmed does not stack.
### Why this effect
The Lamplighter is not literally extinguishing the player’s energy resource.
It creates a small defensive inefficiency without suppressing an entire archetype.
## 22. Phase one pattern
Light Two Lamps.
Lamp Pole.
Tend the Flame.
Sweep the Hall.
Extinguish You.
Repeat.
Lamp state determines the severity of the attacks.
## 23. Core mechanic: Flashpoint
If all five non Broken Lamps are Bright at the beginning of The Lamplighter’s turn:
Instead of its normal move, it uses:
Flashpoint
Deal 22 damage.
All players in multiplayer are affected at reduced values.
Then:
Every Bright Lamp loses 1 Charge.
The Lamplighter gains 15 Guard.
### Why Flashpoint matters
Allowing the entire system to fill becomes dangerous.
But it also consumes Charge.
The player may intentionally allow Flashpoint to reset the board rather than continually spend damage on Lamps.
## 24. Repair Lamps
Whenever three or more Lamps are Broken:
The Lamplighter’s next Tend the Flame becomes:
Replace the Bulb
Restore one Broken Lamp with 1 Charge and 10 Courage.
This prevents the optimal solution from always being:
Destroy every Lamp immediately.
The player can still do that, but The Lamplighter spends tempo rebuilding the system.
That can itself be exploited.
## 25. Wisp interaction theme
Wisp players should recognize the board immediately.
Stored energy is not inherently bad.
The question is:
Who controls when it gets released?
A Wisp deck may be especially willing to let Lamp Row build while preparing its own delayed burst.
## 26. Phase transition
At 210 Courage:
The Lamplighter uses:
One Light Is Enough
Destroy all remaining Lamp Row objects.
Do not trigger Flashpoint.
Every remaining Charge is absorbed into The Lamplighter’s lantern head.
The Lamplighter gains Stored Flame equal to the number of Charge absorbed.
Maximum 10.
Then phase two begins.
### Strategic consequence
How well the player controlled Lamp Row in phase one affects phase two.
If many Lamps were Bright:
Phase two begins with significant Stored Flame.
If the player kept the network suppressed:
The transition is safer.
This makes phase one management matter beyond immediate survival.
## 27. Phase two mechanic: Stored Flame
Stored Flame is now a boss resource.
Maximum 10.
The Lamplighter gains and spends it.
### Every Stored Flame
Does nothing passively.
Specific moves consume it.
This keeps the resource easy to read.
## 28. Phase two moves
### Gather the Sparks
Gain 2 Stored Flame.
Gain 9 Guard.
### Burning Pole
Deal 13 damage.
If Stored Flame is 4 or more:
Spend 2 Stored Flame.
Deal 6 additional damage.
### Lantern Burst
Spend up to 4 Stored Flame.
Deal 6 damage plus 4 per Stored Flame spent.
### Trail of Fire
Deal 5 damage twice.
Gain 1 Stored Flame for each hit that deals Courage damage.
Maximum 2.
### Seal the Lantern
Gain 16 Guard.
The next time Stored Flame would be reduced by a player effect, prevent that reduction.
Then Seal the Lantern ends.
## 29. Player interaction: Snuff Stored Flame
Whenever The Lamplighter takes at least 22 Courage damage during one player turn:
Remove 1 Stored Flame.
Once per turn.
If The Lamplighter is currently at 0 Stored Flame:
Instead become Flickering.
### Flickering
The Lamplighter’s next damaging attack deals 5 less damage.
Then Flickering ends.
This means burst damage remains valuable even after the player has fully suppressed Stored Flame.
## 30. Phase two pattern
Gather the Sparks.
Burning Pole.
Trail of Fire.
Lantern Burst.
Seal the Lantern.
Repeat.
The exact power of Lantern Burst depends on how much Stored Flame remains.
## 31. Phase two tension
The Lamplighter creates a clear resource contest.
It wants Stored Flame.
The player can spend damage suppressing it.
But trying to suppress every single Charge may not be efficient.
Sometimes the correct choice is:
Let it reach 3.
Prepare for Lantern Burst.
Spend the turn improving your own position.
This mirrors Wisp’s central gameplay puzzle without requiring Wisp.
## 32. Final escalation
At 80 Courage or less:
The Lamplighter gains:
Last Light
Whenever it loses Stored Flame due to player damage:
The Lamplighter also loses 3 Courage.
Whenever it spends Stored Flame itself:
It gains 3 Guard per Flame spent.
This creates a final tension.
Suppressing energy directly damages the boss.
Allowing it to spend energy gives it defense.
The fight accelerates naturally toward conclusion.
## 33. Optional rare move: Blackout
Once in phase two, if The Lamplighter begins its turn at 0 Stored Flame:
It may use:
Everything Goes Dark
Deal no damage.
The battlefield enters Blackout until its next turn.
Gain 3 Stored Flame.
During Blackout:
The Lamplighter takes 20 percent additional damage.
But player Guard gains are reduced by 2.
Minimum 0.
### Design purpose
The boss is forced to create darkness itself when its obsession with keeping everything illuminated fails.
That is thematically satisfying.
## 34. Why Wisp feels especially good here
### Stored energy
Almost every major Lampworks enemy demonstrates a hostile version of Wisp’s central resource philosophy.
### Delayed effects
Wisp can often spend safe buildup turns preparing while enemies charge their own releases.
### Charge control
If Wisp has Tricks that consume, transfer, or convert stored energy, those mechanics should feel naturally at home here.
### Burst windows
Dimmed enemies, discharged Lanterns, and boss Flickering states create natural moments for stored Wisp power to explode.
### Risk and patience
A strong Wisp player should often look at a nearly charged enemy and think:
I can survive that. I would rather finish setting up.
That is exactly the kind of decision the region should encourage.
## 35. Boss narrative outcome
### Wisp not yet rescued
The Lamplighter believes Wisp is the perfect living flame.
It has kept Wisp inside increasingly elaborate lamps, trying to produce a supernatural light that never extinguishes.
Wisp keeps escaping from one lamp and being placed into another.
The Lamplighter interprets this as a containment problem.
Defeating The Lamplighter allows Wisp to be a light without being required to illuminate anything.
### Wisp already rescued and currently active
This becomes Wisp’s Legacy homecoming.
The thematic statement is:
A light can guide someone without being kept burning for them forever.
### Another Companion active
Normal region boss reward.
No Wisp Legacy advancement.

## 37. Waxling multiplayer
Wax remains global.
Waxling attacks one player.
At 1 Wax:
Flare targets the player with the lowest percentage Courage.
The target is displayed before the team acts.
## 38. Lamp Moth multiplayer
Glow remains global.
Drink the Flame can remove Charge from any allied enemy.
Wing Spark targets one player.
At 3 Glow:
It deals reduced damage to all players instead.
Recommended:
7 damage to each player.
## 39. Spark Sprite multiplayer
Charge remains shared.
Discharge targets one player.
At 3 Charge:
Discharge hits all players for reduced damage.
Recommended:
14 each.
## 40. Gaslight Ghost multiplayer
Lit and Dim are global.
The first Attack from each player against a Dim Ghost receives the 50 percent reduction.
This prevents one teammate from removing the entire mechanic for everyone.
## 41. Candle Cluster multiplayer
Flames remain global.
Many Little Fires distributes its hits among players.
At least two players are targeted when possible.
## 42. Blackout Blob multiplayer
Blackout affects the entire team.
Its enemy support bonuses remain unchanged.
Swallow the Glow targets a temporary battlefield effect rather than a player specific Companion core resource.
## 43. Chandelier multiplayer
Light states are shared.
A player can extinguish only one Light through the damage threshold during their own turn.
This means multiple players can collectively control several Lights during one round.
Explosion damage hits the player whose turn just ended when the Light reaches its trigger condition, if a specific target is needed.
Otherwise it resolves during the enemy turn against all players at reduced damage.
## 44. Blackout Beast multiplayer
Illumination is shared by the party.
Damage thresholds use total team damage per round:
2 players:
30
3 players:
40
4 players:
49
Maximum one Illumination increase from damage per round.
Snuff Everything hits all players.
## 45. Great Lantern multiplayer
Reservoir is shared.
Open the Lantern hits all players.
Recommended damage:
4 plus 3 per Charge to each player.
At 6 Charge:
22 damage each.
Bleed Charge threshold uses team damage:
2 players:
26
3 players:
35
4 players:
43
At double threshold, remove 2 Charge.
## 46. Lamplighter multiplayer Lamp Row
Lamp Row expands slightly with party size.
1 player:
5 Lamps.
2 players:
5 Lamps.
3 players:
6 Lamps.
4 players:
7 Lamps.
Each Lamp uses reduced Courage scaling because they are secondary objects.
Suggested Lamp Courage:
2 players:
14

17

20

Every active Lamp must be Bright.
Broken Lamps do not count.

## 47. Multiplayer Stored Flame
At phase transition:
Stored Flame gained from remaining Charge is capped at:
2 players:
10
3 players:
12
4 players:
14
Phase two maximum Stored Flame adjusts accordingly.
Lantern Burst spends a maximum of 4 Stored Flame regardless of player count.
This prevents enormous multiplayer spikes.
## 48. Multiplayer cooperative opportunities
One player can focus on breaking Lamps.
Another can damage The Lamplighter.
A defensive player may tell the group to allow Flashpoint and prepare the party for it.
One player can hit the Great Lantern’s Bleed Charge threshold while another saves resources for its Dimmed turn.
A player with strong multi hit attacks may prefer managing Candle Cluster.
A burst oriented Companion can suppress Stored Flame before Lantern Burst.
Lampworks should create repeated team questions about whether future damage should be prevented or simply prepared for.
## 49. Haunt Level progression
### Haunt 1
Ordinary enemies gain approximately 8 percent Courage.
Big Scares and boss gain approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced Lampworks formations enter earlier.
### Haunt 3
Spark Sprite begins advanced encounters with 1 Charge.
### Haunt 4
Waxling begins at 2 Wax instead of 3 in advanced encounters.
It becomes dangerous sooner.
### Haunt 5
Lamp Moth can hold 4 Glow.
Its attack scaling remains 2 per Glow.
### Haunt 6
Gaslight Ghost’s Dim reduction applies to the first two Attack Tricks rather than one.
### Haunt 7
Candle Cluster can reach 6 Flames.
Many Little Fires can therefore hit six times.
### Haunt 8
Blackout lasts until the end of Blackout Blob’s next turn rather than the beginning.
This gives the enemy team one full additional Blackout action.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares gain signature upgrades.
The Chandelier begins with two Lit Lights.
The Blackout Beast begins at Illumination 1.
The Great Lantern begins with 2 Reservoir Charge.
### Haunt 10
The Lamplighter begins phase one with:
Two Lamps already Lit.
One Lamp already Bright.
Their locations are visible before the player acts.
## 50. Higher Haunt behavior upgrades
Potential later upgrades include:
Waxling can transfer one Wax to another Waxling.
Lamp Moth may consume Charge from multiple enemies in one action.
Spark Sprite may choose to delay Discharge to reach higher Charge.
Gaslight Ghost may force another enemy into Lit or Dim style states.
Candle Cluster may split into two smaller candle enemies when reduced below half Courage.
Blackout Blob may extinguish one Lampworks battlefield object controlled by the player.
Chandelier Lights may have different flame colors with different effects.
Great Lantern may store one kind of defensive Charge and one kind of offensive Charge.
The Lamplighter may transfer Stored Flame into temporary secondary lamps during phase two.
These belong at high Haunt Levels because the baseline region already carries substantial resource tracking.
## 51. Lampworks themed run reward concepts
### Spare Matchbook
The first time each combat an enemy reaches maximum Charge or an equivalent stored resource, gain 6 Guard.
### Blue Candle
Every third turn, gain 1 additional Nerve.
Once per combat.
### Smoked Glass
The first time each combat you would take 15 or more damage from one hit, reduce that damage by 4.
### Brass Wick Trimmer
Whenever you reduce an enemy’s Charge, countdown, or similar stored resource through one of your effects, draw 1 Trick.
Once per turn.
### Lantern Hook
At the start of combat, gain 5 Guard.
If the battlefield begins in Darkness or a similar state, gain 8 instead.
### Lampworks Key
Maintenance doors, gas valves, chandeliers, wall lamps, service passages, and lighting control rooms become easier to manipulate during exploration.
## 52. Lampworks Curiosity hooks
### The Lamp With Something Inside
A tiny shadow moves behind blue glass.
Possible choices:
Open the lamp.
Speak to it.
Extinguish it.
Carry it.
The shadow may be a harmless spirit, an old Menagerie trace, or a clue.
### The Hall of Sixteen Lamps
Sixteen lamps are arranged in a circle.
Each flame resembles one Companion’s supernatural coloration.
Some are extinguished before that Companion has been rescued.
After rescue, its corresponding lamp begins burning.
This can become a persistent visual progression element.
### The Neighborhood Map
Gas pipes form the shape of nearby streets.
Several houses are marked with tiny lights.
The homes of the playable Kids are among them.
This provides another clue that the mansion may have been observing the neighborhood.
### The Unlit Lamp
Every lamp in the room burns except one.
Its label bears the active Kid’s missing pet’s name.
Lighting it may reveal a temporary map location or clue.
### The Flame That Remembers
Looking into a supernatural flame shows a brief memory from an animal that once passed through the mansion.
This can provide emotionally significant Companion history.
### The Main Gas Valve
Turn it off.
Increase it.
Redirect it.
Follow the pipes.
Mark the system on the blueprint.
Manipulating it could alter future Lampworks rooms during that expedition.

## 56. What the Lampworks teaches
By the time the player reaches The Lamplighter, the region has taught:
Enemy resources can represent stored future danger.
Stored danger can sometimes be reduced through offense.
An enemy becoming more vulnerable can coincide with becoming more dangerous.
Support enemies may transfer stored power rather than simply generate buffs.
Darkness and illumination can change how otherwise familiar enemies behave.
Multiple independent charge systems can overlap in one encounter.
Maximum Charge does not always mean the player failed. Sometimes letting an enemy release power is strategically correct.
Suppressing an enemy resource completely can create an offensive opportunity.
Resource management can matter more than raw Courage totals.
Potential energy only becomes a problem when someone decides how it will be used.
That final idea is the thematic connection between Wisp and The Lamplighter.
