# The Secret Passages
Companion: Hush Boss: The Whisper Warden Region identity: Stealth, disappearance, stolen resources, false architecture, ambushes, target manipulation, card zone movement, and controlling where threats emerge
The Secret Passages should feel less like a conventional wing of the mansion and more like the space between its other regions.
The walls are too thick.
Some corridors are barely wide enough for a child.
Doors open behind paintings.
Fireplaces connect rooms on opposite floors.
Peepholes look into places that should be hundreds of feet away.
Some passages appear to have been built specifically so the mansion can watch its occupants without being seen.
The central combat lesson is:
Something being out of sight does not mean it is gone.
Hush belongs here because Hush understands shadows, disappearance, stealing, movement between zones, and attacking from places nobody expects.

# 2. Regional mechanic: Hidden
The Secret Passages expands the Hidden mechanic first introduced elsewhere.
While an enemy is Hidden:
Attack Tricks cannot normally target it.
Existing statuses and delayed effects continue functioning.
Area effects can still affect it if their rules allow.
Tricks explicitly capable of reaching Hidden enemies still work.
Hidden never removes already scheduled consequences.
Hidden is primarily a targeting state, not invulnerability.
# 3. Regional mechanic: Passage
Certain enemies can enter a Passage.
Passage differs from Hidden.
While in Passage:
The enemy temporarily leaves the active battlefield.
It cannot normally be affected at all.
A visible marker shows:
Where it went.
When it will return.
What it currently intends to do when it returns.
Passage therefore functions more like temporary relocation than stealth.
# 4. Peephole
Role: Observation support enemy Courage: 24
A brass rimmed eye set directly into the wall.
Sometimes it blinks.
Sometimes a completely different eye opens after it blinks.
### Core mechanic: Seen
Peephole watches the player's turn.
If the player uses the same Trick type at least 3 times during one turn:
The player becomes Seen until the end of the next enemy turn.
Seen does nothing by itself.
Peephole and several other Secret Passage enemies can exploit it.
### Moves
Watch
Gain 5 Guard.
Observe the player's next turn.
Tell the House
If the player is Seen:
Another enemy's next damaging attack deals 4 additional damage.
Then remove Seen.
If no ally exists:
Peephole's next attack gains 4 damage instead.
Shutter Snap
Deal 7 damage.
### Pattern
Watch.
Tell the House.
Shutter Snap.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The player can deliberately vary their turn.
Or simply accept being Seen because repeating Attacks, Skills, or Powers is worth it.
# 5. Wall Whisper
Role: Hidden harassment enemy Courage: 28
A whisper travels through the plaster.
The player can occasionally see the shape of a mouth pressing outward from inside the wall.
### Core mechanic: Inside the Wall
Wall Whisper alternates between:
Exposed
and
Inside the Wall
While Inside the Wall:
It is Hidden.
### Moves while Exposed
Whisper Bite
Deal 8 damage.
Slip Behind Plaster
Gain 5 Guard.
Become Inside the Wall.
### Moves while Inside the Wall
Voice Behind You
Deal 5 damage.
Apply Distracted.
### Distracted
The first Trick the player draws through an effect during their next turn is placed into the discard pile instead.
Then draw another Trick.
Distracted triggers only once.
The player does not lose total draw count.
It alters which Trick becomes available.
### Return
Push Through
Become Exposed.
Gain 7 Guard.
### Pattern
Whisper Bite.
Slip Behind Plaster.
Voice Behind You.
Push Through.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The Whisper attacks from safety, but only modestly.
Its larger function is manipulating future card access.
# 6. False Door
Role: Conditional vulnerability enemy Courage: 36
A perfectly ordinary door appears in the middle of a wall.
The handle turns.
The door opens.
There is something behind it.
Then it closes and becomes part of the wall again.
### Core mechanic: Closed and Open
False Door begins Closed.
While Closed:
The first Attack Trick that damages it each player turn deals 50 percent damage.
### Opening the Door
When the player plays their third Trick during a turn:
False Door becomes Open until the end of that turn.
While Open:
It takes 20 percent additional damage.
The first Attack reduction is removed.
### Moves
Door Slam
Deal 9 damage.
Wrong Room
Gain 10 Guard.
Close if currently Open.
Come Through
Deal 5 damage twice.
If the player was Seen:
Deal one additional 5 damage hit.
### Pattern
Door Slam.
Wrong Room.
Come Through.
Repeat.
### Strategic effect
Fast decks can deliberately create a vulnerability window.
Slower decks can simply fight through the defensive state.
# 7. Key Snatcher
Role: Resource theft enemy Courage: 32
A little shadow creature runs around carrying a ring of stolen keys twice its own size.
### Core mechanic: Stolen Nerve
Key Snatcher can steal 1 Nerve from the player's next turn.
The stolen Nerve becomes a visible Key carried by the Snatcher.
Maximum 2 Keys.
### Moves
Snatch
Gain 1 Key.
The player has 1 less Nerve next turn.
Minimum starting Nerve remains 1.
Key Jab
Deal 6 damage plus 2 per Key.
Jingle Away
Gain 8 Guard.
### Dropping Keys
If Key Snatcher loses at least 13 Courage during one player turn:
It drops 1 Key.
The player's next turn regains the corresponding stolen Nerve.
If the player has already started the affected turn:
Gain 1 Nerve immediately instead.
Once per turn.
### On defeat
All Keys are returned.
### Design purpose
Key Snatcher creates a very visible stolen resource.
The player knows exactly where their Nerve went and can retrieve it through offense.
# 8. Shadow Draft
Role: Trick zone manipulation enemy Courage: 35
A cold black wind runs through cracks in the walls.
It behaves like something alive.
### Core mechanic: Displace
Shadow Draft moves Tricks between deck zones.
It never permanently removes them.
### Moves
Cold Draft
Reveal the top Trick of the draw pile.
Move it directly to the discard pile.
Then gain 5 Guard.
### Backdraft
Take the most recently discarded Trick.
Place it on top of the draw pile.
The Trick is shown.
Deal 5 damage.
### Cross Breeze
Deal 4 damage twice.
If the player is Seen:
Move one random Trick from the discard pile to the bottom of the draw pile.
### Pattern
Cold Draft.
Cross Breeze.
Backdraft.
Cross Breeze.
Repeat.
### Strategic effect
The Draft can occasionally help.
A weak draw can be skipped.
A valuable recently discarded Trick may return sooner.
The player should sometimes exploit the movement rather than merely endure it.
# 9. Crawlspace Thing
Role: Major ambush threat Courage: 46
Nobody sees the whole creature.
Long fingers disappear into vents.
Eyes shine beneath floorboards.
Something moves through spaces far too narrow to contain it.
### Core mechanic: Crawl Away
Crawlspace Thing enters Passage.
A visible Ambush Countdown 1 appears.
During the next player turn, the creature is unavailable as a target.
Its planned return attack is:
From Behind
Deal 18 damage.
### Follow the Noise
If the player plays at least 4 Tricks while Crawlspace Thing is in Passage:
It becomes excited by the noise.
Its return changes to:
Too Early!
Deal only 10 damage.
Return immediately after the player's turn.
Become Exposed.
### Other moves
Long Fingers
Deal 9 damage.
Crawl Away
Enter Passage.
Skitter
Deal 5 damage twice.
Gain 5 Guard.
### Pattern
Long Fingers.
Crawl Away.
Return attack.
Skitter.
Repeat.
### Strategic question
Can the player produce four Tricks?
If not, can they simply prepare for 18 damage?
Can they use the untargetable turn to set up something powerful?
Again, one answer is not mandatory.

# 13. Encounter generation rules
Crawlspace Thing cannot appear before the player's second Secret Passage Scuffle.
Peephole cannot appear alone after the introductory encounter pool.
Two Key Snatchers cannot appear together at Haunt 0.
Two Crawlspace Things cannot appear together at Haunt 0.
Wall Whisper should appear alone before appearing with Peephole.
Key Snatcher cannot reduce a player's starting Nerve below 1.
Scuffle 14 belongs only to the advanced pool.
# 14. Big Scare: The Moving Wall
Courage: 150
Role: Target access and battlefield architecture
The room itself begins sliding sideways.
Doors vanish.
Wall panels overlap.
Something huge moves within the architecture.
### Core mechanic: Wall Sections
The Moving Wall consists of:
Left Section
Center Section
Right Section
The creature's vulnerable Gap appears behind one Section.
Only the Gap can receive full damage.
Attacks directed at other Sections deal 50 percent damage to the main Courage pool.
The active Gap is clearly marked.
### Moving the Gap
At the end of each player turn:
If the player played more Attacks than Skills:
Move the Gap one Section right.
If the player played more Skills than Attacks:
Move the Gap one Section left.
If tied:
The Gap remains.
Powers do not count toward either total.
The movement preview updates as the player acts.
### Moves
Wall Crush
Deal 12 damage.
### Shift the Hall
Gain 12 Guard.
Move the Gap one Section according to the normal rule again.
### Hidden Strike
Deal 6 damage twice.
If the Gap is in Center:
Deal a third 4 damage hit.
### Seal the Passage
Close the current Gap.
A different random Gap opens.
The new location is shown immediately.
### Pattern
Wall Crush.
Shift the Hall.
Hidden Strike.
Seal the Passage.
Repeat.
### Design purpose
The player can deliberately manipulate where the vulnerability appears.
The wall is not a guessing game.
# 15. Big Scare: The Whisper Choir
Role: Multiple unseen threats Structure: Four Whispers rather than one conventional enemy
The room appears empty.
Four voices begin speaking from different parts of the walls.
Each Whisper has:
Courage: 31
Each begins Hidden.
They are identified by their sound.
### The Threatening Whisper
Every second turn:
Deal 10 damage.
### The Nervous Whisper
Every second turn:
Gain 8 Guard.
Give another Whisper 5 Guard.
### The Hungry Whisper
Every third turn:
Steal 1 Nerve from the player's next turn.
### The Lost Whisper
Every third turn:
Move the top Trick of the draw pile into the discard pile.
### Revealing Whispers
Each Whisper has a different reveal condition.
Threatening Whisper:
Revealed after the player gains 10 or more Guard during one turn.
Nervous Whisper:
Revealed after the player deals 12 or more damage during one turn.
Hungry Whisper:
Revealed when the player ends a turn with at least 1 unused Nerve.
Lost Whisper:
Revealed after the player draws at least 2 additional Tricks during one turn.
### Important fallback
If the player's deck cannot reasonably satisfy a reveal condition:
After that Whisper acts twice, it becomes Revealed automatically for one player turn.
This prevents deck hard locks.
### Once Revealed
The Whisper remains targetable until the end of the next player turn.
Then it hides again if still alive.
### Strategic identity
The player chooses which voice to expose according to what their deck can comfortably do.

Its next action is clearly shown as:

### Other moves

Deal 11 damage.
### Hallway Snap
Deal 5 damage twice.
### Sequence

Deadbolt.
Open.

### Strategic question

# 17. The Whisper Warden
Boss Courage: 415
The Whisper Warden is a tall, narrow figure wrapped in an old servant's coat made from strips of wallpaper.
Its face is hidden behind a smooth black mask with a tiny keyhole where the mouth should be.
Dozens of keys hang silently inside the coat.
Its arms can flatten enough to slide between walls.
Sometimes only its hands emerge.
Sometimes its shadow arrives before it does.
The Whisper Warden believes secrecy is safety.
Its philosophy is:
Nothing hidden can be taken away.
That makes it a distorted mirror of Hush.
Hush disappears in order to choose when to be seen.
The Warden hides things so nobody else can choose anything about them.
# 18. Core boss mechanic: Hidden Routes
Three Passage entrances appear around the battlefield.
### Wall Passage
### Floor Hatch
### Ceiling Shaft
Each route has a temporary Latch.
Latch Integrity:
14
The Warden periodically enters one route.
The intended route is always displayed.
While the Warden is in Passage:
The player cannot attack the Warden directly.
Instead, they may attack the Latches.
# 19. Rerouting the Warden
If the player destroys the Latch on the Warden's selected route:
That route becomes Sealed for the current Ambush.
The Warden reroutes clockwise.
Order:
Wall Passage.
Floor Hatch.
Ceiling Shaft.
Wall Passage.
The new Ambush intent updates immediately.
The player may continue sealing routes if they have enough damage.
If all three routes are Sealed:
The Warden becomes:
Trapped Between Walls
It returns without attacking.
Gain 10 Guard.
Become Exposed.
### Important rule
All Latches repair after the Ambush cycle.
The player is manipulating one specific return, not permanently removing boss mechanics.
# 20. Wall Passage Ambush
Through the Wallpaper
Deal 18 damage.
Then gain 6 Guard.
This is the large single hit option.
# 21. Floor Hatch Ambush
Under Your Feet
Deal 6 damage three times.
This is the multi hit option.
# 22. Ceiling Shaft Ambush
From Above
Deal 11 damage.
Steal 1 Nerve from the player's next turn.
The stolen Nerve is returned automatically one turn later.
This is the tempo disruption option.
# 23. Why routes matter
Different decks prefer different Ambushes.
A Companion strong against one large hit may prefer Wall Passage.
A deck vulnerable to repeated hits may avoid Floor Hatch.
A combo deck may strongly dislike Ceiling Shaft.
The player can therefore route the boss toward the least harmful threat for their current build.
# 24. Whisper Warden phase one
From 415 through 236 Courage.
When Exposed, the Warden uses ordinary actions.
### Quiet Knife
Deal 12 damage.
### Pocket Something
Choose one Trick from the player's hand.
That Trick becomes Stashed.
Remove it until the beginning of the player's next turn.
Then return it to hand.
If the hand would exceed its limit, place the Trick on top of the draw pile.
### Listen at the Wall
Gain 11 Guard.
If the player became Seen during the previous turn:
Gain 6 additional Guard.
### Vanish
Enter one Hidden Route.
The chosen route follows this baseline priority:
Ceiling Shaft if the player played 5 or more Tricks last turn.
Wall Passage if the player gained 15 or more Guard.
Floor Hatch otherwise.
The selected route is shown before the player acts.
### Phase one sequence
Quiet Knife.
Listen at the Wall.
Pocket Something.
Vanish.
Resolve Ambush.
Repeat.
# 25. Why Vanish uses player behavior
The Warden is trying to choose an Ambush suited to what it observed.
But because the selection rule is known:
The player can manipulate it.
A player may intentionally finish with 14 Guard instead of 15.
Or play only four Tricks.
This gives the boss a Hush appropriate game of misdirection.
# 26. Stashed Tricks
Pocket Something should never randomly remove a build defining Trick.
The player chooses the Trick.
The question becomes:
What can I afford not to have next turn?
The Warden still gets its theft fantasy without causing arbitrary deck failure.

The Whisper Warden uses:
There Are More Ways Through Than You Know

The surrounding walls open.
The three original routes remain.

### Mirror Passage
### Dumbwaiter Shaft

# 28. Phase two Latches
Each route now has:
Latch Integrity: 12
Lower than phase one.

# 29. Mirror Passage Ambush
Wrong Reflection
Deal 9 damage.
Create a temporary Echo of the last Trick the player played during their previous turn.
The Echo is placed into the player's discard pile.
### Echo
The Echo has the same type as the original Trick but simplified text:
Cost 1 Nerve.
Gain 4 Guard if copied from a Skill or Power.
Deal 5 damage if copied from an Attack.
Remove from combat after playing.
This avoids arbitrary full card copying.
# 30. Dumbwaiter Shaft Ambush
Delivery
Deal 7 damage.
Move the top 2 Tricks of the player's draw pile directly to the discard pile.
Then draw 1 additional Trick at the start of the next player turn.
This disrupts deck order without deleting resources.
# 31. Phase two route order
Clockwise order becomes:
Wall Passage
Mirror Passage
Ceiling Shaft
Dumbwaiter Shaft
Floor Hatch
Back to Wall Passage
Destroying a selected Latch moves the Warden to the next open route.
The player can therefore deliberately steer it through several possibilities.
# 32. Phase two route selection
The Warden's initial chosen route now depends on the last Trick played during the previous turn.
If last Trick was an Attack:
Wall Passage.
If Skill:
Floor Hatch.
If Power:
Ceiling Shaft.
If no Trick was played:
Mirror Passage.
If 6 or more Tricks were played:
Dumbwaiter overrides the normal rule.
Again, the selection rule is always visible in the boss information panel.

### Whisper Blade
Deal 15 damage.
### Stolen Moment

Gain 8 Guard.
### Hide the Evidence
Take the most recently discarded Trick and place it on the bottom of the draw pile.
Gain 7 Guard.
### Lock Every Door

Whisper Blade.

# 34. Key mechanic: Marked Passage
Every time the player successfully reroutes the Warden:
The abandoned Passage becomes Marked.
A Marked Passage remains marked for the rest of phase two.
Maximum five Marks.
### At 3 Marked Passages
The Warden gains:
Cornered
It takes 10 percent additional damage while Exposed.
### At 5 Marked Passages
The Warden becomes:
Mapped
Every future Ambush deals 3 less damage.
The player has learned the Warden's entire network.
### Why this matters
Controlling Ambushes is not only defensive.
It creates long term progress.
The player's mapping knowledge becomes combat power.
That is especially appropriate for Midnight Menagerie's investigation theme.
# 35. Final escalation
At 90 Courage or less:
The Warden uses:
Nowhere Left to Hide
All five Passages become permanently Marked.
The Warden can no longer become fully untargetable.
Vanish instead becomes:
Shadowstep
Gain 12 Guard.
Change the next attack according to the selected Passage.
Remain targetable.
### Final phase effect
Whenever the player destroys a Latch:
The Warden loses 4 Courage.
This transforms the stealth puzzle into the final method of dismantling the boss's control over the architecture.
# 36. Why Hush feels especially good here
### Shadows
Hush already thinks of being unseen as a resource rather than merely protection.
### Movement between zones
Shadow Draft, Stashed Tricks, Dumbwaiter effects, and Hidden Routes all reinforce the concept that something moved elsewhere is not necessarily lost.
### Stealing
Key Snatcher and The Warden present hostile versions of Hush's theft mechanics.
Hush steals opportunistically.
The Warden confiscates.
### Surprise attacks
A Hush deck should often be good at creating value around moments when enemies become Exposed.
### Disappearing
The player using Hush should understand the difference between:
Hidden.
Passage.
Stashed.
Discarded.
Returned.
Those distinctions should feel like a familiar language.
# 37. Boss narrative outcome
### Hush not yet rescued
The Whisper Warden considers Hush the perfect hidden animal.
Quiet.
Fast.
Able to slip through spaces nobody else can enter.
The Warden has used Hush to inspect rooms, retrieve keys, listen behind walls, and move unseen through the mansion.
Whenever Hush tried to leave:
The Warden simply closed another door.
Hush learned to disappear because being found usually meant being put back.
Defeating the Warden gives Hush control over when hiding is useful and when it is time to come out.
### Hush already rescued and currently active
This becomes Hush's Legacy homecoming.
The thematic statement is:
Privacy is freedom only when you can choose when to open the door.
### Another Companion active
Normal region boss reward.
No Hush Legacy advancement.
# 38. Multiplayer scaling
Use the established baseline.
2 players:
160 percent Courage.
3 players:
210 percent.
4 players:
255 percent.
The Secret Passages should be especially cooperative because different players can manage different route threats.
# 39. Peephole multiplayer
Seen is tracked separately for each player.
A player becomes Seen if they repeat one Trick type at least 3 times during their own turn.
Tell the House empowers an attack targeting a Seen player whenever possible.
# 40. Wall Whisper multiplayer
Voice Behind You targets one player.
Distracted affects only that player's deck.
Wall Whisper's Hidden state remains global.
# 41. False Door multiplayer
The Door opens independently for each player after that player plays their third Trick.
While Open for one player:
That player's Attacks receive the 20 percent damage bonus.
Another player's Door state may still be Closed.
This preserves individual sequencing.
# 42. Key Snatcher multiplayer
Stolen Nerve belongs to specific players.
Keys are visibly labeled by player.
Damaging Key Snatcher enough to drop a Key returns the oldest stolen Key.
The team can therefore help recover one another's resources.
# 43. Shadow Draft multiplayer
Cold Draft targets one player's deck.
Targets rotate whenever possible.
Backdraft affects the same player's discard pile.
This prevents the enemy from mixing cards between players.
# 44. Crawlspace Thing multiplayer
When it enters Passage:
One player becomes the Ambush target.
The target is shown.
### Follow the Noise
The team must collectively play:
2 players: 7 Tricks
3 players: 10 Tricks
4 players: 13 Tricks
to reduce the Ambush.
If successful:
Too Early targets the originally marked player for reduced damage.
# 45. Moving Wall multiplayer
The Gap is shared.
Its movement uses total team behavior.
If the team played more Attacks than Skills:
Move right.
More Skills than Attacks:
Move left.
Tie:
Remain.
The entire team can coordinate the vulnerability position.
# 46. Whisper Choir multiplayer
Reveal conditions can be satisfied by any player.
Once a Whisper is Revealed:
All players can target it until the end of the team round.
Hungry Whisper steals Nerve from one player.
Lost Whisper manipulates only one player's deck.
Threatening Whisper announces its target.
# 47. Door That Wasn't There multiplayer
The Go Through offer is made to one player at a time.
If accepted:
Only that player avoids the next Door attack.
The remaining players are still valid targets.
The offer rotates when possible.
# 48. Whisper Warden multiplayer routes
The party shares the same Hidden Route network.
Each Latch has scaled Integrity:
2 players:
20
3 players:
26
4 players:
31
The team may combine damage to Seal a route.
### Rerouting
Every successful Seal reroutes the Warden exactly as in solo.
This means the team can coordinate:
"Seal Ceiling. We can handle Mirror."
That should be one of the region's signature multiplayer interactions.
# 49. Multiplayer Ambush targeting
### Wall Passage
Targets one player for a large hit.
### Floor Hatch
Hits multiple players with smaller strikes.
### Ceiling Shaft
Targets one player and steals 1 Nerve from that player.
### Mirror Passage
Creates an Echo based on the targeted player's previous Trick.
### Dumbwaiter Shaft
Manipulates one targeted player's draw pile.
The intended target is displayed before the team begins the round.
# 50. Multiplayer Marked Passages
Marked Passages are shared.
The whole party benefits from Cornered and Mapped.
This reinforces the idea that one player's scouting improves the mansion map for everyone.
# 51. Multiplayer cooperative opportunities
One player can deliberately vary their Trick types to avoid becoming Seen.
Another can ignore Seen because their defensive plan can absorb the consequence.
A high damage player can recover Keys stolen from a teammate.
A fast deck can trigger Follow the Noise and weaken Crawlspace Thing's Ambush.
A teammate can focus Latches while another saves burst damage for the Warden's Exposed turn.
Players can deliberately reroute the Warden toward an Ambush that a particular Companion handles well.
A Hush player can become the team's natural specialist for exploiting moments when the Warden emerges.
# 52. Haunt Level progression
### Haunt 1
Ordinary enemies gain approximately 8 percent Courage.
Big Scares and boss gain approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced Secret Passage formations appear earlier.
### Haunt 3
Peephole makes the player Seen after 2 repeated Tricks of the same type rather than 3 in advanced encounters.
### Haunt 4
Wall Whisper remains Inside the Wall for one additional enemy action the first time it hides.
### Haunt 5
False Door's Closed reduction increases from 50 percent on the first Attack to the first two Attacks.
### Haunt 6
Key Snatcher can hold 3 Keys.
The player still cannot begin a turn below 1 Nerve.
### Haunt 7
Shadow Draft's Cold Draft moves the top 2 Tricks to discard.
The player sees both first.
### Haunt 8
Crawlspace Thing's normal From Behind attack increases from 18 to 22 damage.
Too Early remains 10.
This makes successfully luring it out substantially more valuable.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares gain signature upgrades.
The Moving Wall begins with the Gap in a guarded state.

Potential later upgrades include:
Peephole can remember the player's most common Trick type across two turns.
Wall Whisper can emerge from a different enemy's shadow.
False Door can briefly protect another enemy by closing in front of it.
Key Snatcher can convert stolen Keys into temporary Guard.

Crawlspace Thing can choose between two visible Ambush routes.

Every one of these should remain understandable from visible information.
The Secret Passages should never become a region where the player loses because the game simply refused to tell them what was happening.

# 55. Secret Passage Curiosity hooks
### The Peephole Into Home
The active Kid looks through a tiny opening.
On the other side is their own bedroom.
Not a copy.
Their actual bedroom.
Someone moves past the doorway at home.
This proves the mansion's impossible passages can reach beyond the property.
### The Key Ring
Hundreds of keys hang from one hook.
Possible choices include:
Take one.
Match them to known doors.
Search for a tag bearing a pet's name.
Leave a marked key for a future expedition.
### The Listening Room
Hundreds of brass tubes emerge from the walls.
Each carries sound from another mansion region.
One carries faint barking, meowing, scratching, or another sound associated with a missing pet.
### The False Wall
The blueprint shows solid masonry.
The wall opens anyway.
Behind it is a child's old hiding place containing notes from someone who explored the mansion decades earlier.
### The Stolen Things Cabinet
The Warden stores objects confiscated from animals and children.
Possible discoveries include:
Collars.
House keys.
Toys.
Backpack equipment.
A Kid's missing pet's familiar object.
Notes from previous investigators.
### The Door to Yesterday
Opening one secret door reveals a mansion room as it looked decades earlier.
The player cannot fully enter.
They can observe a short scene.
This is an excellent vehicle for Companion backstory.
# 56. Important campaign clue
The Secret Passages should reveal something particularly disturbing about the mansion's behavior.
The house does not merely wait for animals to wander inside.
Its passages can extend beyond the mansion.
A crawlspace may temporarily connect to:
A neighborhood garage.
A storm drain.
A backyard shed.
A school basement.
A veterinary clinic storage room.
Another place where a frightened or lost animal might hide.
This does not yet prove that the mansion intentionally kidnaps pets.
It establishes that it possesses the means to reach them.
The distinction matters for the mystery.

Later:

Later:
"Some passages lead outside."

That should be one of the campaign's strongest investigation revelations before reaching the Heart.
# 60. What the Secret Passages teaches
By the time the player reaches The Whisper Warden, the region has taught:
Hidden and removed from the battlefield are mechanically different states.
Enemies can attack from locations where they cannot currently be targeted.
Player resources can be stolen and recovered.
Moving a Trick between zones is not the same thing as destroying it.
Some enemy vulnerabilities depend on player sequencing.
Untargetable turns can be used for setup rather than treated purely as lost offense.
Ambushes are fair when their timing and consequences are visible.
Player behavior can influence where an enemy chooses to emerge.
Mapping an enemy's movement can become a form of combat progression.
Hiding something forever is another way of taking away its freedom.
