# The Forgotten Foyer

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 12426-13185_

## Forgotten Foyer: Full Enemy Design Pass
I am treating this as the reference region for how all later enemy regions should be documented.
All numbers below are Haunt 0 solo balance targets. They are exact for this design pass, but I would not permanently lock numerical balance until the starting decks and expected early expedition damage curves are established.
### Region combat thesis
The Forgotten Foyer teaches one central lesson:
Read the room before playing your hand.
The Foyer repeatedly asks the player to notice timing, enemy preparation, support relationships, and temporary rules.
It does not demand a particular deck strategy.
It does reward players who alter sequencing instead of playing the same turn automatically.
The region has six ordinary enemies:
Dust Bunny
Coatrack Crawler
Lost Luggage
Calling Bell
Red Carpet Runner
Door Greeter
It has three Big Scares:
The Grand Coatcheck
The Unwelcome Guest
The House Bell
Its boss is:
The Butler
# 1. Dust Bunny
Role: Introductory escalation enemy
Courage: 20
Primary lesson: Sometimes dealing a small amount of damage now prevents a larger problem later.
### Special mechanic: Dust
Dust Bunny can accumulate Dust.
Dust increases the damage of its next Tumble.
Whenever the player ends a turn without damaging Dust Bunny, it gains 1 Dust.
Maximum 4 Dust.
### Moves
Gather
Gain 5 Guard.
Gain 1 additional Dust if Dust Bunny was not damaged during the previous player turn.
Tumble
Deal 5 damage plus 3 additional damage for each Dust.
Remove all Dust afterward.
### Behavior
Turn 1: Gather.
Turn 2: Tumble.
Then repeat.
Because Dust gained passively can combine with Gather, ignoring one for several turns can produce a surprisingly large attack.
### Example
A Dust Bunny with 3 Dust shows:
Tumble: 14
The player immediately understands why it has become dangerous.
### Intended player response
The player does not necessarily need to kill it immediately.
Sometimes simply poking it for 3 damage is enough to interrupt its growth.
That distinction is important.
It teaches control rather than pure focus fire.
# 2. Coatrack Crawler
Role: Defensive bruiser
Courage: 33
Primary lesson: Enemy preparation can sometimes be disrupted.
It is a tall wooden coat rack that unfolds into a spidery walking creature.
### Special mechanic: Braced
Whenever Coatrack Crawler uses Brace, it prepares a powerful Umbrella Jab.
If all Guard granted by Brace is removed before the player's turn ends, the prepared attack is weakened.
### Moves
Brace
Gain 10 Guard.
Prepare Umbrella Jab.
Umbrella Jab
Normally deal 12 damage.
If its Brace Guard was completely broken during the previous player turn, deal only 7 damage.
Hat Swipe
Deal 6 damage.
Gain 5 Guard.
### Behavior
Turn 1: Brace.
Turn 2: Umbrella Jab.
Turn 3: Hat Swipe.
Then repeat.
### Intended player response
The player sees:
12 incoming damage next turn.
But that number is not necessarily fixed.
A moderate offensive investment now can reduce future damage.
This teaches that enemy intents can sometimes be manipulated.
# 3. Lost Luggage
Role: Deck interference enemy
Courage: 30
Primary lesson: Some enemies attack the quality of future turns rather than current Courage.
Lost Luggage is a possessed suitcase that snaps, hops, rattles, and occasionally opens to reveal far too much stuff.
### Special Trick: Clutter
Lost Luggage can add Clutter to the player's discard pile.
Clutter:
Cost 0 Nerve.
Does nothing.
Remove it from combat after playing it.
Clutter therefore consumes a future draw but does not consume Nerve.
It is intentionally irritating rather than devastating.
### Moves
Pack Wrong
Add 1 Clutter to the player's discard pile.
Gain 5 Guard.
Baggage Bash
Deal 9 damage.
Snap Shut
Gain 12 Guard.
Its next Baggage Bash deals 12 instead of 9.
### Behavior
Turn 1: Pack Wrong.
Turn 2: Baggage Bash.
Turn 3: Snap Shut.
Turn 4: Baggage Bash.
Then repeat.
### Design purpose
Lost Luggage establishes deck pollution very gently.
Later regions can introduce much nastier versions of this concept.
# 4. Calling Bell
Role: Support enemy
Courage: 18
Primary lesson: Low Courage enemies can sometimes be more important than large enemies.
It is an animated brass service bell hopping around on tiny metal feet.
### Special mechanic: Ring
Whenever Calling Bell Rings, all other enemies gain:
Roused
Their next damaging attack deals 2 additional damage.
Roused is consumed when that attack occurs.
Roused can stack.
### Moves
Ring
Give every other enemy 1 Roused.
Ping
Deal 4 damage.
Call for Service
If fewer than 3 enemies are currently present, summon 1 Dust Bunny with 75 percent of normal Courage.
Calling Bell can use Call for Service only once.
### Behavior
If Calling Bell is alone, use Call for Service.
Otherwise:
Ring.
Ping.
Ring.
Ping.
Repeat.
### Intended player response
Calling Bell creates the first obvious target priority problem in the Foyer.
The biggest enemy is not always the correct enemy to attack first.
# 5. Red Carpet Runner
Role: Major ordinary threat
Courage: 43
Primary lesson: Large attacks can be prevented through preparation.
A formal red runner carpet rolls itself into a coil, launches down the hallway, crashes into the player, then hurriedly rolls itself back into place.
### Special mechanic: Momentum
Red Carpet Runner builds Momentum.
Maximum 2 Momentum.
Each Momentum substantially increases Run the Hall.
### Moves
Unroll
Gain 12 Guard.
Gain 1 Momentum unless all of this Guard is broken during the player's next turn.
Gather Speed
Deal 5 damage.
Gain 1 Momentum.
Run the Hall
Deal 8 damage plus 7 per Momentum.
Lose all Momentum.
### Normal sequence
Unroll.
Gather Speed.
Run the Hall.
Repeat.
### Maximum attack
At 2 Momentum:
22 damage
That should look frightening in an early region.
But the player had multiple turns of warning.
### Disruption
Breaking all 12 Guard after Unroll prevents that Momentum gain.
The resulting attack becomes:
15 instead of 22.
If some other effect prevents its second Momentum, it can fall to 8.
### Design purpose
This is the Foyer's first serious telegraphed threat.
The player should feel clever for preventing the huge attack rather than simply surviving it.
# 6. Door Greeter
Role: Rule enemy
Courage: 29
Primary lesson: Combat rules can temporarily change.
The Door Greeter is an ornate animated door that insists on etiquette before allowing anyone through.
It directly foreshadows The Butler.
### Move: Mind Your Manners
Door Greeter announces:
NO RUNNING
During the next player turn, playing a fourth Trick triggers Reprimand.
Reprimand:
Door Greeter immediately deals 6 damage.
It can trigger only once that turn.
The player can continue playing Tricks afterward.
### Other moves
Threshold Slam
Deal 10 damage.
Hold the Door
All enemies gain 6 Guard.
### Behavior
Mind Your Manners.
Threshold Slam.
Hold the Door.
Repeat.
### Important design rule
These rule effects never prevent the player from taking an action.
They attach a consequence.
That distinction should remain important throughout Midnight Menagerie.
The game should generally say:
You may do this, but something will happen.
Rather than:
You are forbidden from doing this.
That preserves agency.

One of the region's hardest normal formations.

This is exactly the sort of multi layer combat puzzle the later game should increasingly emphasize.
# 10. Encounter selection rules
The procedural system should not treat all formations equally.
For the Foyer:
The first Scuffle cannot contain a Door Greeter, Calling Bell, or Red Carpet Runner.
Red Carpet Runner cannot appear before the player has completed at least two Foyer Scuffles.
Calling Bell cannot initially appear without another enemy.
The same encounter cannot appear twice consecutively.
The same primary enemy should be strongly discouraged from appearing in three consecutive Scuffles.
Scuffle 14 belongs only in the advanced encounter pool.
This allows randomness without sacrificing pacing.
# 11. Big Scare: The Grand Coatcheck
Courage: 96
Role: Adaptability test
The Coatcheck is an enormous rolling brass rack carrying supernatural coats, umbrellas, scarves, hats, and luggage.
It continually changes what it is wearing.
### Core mechanic: Garments
The Grand Coatcheck cycles through three Garments.
The active Garment is shown clearly above its intent.
## Raincoat
At the start of its turn, gain 10 Guard.
## Evening Coat
Damaging attacks deal 4 additional damage.
## Mourning Coat
The first time the player damages Coatcheck each turn, add 1 Clutter to their discard pile.
### Snagging a Garment
If the player deals at least 18 total damage during one player turn, the active Garment becomes Snagged.
Its passive stops functioning until the Coatcheck changes Garments.
This allows aggressive decks to temporarily shut down its current gimmick.
### Move sequence
Check Your Things
Gain 8 Guard.
Change to the next Garment.
Umbrella Sweep
Deal 11 damage.
Hanger Flurry
Deal 4 damage three times.
Everything at Once
Deal 15 damage.
Then change Garment.
### Behavior
Check Your Things.
Umbrella Sweep.
Hanger Flurry.
Check Your Things.
Everything at Once.
Repeat.
### Why it works
Different decks care about different Garments.
Fast multi hit decks dislike Mourning Coat.
Slow decks dislike Evening Coat.
Defensive decks can tolerate Raincoat but may lose tempo.
The player is continually deciding whether to accept the current state or invest enough damage to shut it down.
# 12. Big Scare: The Unwelcome Guest
Courage: 91
Role: Pattern breaking test
It looks almost like a person standing in the Foyer.
Almost.
Its coat hangs incorrectly.
Its shadow faces the wrong direction.
Nobody remembers seeing it enter.
### Core mechanic: Familiarity
At the end of every player turn, The Unwelcome Guest observes which Trick type the player used most:
Attack
Skill
Power
That type becomes Familiar during the next player turn.
Ties favor whichever type was played last.
The familiar type is visibly displayed.
### Familiar reaction
The first Familiar Trick played next turn has no penalty.
The second causes The Unwelcome Guest to gain 6 Guard.
The third causes it to immediately deal 7 damage.
Only one Familiarity attack can trigger per turn.
### Moves
Watching
Gain 8 Guard.
No damage.
Too Familiar
Deal 9 damage.
Deal 3 additional damage for each Familiar Trick played during the previous player turn.
Maximum 15.
Wrong Face
Deal 6 damage twice.
Come In, Then
Gain 12 Guard.
The next Familiarity attack deals 10 instead of 7.
### Behavior
Watching.
Too Familiar.
Wrong Face.
Come In, Then.
Too Familiar.
Repeat.
### Strategic effect
It does not say:
Do not play Skills.
It says:
You played a lot of Skills last turn, so perhaps sequence this turn differently.
That is exactly the Foyer's gameplay identity.
# 13. Big Scare: The House Bell
Courage: 105
Role: Escalation and add management
A gigantic brass bell hangs in the center of the room.
It is attached directly to the mansion.
Something answers when it rings.
### Core mechanic: Resonance
The House Bell begins with 0 Resonance.
Maximum 4.
Every major Ring adds 1 Resonance.
At 4 Resonance, its next action becomes:
MIDNIGHT TOLL
Deal 20 damage.
Then Resonance returns to 0.
The intent appears one full turn in advance.
### Important counterplay
Whenever an enemy summoned by The House Bell is defeated:
Reduce Resonance by 1.
Minimum 0.
The player can therefore delay the Toll by managing summoned enemies.
### Moves
Ring for Service
Gain 1 Resonance.
Summon 1 Dust Bunny.
If two other enemies already exist, instead give all other enemies 1 Roused.
Deep Vibration
Deal 7 damage.
Gain 1 Resonance.
Second Ring
Gain 1 Resonance.
Summon 1 Coatrack Crawler at 60 percent normal Courage if room is available.
Otherwise give every other enemy 8 Guard.
Midnight Toll
Deal 20 damage.
Remove all Resonance.
### Sequence
Ring for Service.
Deep Vibration.
Second Ring.
Deep Vibration.
Then either Midnight Toll if at 4 Resonance, or Ring for Service.
### Design question
Do you race the Bell?
Or kill its summoned creatures to delay the Toll?
Or deliberately accept one Toll while setting up a stronger deck engine?
Different builds can answer differently.
# 14. The Butler
Boss Courage: 250
Region boss: Forgotten Foyer
Companion relationship: Marmalade
The Butler is not simply a possessed servant.
He is one of the mansion's oldest extensions of itself.
His job is to determine who belongs inside.
Unfortunately, his definition of hospitality has become:
Once admitted, guests should remain where they have been placed.
He is impossibly polite even while attacking.
# 15. The Butler's primary mechanic: House Rules
At the beginning of each player turn, The Butler may announce a House Rule.
A large handwritten looking rule appears beside his intent.
House Rules never prohibit an action.
Breaking them causes a consequence.
This becomes the mechanical principle introduced by Door Greeter and fully realized here.
# 16. House Rule: No Running
Rule
Playing a fourth Trick during the turn breaks the rule.
Reprimand
The Butler immediately deals 6 damage.
Only once that turn.
You may continue playing Tricks.
# 17. House Rule: One at a Time
Rule
Playing two Tricks of the same type consecutively breaks the rule.
Attack followed immediately by Attack.
Skill followed immediately by Skill.
Power followed immediately by Power.
Reprimand
The Butler gains 8 Guard.
Only once that turn.
This rule heavily emphasizes sequencing without actually restricting deck composition.
# 18. House Rule: Keep the Hall Clear
Rule
Ending the player turn with 18 or more Guard breaks the rule.
Reprimand
The Butler gains 10 Guard.
This occurs only after the player ends their turn.
Defensive decks can still build enormous Guard when necessary.
They simply give the Butler additional protection in exchange.
# 19. House Rule: No Roughhousing
Rule
If the player deals 20 or more total damage during one turn, the rule is broken.
Reprimand
The Butler's next damaging attack deals 5 additional damage.
This encourages the player to decide whether a burst turn is worth the retaliation.
Often it absolutely will be.
# 20. Flustered
Breaking a House Rule has another consequence.
The Butler gains 1 Flustered whenever a rule is broken.
At 3 Flustered:
The Butler loses all Flustered.
He becomes Discomposed.
### Discomposed
Until the end of the next player turn:
The Butler takes 25 percent additional damage.
He cannot enforce a House Rule.
His next enemy action becomes:
Collect Himself
Gain 12 Guard.
Do nothing else.
This is crucial.
Breaking rules is not simply failure.
It is a strategic option.
The player can deliberately irritate The Butler until he loses composure, creating an offensive window.
That gives his fight substantially more depth.
# 21. Butler phase one
From 250 through 141 Courage.
He enforces one House Rule at a time.
He cannot select the same rule twice consecutively.
### Move 1: Formal Welcome
Used on the first turn only.
Gain 12 Guard.
Announce a House Rule.
### Move 2: Walking Stick
Deal 10 damage.
Announce the next House Rule.
### Move 3: Dust Them Off
Deal 5 damage twice.
Announce the next House Rule.
### Move 4: Service, Please
If no other enemy exists:
Summon 1 Dust Bunny with 12 Courage.
If an enemy already exists:
Give it 8 Guard and 1 Roused.
Announce the next House Rule.
### Sequence
Formal Welcome.
Walking Stick.
Dust Them Off.
Service, Please.
Walking Stick.
Then repeat the latter three moves.
# 22. Butler phase transition
At 140 Courage or lower:
The Butler immediately interrupts his current pattern.
He uses:
This Is Most Irregular
Gain 16 Guard.
Remove all summoned ordinary enemies from combat by ordering them away.
Do not remove negative effects from The Butler.
The player receives a clear visual phase transition.
His jacket becomes disheveled.
His hair loosens.
His perfect posture begins collapsing.
# 23. Butler phase two
From 140 Courage to defeat.
House Rules become more dangerous, but The Butler also becomes easier to Fluster.
### Changes
Flustered now triggers Discomposed at 2 stacks instead of 3.
Reprimand damage from No Running becomes 8.
One at a Time grants 10 Guard instead of 8.
Keep the Hall Clear grants 12 Guard.
No Roughhousing adds 7 damage instead of 5.
### New moves
Remove the Intruder
Deal 7 damage three times.
Enough of This
Deal 15 damage.
Gain 6 Guard.
Restore Order
Gain 14 Guard.
Remove 1 negative effect from himself if any are present.
This cannot remove Companion signature resources attached to him if doing so would invalidate that Companion's central mechanic.
For example, Haunt should not simply disappear because he used Restore Order.
The exact list of removable statuses should be defined globally later.
### Phase two sequence
Remove the Intruder.
Restore Order.
Enough of This.
Remove the Intruder.
Repeat.
House Rules continue throughout.
# 24. Why Marmalade interacts well with The Butler
Marmalade should feel particularly satisfying here without becoming mandatory.
### Ghoststep
Strong against Enough of This.
Less effective against Remove the Intruder's repeated hits.
That naturally demonstrates Ghoststep's intended strength and weakness.
### Zoomies
No Running creates a decision around Marmalade's third and later Trick effects.
Marmalade may deliberately break the rule to activate powerful Zoomies chains.
### Untouched
Following a rule and avoiding damage can set up Untouched.
Breaking rules risks losing that condition.
### Haunt
The Butler's repeated attacking makes Haunt useful.
### Nine Lives
Powerful Life spending can help exploit Discomposed windows.
The boss therefore interacts with almost every major Marmalade mechanic without ever saying:
Marmalade deals bonus damage here.
That is the right approach for Companion home regions.
# 25. Butler victory states
The exact narrative conclusion changes according to campaign state.
### Marmalade has not been rescued
Defeating The Butler reveals where Marmalade has been confined.
The player frees Marmalade.
Marmalade becomes permanently selectable beginning with the next expedition.
### Marmalade already rescued and current Companion is Marmalade
Defeating The Butler advances Marmalade's Legacy Track if the next Legacy Rank remains available.
This is Marmalade's homecoming victory.
### Marmalade already rescued and another Companion is being used
The player receives the normal Foyer boss reward.
Marmalade's Legacy does not advance.
### Marmalade fully mastered
The Foyer remains valuable through its normal boss reward, rare Companion interactions, investigation content, and special region events.
# 26. Multiplayer behavior
I would avoid simply multiplying every enemy's Courage.
The cooperative version should change tactical relationships.
### Courage scaling baseline
For this design pass:
2 players: 160 percent normal Courage.
3 players: 210 percent normal Courage.
4 players: 255 percent normal Courage.
Damage values normally remain unchanged.
Enemy effects gain multiplayer targeting logic instead.
### Dust Bunny
Tracks whether any player damaged it that round.
If nobody did, it gains Dust.
Tumble targets one player.
Prefer the player with the lowest current Guard.
### Coatrack Crawler
Umbrella Jab targets one player.
Its Brace can be disrupted by combined team damage.
This creates cooperative focus fire.
### Lost Luggage
Pack Wrong places Clutter into one player's discard pile.
Prefer the player with the fewest Tricks currently in their draw pile.
This increases the chance that Clutter matters soon.
### Calling Bell
Roused still affects all enemies.
Ping targets one player.
Call for Service remains shared across the whole encounter.
### Red Carpet Runner
Run the Hall targets every player.
Multiplayer damage becomes:
6 plus 5 per Momentum to each player.
At 2 Momentum:
16 to every player.
This makes disrupting Momentum a genuinely cooperative responsibility.
### Door Greeter
Its rule applies separately to each player.
A player who plays a fourth Trick triggers their own Reprimand.
One player's actions do not punish another player.
This prevents multiplayer resentment.
# 27. Big Scare multiplayer adjustments
### Grand Coatcheck
Snag threshold becomes:
18 damage times number of players.
The damage may be contributed by everyone during the round.
### Unwelcome Guest
Tracks Familiarity separately for every player.
Its Too Familiar attack targets whichever player became most Familiar during that round.
### House Bell
Summoned enemies are shared.
Any player defeating a summoned enemy reduces Resonance.
Midnight Toll hits all players.
This should make House Bell one of the Foyer's strongest cooperative encounters.
# 28. The Butler in multiplayer
House Rules apply to each player individually.
If the rule is No Running:
Player A can play six Tricks and receive their Reprimand.
Player B can still play three Tricks without consequence.
Each player can generate at most 1 Flustered per round.
### Flustered thresholds
With multiple players:
Phase one requires 2 plus number of players Flustered.
Phase two requires 1 plus number of players Flustered.
For example, with two players:
Phase one threshold is 4.
Phase two threshold is 3.
This prevents a four player party from instantly forcing Discomposed every round.
### Cooperative opportunity
Players can deliberately coordinate violations.
One player might accept a Reprimand to generate the final Flustered stack.
Another player then spends their turn exploiting Discomposed.
That is exactly the sort of cooperation I would want Midnight Menagerie multiplayer to create.
# 29. Haunt Level progression
I would establish a consistent enemy upgrade framework rather than merely inflating numbers.
The exact maximum Haunt Level can remain undecided.
For now, Foyer enemies gain major behavioral upgrades at standardized milestones.
### Haunt 1
Ordinary enemies gain approximately 8 percent Courage.
Big Scares and bosses gain approximately 6 percent.
### Haunt 2
Advanced Scuffle formations can appear one encounter earlier.
### Haunt 3
Dust Bunny begins with 1 Dust in advanced encounters.
### Haunt 4
Calling Bell's first Ring grants 2 Roused instead of 1.
Later Rings remain normal.
### Haunt 5
Coatrack Crawler's undisturbed Umbrella Jab increases from 12 to 14.
The disrupted version remains 7.
This makes successful disruption more valuable rather than simply increasing everything.
### Haunt 6
Lost Luggage's first Pack Wrong adds 2 Clutter.
Later uses add 1.
### Haunt 7
Red Carpet Runner's Run the Hall gains 8 damage per Momentum instead of 7.
### Haunt 8
Door Greeter's Reprimand increases from 6 to 8.
### Haunt 9
Big Scares gain upgraded signature mechanics.
Grand Coatcheck requires 20 damage to Snag.
Unwelcome Guest's Familiarity attack deals 9.
House Bell's Midnight Toll deals 23.
### Haunt 10
The Butler begins combat with a House Rule already active during the player's first turn.
This means Formal Welcome no longer gives the player a completely unrestricted opening.
# 30. Higher Haunt philosophy
Past this point, I would increasingly change behavior, not simply statistics.
Potential later Foyer modifiers include:
Door Greeter occasionally announces One at a Time instead of only No Running.
Calling Bell can give different Roused bonuses depending on enemy type.
Red Carpet Runner may begin advanced encounters with 1 Momentum.
Lost Luggage may occasionally hide useful Tricks temporarily inside itself alongside Clutter.
The Butler may gain a fifth rare House Rule.
Big Scares may gain alternate opening patterns.
The highest difficulties should feel like enemies have become smarter and stranger, not merely thicker.
# 31. Reward structure
I would keep the region compatible with whatever final terminology we choose for relic equivalents, currencies, and run rewards.
### Standard Scuffle
Normal combat reward package.
Primarily:
Current Companion Trick choices.
Run currency once its official term exists.
Normal consumable opportunities.
### Big Scare
In addition to increased normal combat rewards:
Guaranteed access to the game's elite class run passive reward.
Higher Rare Trick probability.
Small chance of Foyer specific Curiosity or investigation reward afterward.
### Boss
Normal region boss reward.
Plus the appropriate permanent campaign result:
Companion rescue.
Legacy advancement.
Or mastery content.
# 32. Foyer specific run passive concepts
I would eventually give every region a small themed reward pool that can appear after its Big Scares.
For the Foyer, examples include:
### Brass Service Bell
The first time each combat you end a turn with 0 Nerve, draw 1 additional Trick next turn.
### House Slippers
The first attack against you each combat deals 3 less damage.
### Spare Key
The first Secret encountered each region is easier to discover.
### Coatcheck Ticket
At the start of combat, temporarily set aside 1 Trick from your opening hand, then draw a replacement. Return the set aside Trick to your draw pile afterward.
### Welcome Mat
Gain 4 Guard at the beginning of combat.
These are deliberately simple because the Foyer is an early region.
Later regions can have stranger run passives.
# 33. What the player should learn from the Foyer
By the time they defeat The Butler, the player has been taught, primarily through combat rather than pop up tutorials:
Enemy intents matter.
Some enemy attacks can be weakened before they happen.
Small support enemies can be priority targets.
Some enemies become dangerous when ignored.
Deck pollution exists.
Enemy states can change.
Combat rules can temporarily alter optimal sequencing.
Breaking an enemy's rule can sometimes be strategically correct.
Large attacks are usually telegraphed.
Enemy combinations matter more than enemy statistics alone.
That gives the Forgotten Foyer a real mechanical purpose beyond being the Marmalade themed zone.
The Forgotten NurseryCompanion: Mopsy Boss: The Governess Region identity: Repair, attachment, protection, transfer, transformation through damage
The Nursery should initially look safer than the Foyer.
Soft rugs. Rocking horses. Toy chests. Stuffed animals. Tiny beds. Music boxes.
Then the player realizes that everything in the room has been repaired so many times that almost nothing is still what it originally was.
The combat theme is:
Damage changes things.
Enemies mend each other, cover one another, transfer damage, attach improvements, lose pieces, and become different threats as they deteriorate.
Mopsy naturally understands many of these ideas, but every Companion can solve them.
