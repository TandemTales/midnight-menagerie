# Brambleboo, the Haunted Houseplant

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 10674-11016_

# BRAMBLEBOO, THE HAUNTED HOUSEPLANT
Brambleboo is a battlefield cultivation character. He does not kill enemies by applying a conventional damage over time effect. He slowly turns the combat space into a haunted indoor garden, then decides whether to preserve that garden, let it become dangerously Overgrown, or rip it apart for immediate value.
The core tension is simple:
A living garden is valuable. A harvested garden is valuable. You cannot have both at the same time.
That tension should remain relevant from the first hallway fight through the final boss.
## 1. GAMEPLAY IDENTITY
### Overall identity
Brambleboo creates persistent Plants in a four Plot Garden that exists beside his deck.
Plants require time to Mature. Mature Plants provide recurring benefits. They can then be Harvested for stronger immediate effects, sacrificing the ongoing engine.
At the same time, Brambleboo spreads supernatural Vines across enemies. Vines are not poison. They are positional control markers used to interfere with enemy Attacks, fuel certain Tricks, and create large payoff turns.
A Brambleboo run therefore revolves around three decisions:
What should occupy the four Garden Plots?
How long should each Plant remain alive?
Is the current board worth preserving, or should it be torn apart and rebuilt?
### Major strengths
Excellent long combat scaling.
Strong control against enemies that Attack repeatedly.
Highly modular defense.
Excellent multi enemy control once established.
Powerful card selection through Moonflowers.
Can convert preparation into enormous burst turns.
Several viable ways to manipulate his Garden rather than one mandatory build.
### Major weaknesses
Slow initial setup.
Only four Garden Plots exist normally.
Harvesting a Plant destroys part of the engine that created the payoff.
Overgrown strategies gradually contaminate the deck with Weeds.
Vine control is considerably weaker against enemies using non Attack intents.
Briar strategies rely upon enemies actually attacking.
Moonflower engines can find many Tricks without supplying the Nerve required to play them.
Brambleboo can become extremely powerful after several turns, but awkward decks may spend those turns cultivating rather than surviving.
# SIGNATURE SYSTEMS
## 2. The Garden
Brambleboo has a separate Garden containing 4 Plots.
Plants are combat objects. They are not Tricks and do not enter the draw pile, hand, discard pile, or exhaust equivalent.
A Plant effect places the specified Cultivar into an empty Plot.
New Plants enter with 0 Growth.
At the end of Brambleboo's turn, every immature Plant gains 1 Growth.
At 2 Growth, the Plant becomes Mature.
Mature Plants stop gaining Growth unless another effect specifically cares about excess Growth.
A Trick whose only effect is Planting cannot be played if there is no empty Plot.
## 3. The Four Cultivars
### Creeping Ivy
Mature effect: At the start of your turn, Entwine 1 on an enemy of your choice.
Harvest: Entwine 3 divided among enemies as you choose.
Creeping Ivy is the primary enemy control Plant.
### Briar
Mature effect: The first time each enemy attacks Brambleboo during an enemy turn, after that Attack resolves, each Mature Briar deals light damage to that enemy and Entwines it 1.
Harvest: Deal moderate damage to every enemy with at least 1 Vine. Deal light damage to enemies without Vines.
Briar provides retaliation and helps transition from retaliation into Vine control.
### Moonflower
Mature effect: At the start of your turn after your normal draw, draw 1 Trick, then place 1 Trick from your hand on top of your draw pile.
Each Mature Moonflower performs this separately.
Harvest: Draw 2 Tricks, then place 1 Trick from your hand on top of your draw pile. Choose one Trick remaining in your hand. It costs 1 less Nerve this turn.
Moonflower provides selection and combo assembly rather than raw scaling damage.
### Grave Moss
Mature effect: At the start of your turn, gain light Guard.
Harvest: Gain substantial Guard. The Guard gained by this Harvest does not expire at the start of your next turn.
Grave Moss gives Brambleboo time to cultivate and allows prepared defensive bursts.
## 4. Harvest
Harvest means:
Choose a Mature Plant.
Resolve that Cultivar's Harvest effect.
Remove the Plant from the Garden.
Gain 1 Compost.
A Plant can normally be Harvested only through a Trick or another explicit effect.
Harvesting is therefore both a payoff and an engine sacrifice.
## 5. Uproot
Uproot removes any Plant from the Garden without resolving its Harvest effect.
The Plant may be immature or Mature.
Whenever a Plant is Uprooted, gain 1 Compost.
Uprooting is deliberately less efficient than Harvesting, but many Tricks reward the act of restructuring the Garden.
## 6. Compost
Compost is Brambleboo's secondary combat resource.
Whenever a Plant leaves the Garden through Harvesting or Uprooting, gain 1 Compost.
Once during each of Brambleboo's turns, he may use the free Fertilize action:
Spend 1 Compost. Give one immature Plant 1 Growth.
Fertilize does not cost Nerve and is limited to once per turn unless another effect explicitly grants additional use.
Compost therefore translates yesterday's sacrificed Garden into tomorrow's Garden.
## 7. Vines, Entwine, and Snare
Enemies can have up to 6 Vines.
Entwine X means apply X Vines to the chosen enemy or enemies.
Vines do nothing simply by existing.
At the beginning of an enemy's Attack action, if that enemy has at least 4 Vines, consume 4 Vines and Snare that Attack.
A Snared multi hit Attack loses its final hit.
A Snared single hit Attack deals substantially less damage instead.
Snare cannot completely negate a single hit unless another effect specifically says so.
An enemy can be Snared only once per turn.
This means Vines are delayed battlefield control, not damage over time.
They can also be consumed or manipulated by Brambleboo's Tricks, creating tension between saving Vines for Snare and spending them offensively.
## 8. Propagate
Propagate means create a new Plant of the same Cultivar as the chosen Plant in an empty Plot.
Unless otherwise specified, the new Plant enters with 0 Growth.
The original Plant is unaffected.
## 9. Overgrown
The Garden is Overgrown whenever all 4 normal Garden Plots contain Mature Plants.
Overgrown does not automatically improve Brambleboo.
Instead, certain Tricks become dramatically stronger while the Garden is Overgrown.
At the end of Brambleboo's turn, if the Garden is Overgrown, add 1 Weed to the discard pile.
If the Garden stops satisfying the condition, it immediately stops being Overgrown.
### Weed
Weed is an unplayable Status Trick.
It occupies space when drawn and is discarded normally at the end of the turn.
Weeds disappear after combat.
Several Brambleboo Tricks can intentionally compost or remove them.
This makes Overgrown a dangerous engine state rather than a simple reward for filling the Garden.
## 10. Vanish
A Trick with Vanish is removed from the combat after being played.
# WHY THE SYSTEMS DO NOT HAVE A SINGLE CORRECT ANSWER
Mature Plants are powerful, but preserving them prevents you from gaining Compost and accessing Harvest effects.
Harvesting gives immediate power, but dismantles your recurring engine.
Compost accelerates future Plants, but usually requires destroying something useful first.
Vines can protect you through Snare, but offensive Vine payoffs consume or reposition the same resource.
Overgrown unlocks Brambleboo's most extreme scaling, but gradually clogs the deck.
Propagation fills the Garden rapidly, but copying one Cultivar reduces space available for other Cultivars.
Moonflowers improve consistency, but consume Garden space that could have provided damage or defense.
Grave Moss buys enormous amounts of setup time, but excessive Moss can leave the deck incapable of ending fights efficiently.
The intended expert decision is therefore rarely, “Which keyword am I drafting?”
It is, “What does my Garden need to look like three turns from now?”

# MAJOR DECK ARCHETYPES
## 1. Creeping Ivy Control
### Core strategy
Maintain one or more Mature Ivies and continuously distribute Vines until enemy Attacks are repeatedly Snared.
Vines then become a tactical resource. Sometimes four Vines are left untouched for defense. Sometimes they are removed for Snapback Tendril, Tug the Whole Room, Compost Catapult support, or other offensive conversion.
### Important mechanics
Creeping Ivy
Entwine
Snare
Vine movement
Vine consumption
### Typical strengths
Excellent against repeated Attack patterns.
Strong against multi hit enemies.
Good multi enemy coverage.
Can prevent dangerous turns without relying exclusively on Guard.
### Typical weaknesses
Enemies using buffs, summons, debuffs, or other non Attack intents largely ignore Snare.
Vines are consumed by Snare, requiring continuous maintenance.
The Mansion Is My Trellis accelerates Snare but eliminates leftover Vines, which can weaken offensive Vine cards.
### Hybridization
Moonflower improves access to Vine manipulation on the exact turns it is needed.
Briar automatically reapplies Vines after enemies attack.
Garden churn can repeatedly Harvest Ivy for immediate Entwine 3 while Compost rebuilds the Ivy engine.
## 2. Briar Retaliation
### Core strategy
Establish several Mature Briars and make enemy aggression increasingly expensive.
Briar does more than retaliate. Every attack also places Vines after resolving, which makes the enemy increasingly vulnerable to Snare on subsequent turns.
### Important mechanics
Briar
Enemy Attack tracking
Vines
Snare
Reactive Attacks
### Typical strengths
Efficient against enemies attacking every turn.
Excellent in encounters with several aggressive enemies.
Scales offense and defense simultaneously.
### Typical weaknesses
Poor against enemies that spend turns preparing instead of attacking.
The first dangerous attack may arrive before Briars have Matured.
Massive single hits remain threatening because Briar retaliation occurs after the Attack.
### Hybridization
Grave Moss survives the initial setup period.
Ivy supplies enough Vines to make Briar generated Vines cross Snare thresholds.
Prune and Regrow decks can Harvest Briars after dangerous enemy phases and convert them into more appropriate Cultivars.
## 3. Moonflower Harvest Engine
### Core strategy
Use Moonflowers to sculpt upcoming hands, then repeatedly Harvest and regrow Plants.
This archetype cares about selecting the correct Trick for the current Garden state rather than merely drawing a huge hand.
Moonflower Harvests enable explosive turns because they provide both extra access and a temporary Nerve discount.

### Typical strengths

### Typical weaknesses

Moonflowers occupy Garden Plots while contributing little direct damage or protection.
Excessive cycling can draw Weeds faster.
### Hybridization
Moonflower plus Ivy creates highly consistent control.
Moonflower plus Briar finds reactive cards at the correct time.
Moonflower plus Grave Moss gives slower decks the consistency required to safely cultivate four Mature Plants.

### Core strategy
Treat Plants as expendable machinery rather than permanent fixtures.
Harvest them.
Uproot them.
Repot them.
Turn them into Compost.
Replant them.
This archetype often enters a turn with a nearly complete Garden and ends the turn with an entirely different Garden.

### Typical weaknesses
Repeatedly dismantles its own passive engine.
Can accidentally enter an enemy turn with several immature Plants.

Briar becomes an area damage Harvest.
Moonflower becomes repeated hand manipulation.
Grave Moss becomes repeatable defensive burst.
This is probably Brambleboo's most flexible high skill archetype.
## 5. Overgrown Conservatory
### Core strategy
Build several Mature Plants and deliberately leave them alive.
Instead of constantly Harvesting, this deck treats the completed Garden itself as the engine.
Room Full of Leaves, Over the Doorframe, No More Empty Rooms, Secret Greenhouse, and other effects reward maintaining a packed Garden.
The catch is Weed accumulation.

Exceptional long combat scaling.

### Typical weaknesses
Slow.

Prune and Regrow provides an exit strategy when the Overgrown engine becomes unsustainable.
# IMPORTANT CROSS ARCHETYPE RELATIONSHIPS
Brambleboo is intentionally designed so that the strongest deck often does not have four copies of the same Plant.
An Ivy and Briar combination is particularly natural. Ivy prepares Snare. Briar punishes attacks and restores Vines afterward.
Moonflower is an excellent supporting Plant because Brambleboo has many highly contextual Tricks. Drawing exactly Pin to the Carpet, Four Seasons in One Night, or Midnight Bloom at the appropriate time can matter more than having another damage source.
Grave Moss performs an important structural role. It buys the turns required for all the slower engines to function.
Compost connects every Cultivar because any Plant can eventually become fuel for its replacement.
Overgrown creates a direct conflict with Harvest. The player who wants No More Empty Rooms active does not necessarily want to play Prune to the Heart, even though Prune to the Heart may be extraordinarily powerful.
That conflict is intentional.

The Garden Remembers causes repeatedly used Cultivars to return partially grown.
Every new maturation activates Creeping Through the Walls.
Eventually, the player can intentionally stop Harvesting for a turn and allow four Plants to Mature simultaneously, briefly entering Overgrown for a major payoff.
Then the Garden can be torn apart again before Weed accumulation becomes dangerous.
The strongest version of this deck therefore does not permanently choose between Harvest and Overgrown.
It oscillates between them.
That is the deeper Brambleboo play pattern.
# DESIRED PLAYER EXPERIENCE
A new player thinks:
“I grow spooky little Plants. If I wait, they get better. If I need something right now, I Harvest them.”
An experienced player thinks:
“I need this Ivy Mature before the boss's next Attack, but I also need its Plot free for Moonflower. If I Harvest it now I get the three Vines I need, one Compost for Fertilize, and Perennial Problem replants it. That lets Creeping Through the Walls add another Vine when it Matures next turn. I can deliberately enter Overgrown for one turn, resolve the doubled Garden, then break it before the second Weed arrives.”
That is the mastery curve I would aim for with Brambleboo.
