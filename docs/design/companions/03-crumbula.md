# Count Crumbula, the Vampire Chinchilla

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 1831-2472_

# Count Crumbula, the Vampire Chinchilla
Count Crumbula should feel like a tiny aristocratic predator who is constantly negotiating with his own appetite.
His defining question is not simply, “How much can I heal?”
It is:
“How long can I resist feeding before I get into trouble, and when I finally give in, how much can I get away with?”
Crumbula deliberately cycles between dangerous hunger, controlled feeding, and ridiculous overindulgence. His Courage is both his life total and, through Indulge, a resource he can gamble for explosive turns.
## Overall gameplay identity
Crumbula is a risk management and state cycling character.
He creates Bite Marks on enemies, which function as stored feeding opportunities. He can consume those marks later to recover Courage, but feeding also raises his Appetite, potentially shutting off powerful Hungry effects and eventually making him Queasy.
Meanwhile, Indulge effects let Crumbula voluntarily spend Courage for stronger Tricks. This creates an unusual relationship between offense and healing. He often hurts himself intentionally, stays alive by maintaining a prepared food supply, then chooses exactly when to cash that supply in.
A good Crumbula player manages three things simultaneously:
How much Courage can safely be risked.
How many Bite Marks should be spent now versus saved.
Whether Crumbula wants to be Hungry, Sated, or transitioning between the two.
His strongest turns frequently involve moving from one extreme to the other rather than remaining permanently in one state.
## Major strengths
Excellent control over his own Courage when properly prepared.
Strong burst turns fueled by voluntary Courage expenditure.
Bite Marks can store value across several turns before being consumed.
Very flexible between offensive and defensive roles.
Strong boss scaling because a single durable target can accumulate many Bite Marks.
Several powerful ways to convert one resource into another, including Bite Marks into healing, Bite Marks into damage, Courage into tempo, and feeding into defense.
## Major weaknesses
Crumbula's healing requires setup. He cannot simply restore Courage whenever he wants.
Bite Marks placed on an enemy are normally lost when that enemy dies.
Staying Hungry increases offensive potential but deprives him of much of his reliable recovery.
Staying Sated sacrifices many aggressive bonuses.
Excessive feeding causes Queasy and can cripple the following turn.
Indulging irresponsibly can leave Crumbula within lethal range before his feeding engine is ready.
Many of his strongest Tricks depend on sequencing. Playing the right Tricks in the wrong order substantially reduces their value.
# Signature mechanics
## 1. Appetite
Crumbula has an Appetite meter from 0 to 6.
He begins each combat at 2 Appetite.
At the end of each player turn, Appetite decreases by 1, to a minimum of 0.
Appetite has three relevant ranges.
Hungry: 0 or 1 Appetite.
Neither: 2 or 3 Appetite.
Sated: 4 through 6 Appetite.
Many Tricks behave differently depending on Crumbula's current state.
This means feeding is not automatically desirable. Recovering Courage may remove Hungry bonuses, while deliberately refusing food can eventually create powerful aggressive turns.
## 2. Bite Marks
Bite Mark is a stackable enemy status.
Most of Crumbula's feeding requires Bite Marks.
Bite Marks do nothing by themselves.
They are essentially prepared meals waiting to be consumed.
If an enemy dies, its Bite Marks disappear unless another effect specifically saves or transfers them.
This makes enemy selection important in encounters containing several targets.
## 3. Feed
Feed X means:
Choose the indicated enemy and remove up to X Bite Marks from it.
For every Bite Mark removed:
Recover a small fixed amount of Courage.
Increase Appetite by 1.
Feed resolves one Bite Mark at a time.
Cards may therefore care about Crumbula becoming Sated partway through a large Feed effect.
Feeding at full Courage still raises Appetite.
## 4. Queasy
If Crumbula attempts to Feed while already at maximum Appetite, or a Feed effect continues beyond his maximum Appetite, he becomes Queasy.
Each Feed effect can produce at most 1 Queasy.
Queasy stacks to a maximum of 2.
At the start of Crumbula's next turn, he begins with 1 less Nerve for each Queasy, then all Queasy is removed.
This makes emergency healing possible even while completely full, but repeated overeating carries a serious tempo penalty.
## 5. Indulge
Some Tricks contain an optional or mandatory Indulge effect.
Indulge requires Crumbula to voluntarily lose the stated amount of Courage.
This loss:
Ignores Guard.
Counts as Courage lost.
Is not enemy Attack damage.
Cannot reduce Crumbula below 1 Courage.
Cannot be chosen if Crumbula cannot safely pay it.
Indulge is deliberately dangerous.
It allows Crumbula to turn his life total into immediate combat tempo, but only if his feeding infrastructure can eventually repay the risk.
## 6. Leftovers
Several Tricks can convert Bite Marks into Leftovers instead of immediately feeding.
A Leftover is a temporary generated Trick:
Leftover
Skill
0 Nerve.
Retain.
Feed 1 without requiring an enemy or Bite Mark.
Vanish after use.
Playing a Leftover counts as Feeding for all Crumbula effects.
Leftovers therefore allow Crumbula to move healing opportunities away from vulnerable enemies and save them for later.
The drawback is that creating a Leftover generally requires giving up a Bite Mark without healing immediately, and eating Leftovers still increases Appetite and can cause Queasy.
# Why these mechanics are not universally optimal
Hungry provides strong aggression and efficiency, but Crumbula cannot stay Hungry while freely consuming his prepared healing.
Sated provides stability and defensive benefits, but reaching it consumes Bite Marks and shuts down Hungry effects.
Bite Marks represent future value but produce no benefit until another Trick exploits them.
Indulge produces exceptional tempo at the cost of actual survivability.
Leftovers preserve feeding opportunities but delay their payoff and can clutter the hand or deck when heavily supported.
The ideal Crumbula deck therefore usually wants more than one system.

# MAJOR DECK ARCHETYPES
## 1. Famished Fangs
### Core strategy
Stay at 0 or 1 Appetite for as much of combat as practical and exploit Crumbula's Hungry bonuses.
The deck treats feeding as emergency medicine rather than routine value.
### Important mechanics
Key Tricks include Velvet Nibble, Midnight Rush, First Course, Empty Plate, Hunger Pangs, Starving Artist, Refuse Dessert, and Eternal Hunger.
### Strengths
Very efficient damage.
Several unusually cheap Attacks.
Excellent short encounter performance.
Can convert Appetite reduction into card advantage.
### Weaknesses
Healing directly conflicts with the deck's preferred state.
A poorly timed enemy burst can force Crumbula to Feed and dismantle his offensive engine.
Eternal Hunger solves the state problem but permanently forfeits all Sated synergies.
### Hybridization
Hungry decks pair naturally with Indulge because both favor aggressive combat.
That combination is extremely dangerous unless Bite Marks or Leftovers are maintained as an emergency reserve.
## 2. Velvet Banquet
### Core strategy
Generate Bite Marks steadily, Feed frequently, and remain Sated.
Instead of treating Appetite as a drawback, the deck exploits being full as a source of defense and long term efficiency.
### Important mechanics
Seconds?, Cape Closed, Dessert Bite, After Dinner Nap, Well Fed, Well Dressed, Private Cellar, and Bottomless Tummy.
### Strengths
Excellent endurance.
Reliable defensive scaling.
Can repeatedly turn offensive setup into Courage recovery.
Very comfortable in long boss encounters.
### Weaknesses
Requires Bite Mark generation before the defensive engine begins functioning.
Heavy Feeding depletes those marks.
Queasy can destroy the tempo of an otherwise powerful following turn.
Hungry rewards become largely inaccessible.
### Hybridization
Sated decks work particularly well with Bite Mark accumulation because a large stockpile lets Crumbula choose exactly when to top himself up.
They can also use Indulge as a controlled Courage sink, deliberately creating missing Courage before a planned Feed.
## 3. The Temptation Deck
### Core strategy
Treat Courage as a spendable combat resource.
Indulge aggressively to gain Nerve, cards, damage, and defensive efficiency, then restore the lost Courage through carefully prepared feeding.
### Important mechanics
Worth It, Too Cute to Refuse, A Little Dramatic, Borrowed Bravery, The Count's Cut, I Regret Nothing, The Good Napkin, On the House, and Bite the Hand That Pets Me.
### Strengths
Potentially explosive turns.
Excellent conversion between different resources.
Can generate unusually large bursts of Nerve and card access.
The Count's Cut turns health expenditure into future healing infrastructure.
### Weaknesses
Crumbula can put himself into lethal range without enemies touching him.
The deck is heavily sequencing dependent.
Healing must already be prepared before Courage is spent recklessly.
On the House delays risk but does not erase it.
### Hybridization
This archetype strongly rewards mixing with either Bite Mark or Sated strategies.
A pure Indulge deck is intentionally fragile.
A good Temptation deck usually has a recovery plan before it begins spending Courage.
## 4. Marked Menu
### Core strategy
Accumulate Bite Marks without immediately eating them.
Treat enemies as storage containers containing future damage, healing, Nerve, or Leftovers.
### Important mechanics
Dinner Bell, Pocket Snack, Personal Favorite, Table for One, Reserve Vintage, Choice of Vintage, House Guest, House Rules, Royal Taste Test, Midnight Reservation, and Open Bar.
### Strengths
Excellent long fight scaling.
Strong tactical decisions in multiple enemy encounters.
Can convert the same basic resource into several different forms of value.
Has exceptional boss performance when Bite Marks can accumulate on one durable target.
### Weaknesses
Bite Marks do absolutely nothing until another Trick converts them.
Enemies dying unexpectedly can destroy stored value.
Heavy Feed decks and heavy offensive Bite Mark consumption compete for the same resource.
### Hybridization
Marked Menu is Crumbula's most flexible supporting package.
Hungry decks can accumulate marks while refusing to Feed.
Sated decks can eventually consume the stockpile.
Indulge decks can use the marks as an emergency Courage reserve.
## 5. The Pantry
### Core strategy
Turn Bite Marks into Leftovers and move healing opportunities out of enemies entirely.
Crumbula effectively creates a supernatural snack cupboard inside his deck.
### Important mechanics
Pocket Snack, Snack Attack, Finishing Nibble, Reserve Vintage, Emergency Rations, Secret Pantry, Connoisseur, One for the Road, Private Cellar, and Endless Pantry.
### Strengths
Extremely flexible timing.
Preserves value that would otherwise disappear when an enemy dies.
Leftovers can be converted into healing, defense, Nerve, or damage.
Strong preparation between dangerous turns.
### Weaknesses
Pocketing a Bite Mark provides no immediate Courage.
Leftovers occupy hand space.
Eating them still increases Appetite.
Endless Pantry intentionally turns them into actual deck clutter in exchange for renewable resources.
### Hybridization
Pantry decks work particularly well with Indulge because stored Leftovers provide a visible recovery reserve.
They also enable Hungry decks to accumulate healing without immediately abandoning Hungry.
# THE IMPORTANT INTERACTIONS BETWEEN ARCHETYPES
Crumbula is designed so that the archetypes continuously interfere with and enable one another.
A Hungry deck wants Bite Marks because it can prepare healing without consuming it.
An Indulge deck wants Hungry cards because aggressive Courage spending naturally encourages short, explosive turns.
A Sated deck wants Indulge because voluntarily losing Courage prevents healing from being wasted while already near maximum Courage.
A Leftover deck wants Bite Mark generation but does not necessarily want Feed cards.
A Bite Mark damage deck may actively resent Feed cards because both systems consume the same marks.
This competition is intentional.
The player should repeatedly have to decide:
Is this Bite Mark food, ammunition, stored healing, or something I should save for later?
That is Crumbula's central resource puzzle.
# ADVANCED HYBRID DECK
One particularly sophisticated build would revolve around Feast and Famine.
The deck intentionally travels through both Appetite states in a single turn.
A typical sequence could begin Hungry.
Hunger Pangs discounts the first Attack.
Velvet Nibble or Midnight Reservation rapidly establishes Bite Marks.
Worth It or I Regret Nothing spends Courage for additional tempo.
Crumbula then uses Clean Plate Club, Open Bar, or several Leftovers to climb rapidly through Appetite and enter Sated.
Feast and Famine rewards the transition.
Sated effects such as Well Fed, Well Dressed provide protection.
Then Stuffed and Dangerous, Refuse Dessert, Loosen the Cravat, or Save Room drives Appetite back downward.
If Crumbula reaches Hungry again after having been Sated earlier that turn, Feast and Famine triggers its second reward.
This deck is much stronger than merely taking every Hungry card or every Sated card.
It depends on understanding the entire Appetite trajectory of a turn.
The expert player begins thinking about Appetite not as a state but as a route.
# DESIRED PLAYER EXPERIENCE
A new player thinks:
“I bite enemies so I can eat later. When I'm hungry I hit harder, and when I'm full I'm safer. I can spend some Courage for powerful effects and heal it back afterward.”
An experienced player thinks:
“I need three more Appetite this turn, but only two points of actual healing. I should pocket this mark instead of consuming it, Indulge before I Feed so none of the healing is wasted, cross into Sated to trigger Velvet Appetite, cash the remaining Appetite into Stuffed and Dangerous, then reduce myself back into Hungry before ending the turn.”
That distinction is the intended mastery curve.
Crumbula begins as a cute vampire who bites things and eats too much.
At high level play, he becomes a character about resource timing, self imposed risk, state transitions, and deciding when not to take the healing that is sitting directly in front of you.
## Verification
Regular pool contains exactly 80 Tricks.
Commons: 20, numbered 1 through 20.
Uncommons: 35, numbered 21 through 55.
Rares: 25, numbered 56 through 80.
There are 5 additional multiplayer Tricks, consisting of 3 Uncommons and 2 Rares.
All Count specific mechanics are explicitly defined.
His primary mechanics are mechanically distinct from Marmalade's Ghoststep, Haunt, Nine Lives, Zoomies, and Untouched systems.
The pool contains explicit bridges between Hungry, Sated, Bite Mark, Indulge, and Leftover strategies, while each mechanic retains costs that prevent it from becoming automatically correct in every deck.
