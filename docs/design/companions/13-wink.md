# Wink, the Eyeball Spider

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 9061-9887_

# Wink, the Eyeball Spider
Wink is the Menagerie Companion for players who want to know what is coming, decide whether knowing is actually worth the cost, and then interfere with the future just enough to make themselves look psychic.
Wink is not a conventional defensive character. Wink does not primarily prevent damage by piling up Guard, nor does Wink simply receive bonuses for attacking an enemy with an Attack intent. The central gameplay puzzle is planning against an enemy action queue.
At low mastery, Wink looks ahead and prepares for danger.
At high mastery, Wink decides which information should remain hidden, places predictions before acquiring certainty, engineers those predictions into being correct, rearranges enemy actions without actually deleting them, and converts delayed Set Tricks into a second layer of action economy.
## 1. Overall gameplay identity
Primary identity: predictive control, information management, delayed preparation, and limited manipulation of enemy action sequences.
Wink specializes in four questions:
What is this enemy doing after the thing I can already see?
Do I want to know, or would I rather gamble on it?
Can I rearrange that sequence into something more convenient?
Can I prepare now so that a future event effectively gives me an extra action later?
Wink should feel like a tiny spider building an increasingly elaborate plan in the upper corners of the haunted mansion.
Wink is strongest when the player understands enemy behavior well enough to exploit partial information.
## 2. Major strengths
Exceptional future information. Wink can reveal enemy actions several turns ahead.
Unusual control. Wink can reorder enemy actions rather than merely weakening them.
Strong delayed efficiency. Set Tricks allow Wink to spend Nerve now for an effect that happens automatically later.
Excellent exploitation of predictable enemies. Repeating patterns become resources.
Sophisticated multiplayer support. Wink can warn allies, prepare protection for them, and let other players participate in predictions.
Multiple forms of scaling. Wink can scale through accumulated Web, Open Eyes, persistent Powers, active Sets, or an expanding network of Reads.
## 3. Major weaknesses
Setup costs real tempo. Previewing, placing Reads, applying Web, and preparing Sets can consume turns that another Companion would spend dealing damage.
Web does nothing by itself. A deck full of Web generation without good Web spenders is inefficient.
Information has diminishing returns. Knowing three future actions is useless if the enemy dies this turn.
Some dangerous enemy actions are Anchored. Wink cannot simply rearrange every boss mechanic.
Predictions can fail. Conditional enemy behavior can invalidate a Read.
Set slots can become clogged. Preparing the wrong effects can temporarily remove useful Tricks from deck circulation.
Open Eyes create competing incentives. Some decks want to maintain Full Gaze. Others constantly spend Eyes.
Immediate emergency defense is merely adequate. Wink becomes much safer after preparation than when surprised.
SIGNATURE SYSTEMSIntent FamiliesEvery enemy Intent has one implementation tag called its Intent Family.
There are four families.
Attack: The action primarily deals damage.
Defense: The action primarily gains protection or creates a defensive state.
Scheme: The action primarily buffs, debuffs, heals, prepares, or alters combat state.
Special: Summoning, transformation, escape, unusual scripted actions, and other actions that do not fit the first three categories.
An Intent containing several effects still has exactly one primary Intent Family.
This lets Wink make predictions without requiring the player to name exact enemy moves.
## Preview
Normally, players see only an enemy's current Intent.
Preview X reveals X additional future Intent positions for that enemy.
Wink can normally see no further than the next three future positions.
If one future Intent is already Previewed and Wink uses Preview 1 again, the second future Intent is revealed.
Previewed Intents remain visible until they become current, leave the queue, or combat ends.
If enemy AI changes a Previewed future action because conditions change, its revealed icon changes as well.
Preview provides information. It does not inherently make an Intent immutable.
## Reads
A Read is Wink's prediction mechanic.
When placing a Read, choose one of the four Intent Families for an enemy's next future position.
Only one Read may normally occupy each position.
When that position becomes the enemy's current Intent, the Read resolves before other effects that care about the new Intent.
If the prediction was correct:
Open 1 Eye.
If the prediction was incorrect:
Close 1 Eye.
The Read then disappears.
Reads are attached to positions in the enemy sequence, not to specific actions. If Wink rearranges Intents, the Read continues predicting whatever eventually occupies that position.
This interaction is deliberate. A sophisticated Wink player can sometimes make their own prediction become correct.
### Blind Reads
If the future position was hidden when its Read was placed, that Read is a Blind Read.
Its Blind status is remembered even if Wink later Previews the position.
This creates an important advanced interaction.
A player can gamble first, acquire information afterward, then potentially use Web manipulation to improve the odds of the original Blind Read succeeding.
Blind Reads receive no inherent additional reward, but several Tricks make them much more lucrative.
## Open Eyes
Wink has eight supernatural eyes.
Wink begins each combat with 3 Open Eyes.
Maximum: 8 Open Eyes.
Opening and Closing Eyes does not use Nerve unless a Trick says otherwise.
Eyes persist between turns.
If a Trick requires Wink to Close Eyes as a cost, Wink must have enough Open Eyes to pay that cost.
At 8 Open Eyes, Wink is in Full Gaze.
Full Gaze has no automatic benefit. Specific Tricks reward maintaining it.
This creates a constant tension between accumulating Eyes and spending them.
## Web
Web is a persistent resource attached to individual enemies.
Web has no effect by itself.
Instead, Wink's Tricks can use Web to:
Rearrange future Intents.
Postpone dangerous actions.
Power attacks.
Generate Guard.
Support Sets.
Convert information into battlefield control.
Because Web is inert without a spender, simply stacking enormous amounts of Web is not automatically a winning strategy.
## Intent Reordering
Some Tricks swap or postpone enemy Intents.
Wink generally needs the affected future Intent to be Previewed before manipulating it.
Reordering changes when an action happens. It does not normally erase that action.
This distinction is fundamental to Wink.
Wink often solves:
"I cannot handle that attack now."
by changing it into:
"I will handle that attack two turns from now."
### Anchored Intents
Certain major enemy actions are Anchored.
Anchored Intents may still be Previewed and Read, but they cannot be:
Swapped.
Postponed.
Deleted.
Replaced through Wink's Intent manipulation.
This is an important boss balance lever.
## Set Tricks
Some Tricks contain Set effects.
Playing one places that Trick face up outside Wink's draw pile, attached to an enemy or to the battlefield.
Wink normally has 3 Set slots.
A Set Trick:
Counts as a Trick when initially played.
Pays its Nerve cost normally.
Remains face up until its listed trigger occurs.
Resolves automatically when triggered.
Does not cost additional Nerve when triggered.
Goes to the discard pile after resolving unless stated otherwise.
If its attached enemy dies before the Set triggers, the Trick returns to Wink's discard pile without triggering.
Sets therefore create delayed action economy, but they temporarily remove cards from deck circulation and occupy limited slots.
## Vanish
When a Trick Vanishes, remove it from combat after its effect resolves.
This terminology is used sparingly in Wink's pool.

# STRATEGY ANALYSIS
## 1. The Seer
### Core strategy
Reveal enemy action sequences and turn accurate Reads into reliable value.
Typical core cards include:
Read the Room
Double Check
Pattern Library
Three Steps Ahead
Clairvoyant Lattice
Triple Prediction
Every Angle Covered
### Strengths
Very consistent.
Excellent against bosses with recognizable patterns.
Builds Open Eyes reliably.
Provides information that makes defensive planning much easier.
Can create powerful Read chains later in combat.
### Weaknesses
A large portion of the deck may be spent learning things instead of killing enemies.
Preview becomes almost worthless against enemies about to die.
An enemy whose future actions depend heavily on changing battlefield conditions can invalidate information.
### Hybridization
The Seer naturally supplies information to the Web Editor.
It also makes Trap Architect decks dramatically more reliable because the player knows when Sets will trigger.
A Seer can intentionally transition into Blind Read gameplay by making a prediction before acquiring the usual certainty.
2. The Blind GamblerCore strategyPredict actions before revealing them, then profit whether the prediction succeeds or fails.
This is intentionally much stranger than merely playing Reads correctly.
Core cards include:
Make a Guess
Long Shot
Blindside Probability
Wrong Answer
House Odds
Wrong on Purpose
Eyes Shut
### Strengths
Very high payoff for relatively little information investment.
Generates Web from failed Reads with House Odds.
Can use failure as an intentional resource.
Can exploit extensive enemy knowledge without actually spending Tricks on Preview.
### Weaknesses
Failure can rapidly deplete Open Eyes.
Poor enemy knowledge makes results volatile.
Several payoff cards become mediocre if the player never creates Blind Reads.
### Advanced interaction
Blind status is determined when the Read is placed.
That means an expert player can:
Make a Blind Read.
Preview afterward.
Discover that the prediction is wrong.
Build Web.
Rearrange the queue.
Put the predicted family into the Read's position.
Resolve what is still technically a successful Blind Read.
That interaction is one of Wink's principal mastery rewards.
3. The Web EditorCore strategyTreat enemy actions as an editable schedule.
The objective is usually not to cancel actions permanently.
Instead:
Not that attack now.
Put the buff first.
Move the huge attack behind the harmless setup action.
I will deal with that later.
Core cards include:
Tighten the Silk
Thread Map
Tug the Thread
Stall the Bad Part
Loom Logic
Rewrite the Script
Postpone the Inevitable
Snip Here
Master of the Web
### Strengths
Some of the best tactical control in the Menagerie.
Can transform very dangerous turns into manageable ones.
Particularly strong when an enemy alternates threatening and harmless actions.
Excellent at engineering Reads.
### Weaknesses
Requires both information and Web.
The dangerous action usually still exists afterward.
Poor rearrangement can actually make future turns worse.
Anchored boss mechanics remain untouchable.
A deck that generates Web faster than it can spend Web wastes large amounts of value.
### Hybridization
This archetype particularly likes Reads because a Read remains on its queue position when Intents move.
It also works exceptionally well with Sets because the player can deliberately bring a trigger condition forward.
4. The Trap ArchitectCore strategySpend resources on one turn so future turns receive automatic effects.
The player effectively constructs a little spiderweb of conditional actions around the battlefield.
Core Tricks include:
Tripline
Watch This One
Rehearsed Pounce
Set the Table
Doorframe Tripline
Lampshade Lookout
False Floor
Extra Corner
Deadline
Back Pocket Web
Patient Hunter
### Strengths
Excellent Nerve efficiency across long encounters.
Powerful interaction with predictable enemy patterns.
Can front load preparation during a safe enemy turn.
Patient Hunter can create very strong recurring Set sequences.
### Weaknesses
Set slots are limited.
A badly selected Set can occupy a slot for several turns.
Enemies may die before a Set triggers.
Sets remove Tricks from normal deck circulation while active.
Immediate fights can end before the engine matters.
### Hybridization
Preview tells the player which Set to prepare.
Reads support Watch This One and Rehearsed Pounce.
Web manipulation can intentionally cause the exact Intent that activates a Set.
The Trap Architect therefore becomes much more impressive when it is not actually a pure Set deck.
5. Full GazeCore strategyAccumulate all eight Open Eyes and remain there long enough to exploit Full Gaze effects.
Core cards include:
Wide Eyes
House Pattern
Eight Eyes, One Target
Double Check
Reflexive Blink
Eight Cornered View
All Eyes Open
### Strengths
Provides a powerful strategic reserve.
All Eyes Open can dramatically reduce the continuing cost of information.
Eight Eyes, One Target creates considerable burst damage.
High Eye counts also give Wink plenty of emergency fuel for Blink and other spenders.
### Weaknesses
Every Eye spent breaks Full Gaze.
Failed Reads become unusually painful when attempting to maintain exactly eight.
A player who refuses to spend Eyes may die while sitting on a theoretically valuable resource.
Some fights end before reaching eight.
### Hybridization
Reliable Certain Reads are the safest way to reach Full Gaze.
Blind decks can reach it much faster but are also far more likely to lose it.
Set decks can turn spare Eyes into delayed Nerve efficiency with Blink Twice.
IMPORTANT CROSS ARCHETYPE INTERACTIONSWink's best designs should create questions where two mechanics disagree about what the player should do.
### Preview versus Blind Reads
Previewing first provides certainty.
Predicting first provides Blind Read bonuses.
The player must decide which matters more.
### Open Eyes versus spending Eyes
Eight Eyes gives access to Full Gaze effects.
Blink, Blink Twice, All Eight at Once, and several Rares reward spending them.
There should never be one universally correct Eye count.
### Web versus damage
Silk Saw and Silk Snap consume Web for damage.
Tug the Thread and Rewrite style strategies want that Web preserved for battlefield control.
A large Web stack is therefore contested by multiple systems.
### Intent manipulation versus preparation
Moving a future Attack away may save Wink now.
But that Attack might have been about to trigger Deadline or Tripline.
Sometimes the correct choice is to leave the frightening action exactly where it is because the deck is already prepared for it.
### Reads versus manipulation
A Read can become incorrect after queue manipulation.
Or queue manipulation can deliberately make an incorrect prediction correct.
That creates a genuinely strategic relationship between the two systems instead of a simple additive synergy.
# EXAMPLE OF AN ADVANCED HYBRID DECK
A sophisticated Wink deck might combine:
Long Shot
House Odds
Double Check
Thread Map
Tug the Thread
False Floor
Rehearsed Pounce
Pattern Library
Closed Loop
Rewrite the Script
Patient Hunter
Every Angle Covered
The sequence might work like this:
Use Long Shot before obtaining information, creating a high value Blind Read.
Preview afterward with Double Check.
Discover whether the prediction is correct.
If incorrect, use Thread Map to accumulate Web.
Tug the Thread or Rewrite the Script to move the predicted Intent Family into the Read's position.
False Floor triggers because Wink manipulated the sequence.
The Blind Read resolves successfully.
House Odds Opens additional Eyes.
Pattern Library draws a Trick.
Rehearsed Pounce triggers its delayed attack.
Every Angle Covered automatically places a new prediction farther into the queue.
Closed Loop becomes eligible because both a successful Read and Intent manipulation occurred.
Patient Hunter retrieves the first triggered Set for reuse.
At that point the player is no longer merely reacting to enemy intentions.
They are managing information, predictions, queue positions, Set timing, Web expenditure, and Eye economy simultaneously.
That is the intended high mastery Wink experience.
# SHORT FIGHT VERSUS LONG FIGHT BEHAVIOR
## Short fights
Wink should favor:
Future Tense
Nibble, Then Stare
Gotcha!
Forecast Fang
Threadbare Pounce
Extensive Preview and Set engines are less attractive because they may never repay their setup cost.
## Long fights
Wink can establish:
Multiple Previewed positions.
Persistent Reads.
Large Web reserves.
Several active Sets.
Full Gaze.
Pattern Library or House Odds engines.
Intent manipulation.
This makes Wink substantially better after several turns, but the scaling comes from control complexity and accumulated preparation, not simply increasing a damage stat.
# MULTI ENEMY ENCOUNTERS
Wink becomes especially interesting when several enemies have different action sequences.
The player might see:
Enemy A: Attack now, Defense next.
Enemy B: Scheme now, Attack next.
Enemy C: Special now, Attack later.
The challenge becomes deciding whose future matters enough to investigate.
Cards such as Read the Room and Clairvoyant Lattice alleviate the information burden, but taking too many broad Preview effects costs deck space.
Web can also become distributed inefficiently between enemies.
This is why Catch and Release and Loom Logic exist.
# BOSS ENCOUNTERS
Wink should be excellent at learning and exploiting boss patterns without invalidating them.
Anchored Intents are the primary protection against hard control.
A massive scripted boss attack may be impossible to move, but Wink can still:
Preview it early.
Read it.
Prepare Tripline or Deadline style effects.
Accumulate Eyes before it arrives.
Save appropriate Tricks.
Rearrange surrounding non Anchored actions.
The boss remains dangerous.
Wink simply gets more time and information with which to solve the problem.
# INTENDED PLAYER EXPERIENCE
A new player thinks:
“I can see what they're doing next. I'll put Web on the scary one and prepare for it.”
An experienced player thinks:
“I should predict before Previewing so the Read stays Blind. If I'm wrong, House Odds gives me Web anyway. Then I can reveal the second position, swap it into the Read slot, trigger False Floor, preserve the Attack because Deadline wants it, and spend the Eyes afterward instead of breaking Full Gaze before the Read resolves.”
That gap between basic comprehension and advanced manipulation is Wink's intended skill ceiling.
