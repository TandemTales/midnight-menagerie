_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 7566-8342_

# Drizzle, the Raincloud Ghost
Drizzle is a weather control Companion built around creating a battlefield climate, preparing delayed Tricks, soaking enemies, and deciding whether to preserve a useful rainstorm or deliberately push it into a violent Thunderstorm that will inevitably collapse.
The central decision is not simply, “How do I make the weather stronger?”
It is, “What weather do I want now, what weather do I want next, and what am I willing to lose when the storm breaks?”
## 1. Overall gameplay identity
Drizzle controls a global Weather state shared by the entire combat.
Most Companions primarily manipulate their hand, their resources, or enemies. Drizzle manipulates the conditions under which the whole combat is taking place.
Her ideal turn often begins several turns earlier.
She may deliberately hold the battle at Downpour because it continuously Soaks enemies. She may instead race into Thunderstorm for explosive Conduct effects. She may prepare several Forecast Tricks before changing the weather. She may intentionally collapse her own Thunderstorm because her deck is actually built around what happens immediately after the rain stops.
Drizzle therefore plays as a mixture of:
Battlefield state management
Delayed sequencing
Conditional area effects
Controlled escalation
Intentional weather cycling
She has strong turns when her sequencing is correct, but she is less naturally efficient when forced to react immediately.
# 2. Major strengths
Excellent multi enemy control
Drizzle can turn several Soaked enemies into a connected network through Conduct.
Strong delayed efficiency
Forecast Tricks can produce very efficient effects because their benefit is postponed and requires specific Weather.
Flexible combat pacing
Drizzle can intensify Weather, stabilize it, or intentionally ease it.
Excellent medium and long fight scaling
A developed weather engine repeatedly turns Weather changes into cards, Guard, attacks, Forecast triggers, and Stormbreak bonuses.
Strong hybridization
Her most powerful decks generally use several systems together rather than maximizing a single keyword.
# 3. Major weaknesses
Setup cost
Clear Weather is intentionally Drizzle's weakest baseline state. Many of her Tricks become substantially better after the battlefield has been prepared.
Timing sensitivity
A Forecast can sit uselessly for several turns if the deck cannot reach its trigger.
Thunderstorm instability
Thunderstorm is powerful but automatically collapses. Entering it carelessly can destroy a carefully maintained Downpour.
Single enemy inconsistency
Conduct naturally prefers multiple enemies. Thunderstorm partially compensates for this, but dedicated Conduct decks still need answers for bosses.
State conflict
Some Tricks want Downpour maintained. Others want repeated Stormbreaks. Some want Clear. Others want rapid escalation. Taking every Weather manipulation card can make the deck less consistent rather than more consistent.
Forecast capacity
Only three Forecast slots exist normally. Powerful Forecast decks must decide what deserves to occupy them.
# 4. Signature mechanics
## Weather
Weather is a global combat state.
Every combat begins at Clear.
Weather normally progresses in this order:
Clear → Sprinkle → Downpour → Thunderstorm
An effect that says Advance Weather moves one step toward Thunderstorm.
An effect that says Ease Weather moves one step toward Clear.
Weather cannot move beyond Thunderstorm or below Clear unless a Trick explicitly replaces the state.
### Clear
If an enemy turn began in Clear, all Soaked enemies dry at the end of that enemy turn.
This means an enemy does not immediately dry merely because a Stormbreak changed the Weather to Clear at the end of its turn.
### Sprinkle
Soaked enemies do not naturally dry.
Sprinkle is the easiest state to maintain and is often used while setting up Forecasts.
### Downpour
At the start of Drizzle's turn, all enemies become Soaked.
Downpour is therefore Drizzle's most stable setup state.
It is deliberately valuable enough that advancing into Thunderstorm is not automatically correct.
### Thunderstorm
When Thunderstorm is entered, all enemies immediately become Soaked.
The first Conduct effect Drizzle activates during each of her turns receives an additional Thunderstorm bonus. After the Conduct effect reaches every other eligible Soaked enemy, its marked Conduct effect also repeats once on the original target.
This allows Conduct to retain some value against single enemies.
At the end of an enemy turn spent in Thunderstorm, a Stormbreak normally occurs.
## Stormbreak
A Stormbreak immediately changes Weather from Thunderstorm to Clear.
Stormbreak:
Counts as Weather changing.
Counts as entering Clear unless an effect changes the Stormbreak destination.
Triggers Tricks and Powers that specifically reference Stormbreak.
Does not immediately dry Soaked enemies because the preceding enemy turn did not begin in Clear.
Some Tricks can force a Stormbreak during Drizzle's turn.
Whenever an effect directly sends Weather from Thunderstorm to Clear, that is also considered a Stormbreak unless the effect explicitly says otherwise.
## Soaked
Soaked is a binary enemy condition.
An enemy is either Soaked or not Soaked.
Soaked does not stack.
Soaked does not inherently deal damage or weaken an enemy.
Its purpose is to make the enemy interact with Drizzle's weather based effects.
An enemy normally remains Soaked until an effect removes Soaked or until it dries at the end of an enemy turn that began in Clear.
This makes Soaked a battlefield setup condition rather than another poison equivalent.
## Conduct
Some Tricks contain a marked effect beginning with Conduct.
Conduct activates only when the primary target is Soaked.
The marked Conduct effect is repeated against every other Soaked enemy.
Example:
Conduct: Deal light damage.
If three enemies are Soaked, the primary enemy receives the normal attack and the other two receive the Conduct damage.
During Thunderstorm, the first Conduct effect each turn also repeats its Conduct portion once on the primary target.
Conduct can carry effects other than damage.
## Forecast
Some Tricks may be placed into Drizzle's Forecast row.
Drizzle normally has three Forecast slots.
When a Forecast Trick is played:
Pay its Nerve cost normally.
Resolve any immediate text.
Place the Trick face up into an empty Forecast slot.
It remains outside the draw pile, hand, and discard pile.
When its listed trigger occurs, resolve its Forecast effect without paying Nerve.
Then place it into the discard pile.
A Forecast resolution does not count as playing a Trick.
If Drizzle is already in the listed Weather when a Forecast is set, it waits for the next time that Weather is entered.
Multiple Forecasts triggered by the same event resolve in the order they were set.
If all Forecast slots are occupied, another Forecast cannot normally be set.
Forecasts may be triggered by a specific Weather state or by Stormbreak.
# 5. Primary deck archetypes
## Stormchaser
Rapidly escalate toward Thunderstorm, exploit its enhanced Conduct, then profit from Stormbreak.
## Rainkeeper
Reach Downpour and deliberately remain there, using guaranteed Soaked enemies and stable defensive value.
## Conduct Network
Build efficient effects that propagate through groups of Soaked enemies.
## Forecast Engine
Prepare future effects and manipulate Weather so several Forecasts resolve at exactly the right moment.
## Silver Lining
Deliberately create repeated Stormbreaks and exploit the strange moment immediately after the storm collapses.
These archetypes intentionally conflict in places.
A Rainkeeper deck does not always want Storm Chaser.
A Stormbreak deck may actively dislike Never Quite Clears.
A Forecast deck can run out of slots.
A Conduct deck can struggle in fights with only one enemy.
Those tensions are essential to Drizzle's drafting game.

Reach Downpour and stay there.

The Rainkeeper then extracts consistent value from Soaked enemies without taking the final step into unstable Thunderstorm.

### Core strategy
Treat Stormbreak as a payoff instead of a punishment.
Where many Drizzle decks reluctantly accept Thunderstorm collapsing, Silver Lining decks intentionally cause that collapse repeatedly.
The important resource is therefore the transition from maximum Weather back to minimum Weather.

# CROSS ARCHETYPE TENSIONS
Drizzle is deliberately designed so that several individually powerful cards are not universally correct picks.
Low Pressure System is excellent when racing toward Thunderstorm but potentially harmful to a deck trying to remain at Sprinkle.
Hold the Downpour is excellent for Rainkeeper but can strand Forecasts.
Damp Forever makes Clear much safer but is nearly unnecessary in a deck that rarely enters Clear.
Never Quite Clears protects Drizzle from her weakest Weather state but disables ordinary Stormbreak activation of Clear Forecasts.
Storm in a Teacup is excellent for Thunderstorm exploitation but delays Stormbreak payoffs.
Electric House is devastating in wide fights but can be an expensive near blank against a single boss.
Forecast Says Me is extremely powerful only if the deck has enough worthwhile Forecast Tricks to justify five slots.
Three Days of Rain provides extraordinary stability but prevents Weather cycling for a meaningful period.
These are intended drafting decisions, not incidental drawbacks.
# ADVANCED HYBRID EXAMPLE
A sophisticated Drizzle deck might begin as a Downpour Conduct deck but gradually become a controlled Stormbreak Forecast engine.
The player reaches Downpour using Drip Drip Drip or Build a Cloud.
Downpour automatically Soaks the battlefield.
While remaining there, the player sets Tomorrow's Umbrella for Thunderstorm and Watch the Glass for Clear.
Weather Station generates incremental value while those Forecasts are prepared.
Once the Forecast row is ready, Cloudburst attacks the Soaked battlefield and advances Downpour into Thunderstorm.
Entering Thunderstorm triggers Tomorrow's Umbrella.
Drizzle then uses Hallway Thunder or Just One More Rumble to exploit Thunderstorm Conduct.
Instead of allowing the storm to collapse at an inconvenient time, Open the Window forces the Stormbreak during Drizzle's own turn.
That Stormbreak:
Triggers Silver Lining.
Triggers Watch the Glass by entering Clear.
Enables Rainbow Crash.
Activates Quiet After, making the next Trick cost 0.
The enemies remain Soaked because the enemy turn did not begin in Clear.
Drizzle therefore has a brief wet Clear window where Conduct still functions even though the Weather track has reset.
A Strange Weather Vane can alter the next cycle so Stormbreak returns directly to Downpour, trading away Clear Forecast value in exchange for immediate battlefield stability.
At that point, Weather is not simply a four stage power meter.
It has become a route through several mechanically different states, and the player is choosing which transitions to exploit.
# DESIRED PLAYER EXPERIENCE
A new player thinks:
“I make it rain, wet the enemies, and then make a big thunderstorm.”
That is enough understanding to build a functional Drizzle deck.
An experienced player thinks:
“I want Downpour at the start of next turn, but I need Thunderstorm before this Forecast leaves my useful cycle. I can force the Stormbreak myself, keep the enemies Soaked during Clear, trigger my Clear Forecast, use the free Trick from Quiet After, then return to Downpour before they get a chance to dry.”
That is Drizzle's mastery layer.
The expert puzzle is not maximizing Weather.
It is controlling when Weather changes, which states are entered, which states are skipped, what is waiting for each transition, and whether the storm ending is actually more valuable than the storm itself.
