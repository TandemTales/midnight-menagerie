# Marmalade
## The Ghost Cat
### Identity
Marmalade should be the game's agile technical character.
She has relatively poor conventional defense and only moderate straightforward damage. What makes her powerful is avoiding attacks entirely, punishing enemies for trying to hit her, spending her Nine Lives for bursts of power, and chaining lots of small actions together.
Her skill ceiling should be high because she rewards reading enemy intentions and planning a turn ahead.
### Major strengths
High card mobility
Exceptional protection against large individual attacks
Powerful reactive damage
Strong card draw
Excellent zero cost and low cost chains
Can convert defense into offense
Several ways to recover from disastrous turns
### Major weaknesses
Less reliable conventional Guard
Multi hit attacks are dangerous because they rapidly strip Ghoststep
Limited straightforward heavy damage
Several builds require setup
Nine Lives are finite within combat
Haunt performs poorly against enemies that rarely attack
Zoomies builds become vulnerable when Status cards clog the deck
# Marmalade's four signature mechanics
### Ghoststep
Each stack of Ghoststep prevents the next hit of enemy Attack damage. Unused Ghoststep expires after the enemy turn.
This is not Guard. Twenty damage from one giant attack can be completely avoided by one Ghoststep.
But an enemy attacking six times can rapidly chew through it.
That creates a very different defensive puzzle.
### Haunt
Haunted enemies hurt themselves whenever they attack.
Haunt gradually dissipates when triggered.
Marmalade can therefore punish aggressive enemies without directly attacking them.
### Nine Lives
Marmalade begins each combat with 9 Lives.
Lives are a special combat resource, not health.
Certain powerful Tricks spend Lives.
Normally they do not regenerate until the next battle.
This gives Marmalade an unusually large reserve of emergency power, but careless players can burn through it before a boss battle becomes dangerous.
### Zoomies
Zoomies effects activate when a Trick is the third or later Trick you play during the same turn.
This supports rapid card cycling and low cost builds.
### Untouched
Several Tricks gain bonuses if Marmalade lost no Courage during the previous enemy turn.
This connects all of her mechanics together.
Ghoststep helps you remain Untouched.
Being Untouched strengthens Pounces.
Haunt punishes enemies for attacking.
Zoomies allows you to capitalize once an opening appears.

# Marmalade's major deck archetypes
The important part is that these should not behave like four isolated premade builds.
Cards should cross pollinate between them.
### 1. Ghoststep and Untouched
Avoid damage completely.
Remain Untouched.
Use that state to make expensive attacks cheaper and stronger.
Build escalating benefits for consecutive perfect enemy turns.
Weakness: Multi hit attackers and non Attack damage.
### 2. Haunt
Load enemies with Haunt.
Invite them to attack.
Protect yourself with Ghoststep.
Watch them destroy themselves.
Use cards such as Claws in the Dark to turn accumulated Haunt into direct burst damage when necessary.
Weakness: Defensive or buff focused enemies.
### 3. Nine Lives
Treat Marmalade's nine Lives as a second resource pool.
Spend aggressively for Nerve, cards, defense, and huge attacks.
Or preserve Lives until the boss reaches its dangerous phases.
Certain Rares can construct an entire deck around burning through and recovering Lives.
Weakness: The player can simply run out of Lives.
### 4. Zoomies
Cheap Tricks.
Card draw.
Discard.
Vanishing.
Repeated small attacks.
Rewards for playing three, four, five, six, or more Tricks in one turn.
This should feel like the screen suddenly exploding into frantic cat activity.
Weakness: Status cards, expensive hands, and enemies that punish repeated card play.
### 5. Hybrid Ghost Cat
The strongest high level Marmalade decks probably combine systems.
A particularly elegant interaction is:
Ghoststep → remain Untouched → cheap Pounce → Zoomies chain → apply Haunt → enemy attacks → Ghoststep protects Marmalade while Haunt damages enemy.
That makes Marmalade feel like one character with interacting mechanics, rather than four unrelated archetypes occupying the same card pool.
Her defining experience should be that a new player thinks, “This cat is really hard to hit.”
An experienced Marmalade player thinks, “I know exactly what the enemies are doing for the next two turns, they are going to hit nothing, and somehow that is going to kill all of them.”
