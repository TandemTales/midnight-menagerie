# Truffle, the Zombie Hedgehog

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 6030-6797_

# Truffle, the Zombie Hedgehog
Truffle is a tiny undead hedgehog who looks like he has been dead for quite a while and has somehow failed to notice.
He sheds quills everywhere. He occasionally loses structural integrity. He curls into the wrong shape. Bits that really ought to stay attached do not always cooperate.
None of this particularly bothers him.
Mechanically, Truffle should create a very different defensive problem from a conventional tank. He does not primarily ask, “How do I stop the enemy from hurting me?”
He asks:
“How much can I safely let them hurt me?”
His strongest defenses frequently leave a deliberate opening. His retaliation only works if an Attack actually reaches his Courage. His body gradually converts those impacts into scattered Quills, and those scattered Quills become a second economy that can be reclaimed, weaponized, or used to rebuild his defenses.
The result should feel like piloting a cheerful little wreck.
# 1. Overall gameplay identity
Truffle is a controlled damage, retaliation, and recyclable ammunition character.
He is strongest when the player carefully regulates incoming damage instead of simply preventing it.
His normal combat cycle is:
Grow or preserve Quills.
Raise some of them with Bristle.
Use partial Guard to survive an incoming Attack without completely blocking it.
Let a small amount of Courage damage through.
Bristle fires and sheds Quills onto the battlefield.
Loose Quills become ammunition, defensive material, or future body Quills.
Rebuild.
Repeat.
A second axis revolves around Ragged, Truffle’s dangerous state at half Courage or below.
Some decks desperately avoid becoming Ragged.
Others intentionally hover around that threshold because Truffle becomes faster, meaner, and surprisingly difficult to finish off once sufficiently battered.
The important distinction is that Truffle is not a character who simply gains enormous Guard and deals passive thorn damage.
Too much Guard can actively sabotage him.
# 2. Major strengths
Excellent retaliation against regular Attack patterns. Enemies who repeatedly make manageable Attacks are ideal prey.
Strong resource conversion. Quills can become retaliation, direct damage, Loose Quills, Guard, card flow, or future Nerve.
Excellent sustained combat scaling. A developed battlefield full of Loose Quills becomes increasingly useful.
Flexible recovery from poor turns. Regrowing Quills and reclaiming Loose Quills gives Truffle substantial resilience.
Powerful wounded state. Ragged builds can turn what would normally be a crisis into an offensive window.
Interesting multi enemy performance. Multiple attacking enemies provide more Bristle opportunities, while Loose Quill effects naturally support area damage.
# 3. Major weaknesses
Large single Attacks are dangerous. Bristle does not make taking a devastating hit desirable.
Perfect defense can be counterproductive. Completely blocking every Attack may prevent important engines from functioning.
Non Attack damage is troublesome. Bristle specifically responds to enemy Attacks that reach Courage.
Enemies that spend turns buffing, summoning, or applying statuses can disrupt his rhythm.
Quill starvation is real. An aggressive deck can Shed everything and discover that its retaliation engine is empty.
Loose Quills do nothing by themselves. A deck that produces many without enough ways to exploit them can become inefficient.
Ragged is genuinely dangerous. Half Courage is still half Courage. A bad draw or enemy burst can end the expedition.
Healing can create an awkward decision. Recovering enough Courage to leave Ragged may shut off important bonuses.
# 4. Signature systems
## Quills
Truffle begins each combat with 6 Quills.
His normal maximum is 12 Quills.
Quills are a combat resource representing the spines currently attached to him.
They are not Tricks and are not placed in the draw or discard piles.
Many effects require Truffle to Shed, Regrow, or otherwise manipulate them.
## Shed
Shed X moves X Quills from Truffle into the Loose Quill pool.
If an effect says “Shed X to…”, Truffle must have enough Quills to pay the cost.
Otherwise, an instruction to Shed simply moves as many as are available.
## Loose Quills
Shed Quills become Loose Quills.
Loose Quills remain scattered around the battlefield until Gathered or spent.
There is no normal maximum number of Loose Quills.
Loose Quills disappear when combat ends.
This gives Truffle two related resource pools:
Quills on Truffle
and
Quills scattered around the room
Many of his strongest decks deliberately move Quills back and forth between those pools.
## Gather
Gather X moves up to X Loose Quills back onto Truffle.
Gathering cannot normally exceed his maximum Quills.
If Truffle is full, any remaining Loose Quills stay on the floor.
## Regrow
Regrow X creates up to X new Quills on Truffle.
Regrow does not consume Loose Quills.
It cannot normally raise Truffle above his maximum.
## Bristle
Bristle X lasts through the next enemy turn.
Whenever an enemy Attack deals any Courage damage to Truffle after Guard and other prevention:
Consume 1 Bristle.
Shed 1 Quill if one is available.
Deal moderate retaliatory damage to that attacker.
One enemy Attack action can trigger Bristle only once, regardless of how many individual hits that Attack contains.
If Truffle has no Quill available, the Bristle stack is still consumed, but no retaliation occurs.
Unused Bristle normally expires at the start of Truffle’s next turn.
This is extremely important.
Bristle does not activate merely because Truffle is attacked.
The Attack must actually reduce his Courage.
## Ragged
Truffle is Ragged while at or below 50 percent of his maximum Courage.
Ragged has no intrinsic benefit by itself.
Individual Tricks become stronger while Truffle is Ragged.
This makes remaining wounded an intentional but dangerous strategy.
## Vanish
A Trick that Vanishes is removed from the combat after use.
# 5. Signature gameplay tensions
### Guard versus Bristle
Guard protects Truffle, but excessive Guard prevents Bristle from activating.
### Attached Quills versus Loose Quills
Attached Quills are ammunition for future Bristle triggers.
Loose Quills fuel different offensive and economic engines.
A player who moves everything into one pool loses access to the other.
### Regrowth versus recycling
Regrow creates fresh Quills.
Gather recycles existing ones.
Some decks specialize in one. Stronger decks often use both.
### Ragged versus safety
Ragged makes many Tricks substantially more efficient.
It also places Truffle within striking distance of defeat.
### Immediate value versus battlefield buildup
Loose Quills can be spent immediately for damage or allowed to accumulate for Carpet engines and larger payoffs.
# 6. Recognizable deck archetypes
### Bristle Counterattack
Uses deliberate Courage leakage and Bristle to punish enemy Attacks.
### Quill Carpet
Generates large numbers of Loose Quills and treats the floor as a secondary resource bank.
### Ragged Survivor
Operates near or below half Courage and gains major efficiency from being wounded.
### Regrowth Engine
Constantly Regrows, Sheds, Gathers, and reuses Quills.
### Damage Valve
Manipulates Guard extremely precisely so that enemy Attacks hurt Truffle, but only by an amount he can afford.
These archetypes intentionally overlap.
For example, a Bristle deck naturally produces Loose Quills. A Ragged deck likes Damage Valve cards. A Quill Carpet deck appreciates Regrowth because it lets Truffle continue shedding without exhausting his body supply.
No single keyword supplies an entire finished deck.

## Common pool purpose
The Commons establish the basic combat language.
A new Truffle deck can obtain Bristle, deliberately lower Guard, Shed Quills, reclaim them, maintain attached Quills, and begin exploring Ragged without requiring a rare engine.
Importantly, Commons already contain bridge pieces such as Shake It Loose, Carpet Check, Shake and Scoot, and All Spines, No Plan. These prevent the reward pool from dividing into isolated keyword packages.

# ARCHETYPE ANALYSIS
## 1. Bristle Counterattack
### Core strategy
Generate Bristle, deliberately allow manageable enemy Attacks to penetrate Guard, and convert those hits into automatic retaliation.
The ideal Bristle turn is not:
“Gain no Guard and get punched.”
It is:
“Reduce an incoming Attack from dangerous to trivial, then deliberately allow its final portion through.”
### Important Tricks
Back Into Them
Just Enough
Pointy Side Out
Shake It Loose
Hedgehog Handshake
Roll With It
Hard to Finish
Double Barbed
Permanent Bad Hair Day
### Strengths
Excellent against predictable Attack patterns.
Strong Nerve efficiency because some damage occurs during the enemy turn.
Naturally generates Loose Quills.
Can turn defensive setup into offense.
### Weaknesses
Weak against enemies that rarely Attack.
Poor against enormous individual blows unless defensive support is present.
Can run out of attached Quills.
Bristle normally expires, making mistimed setup costly.
### Hybrid potential
Bristle naturally feeds Quill Carpet because every successful retaliation Sheds a Quill.
It also feeds Ragged because controlled Courage loss can eventually push Truffle beneath half Courage.
This is one of the character’s most natural hybrid foundations.
# 2. Quill Carpet
### Core strategy
Treat the Loose Quill pool as a battlefield resource.
Shed aggressively, let Quills accumulate, then decide whether to:
Gather them,
spend them,
use their mere presence,
or preserve them for later.
### Important Tricks
Floor Sweep
Carpet Check
Quillstorm
Loose Change
Old Quill Under the Rug
Quill Carpet
The Floor Is Mine
Carpet Skewer
Carpet Launcher
The Carpet Remembers

### Strengths
Excellent long fight scaling.
Strong area damage.
Large turns become possible after several rounds of preparation.
Loose Quills can support damage, Nerve, draw, Guard, and Quill recovery.
### Weaknesses
Slow starts.
Loose Quills have no inherent effect.
A deck full of producers with too few consumers can create an impressive pile of useless floor debris.
Spending everything can erase several turns of setup.
### Hybrid potential
Bristle provides natural Loose Quill generation.
Regrowth allows Truffle to keep shedding without depleting his attached Quills.
Ragged provides cheaper attacks that can exploit the Nerve generated by Loose Change.
# 3. Ragged Survivor
### Core strategy
Operate near half Courage and exploit the increased efficiency of Ragged Tricks.
This is not primarily a healing deck.
It is a wounded state deck.
A strong Ragged player constantly evaluates whether recovery is worth losing access to Ragged bonuses.
### Important Tricks

### Strengths
Very high efficiency.
Strong burst damage.
Excellent Nerve economy.
Can turn a failing fight into a comeback.
### Weaknesses
Obvious lethal risk.
Poor draw sequences become much more dangerous.
Healing can deactivate the deck.
Enemy damage spikes are disproportionately threatening.
### Hybrid potential
Damage Valve is almost mandatory for sophisticated Ragged play.
Bristle also becomes attractive because Ragged decks are already willing to permit limited Courage loss.
Close Enough to Dead makes the archetype dramatically safer by expanding the operating window.
# 4. Regrowth Engine
### Core strategy
Move Quills through Truffle’s resource ecosystem repeatedly rather than treating them as a finite supply.
A Regrowth deck sees Quills as circulating material:
Attached Quill becomes Loose Quill.
Loose Quill becomes ammunition.
Gather returns it.
Regrow creates replacements.
Excess Regrowth can eventually become even more Loose Quills.

### Strengths
Very resilient resource economy.
Can support repeated expensive Shed effects.
Excellent in long boss encounters.

### Weaknesses
Regrowing Quills does not itself kill enemies.
Can spend too many deck slots on resource circulation.
Poorly constructed versions simply move counters around without achieving anything.
### Hybrid potential
Almost everything.
This is deliberately Truffle’s strongest support architecture, rather than a complete win condition by itself.
# 5. Damage Valve
### Core strategy
Manipulate Guard with unusual precision.
The goal is often to block most of an Attack rather than all of it.
This is the archetype that most clearly distinguishes Truffle from a normal defensive character.
### Important Tricks
Just Enough
Shake It Loose

Lower the Guard
Down in Front
Quill Reserve
Hold Still, Almost
Play Dead-ish

### Strengths
Makes Bristle much more reliable.
Lets the player choose when Ragged is approached.
Turns enemy intent into an active puzzle.
Allows extremely efficient defense in skilled hands.
### Weaknesses
Requires accurate arithmetic and enemy knowledge.
Enemy damage modification can ruin carefully planned thresholds.
Multiple attackers complicate the calculation.
A mistake that lets 2 Courage through instead of 1 is tolerable.
A mistake that lets 20 through may not be.
### Hybrid potential
This is the primary bridge between Bristle and Ragged.
A sophisticated Truffle player often includes a small Damage Valve package regardless of the deck’s nominal archetype.
# ADVANCED HYBRID EXAMPLE
Consider a deck containing:
Shake It Loose
Hedgehog Handshake
Roll With It
Built Wrong
Loose Change
The Floor Is Mine
Unpleasant Geometry
Dead Hedgehog Theory
and
Permanent Bad Hair Day
The deck might begin a turn with 5 attached Quills, 7 Loose Quills, several persistent Bristle stacks, and an enemy intending a moderate Attack.
The player does not simply maximize Guard.
They use Roll With It to soften the incoming hit while leaving a small opening.
They Gather Quills, which activates Unpleasant Geometry and The Floor Is Mine.
They use Loose Change to turn the remaining floor resources into additional Nerve for the next turn.
During the enemy turn, the Attack breaks Guard and clips Truffle’s Courage.
That single event:
activates Bristle,
Sheds another Quill,
deals retaliation damage,
activates Built Wrong and Regrows Quills,
and activates Dead Hedgehog Theory, preparing additional Nerve and card draw.
Because Permanent Bad Hair Day makes unused Bristle persistent, excess preparation is no longer wasted.

# WHAT PREVENTS EACH MECHANIC FROM BEING UNIVERSALLY OPTIMAL
Mechanic
Why it is not automatically correct
Bristle
Requires Courage damage, expires normally, and consumes attached Quills when triggered.
Loose Quills
Require generation and payoff cards. They provide nothing automatically.
Gather
Moves resources away from Loose Quill payoffs and can waste potential when already near the Quill cap.
Regrow
Produces no immediate damage unless supported and can be wasted at maximum Quills.
Ragged
Requires remaining at a genuinely dangerous Courage level.
Guard
Too little can kill Truffle. Too much can prevent Bristle and delay Ragged.
Shedding
Enables powerful effects but can leave Truffle unable to retaliate.
Healing
Improves survival but may turn off Ragged bonuses.
# COMBAT EXPERIENCE TARGET
A new player should think:
“I’m a zombie hedgehog. When enemies hit me, they get poked, and I can throw all the Quills that fall off.”
An experienced player should think:
“I need this Attack to penetrate Guard but only barely, because that triggers Bristle, moves a Quill to the floor, activates my Regrowth engine, turns on next turn’s Nerve bonus, and leaves exactly enough Loose Quills for Carpet Launcher without emptying the pool I need for The Floor Is Mine.”
That is Truffle’s mastery curve.
He begins as the funny zombie hedgehog who hurts things that touch him.
He eventually becomes a character about carefully engineering imperfect defense and converting controlled failure into resources.
