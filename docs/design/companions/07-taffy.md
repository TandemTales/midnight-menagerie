_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 4513-5239_

# Taffy, the Candy Slime
Taffy is the deck manipulation Companion.
Her central fantasy is not merely “slime that makes copies.” Taffy treats Tricks as physical objects. She can stretch them, swallow them, spit them back out, make temporary gummy replicas, pull their costs out of shape, split her own body into usable mass, then smoosh herself back together for payoff.
A Taffy deck should frequently create turns where the player looks at a hand of familiar Tricks and realizes that none of them currently behave quite the way they normally do.
## Gameplay identity
Taffy is an adaptive combo architect whose strongest resource is the mutable state of her own deck during combat.
She is particularly good at doing five things.
Holding Tricks in hand until they become unusually valuable.
Temporarily removing Tricks from circulation to sculpt future draws.
Creating expendable copies of carefully selected Tricks.
Rearranging the costs and properties of Tricks.
Alternating between a dispersed, unstable body and a recombined, concentrated body.
She is not intended to have the explosive turn one efficiency of some Companions. Her best decks manufacture favorable card states over several turns and then exploit them.
## Major strengths
Taffy has exceptional control over what is actually circulating through her deck.
She can turn mediocre cards into valuable ingredients.
She can temporarily remove awkward Tricks instead of permanently suffering from drawing them.
She can reuse important Attacks and Skills without relying on ordinary discard recursion.
She has unusually strong interactions with expensive Tricks because she can manipulate their costs.
She adapts extremely well to unusual reward combinations.
Her best builds have enormous late combat flexibility.
## Major weaknesses
Taffy has substantial setup costs.
Stretching Tricks can clog the hand.
Absorbing too many Tricks can leave the active deck unable to solve an immediate problem.
Generating too many Globs exposes Taffy to Courage loss.
Gummy copies are temporary and usually less efficient than simply drawing the original.
Several engines require specific Tricks to be in the correct zones at the same time.
Heavy enemy Status generation can interfere with carefully sculpted hands.
Taffy can produce spectacular turns, but badly sequenced Taffy decks can effectively sabotage themselves.
# Signature mechanics
## Globs
Globs represent pieces of Taffy that have separated from her main body.
Taffy starts each combat with 0 Globs.
Maximum Globs is 6.
Split X means gain X Globs.
Recombine X means spend X Globs.
Some effects say to Recombine any number. In that case, the player chooses how many Globs to spend.
### Runny
At 5 or 6 Globs, Taffy is Runny.
At the end of the enemy turn, if Taffy is Runny, she loses a small amount of Courage.
This happens once per enemy turn rather than once for every Glob.
Runny therefore represents an intentionally dangerous high resource state. Some Tricks become extremely strong while Taffy is Runny, but remaining there indefinitely is undesirable.
Globs are not Nerve and cannot normally pay Trick costs.
## Stretch
Certain effects can Stretch an Attack or Skill.
When a Trick becomes Stretched:
It gains 1 Stretch immediately.
It Retains.
At the end of each player turn that it remains in hand, it gains 1 additional Stretch.
Maximum Stretch is 3.
When the Trick is played, all Stretch is removed unless its effect says otherwise.
Stretch counters remain attached to that physical Trick if another effect moves it between zones.
A Stretched Trick continues to Retain whenever it is in hand.
Many Taffy Tricks gain different effects according to their Stretch.
Stretch creates deliberate hand congestion. Saving three Tricks for later means drawing into less usable hand space now.
## Belly
Taffy has a special combat zone called her Belly.
The Belly normally has 2 slots.
To Absorb a Trick is to place that Trick face up in the Belly.
An Absorbed Trick:
Is removed from normal deck circulation.
Cannot be drawn or played.
Keeps its combat modifications.
Returns normally after combat.
Does not count as Vanished.
To Spit Out a Trick moves it from the Belly to Taffy's hand. If the hand cannot accept it, it goes to the discard pile instead.
Taffy can therefore deliberately remove weak cards, store expensive cards for later, or preserve a specific Trick while cycling the rest of her deck.
## Gummy copies
A Gummy copy is a temporary replica of an Attack or Skill.
A Gummy copy:
Copies the original Trick's text and current Nerve cost unless stated otherwise.
Starts with 0 Stretch.
Has Vanish.
Cannot itself be copied.
Cannot normally be Absorbed.
Cannot generate another copy of itself.
Ceases to exist after combat.
Vanish means remove the Trick from combat after it is played.
This restriction is important. Taffy's copying mechanics are designed to create tactical duplication, not uncontrolled recursive loops.
## Card shaping
Taffy does not have one universal keyword for card shaping. Instead, many of her Tricks directly manipulate properties of other Tricks.
She can:
Change current Nerve costs.
Swap the costs of two Tricks.
Move Stretch between Tricks.
Grant Retain.
Grant or remove Vanish in tightly controlled situations.
Move Tricks between the hand, deck, discard pile, and Belly.
Taffy's cost manipulation is deliberately strongest when the player has a mixture of cheap and expensive Tricks. A deck where every Trick costs roughly the same gains much less from it.
# Major archetypes
## Puddle Cycle
Generate Globs aggressively, benefit from high Glob counts, then Recombine them into defense or burst damage.
## Long Pull
Stretch important Tricks over several turns and cash them out at precisely chosen moments.
## Snack Pocket
Use the Belly to sculpt the deck, hide situational cards, and retrieve them on demand.
## Candy Factory
Manufacture Gummy copies of carefully chosen Tricks and exploit temporary card volume.
## Cost Sculptor
Turn the hand into a puzzle of interchangeable costs and properties, making expensive Tricks cheap by pushing their cost onto disposable cards.
These strategies deliberately overlap. The strongest Taffy decks are frequently hybrids.

### Uncommon pool purpose
This is where Taffy's deck stops behaving like a conventional deck.
The player can now build actual engines around card zones and card states.
The most important branching choices appear here.
A player leaning into Globs wants Blob Barrage, Recombination Slam, Surface Tension, and Sweet Spot.
A Stretch deck starts wanting Elastic Reversal, Overstretch, Let It Sag, and Elastic Memory.
A Belly deck strongly values Better Save That, Taste Memory, Regurgitate, and Snack Pocket.
A copy deck wants Second Serving, Same Again, Multipack, and Chew Cycle.
The cards deliberately cross those lines. Smoosh Together, Pull Apart, Snapback Special, and Borrowed Price are especially important bridge Tricks.

# Why the Rare pool matters
The Rares are not simply oversized versions of existing Tricks.
Mix Everything can fundamentally change the economic structure of an entire hand.
Bottomless Belly converts Taffy's temporary card removal mechanic into a full deck compression strategy but creates a retrieval tax.
Runaway Puddle turns the normally dangerous Runny state into the foundation of a copy engine.
One Big Piece pushes in exactly the opposite direction, asking the player to avoid splitting entirely.
Family Size changes the expected lifetime of every Gummy copy.
Candy Surgery gives the player direct control over the properties of physical Tricks rather than just their effects.
These create substantially different endgame Taffy decks.

# STRATEGY ANALYSIS
## 1. Puddle Cycle
### Core strategy
Puddle Cycle decks repeatedly Split into several Globs, exploit the dispersed state, then Recombine before the Runny penalty becomes dangerous.
This is not intended to be a simple resource accumulation deck. The relevant question is where in the Glob cycle you want to end each turn.
### Important mechanics
Split
Recombine
Runny
Exact Glob thresholds
Cards such as Blob Barrage, Surface Tension, Sugar Sling, Sweet Spot, and Whole Body Slam
### Typical strengths
Strong burst turns.
Good multi enemy damage.
Flexible conversion of resources between offense and defense.
Can profit from dangerous Runny turns that end combat before the penalty matters.
### Typical weaknesses
Poor sequencing can strand the player at 5 or 6 Globs.
Whole Body Slam and similar spenders can leave subsequent cards without fuel.
Some cards favor 0 Globs, some favor 2, some favor 3 or 4, and others favor Runny. There is deliberately no universally correct amount.
### Hybridization
Puddle Cycle combines naturally with copies because Gummy cards can fill turns while Globs are being accumulated.
It also combines well with Stretch because a large Stretched payoff can wait while Taffy constructs the desired Glob state.
Runaway Puddle creates a particularly strong Puddle plus Gummy hybrid.
## 2. Long Pull
### Core strategy
Stretch a small number of high value Tricks and allow them to mature while handling the current turn with the rest of the deck.
The main difficulty is deciding how much future power is worth sacrificing present hand space.
### Important mechanics
Stretch
Retain
Stretch Transfer
Overstretch
Let It Sag
Elastic Memory
Slow Pull
Jawbreaker Drop
### Typical strengths
Excellent delayed burst.
Strong planning against bosses.
Can make ordinarily inefficient Tricks extremely valuable.
Stretch Transfer allows accumulated setup to be redirected when circumstances change.
### Typical weaknesses
Stretched cards occupy hand space.
A hand full of unfinished Tricks can leave Taffy defenseless.
Short encounters may end before the investment pays off.
Forced discard or Status generation can damage carefully prepared sequencing.
### Hybridization
Belly manipulation can temporarily remove unrelated cards so the important Stretched cards are easier to support.
Cost manipulation is especially effective because heavily Stretched Tricks can be made dramatically cheaper before their payoff turn.
A Puddle Cycle deck can also spend several turns generating Globs while a major Attack stretches in the background.
## 3. Snack Pocket
### Core strategy
Use the Belly to decide which Tricks are actually allowed to participate in the current deck cycle.
This can function as temporary deck thinning, tactical storage, or a way to save a specific Trick until its ideal turn.
### Important mechanics

### Typical strengths
Exceptional draw consistency.
Strong answers to situational cards.
Can temporarily remove expensive or awkward Tricks rather than repeatedly drawing them.
Protects important cards from normal deck cycling.
### Typical weaknesses
Absorbing the wrong Trick can remove the exact answer needed next turn.
A full Belly prevents further storage.
Spitting several Tricks out at once can create hand congestion.
Bottomless Belly creates a substantial cost penalty on retrieval.
### Hybridization
Snack Pocket is one of the strongest support shells for virtually every other archetype.
It improves Stretch decks by removing unrelated Tricks.
It provides templates for Taste Memory and Copycat Cannon.
It creates cost manipulation opportunities through Borrowed Price.
Three Course Chomp rewards players for storing a deliberately mixed collection rather than simply Absorbing their strongest Attack.
## 4. Candy Factory
### Core strategy
Identify Tricks worth duplicating and manufacture temporary Gummy copies of them.
The challenge is that copying an inefficient Trick simply creates more inefficiency.
### Important mechanics

### Typical strengths
Highly adaptable.
Excellent at exploiting particularly powerful cards acquired during a run.
Allows one scarce utility Trick to appear several times in a combat.
Can generate tremendous short term card volume.
### Typical weaknesses
Gummy cards normally disappear after one play.
Copying already expensive Tricks can overload Nerve requirements.
Copy effects themselves occupy deck slots and can become weak when no good template is available.
Recursive copying is intentionally prohibited.
### Hybridization
Cost Sculptor cards are perhaps the strongest partner because they solve the economic problem created by having multiple expensive copies.
Belly builds provide protected templates.
Runaway Puddle converts high Glob counts directly into cheap Gummy plays.
Stretch decks can prepare the original card while using replicas for less important turns.
## 5. Cost Sculptor
### Core strategy
Treat Nerve costs as movable properties rather than permanent characteristics of Tricks.
A classic Cost Sculptor hand might contain a 0 cost utility Trick and an expensive build payoff. Instead of merely playing the cheap Trick, Taffy gives the cheap Trick the expensive cost and gives the payoff the cheap cost.
The cheap card can then be discarded, Absorbed, or simply left unplayed.
### Important mechanics
Mix the Costs
Borrowed Price
Let It Sag
Sugar Coat
Mix Everything
Candy Surgery
Spit Take
Regurgitate
### Typical strengths
Can make expensive Rares unusually efficient.
Creates very high skill ceiling turns.
Makes seemingly mismatched card rewards unexpectedly compatible.
Can turn unwanted cards into useful repositories for undesirable costs.
### Typical weaknesses
Cost swaps do not create value when every Trick has the same cost.
Bad sequencing can make essential defensive Tricks unaffordable.
Some changes last only a turn.
The archetype needs actual high value cards to justify its manipulation infrastructure.
### Hybridization
Nearly every Taffy strategy can use cost sculpting, but excessive cost manipulation without actual payoff cards produces a deck that does impressive bookkeeping and accomplishes nothing.
That is intentional.
# Advanced hybrid example
Consider a deck built around:
Snack Pocket
Elastic Memory
Taste Memory
Smoosh Together
Stretch Transfer
Mix Everything
Sweet Spot
Jawbreaker Drop
One Big Piece
A suitable expensive utility Skill
The player begins whole at 0 Globs, benefiting from One Big Piece.
An inconvenient Trick is Absorbed to improve deck density.
Jawbreaker Drop is Stretched rather than played.
A second Trick accumulates Stretch, then Stretch Transfer can redirect that investment if the original target becomes unnecessary.
Taste Memory creates a temporary duplicate of a useful Belly Trick.
Eventually the player deliberately Splits, turning off One Big Piece.
The deck enters its Glob cycle.
Sweet Spot rewards passing through exactly 2 Globs.
Smoosh Together converts those Globs into another copy.
Mix Everything rearranges the hand's costs so the fully prepared Jawbreaker Drop receives the cheapest available price.
The player then Recombines completely, returns to 0 Globs, and One Big Piece comes back online for the next cycle.
Nothing about that deck belongs exclusively to one keyword package.
The Belly changes draw consistency.
Stretch stores future value.
Globs determine temporal state.
Copies increase access to premium Tricks.
Cost manipulation converts all of that preparation into affordable turns.
That is the level of cross system play Taffy is intended to reward.
# What prevents a single mechanic from dominating
Globs have the Runny threshold and compete between effects that want different quantities.
Stretch consumes hand space and time.
Belly manipulation can remove too much immediate functionality from the deck.
Gummy copies require good originals, normally Vanish after one use, and cannot recursively copy themselves.
Cost manipulation is nearly worthless without meaningful differences between the costs and values of the Tricks being manipulated.
Taffy's most dangerous deckbuilding trap should be taking too many enablers and not enough actual things worth enabling.
# Intended player experience
A new player thinks: “I can stretch my good cards, split into little slime pieces, and save cards in Taffy's tummy until I want them.”
An experienced player thinks: “I do not have a nine card deck. I currently have six active cards, two Belly cards, one three Stretch payoff, four Globs, a 0 cost Gummy template in discard, and three Nerve costs I can redistribute. What arrangement produces the best next two turns?”
That distinction is the core of Taffy.
