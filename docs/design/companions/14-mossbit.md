# Mossbit, the Tombstone Turtle
Mossbit is a tiny supernatural turtle whose shell has slowly become a weathered grave marker. Names, dates, scratches, bits of moss, and fragments of old epitaphs appear across the stone as the mansion changes around him.
His defining fantasy is not simply “turtle equals defense.”
Mossbit survives because he has time.
He postpones consequences, writes events into the future, lets Tricks improve by sitting untouched, and turns enemy damage into a problem for tomorrow. His strongest play frequently involves refusing the immediate payoff in exchange for a much larger or more flexible one later.
The danger is that tomorrow eventually arrives.
## COMPLETE MECHANICAL IDENTITY
### Overall gameplay identity
Mossbit is a delayed resolution and temporal planning Companion.
Much of his power exists several turns away from the moment it is created.
The central gameplay question is:
What must happen now, and what can I afford to let happen later?
Mossbit creates delayed effects called Epitaphs, earns Patience for allowing them to resolve on schedule, lets certain Tricks become stronger by Weathering in his hand, and can Bury incoming Attack damage rather than actually preventing it.
He therefore has several overlapping clocks:
Enemy intents
Active Epitaph countdowns
Weather counters on held Tricks
Buried Harm that must be dealt with before the end of his next turn
His limited Epitaph capacity
His Patience reserve
The best Mossbit play is rarely about maximizing one of those systems independently. It is about aligning them.
A player might deliberately delay an Epitaph so that a Weather Trick matures before it resolves, then Bury a dangerous enemy hit to survive the setup turn, convert the resulting Buried Harm into another Epitaph, and finally allow several inscriptions to resolve naturally together for a large Patience powered payoff.
That is Mossbit.
Signature mechanics1. EpitaphAn Epitaph is a delayed effect maintained by Mossbit.
An Epitaph has:
Countdown: The number of scheduled ticks remaining.
Effect: What occurs when it resolves.
Target: If applicable.
At the start of Mossbit's turn, all active Epitaphs lose 1 Countdown in the order they were created.
When an Epitaph reaches 0, its effect resolves and the Epitaph disappears.
Mossbit normally has 5 Epitaph slots.
If all 5 are occupied, another Epitaph cannot be created unless an effect explicitly creates additional capacity or Mossbit first erases an existing one.
An offensive Epitaph remembers its chosen enemy. If that enemy is gone when the Epitaph resolves, the Epitaph normally fizzles.
Values and choices used to create an Epitaph are locked when it is created unless its text explicitly checks something again when resolving.
### Advance
Advance an Epitaph by 1 means reduce its Countdown by 1 immediately.
If this reduces it to 0, resolve it immediately.
An Epitaph forced to resolve through Advance does not count as resolving naturally.
### Delay
Delay an Epitaph by 1 means increase its Countdown by 1.
### Erase
Erase an Epitaph means remove it without resolving its effect.
This is sometimes useful because Epitaph slots themselves are a resource.
2. PatienceMossbit can hold up to 3 Patience by default.
Whenever one of his Epitaphs reaches 0 from its ordinary start of turn tick, it resolves naturally and Mossbit gains 1 Patience after the effect resolves.
Patience remains until spent or combat ends.
Manually Advancing an Epitaph to 0 sacrifices the Patience that natural resolution would have produced.
This creates Mossbit's fundamental timing conflict.
Advance for immediate value.
Or:
Wait for better long term value.
Several Tricks become powerful when Mossbit has accumulated Patience or allow him to spend it for exceptional effects.
3. WeatherSome Tricks have Weather X.
An unweathered Weather Trick left in Mossbit's hand at the end of his turn is automatically Retained and loses 1 Weather.
When its Weather reaches 0, it becomes Weathered for the remainder of that combat.
A Weathered Trick uses its improved effect.
If an unweathered Weather Trick leaves Mossbit's hand before becoming Weathered, its Weather resets to its printed value.
Once it becomes Weathered, that state persists even if the Trick is played, discarded, or shuffled.
Weather therefore asks Mossbit to sacrifice hand capacity and immediate tempo in exchange for stronger future deck cycles.
4. BuryBury allows Mossbit to postpone Attack damage instead of truly preventing it.
If a Trick says:
Bury a moderate amount of Attack damage during the next enemy turn
Mossbit receives a temporary Burial capacity for that enemy turn.
Enemy Attack damage up to that capacity is prevented and instead becomes Buried Harm.
During Mossbit's next player turn, he may reduce, convert, or otherwise manipulate that Buried Harm.
At the end of that turn, Mossbit loses Courage equal to all remaining Buried Harm, then Buried Harm becomes 0.
This Courage loss is not enemy Attack damage. Guard cannot stop it and it cannot be Buried again unless a Trick specifically says otherwise.
Bury therefore does not inherently make Mossbit tougher.
It buys him time.
Sometimes that is much more valuable.
Sometimes it merely delays his defeat by one turn.
Major strengthsMossbit excels at:
Long encounters where delayed investments have time to mature.
Predictable bosses whose dangerous turns can be planned around.
Converting future resources into unusually strong later turns.
Temporarily surviving enormous Attack bursts through Burial.
Scaling without relying primarily on conventional stat increases.
Creating complex sequences involving several different future turns.
Turning otherwise idle turns into meaningful setup.
Rewarding careful deck knowledge and draw prediction.
# Major weaknesses
Mossbit struggles with:
Extremely fast encounters.
Sudden danger before his Epitaph network is established.
Hand congestion from Weather Tricks.
Filling all 5 Epitaph slots with effects that are no longer useful.
Spending too much Nerve preparing future turns while ignoring the current one.
Advancing everything too aggressively and therefore generating little Patience.
Refusing to Advance anything and dying while waiting.
Buried Harm that becomes impossible to resolve before payment comes due.
Encounters where enemies die before targeted Epitaphs resolve.
Status heavy enemies that make retaining Weather Tricks inconvenient.
Why none of Mossbit's systems is automatically optimalEpitaphs: Powerful but slow and restricted by 5 slots.
Patience: Valuable but capped and requires allowing Epitaphs to mature naturally.
Advancing: Produces immediate tempo but sacrifices Patience.
Delaying: Improves scheduling and Patience opportunities but risks making effects irrelevant.
Weather: Produces exceptional Tricks but consumes hand space and delays access to their improved versions.
Burial: Handles enormous immediate threats but merely converts them into future Courage loss.
A successful Mossbit deck therefore cannot simply take every Trick containing one particular keyword.
Recognizable Mossbit archetypesThe Inscription EngineMaintains several Epitaphs and manipulates their Countdown values.
### Buried Weight
Intentionally accumulates Buried Harm and converts it into offense or delayed effects before payment comes due.
### Weathered Relics
Retains key Tricks until they become dramatically better.
### Patient Monument
Prioritizes natural Epitaph resolution and uses Patience as a secondary economy.
### Gravekeeper
Treats Epitaph slots themselves as a manipulable board state, creating, erasing, copying, rescheduling, and recycling inscriptions.
These strategies deliberately overlap.
The Inscription Engine naturally generates Patience.
Burial provides the survival time Weather requires.
Weather can improve the Tricks used to manipulate Epitaphs.
Buried Harm can become new Epitaphs.
Gravekeeper decks create the slot space needed for all of those interactions.

### Common design role
The Common pool establishes the entire gameplay language.
A player can already discover several important decisions:
Chip the Clock provides immediate tempo but denies Patience if it forces resolution.
One More Minute makes an effect slower but can improve future timing.
Scratch It Out establishes that a useless Epitaph is not sacred. Slots are resources.
Put It Off introduces the dangerous distinction between damage prevention and damage postponement.
Old Reliable, Warm Flagstone, and Let It Settle introduce the hand capacity cost of Weather.

### Uncommon design role
The Uncommon pool is where Mossbit stops being merely slow and begins actively engineering time.
Several important tensions appear.
Set in Stone rewards making an already delayed strategy even slower.
Lichen Clock accelerates the second half of an Epitaph chain but forfeits potential Patience from the forced resolution.
Weight to Words turns temporary damage postponement into future offense.
Make Space makes deliberately destroying your own setup a legitimate strategy.
Weatherproofing accelerates Weather but still refuses to give the player immediate access to the matured Trick that same turn.
That last restriction is important. Otherwise Weather becomes another ordinary upgrade counter instead of an exercise in planning.

2. Buried WeightCore strategyTreat incoming damage as a temporary resource.
Mossbit intentionally Buries dangerous damage, then tries to do something profitable with the Buried Harm before the payment deadline.
Buried Harm can become:
Damage through Debt Collector
Future damage through Weight to Words
Card velocity through Carry It Properly
Epitaph scaling through Cemetery Shift
Direct offense through Borrowed Weight
Permanent combat scaling through Monument to Small Things
### Strengths
Exceptional emergency response against enormous Attack turns.
Turns enemy offensive power into Mossbit's own offensive resource.
Can generate some of Mossbit's largest burst turns.
Allows the player to survive damage before having the solution in hand.
### Weaknesses
Bury is not true prevention.
The player can finish an enemy turn looking completely safe while actually being in severe danger.
Poor draws after Burial are extremely punishing.
Non Attack Courage loss bypasses the system.
Using too much Burial without adequate conversion makes the deck self destructive.
### Hybridization
Weight to Words turns Buried Harm directly into an Epitaph.
Cemetery Shift makes Burial an Inscription engine.
Fossil Snack allows Weather to become Buried Harm cleanup.
Monument to Small Things rewards a deck that successfully solves repeated Burial problems over a long encounter.
3. Weathered RelicsCore strategyDraft unusually powerful Weather Tricks and deliberately hold them through otherwise tempting opportunities to play them.
The deck accepts weaker early cycles in exchange for superior later cycles.

The deck can become too committed to being patient when immediate action is actually necessary.

It also improves Epitaph heavy decks by rewarding the very process those decks naturally perform.
Burial is valuable because it can prevent an emergency from forcing Mossbit to prematurely Advance his entire schedule.

# Important cross archetype interactions
Mossbit's strongest interactions intentionally cross mechanic boundaries.
### Put It Off plus Weight to Words
Enemy damage becomes Buried Harm.
Buried Harm becomes a future offensive Epitaph.
The enemy effectively wrote its own epitaph by attacking Mossbit.
### Set in Stone plus Patience
Set in Stone makes Epitaphs slower but more powerful.
The longer Countdown is normally a drawback.
A Patient Monument deck actually appreciates the additional scheduled time because it wants natural resolutions rather than immediate ones.
### Weatherproofing plus Geologic Headbutt
Weatherproofing can accelerate the three turn incubation of Geologic Headbutt.
It cannot let Mossbit immediately play the newly Weathered Headbutt, preserving a full turn of telegraphing and preparation.
### Emergency Burial plus Borrowed Weight
Mossbit can absorb an enormous hit into Buried Harm, then convert the stored quantity into a major Attack.
He still has to deal with the Buried Harm afterward.
The damage output therefore comes from accepting danger rather than obtaining free scaling.
### Grave Moss plus Make Space
A full Epitaph board can intentionally become a defensive resource.
Mossbit destroys inscriptions he no longer needs, gains the immediate Make Space rewards, then triggers Grave Moss for additional stabilization.
### Centuries in a Pocket plus Geological Patience
Several Epitaphs are arranged to resolve naturally together next turn.
If Patience fills early in the sequence, later natural resolutions become damage and Guard through Geological Patience instead of wasting the excess.
### Cemetery Shift plus Monument to Small Things
Burying damage creates offensive Epitaphs.
Successfully clearing that Buried Harm then improves future Epitaph output.
The enemy's pressure effectively becomes a scaling engine, but only if Mossbit repeatedly survives the debt.

Next turn they resolve naturally in sequence.
Patience fills.
Geological Patience converts later overflow into additional offense and protection.
Deep Inscription resolves among the chain, restoring card draw and Nerve exactly on the turn Mossbit finally begins cashing in the setup.
That is the desired advanced Mossbit experience.
The player is not simply asking:

They are asking:
“What should the battlefield look like two turns from now, and which problem can I afford to leave unresolved until then?”
Desired player experienceA new player thinks:“Mossbit puts strong things on timers. If I can survive until they go off, I get rewarded for waiting.”

### An experienced player thinks:
“I need this Epitaph to resolve naturally, but not until after Fossil Snack matures. Mossbit should feel calm on the surface and increasingly intricate underneath.
