# Pipkin, the Pumpkin Frog

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 3831-4512_

# Pipkin, the Pumpkin Frog
Pipkin should feel like a tiny haunted garden problem with legs.
His gameplay is built around three questions:
How high do I want to jump before I come down?
How long can I afford to let my Patch grow before I need its Pumpkins?
How big do I want Pipkin to become before his own weight starts getting in the way?
Unlike Brambleboo, Pipkin does not gradually cover enemies in vegetation or build an expanding control state. His garden is a private production engine. Seeds mature into Pumpkins, Pumpkins are harvested as resources, and Pipkin physically changes size while bouncing around the battlefield.
His strongest decks repeatedly shift between being a nimble little frog, an enormous round frog, and an opportunistic pumpkin farmer.
## 1. Overall Gameplay Identity
Pipkin is a rhythm and maturation character.
His turns frequently involve setting up a sequence rather than simply evaluating Tricks individually. A typical Pipkin turn might look like this:
Hop.
Hop again.
Accelerate a Sprout into a Pumpkin.
Harvest that Pumpkin.
Use the Harvest to swell Pipkin.
Then come crashing down with a Landing Trick.
His longer term battle state matters too. Seeds planted now usually do not become useful immediately. The player must think ahead about what their Patch will contain one or two turns later.
At the same time, Pipkin can become increasingly Plump, improving his heavy body based Tricks and certain defensive options. The catch is that a very Plump frog is bad at getting airborne.
That creates the main tension of the character.
Small Pipkin is mobile. Big Pipkin is powerful. The Patch lets him transition between the two.
## 2. Major Strengths
Excellent burst turns once several systems are prepared together.
Strong multi enemy damage through Landing effects and thrown Pumpkins.
Flexible delayed resource generation.
Can convert setup into damage, Guard, Nerve, draw, or Courage recovery depending on deck construction.
Strong scaling without relying on a conventional permanent damage stat.
Many ways to manipulate the timing of future turns.
Particularly good at turning apparently quiet turns into preparation for enormous future turns.
## 3. Major Weaknesses
Many Patch Tricks are weak before Pumpkins have had time to ripen.
A Patch filled with the wrong stages can become awkward because it has limited capacity.
Harvest payoffs can become dead Tricks if the player has no Pumpkins.
Height disappears at the end of the turn unless specifically preserved.
Plump improves several powerful cards but eventually makes Hop Tricks more expensive.
The character can become too elaborate for his own good. A deck containing too many setup Tricks may spend several turns preparing without actually winning the fight.
Fast enemies can punish overly ambitious farming strategies.
# Signature Systems
## Height
Pipkin can gain Height, representing how far off the ground he has bounced.
Height ranges from 0 to 3.
Pipkin normally begins each turn at 0 Height.
### Hop
When a Trick says Hop, Pipkin gains 1 Height, to a maximum of 3.
A Trick that says Hop twice gains 2 Height.
Height itself has no automatic benefit.
It exists to power Landing effects.
### Land
Some Tricks contain a Land effect.
When a Land effect resolves while Pipkin has at least 1 Height, Pipkin spends all current Height and resolves the Land effect using the amount spent.
If Pipkin has 0 Height, the normal portion of the Trick still resolves, but its Land text does not.
Unless another effect says otherwise, unused Height disappears at the end of Pipkin's turn.
This makes Height primarily a sequencing mechanic rather than a resource intended to accumulate forever.
## The Patch
Pipkin has a personal Patch containing up to 6 objects.
The Patch begins each combat empty.
Objects can exist in three stages:
Seed
Sprout
Pumpkin
Planting creates Seeds.
At the end of Pipkin's turn, the Patch performs one growth step.
During a growth step:
Existing Sprouts become Pumpkins.
Existing Seeds become Sprouts.
Existing Pumpkins remain Pumpkins.
All changes happen as part of the same growth step. A Seed therefore normally requires two growth steps to become a Pumpkin.
If the Patch has 6 objects, further Plant effects cannot add Seeds unless another effect creates space.
## Plant
Plant X means add X Seeds to the Patch, subject to its capacity.
## Harvest
Harvest X means remove up to X Pumpkins from the Patch to activate the associated effect.
Harvesting is normally optional.
A Pumpkin has no automatic benefit when harvested. Its value comes from the Trick or Power causing or reacting to the Harvest.
This is important because a Pumpkin can be saved for a stronger Harvest Trick later.
## Plump
Pipkin can have between 0 and 3 Plump.
Plump begins at 0 each combat and normally persists between turns.
Many body based Tricks become stronger when Pipkin is Plump.
Some Tricks can Deflate Pipkin to spend Plump for other effects.
### Heavy Feet
While Pipkin has 3 Plump, Tricks containing Hop cost 1 additional Nerve.
A single Trick that says Hop twice still receives the additional cost only once.
This is Pipkin's primary internal tension.
A large Pipkin is dangerous, but a large Pipkin has trouble bouncing.
## Deflate
Deflate X means spend X Plump.
If Pipkin does not have enough Plump, that optional clause cannot be used.
## Vanish
A Trick that Vanishes is removed from the combat after resolving.
This is used on several unusually efficient sequencing Tricks to prevent trivial loops.
# Why These Systems Are Not Universally Optimal
### Height
Height requires sequencing and usually demands several Tricks before the payoff. Drawing Landing Tricks without enough setup can make them mediocre.
### Patch
Seeds are delayed value. Harvest Tricks without mature Pumpkins can be poor draws, and an overcrowded Patch can prevent further planting.
### Plump
Plump improves heavy attacks and several defensive effects, but reaching maximum Plump directly interferes with Pipkin's Hop engine.
### Growth Acceleration
Accelerating the Patch costs Tricks and Nerve that could have been spent immediately affecting the enemy. Fast farming therefore has a meaningful tempo cost.

# Major Deck Archetypes
## 1. The Hopper
### Core Strategy
The Hopper deck treats the player's hand as a sequence puzzle.
It wants to build Height efficiently, then spend it on one or more powerful Landing Tricks.
Typical turns might involve Puddle Jumper, Hopscotch, Springboard, then Cannonball.
The question is often not merely which Trick is strongest, but which Trick should be the one that finally makes Pipkin come down.
### Important Mechanics
Height, Hop, Land, Nerve discounts, retaining Height, and Land effect amplification.
### Important Tricks
Puddle Jumper, Drop In, Soft Landing, Hopscotch, Cannonball, Three Hop Combo, Springboard, Leapfrog, Triple Jump, Spring Eternal, and Boing Without End.
### Strengths
Excellent burst.
Strong multi enemy damage.
Can produce powerful defensive turns through Soft Landing and Cannonball.
Rewards precise hand sequencing.
### Weaknesses
Landing Tricks are mediocre when drawn without Hop support.
Height disappears normally at turn end.
Heavy Feet can severely disrupt the deck if Pipkin becomes too Plump.
A deck containing too many Hop Tricks and too few worthwhile Land effects generates motion without payoff.
### Hybrid Possibilities
The Hopper naturally combines with the Patch through Fertile Footprints, Tongue from Above, Scatter on Landing, and The Patch Fights Back.
It also combines with Plump, but doing so requires careful use of Deflate Tricks.
# 2. Pumpkin Farmer
### Core Strategy
This deck views combat across multiple turns.
It Plants Seeds early, watches the Patch mature, then converts Pumpkins into explosive value.
Unlike a poison deck, the Patch itself does nothing to enemies.
A Pumpkin sitting in the Patch is potential.
Its final purpose is determined by the Harvest Trick eventually used on it.
One Pumpkin might become damage.
Another might become draw.
Another might become Courage.
Another might become Nerve.
### Important Mechanics
Plant, growth steps, Harvest, Patch capacity, and growth acceleration.
### Important Tricks
Pocket Seeds, Damp Corner, Pumpkin Pitch, Little Harvest, Warm Windowsill, No Room, No Problem, Moonbeam on the Patch, Pantry Raid, Prize Pumpkin, Garden in Motion, Overnight Miracle, Perfect Harvest, Moonlit Garden, and Heirloom Seeds.
### Strengths
Exceptional long fight scaling.
Highly flexible resource conversion.
Excellent consistency once the Patch engine is mature.
Can store Pumpkins without immediately committing them to a particular purpose.
### Weaknesses
Slow opening turns.
Patch Tricks can draw badly in short encounters.
A Patch containing six immature objects can prevent additional planting.
A Patch containing six Pumpkins is also wasteful if the deck cannot Harvest them efficiently.
### Hybrid Possibilities
Harvest has direct links to Plump through Swallow the Sun, Eat the Whole Thing, Great Pumpkin Frog, and Choose the Biggest.
Harvest also feeds Landing through The Patch Fights Back and Pick of the Patch.
# 3. Big Frog
### Core Strategy
The Big Frog deck deliberately pushes Plump upward and exploits Pipkin's increasing mass.
Belly Bop becomes more threatening.
Mud Jacket becomes stronger.
Squash Tackle produces efficient defense.
Three Story Belly Flop becomes absurd when a sufficiently round Pipkin somehow manages to get airborne.
The gameplay is not simply about staying at maximum Plump.
The interesting decks repeatedly inflate and deflate.
### Important Mechanics
Plump, Deflate, Heavy Feet, body based damage, and Plump dependent Guard.
### Important Tricks
Belly Bop, Big Breath, Squat Low, Squash Tackle, Inflate, Pffft, Mud Jacket, Heavy Hopper, Big Frog Energy, Emergency Deflation, Frogapult, Big Little Frog, and Bigger Than the Doorway.
### Strengths
Reliable large attacks.
Strong defensive conversion.
Excellent resource bursts from Emergency Deflation.
Can become very efficient without requiring a mature Patch.
### Weaknesses
Maximum Plump interferes with Hop Tricks.
Repeated inflation without appropriate payoffs wastes resources.
Repeated deflation without enough ways to grow again leaves the deck weak.
### Hybrid Possibilities
The best Big Frog builds usually use either Harvest or Hop as a secondary engine.
Harvest provides efficient ways to regain Plump.
Hop provides devastating uses for Pipkin's weight once the player can solve Heavy Feet.
# 4. The Fast Garden
### Core Strategy
This archetype does not patiently wait for the normal Patch clock.
It manipulates growth stages directly.
The player turns Seeds into Sprouts, Sprouts into Pumpkins, and occasionally moves things backward to engineer future turns.
This becomes almost a miniature resource scheduling game inside combat.
### Important Tricks
Damp Corner, Weed the Patch, Warm Windowsill, Crop Rotation, No Room, No Problem, Crouch and Count, Moonbeam on the Patch, Garden in Motion, Overnight Miracle, Back to Seed, and Moonlit Garden.
### Strengths
Can manufacture Pumpkins far faster than ordinary farming.
Excellent control over when Harvest engines activate.
Can recover from poor Patch composition.
Excellent with expensive Harvest payoffs.
### Weaknesses
Growth manipulation itself does not defeat enemies.
Drawing too many gardening Tricks together can produce turns with virtually no immediate combat impact.
The archetype is extremely sensitive to deck composition.
### Hybrid Possibilities
This is rarely a complete deck by itself.
It is primarily an engine that feeds Harvest damage, Plump cycling, or Landing synergies.
# Important Cross Archetype Bridges
Tongue from Above connects Height with planting.
Seed Slam turns combat pressure into Patch development.
Fertile Footprints causes Landing turns to create future Patch value.
Choose the Biggest converts Harvest into both defense and offense.
Heavy Hopper turns Plump into a new Hop sequence.
Frogapult converts stored body mass directly into Height and Landing damage.
Swallow the Sun turns a Pumpkin into maximum Plump.
Great Pumpkin Frog creates a loop between Harvesting, inflation, deflation, and planting.
The Patch Fights Back converts agricultural preparation into Landing burst.
These bridge Tricks are important because they keep the optimal deck from becoming simply twenty cards containing the same keyword.

One of the most interesting Pipkin decks might begin as a farming deck and gradually transform into a Landing engine.
The deck could use:

The engine might work like this.
Pipkin Lands.
Fertile Footprints Plants a Seed.
The Patch grows.
A Pumpkin eventually ripens.
Prize Pumpkin improves next turn's Nerve.
Pipkin Harvests it.
Great Pumpkin Frog gives Plump.
The Patch Fights Back prepares bonus Landing damage.
Pipkin uses Pffft to Deflate.
Pffft produces Nerve and draw.
Great Pumpkin Frog Plants another Seed because Pipkin Deflated.
That extra Nerve funds Springboard.
Pipkin reaches Height.
Cannonball brings him crashing back down.
The Landing activates the stored Patch payoff and begins the agricultural cycle again.
This is the kind of Pipkin deck that should feel dramatically stronger than any individual mechanic viewed in isolation.
# Player Experience Target
A new player thinks:
“I plant Pumpkins, get really round, then jump on things.”
That is enough understanding to begin playing Pipkin successfully.
An experienced player thinks:
“I need this Seed to become a Pumpkin one turn from now, because that Harvest gives me the Plump I need to convert through Pffft into the Nerve that lets Springboard reach 3 Height before my Landing payoff. So I should not Harvest the Pumpkin I already have.”
That is the mastery layer.
Pipkin should gradually reveal himself as a character about timing, conversion, and deliberately changing what kind of frog you are from turn to turn.
