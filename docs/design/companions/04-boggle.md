# Boggle, the Monster Under the Bed

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 2473-3137_

# Boggle, the Monster Under the Bed
Boggle should feel like a predator who is also a coward. He is dangerous precisely because he does not want to stand in front of anything and fight fairly.
His central gameplay question is not simply, “How do I avoid damage?” It is:
Who currently knows where I am, who is looking for me, who is terrified of what might be under the furniture, and when do I ruin my own hiding place by jumping out?
This gives Boggle a very different defensive structure from Marmalade. Marmalade protects herself reactively through Ghoststep. Boggle manipulates enemy awareness states before attacks occur, then deliberately sacrifices that protection to Ambush enemies.
Costs below are provisional balance targets. Damage and Guard remain qualitative unless an exact quantity is mechanically important.
## 1. Overall gameplay identity
Boggle is a setup, concealment, burst, and tempo control Companion.
He alternates between two broad phases.
Hidden phase: Boggle manipulates enemies into becoming Unaware, accumulates Fright and Lurk, interferes with directed enemy attacks, and prepares Ambushes.
Exposed phase: Boggle has jumped out, been discovered, or allowed an enemy to Search. Enemies become Suspicious, temporarily preventing Boggle from hiding from them again. During this phase he uses defensive Tricks, exploits their paranoia, and prepares to disappear again once their attention lapses.
The strongest Boggle decks deliberately move between these phases rather than attempting to remain hidden forever.
## 2. Major strengths
Excellent control over dangerous directed enemy Attacks.
Extremely strong prepared burst damage.
Fright can be accumulated for several different payoffs rather than representing damage by itself.
Strong tactical decisions in multi enemy encounters because every enemy can occupy a different Awareness state.
Excellent long fight scaling through Lurk and Fright.
Many defensive tools become offensive setup and vice versa.
## 3. Major weaknesses
Boggle cannot repeatedly hide from the same enemy every turn.
Room wide attacks and attacks affecting the whole party bypass his main concealment defense.
Aggressive Ambush play deliberately reveals Boggle.
Fright does absolutely nothing by itself. A deck that creates large quantities of Fright without adequate Scare payoffs can fail badly.
Lurk accumulates slowly and has a hard cap.
Some enemies spend many turns buffing, summoning, or using area effects, reducing the immediate defensive value of Unaware.
Newly summoned enemies begin Aware.
Poorly timed Ambushes can leave several enemies Suspicious simultaneously, creating a very dangerous exposed turn.
# 4. Signature systems
## Awareness
Every enemy has one Awareness state with respect to Boggle.
### Aware
The normal state.
An Aware enemy can target Boggle normally.
Most Tricks that hide Boggle can only make an Aware enemy Unaware.
### Unaware
The enemy does not currently know where Boggle is.
If an Unaware enemy has a directed Attack whose only target is Boggle, that Attack becomes Search.
The intent display updates immediately so the player can see this change.
If Boggle attacks an Unaware enemy, the entire Trick resolves first. Afterward, that enemy normally becomes Suspicious.
Non Attack enemy actions do not remove Unaware.
Room wide Attacks still hit Boggle and do not remove Unaware.
### Search
Search is an enemy action created by Unaware.
Search:
Deals no Courage damage.
Replaces the entire directed Attack action, including all hits of a multihit Attack.
Removes Unaware.
Gives that enemy 2 Fright.
Makes that enemy Suspicious.
This means hiding is extremely powerful, but it cannot simply be repeated indefinitely.
### Suspicious
The enemy thinks Boggle is nearby and is actively watching for him.
A Suspicious enemy cannot become Unaware.
After that enemy completes its next action, Suspicious ends and it becomes Aware.
Example:
Boggle hides from a monster.
The monster Searches instead of attacking and becomes Suspicious.
On Boggle's next turn, he cannot hide from that monster again.
The monster takes one normal action.
Afterward it becomes Aware, allowing Boggle to hide from it again on a later turn.
This creates Boggle's fundamental concealment cycle.
## Ambush
Ambush effects receive their listed bonus if the target is Unaware when the Trick begins resolving against it.
The target remains Unaware until the complete Trick finishes, including all hits and Scare clauses.
After an Ambush Attack resolves, the target normally becomes Suspicious.
A multi enemy Attack checks Ambush separately for every target.
This creates the key tradeoff:
Staying hidden provides control. Attacking from hiding provides power but sacrifices that control.
## Fright
Fright is a persistent enemy resource.
Fright has no inherent effect.
It does not cause damage.
It does not weaken enemies.
It does not expire naturally.
Search normally gives 2 Fright, and many Tricks create additional Fright.
Fright exists to fuel Scares.
## Scare N
A Scare N clause checks whether the target has at least N Fright.
If it does, remove N Fright and resolve the listed Scare effect.
If it does not, the rest of the Trick still resolves normally unless the Trick specifically says otherwise.
Effects resolve in printed order. This means a Trick may apply Fright and immediately reach its own Scare threshold.
## Lurk
Lurk represents Boggle patiently remaining out of sight while tension builds.
Boggle begins each combat with 0 Lurk.
Base maximum Lurk is 5.
At the end of Boggle's turn, if at least one living enemy is Unaware, gain 1 Lurk.
Lurk does not naturally decay.
Several Tricks can generate additional Lurk.
Powerful Tricks can spend it.
This creates a second tension alongside Fright. Fright is stored on enemies. Lurk is stored on Boggle.
# 5. Recognizable deck archetypes
### Quiet Ambusher
Repeatedly cycles enemies through Aware, Unaware, Ambush, and Suspicious.
Its challenge is determining when the damage from an Ambush is worth surrendering the defensive value of Unaware.
### Fright Engine
Builds Fright through eerie noises, Search actions, and enemy paranoia, then uses several different Scare thresholds.
Its challenge is deciding how much Fright to consume now versus preserve for stronger later effects.
### Patient Lurker
Accumulates Lurk and converts it into explosive Tricks or broad Fright generation.
It is powerful in longer encounters but can be too slow in hallway fights.
### Caught Red Pawed
Instead of treating Suspicious as pure failure, this archetype uses enemies' Suspicious turns as its productive phase.
It gains Guard, Fright, discounts, and attacks specifically while enemies are watching for Boggle.
### Houseful of Monsters
Specializes in encounters containing several enemies. Boggle deliberately shifts attention around the room, revealing one enemy while another loses track of him.
This is particularly good at creating complicated states where one enemy is Unaware, another Suspicious, and another loaded with Fright.

The Common pool gives Boggle functional access to every major system without providing a complete engine.

Attacking an Unaware enemy is powerful but dangerous.
Suspicious enemies are not simply dead turns.
Fright can become defense, damage, or card flow.
Lurk asks the player to spend some turns behaving patiently.
Multi enemy encounters reward managing different Awareness states rather than simply applying the same status to everything.

# STRATEGY ANALYSIS
## Quiet Ambusher
### Core strategy
Create Unaware enemies, exploit Ambush bonuses, then manage the resulting Suspicious turn.
Important Tricks include Hide Under Something, From Under Here, Underbed Uppercut, Big Eyes in the Dark, Surprise From Behind, Quiet as Dust, Keep the Lights Off, and Hide in Plain Sight.
### Strengths
Excellent burst.
Can turn dangerous directed enemy Attacks into Search.
Ambush bonuses are strong enough that even ordinary Attacks become meaningful build pieces.
### Weaknesses
Every normal Ambush creates Suspicious.
Area damage ignores the defensive portion of Unaware.
Too many Ambush cards without enough ways to establish Unaware creates a clumsy deck.
### Hybridization
Fright is a natural partner because Search already produces Fright.
Lurk also works because ending turns while enemies remain Unaware builds Lurk before the eventual Ambush.
## Fright and Scare
### Core strategy
Generate Fright through Search, noises, Suspicious enemies, and Powers, then spend it selectively through Scare clauses.
The key is that Fright is a currency, not damage over time.
A player might use 3 Fright for Guard now, preserve 6 for BOO!, or accumulate 8 for Too Late to Run.
### Important Tricks
Creak..., Ankle Grab, Nervous Giggle, BOO!, Shiver in the Walls, Practice Your Scream, Imagination Does the Rest, Every Creak Means Me, Don't Scream Yet, Fear of the Dark, Bigger in Your Head.
### Strengths
Extremely flexible.
Good scaling against high Courage enemies.
Can transform one resource into offense, defense, card flow, hiding, or Lurk.
### Weaknesses
Fright alone does nothing.
Repeatedly consuming small Scares can prevent the player from reaching major thresholds.
A deck containing lots of Fright creation but too few Scares effectively spends cards doing nothing.
### Hybridization
Almost every other Boggle archetype naturally generates Fright.
The strongest Fright decks therefore usually borrow either concealment or Suspicious exploitation rather than becoming pure status decks.
## Patient Lurker
### Core strategy
End turns with enemies Unaware, accumulate Lurk, expand the cap, and eventually use it for extremely efficient attacks or broad setup.
### Important Tricks
Wait For It, Did You Hear That?, From Under Here, Closer... Closer..., Underbed Kingdom, Beneath Every Bed, Wrong Side of Bed, The Big One, The Shadow Is Bigger, Everybody Heard That.
### Strengths
Excellent long fight scaling.
Lurk can be converted into either direct damage or Fright.
The player can hoard Lurk without being forced to spend it.
### Weaknesses
Slow.
Base maximum 5 prevents endless stockpiling.
Several Lurk generators require the player to sacrifice aggression.
Spending Lurk weakens Tricks such as The Shadow Is Bigger.
### Hybridization
The archetype naturally joins Quiet Ambush because remaining hidden generates Lurk.
It also pairs strongly with Fright because Everybody Heard That and Bedtime Jitters convert stored Lurk into enormous Scare setup.
## Caught Red Pawed
### Core strategy
Treat Suspicious enemies as a deliberate combat state rather than a punishment.
Boggle Ambushes or allows an enemy to Search, then spends the exposed turn exploiting the enemy's paranoia.
### Important Tricks
Toe Nibbler, Blanket Snap, Hold Very Still, Sock Drawer Lunge, Shiver in the Walls, Emergency Pillow, One Eye Open, I Was Behind You, The Room Is Looking Back, Feed the Imagination.
### Strengths
Turns Boggle's mandatory vulnerability window into productive tempo.
Has reliable Guard.
Generates substantial Fright.
Often prefers letting Search happen rather than avoiding it.
### Weaknesses
Cannot function properly if enemies remain Aware without cycling through Unaware first.
Suspicious bonuses may arrive at inconvenient moments.
Area focused enemies interact less with the concealment cycle.
### Hybridization
This is one of Boggle's strongest secondary packages because every Ambush deck eventually produces Suspicious enemies.
An advanced player may deliberately create Suspicious early rather than attempting to maximize time spent Unaware.
## Houseful of Monsters
### Core strategy
Exploit multiple enemies occupying different Awareness states.
One enemy might Search.
Another gets Ambushed.
A third loses track of Boggle.
Fright can be transferred between them to reach specific thresholds.
### Important Tricks
Shadow Puppet, No One Here But Dust, Hide and Shriek, Creaky Floorboard, Ceiling Creep, Monster in the Mirror, Everywhere at Once, Again, But Over There, Bedframe Geography.
### Strengths
Excellent multi enemy control.
High efficiency because many Tricks affect several Awareness states simultaneously.
Can convert attacking one enemy into concealment against another.
### Weaknesses
Several cards become less efficient against lone bosses.
More difficult to pilot.
Revealing too many enemies simultaneously can create a disastrous turn where everything is Suspicious.
### Hybridization
Fright redistribution gives it natural Scare access.
Bedframe Geography also lets an Ambush focused build move concealment from one enemy to the next.
# An advanced hybrid Boggle deck
A sophisticated Boggle deck might combine Quiet as Dust, Feed the Imagination, Practice Your Scream, moderate Lurk generation, and a handful of strong Ambush attacks.
Suppose an enemy begins Aware.
Boggle makes it Unaware. The House Settles could apply Fright if present.
Boggle ends the turn while it is Unaware and gains Lurk.
Rather than immediately spending that Lurk, he may allow the enemy to Search. Search adds Fright and creates Suspicious. Feed the Imagination adds even more Fright.
The next turn is therefore not simply the “bad turn.” Boggle can use Emergency Pillow, Shiver in the Walls, I Was Behind You, or The Room Is Looking Back to exploit Suspicious.
The enemy then takes an action and becomes Aware.
Now Boggle can hide from it again.
Later, Quiet as Dust allows his first Ambush to leave the enemy merely Aware rather than Suspicious. This creates a small tactical window where another hiding Trick can immediately restore Unaware, potentially enabling a second Ambush or another defensive Search.
Meanwhile, Practice Your Scream reduces the amount of Fright needed for the first Scare, while Bigger in Your Head or Fear of the Dark can preserve enough Fright to maintain later thresholds.
At this level, the player is no longer simply asking:
“Can I make this enemy Unaware?”
The questions become:
Should I make it Unaware now or wait until its stronger Attack?
Do I want it to Search, or is the Ambush more valuable?
Do I want Suspicious next turn because my hand rewards Suspicious?
Can I spend 6 Fright now and still reach 8 later?
Should I spend my Lurk on The Big One, or preserve it to power The Shadow Is Bigger?
Which enemy should become Suspicious so Bedframe Geography can hide me from another?
That is the intended mastery curve.
# Why the mechanics are not universally optimal
Unaware is powerful defense, but Suspicious prevents repeated lockdown and area attacks bypass it.
Ambush provides exceptional efficiency, but usually destroys the concealment that enabled it.
Fright can create tremendous value, but it has no passive benefit whatsoever.
Scare rewards accumulation, but spending Fright on one threshold can prevent another.
Lurk provides long term scaling, but it accumulates slowly, has a cap, and must often be spent to realize its strongest value.
Suspicious exploitation makes Boggle's vulnerable turns productive, but cards specialized around Suspicious can be weak when enemies are in other states.
This produces deliberate friction between the archetypes rather than allowing the player to simply take every Trick containing one favored keyword.
# Desired player experience
A new player thinks: “Hide from the scary Attack. Jump out when they aren't looking. Make them frightened. Save Lurk for a really enormous BOO!”
An experienced player thinks: “I need that enemy Unaware at the end of this turn, but I actually want the other one Suspicious. I can let the first Search to reach my Scare threshold, use the exposed turn to exploit Suspicious, preserve enough Fright for the next cycle, then convert Quiet as Dust into another concealment window before spending my Lurk.”
## Final structural verification
Basic starting deck: 10 Tricks, outside the reward pool.
Commons: 20, numbered 1 through 20.
Uncommons: 35, numbered 21 through 55.
Rares: 25, numbered 56 through 80.
Regular Trick pool: exactly 80.
Multiplayer only Tricks: exactly 5, consisting of 3 Uncommon and 2 Rare.
Regular numbering: continuous from 1 through 80 with no missing or duplicated numbers.
Defined unique systems: Awareness, Aware, Unaware, Search, Suspicious, Ambush, Fright, Scare, and Lurk.
Primary limitation on concealment: Suspicious prevents permanent hiding loops.
Mechanical distinction from Marmalade: Boggle manipulates enemy targeting state and alternating exposure windows rather than accumulating self contained hit negation.
Archetype bridges: Fright connects to Awareness, Search, Ambush, Suspicious, and Lurk; Lurk connects patient defense to burst offense; Ambush itself deliberately feeds the Suspicious archetype.
Rare pool: includes multiple genuine rule changing effects such as Fear of the Dark, Hide in Plain Sight, Nobody's Here, Bedframe Geography, Bigger in Your Head, You Didn't See Anything, and Good Night, Sleep Tight.
