# Mopsy, the Rag Doll Bunny
Mopsy is a small, well loved stuffed rabbit whose seams have been repaired so many times that nobody can tell which fabric was original anymore. The mansion did not merely bring her to life. It taught every patch, button, loose thread, and clump of stuffing to remember what it had once been attached to.
Mechanically, Mopsy treats her deck like a physical rag doll she is continually taking apart and sewing back together during combat.
Her defining question is not simply, “What Trick should I play?”
It is:
“Which Tricks are worth improving, how long should I preserve those improvements, when should I rip my deck apart, and how much of myself can I afford to unstuff?”
She is a medium complexity Companion at first and a very high complexity Companion at mastery.
## 1. Overall gameplay identity
Mopsy is a combat modification, controlled deck destruction, and reconstruction Companion.
She places temporary but persistent Patches onto Tricks. A Patch physically changes what that Trick does for the remainder of the combat, or until the Patch wears out.
She can also Tear Tricks out of her deck temporarily, then Mend them back later. This allows her to compress her deck, preserve modified cards, manipulate her draw cycle, and repeatedly reconstruct the exact deck she needs.
Her third major axis is Stuffing, a limited internal resource that serves both as crafting material and emergency protection. Spending Stuffing aggressively improves her turns, but leaving herself Hollow removes an important defensive safety mechanism.
The result should feel like playing with a rag doll whose limbs, stuffing, pockets, buttons, and patches are all being rearranged while the fight is happening.
2. Signature systemsStuffingMopsy begins each combat with 3 Stuffing.
Maximum Stuffing is 6 unless another effect changes it.
Stuffing persists between turns but resets between combats.
Cards can spend Stuffing for additional effects, convert it into card advantage, or deliberately empty Mopsy out.
### Cushion
Mopsy has an inherent defensive ability called Cushion.
Once during each enemy turn, when an enemy Attack would cause Mopsy to lose Courage after Guard is applied, she may spend 1 Stuffing to halve that Courage loss, rounding the remaining Courage loss up.
Example:
An Attack would deal 13 Courage damage after Guard.
Mopsy spends 1 Stuffing.
She loses 7 Courage instead.
Cushion is deliberately much better against one large hit than against many small hits.
This is important. Mopsy should not simply be another Companion who acquires a giant defensive resource and becomes impossible to hurt.
### Plump
Mopsy is Plump while she has 5 or 6 Stuffing.
Plump is a condition checked by Tricks, not a separate buff.
Plump decks tend to be safer, heavier, and able to convert accumulated Stuffing into large attacks or strong setup turns.
### Hollow
Mopsy is Hollow while she has 0 Stuffing.
Hollow is also a condition rather than a separate buff.
Hollow enables several powerful Tricks, but Mopsy cannot use Cushion while Hollow.
This creates an intentional temptation to empty herself for offensive or engine benefits.
3. PatchesA Patch is a temporary modification attached directly to a Trick.
Most Patch effects add an additional line of rules text to the modified Trick.
Unless otherwise specified:
A Trick can hold one Patch.
A newly applied Patch begins with 2 Stitches.
When its effect triggers, it loses 1 Stitch afterward.
When it reaches 0 Stitches, the Patch falls off.
The Patch remains attached while its Trick moves between hand, draw pile, discard pile, and Torn pile.
Patches disappear when combat ends.
Statuses, Curses, and temporary Tricks cannot normally receive Patches.
If a Trick has no available Patch slot, applying another Patch replaces one already attached unless the effect explicitly grants another slot.
Temporary copies of Tricks do not copy Patches, extra Patch slots, or Stitches unless specifically stated.
Most Patches trigger when their Trick is played.
Some explicitly have a different trigger.
### Reinforce
Reinforce adds 1 Stitch to a Patch.
A Patch can normally have no more than 4 Stitches.
If a Trick has multiple Patches, the player chooses which one to Reinforce unless otherwise specified.
### A Patch breaking
A Patch breaks when it reaches 0 Stitches and falls off.
Several Mopsy Tricks care specifically about a Patch breaking rather than simply being manually removed.
4. Tear and MendTearTo Tear a Trick is to move it to Mopsy's Torn pile for the remainder of combat until something Mends it.
A Torn Trick:
Cannot normally be drawn or played.
Keeps any Patches and Stitches attached to it.
Is not considered Vanished.
Returns to Mopsy's deck normally after combat.
Temporary Tricks Vanish if an effect would Tear them.
Tearing is therefore both deck thinning and storage.
Mopsy can remove weak Basics temporarily, hide an important patched Trick until she can recover it, or Tear expensive cards to fuel other effects.
## Mend
To Mend a Trick is to return it from the Torn pile to the zone specified by the effect.
If no destination is specified, it returns to the discard pile.
To prevent trivial recursion loops, the same physical Trick cannot be Mended more than once during the same turn unless a card explicitly overrides this rule.
5. ScrapSome Tricks generate a temporary Trick called Scrap.
Scrap
Type: Skill
Cost: 0 Nerve
Choose one:
Gain 1 Stuffing.
Or Reinforce a Patch in your hand by 1.
Vanish.
Scrap connects the Stuffing and Patch systems without making either one self sustaining for free.
6. Major strengthsMopsy has excellent long combat scaling because individual Tricks can become significantly better as the fight continues.
She is unusually good at controlling the composition of her active deck through Tear and Mend.
She adapts well to what the player actually finds during an expedition because Patches can turn otherwise ordinary Tricks into important engine pieces.
Cushion gives her reliable protection against dangerous single attacks without requiring her entire deck to consist of Guard Tricks.
Her strongest engines cross archetype boundaries naturally. Tear produces Stuffing. Stuffing powers Patches. Patches create reasons to Mend particular Tricks. Mend can preserve or exploit valuable Patches.
7. Major weaknessesMopsy requires setup.
A Patch that never gets drawn again was a poor investment.
A heavily modified Trick can become awkward if enemies change behavior or a fight ends quickly.
Patches deteriorate, forcing the player to decide between Reinforcing them and allowing them to break.
Tearing too many Tricks can leave Mopsy with a tiny but dysfunctional deck.
Spending Stuffing offensively weakens Cushion.
Remaining Plump is safe but can waste potential resource generation.
Remaining Hollow enables strong effects but leaves Mopsy vulnerable.
Cushion naturally struggles against repeated attacks and encounters containing several attacking enemies.
Retaining important Tricks can clog the hand.
A deck focused too heavily on one incredible patched Trick can suffer badly when that Trick is buried in the draw pile.
8. Primary deck archetypesPatchwork ToolboxModify many different Tricks with different Patches.
The player gradually turns an ordinary deck into a collection of specialized tools.
Especially compatible with Pattern Book, The Whole Pattern, Button Box, and Grand Refit.
## Masterpiece
Rather than modifying everything, create one or two extremely valuable Tricks with multiple Patch slots.
Retain them, Reinforce them, Tear them for safekeeping, Mend them, and repeatedly exploit them.
Room for One More and Master Seamstress are major enablers.
## Tear and Mend
Use the Torn pile as a second deck.
Remove Basics and situational cards, exploit Tear bonuses, then recover exactly the Trick needed.
This strategy becomes particularly powerful with Ship of Mopsy.
## Stuffing Oscillation
Deliberately move between Plump and Hollow.
Plump provides safety and stored potential.
Hollow provides riskier offensive and engine bonuses.
The player repeatedly fills and empties Mopsy rather than simply maximizing Stuffing.
## Rag Doll Endurance
Use Cushion, controlled Courage loss, Patch reinforcement, and efficient reconstruction to survive through awkward fights.
This is not intended to become a conventional Guard stacking archetype. Its defense is based on deciding which damage is worth absorbing and what resources should be sacrificed to survive it.

## Common design function
The Common pool deliberately does not hand the player a complete archetype.
It establishes the vocabulary.
Quick Patch and Pocket Patch introduce modification.
Running Stitch and Neat Hem teach Patch preservation.
Snip Snip and Find That Piece establish temporary deck destruction and reconstruction.
Stuff It Back In, Shake Out the Fluff, and Unstuff Yourself establish Stuffing as something that should circulate rather than simply remain full.
Cushion Check reveals Mopsy's particular vulnerability to repeated hits.
Temporary Fix shows that Patches can change fundamental card properties rather than merely append damage.

# STRATEGY ANALYSIS
## 1. Patchwork Toolbox
### Core strategy
Patch a broad selection of Tricks rather than constructing one centerpiece.
A modest Attack becomes a defensive card.
A defensive Skill becomes a draw engine.
An inexpensive Trick becomes an excellent carrier for Lucky Button.
The player continuously evaluates not only the original Trick, but how often that Trick is likely to cycle back before its Patch breaks.
### Important mechanics
Quick Patch
Pocket Patch
Quilted Lining
Lucky Button
Weighted Hem
Pattern Book
Button Box
The Whole Pattern
Heirloom Quilt
### Typical strengths
Extremely adaptable.
Strong in long encounters.
Less dependent on drawing one specific rare card.
Can turn mediocre reward picks into useful engine components.
The Whole Pattern gives a substantial reward for intentionally maintaining Patch diversity.
### Typical weaknesses
Requires considerable setup Nerve.
Some Patches may end up attached to Tricks that are drawn at bad moments.
Patch durability becomes a real constraint.
A deck containing too many Patch applicators and too few useful Patch carriers can spend several turns accomplishing very little.
### Hybridization
Toolbox decks naturally incorporate Tear and Mend because an especially valuable patched Trick becomes worth recovering deliberately.
They also appreciate Stuffing generation because Scrap can Reinforce important Patches.
# 2. The Masterpiece
### Core strategy
Create one extraordinary Trick rather than modifying ten average ones.
Double Stitch, Room for One More, and Master Seamstress let a single Trick carry several modifications.
Secret Pocket can keep it available.
Mend With Love can recover it after intentional Tearing.
Thirty Two Tiny Stitches can become absurdly strong if the player successfully constructs it as a multi Patch centerpiece.
### Typical centerpiece
Imagine Thirty Two Tiny Stitches carrying:
A Velvet Heart Patch
A Lucky Button Patch
A Pocket Patch
The card now attacks repeatedly while simultaneously contributing defense, Scrap generation, and draw.
But every use consumes the finite Stitches maintaining that machine.
The player has constructed a temporary engine rather than obtained one automatically.
### Typical strengths
Enormous concentration of value.
Excellent use of Reinforce effects.
Strong interaction with cost reduction and Retain.
Very satisfying long term combat construction.
### Typical weaknesses
Draw dependency.
Heavy setup.
Requires Patch slots.
One poorly timed use can consume several valuable Stitches at once.
The deck can become structurally dependent upon a single card.
### Hybridization
Masterpiece decks are especially compatible with Tear and Mend.
Tearing the masterpiece can actually be desirable because the Torn pile functions as a safe storage area until the player is ready to retrieve it.
Emergency Transplant can even transfer a valuable Patch from a disposable Trick onto the masterpiece.
# 3. Tear and Mend
### Core strategy
Treat the Torn pile as an actively managed secondary deck.
Remove weak cards.
Store cards temporarily.
Generate Stuffing.
Trigger Tear effects.
Recover exactly the cards needed later.
### Important mechanics
Snip Snip
Find That Piece
Seam Ripper
Mend and Maul
Mend With Love
Ripcord
Rag Bag
Perfect Repair
Rabbit of Theseus
Ship of Mopsy
### Typical strengths
Excellent deck compression.
Very good control over repeated draw cycles.
Can temporarily eliminate undesirable Basics.
Can isolate a small set of highly efficient Tricks.
Provides a natural way to exploit patched cards repeatedly.
### Typical weaknesses
Over Tearing is dangerous.
A player can remove so many Tricks that they no longer possess enough defense, attacks, or playable cards.
Mend cards can become dead draws if nothing worthwhile is Torn.
Tear effects can be poor in very short encounters where the deck never completes another meaningful cycle.
### Hybridization
Nearly every Mopsy build can benefit from some Tear and Mend infrastructure.
Patchwork decks want to retrieve modified cards.
Stuffing decks can convert Torn cards into additional Stuffing.
Masterpiece decks use the Torn pile as storage.
Ship of Mopsy transforms Tear into an entirely different strategy by letting Mopsy remove the original card while temporarily using a disposable copy.
4. Stuffing OscillationCore strategyDo not try to remain Plump forever.
Do not try to remain Hollow forever.
Exploit the transition.
A typical sequence might be:
Begin at 3 Stuffing.
Use Stuff It Back In to become Plump.
Use discounted Flop With Confidence.
Use Stuffed to Bursting and immediately become Hollow.
Use Hollow Heel Kick.

The resource repeatedly travels from full to empty and back again.

Why none of the systems should be universally optimalPatching everything is not automatically correctEvery Patch costs a card, Nerve, or opportunity.
Patch durability is limited.
The combat must last long enough for a modified Trick to cycle repeatedly.
Some Tricks are terrible Patch targets.
## Reinforcing everything is not automatically correct
Reinforce effects occupy deck space that could instead advance the fight.
Keeping a mediocre Patch alive forever is worse than allowing it to break and replacing it.
Loose Ends and Heirloom Quilt can actively reward letting Patches break.
## Tearing everything is not automatically correct
Deck compression becomes self destructive beyond a certain point.
Torn defensive cards cannot help when Mopsy suddenly needs them.
Torn payoff cards require Mend access.
## Staying Plump is not automatically correct
Plump preserves Cushion and supports some powerful Tricks, but the player forfeits Hollow bonuses and may waste further Stuffing generation.
## Staying Hollow is not automatically correct
Hollow turns can be extremely efficient, particularly with Threadbare and Thriving, but Mopsy loses her signature large hit protection.
## Building one masterpiece is not automatically correct
Multi Patch cards are extremely efficient targets for Reinforcement, but concentrating the entire engine on one card creates draw dependency.
A distributed Patchwork deck is slower but more resilient.

This deck is not purely a Patch deck, Tear deck, or Hollow deck.
The player might put several Patches onto Rabbit of Theseus.
Then Tear it with Ripcord to gain Stuffing.
Ship of Mopsy creates an unmodified temporary copy, allowing Mopsy to attack without consuming the original card's valuable Patches.
On a later turn, Mend With Love retrieves the original Rabbit.
Because it was Mended, Rabbit of Theseus generates another temporary copy when played.
The patched original triggers Pattern Book and consumes its finite Patch durability.
If a Patch breaks, Loose Ends converts the breakage into draw and Scrap.
The Scrap can Reinforce another Patch or restore Stuffing.
Mopsy can then deliberately spend herself down to Hollow.
Threadbare and Thriving amplifies the next patched Trick.
Stuffing Exchange eventually refills her.
The deck continuously changes state.
Cards move from normal deck to Torn pile and back.
Patches migrate.
Stitches disappear and are repaired.
Stuffing rises and falls.
The correct play depends heavily on what the player expects to need several turns later.
That is the intended high mastery Mopsy experience.
# Desired player experience
A new player thinks:
“I can sew useful bonuses onto my best cards, and if I run out of Stuffing I get fragile.”
An intermediate player thinks:
“I should Patch the Tricks I expect to play repeatedly, Tear the cards I do not currently need, and keep enough Stuffing available for dangerous enemy turns.”
An experienced player thinks:
“My draw pile, discard pile, Torn pile, Patch durability, Patch slots, Stuffing total, enemy intent, and future Mend targets are all parts of the same machine. Sometimes I should deliberately break a Patch. Sometimes I should Tear my strongest Trick. Sometimes taking Courage damage is better than spending Stuffing. I am not maintaining a deck. I am continually rebuilding one.”
# Verification
Common Tricks: 20.
Uncommon Tricks: 35.
Rare Tricks: 25.
Regular Tricks: exactly 80, numbered continuously from 1 through 80.
Basic starting deck: 10 Tricks, separate from the reward pool.
Multiplayer only Tricks: exactly 5, consisting of 3 Uncommon and 2 Rare.
Explicit Mopsy mechanics: Stuffing, Cushion, Plump, Hollow, Patch, Stitches, Reinforce, Tear, Mend, Torn pile, Scrap.
Primary archetypes: Patchwork Toolbox, Masterpiece, Tear and Mend, Stuffing Oscillation, Rag Doll Endurance.
Mopsy has substantial mechanical weaknesses, particularly setup time, Patch deterioration, Stuffing tension, multi hit vulnerability, Tear overextension, and dependence on correctly selecting modification targets.
Her core defensive system is materially different from Marmalade's Ghoststep. Cushion mitigates one damaging hit by consuming a resource and still allows Courage loss. It does not negate successive hits.
Her central card modification and deck reconstruction systems have no functional equivalent in Marmalade's Ghoststep, Haunt, Nine Lives, Zoomies, or Untouched gameplay.
Multiple cards deliberately bridge archetypes, especially Running Stitch, Ripcord, Mend With Love, Stuffing Exchange, Well Loved, Rabbit of Theseus, Emergency Transplant, Ship of Mopsy, Heart on Her Sleeve, and Threadbare and Thriving.
