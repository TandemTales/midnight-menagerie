# Hush, the Shadow Ferret
Hush is the Menagerie Companion for players who like hiding useful things, rearranging their deck into unnatural shapes, and suddenly producing exactly the wrong object for the enemy at exactly the right moment.
He is not primarily an evasion character. Hush does not become hard to hit merely because he is stealthy. His stealth is offensive and logistical. He disappears so that he can prepare an Ambush, protect hidden Tricks from normal cycling, and quietly rearrange his resources while enemies are busy looking in the wrong places.
His central gameplay question is:
What should be in my hand, what should be in my deck, and what should be hidden in the walls right now?
## 1. Overall gameplay identity
Hush is a zone manipulation, preparation, theft, and sudden burst archetype.
His Shadow Pocket functions almost like a second hand, but one with very limited capacity. Tricks can be hidden there for future turns, pulled out when needed, exchanged with other zones, or used to trigger Ambush effects.
His strongest turns are rarely created by simply drawing well. They are created several turns earlier by deliberately placing the correct Tricks where he will need them.
Hush rewards:
Planning one or two turns ahead.
Preserving specific Tricks without ordinary Retain effects.
Rearranging the draw pile, discard pile, hand, and Shadow Pocket.
Entering Unseen at the correct moment.
Creating one explosive Ambush rather than simply playing every Attack available.
Reading enemy intent to steal useful temporary tools.
Treating card location as an actual resource.
He should feel like a ferret that has somehow hidden six household objects in three impossible locations.
## 2. Major strengths
Exceptional card access
Hush can isolate important Tricks from normal deck cycling and retrieve them later.
Powerful planned turns
A carefully prepared Shadow Pocket can turn a mediocre turn into an exceptional one.
Flexible responses
Pilfer gives Hush defensive, offensive, and utility options based on enemy behavior.
Strong tactical burst
Ambush Tricks can achieve excellent efficiency once Hush successfully establishes Unseen.
Unusual deck control
Hush manipulates card location far more aggressively than most Companions.
Good resistance to awkward draws
Important cards can be stored rather than discarded simply because they appeared at the wrong time.
## 3. Major weaknesses
Limited immediate reliability
Hush often needs one turn to prepare what another Companion can do immediately.
Shadow Pocket congestion
Three spaces disappear very quickly. Storing the wrong Tricks can make subsequent effects substantially worse.
Unseen is not invulnerability
Enemies still hit Hush normally. If enemy Attack damage reaches Courage, Hush becomes Seen.
Ambush is bottlenecked
Normally only one Attack can receive an Ambush before Hush becomes Seen again.
Poor play can sabotage future draws
Moving Tricks around without understanding the deck's upcoming sequence can create awful future hands.
Pilfer depends on enemy behavior
Certain bosses and unusual enemies produce less useful intent patterns.
Several engines are opportunity cost engines
A Trick hidden in the Shadow Pocket is not cycling through the deck. This is beneficial only if Hush actually intends to use it.
# 4. Signature mechanics
## Shadow Pocket
Hush has a separate combat zone called the Shadow Pocket.
Its default capacity is 3 Tricks.
Tricks in the Shadow Pocket:
Remain there between turns.
Do not count toward hand size.
Are not discarded at the end of Hush's turn.
Can normally be played directly from the Shadow Pocket by paying their normal Nerve cost.
Go to the normal destination after being played unless another effect says otherwise.
If the Shadow Pocket is full, additional Tricks cannot enter it.
The player can inspect the Shadow Pocket at any time.
This is not a second unlimited hand. The three card capacity is one of Hush's fundamental constraints.
## Stash
Stash means:
Move a specified Trick from your hand into the Shadow Pocket.
Stashing is not playing the Trick.
A Stashed Trick retains combat specific modifications unless another rule explicitly removes them.
## Scurry
A Scurry occurs whenever an effect explicitly moves one of Hush's non temporary Tricks directly between the following zones without playing it:
Hand
Draw pile
Discard pile
Shadow Pocket
Normal drawing is not a Scurry.
Normal end of turn discarding is not a Scurry.
A played Trick naturally entering the discard pile is not a Scurry.
If one effect moves three eligible Tricks, that produces three Scurries.
This allows Hush decks to care not merely about drawing and discarding cards, but about physically relocating them.
## Unseen
Hush begins combat Seen.
Certain Tricks make Hush Unseen.
Being Unseen does not inherently prevent damage.
Hush remains Unseen until one of the following occurs:
Hush loses Courage from enemy Attack damage.
Hush plays an Attack from anywhere other than the Shadow Pocket. Hush becomes Seen before that Attack resolves.
Hush plays an Attack from the Shadow Pocket. The Attack resolves first, including any Ambush effect, then Hush becomes Seen.
If Guard absorbs all incoming enemy Attack damage, Hush remains Unseen.
Skills and Powers do not normally make Hush Seen.
This distinction is crucial. Hush hides to prepare an attack, not simply to dodge one.
## Ambush
A Trick with Ambush has additional text that activates when the Trick is played from the Shadow Pocket while Hush is Unseen.
Example:
Ambush: Deal additional moderate damage.
After an Attack resolves its Ambush, Hush normally becomes Seen.
A Skill with Ambush does not automatically make Hush Seen.
Several Rare Tricks can bend this rule.
## Pilfer
Pilfer an enemy means:
Inspect that enemy's current intent and create the corresponding temporary Contraband Trick directly in the Shadow Pocket.
Pilfer requires an open Shadow Pocket slot unless the Trick producing the Pilfer says otherwise.

# 5. Primary deck archetypes
Hush has five major strategic families.
### Ambush Hush
Stores specialized Attacks, becomes Unseen, and converts one carefully selected Attack into an extremely efficient strike.
### Pocket Architect
Treats the Shadow Pocket as a persistent tactical inventory, expanding its capacity and continually exchanging cards between zones.
### Scurry Engine
Moves Tricks constantly, extracting Guard, damage, card access, and other benefits from relocation.
### Little Thief
Uses Pilfer and Contraband to exploit enemy intent and generate adaptable zero cost tools.
### Hallway Phantom
Attempts to remain Unseen across turns through Guard and careful Attack timing, converting stealth preservation into Nerve and repeated setup opportunities.
The best decks frequently combine at least two.
A pure Ambush deck may lack enough card manipulation to find its setup.
A pure Scurry deck can spend entire turns rearranging itself without killing anything.
A pure Pilfer deck can become dependent on enemy intent.
A pure stealth deck can invest too heavily in staying Unseen without possessing sufficiently valuable Ambush payoffs.

### Common pool purpose
The Commons establish four fundamental habits.
Move cards intentionally.
Make Room, Laundry Chute, Found It, and False Trail introduce Scurry without requiring an engine.
Treat the Pocket as valuable space.
Furniture Gap Lunge wants it full. Pocket This Too wants to fill it. Make Room prevents it from becoming uselessly clogged.
Unseen requires preparation.
Behind the Curtain and Nobody Saw That provide access without making stealth automatic.
Pilfer is optional rather than mandatory.
Sticky Fingers introduces the mechanic, but Hush does not need Pilfer to function.

### Uncommon ecosystem
This is where Hush branches substantially.
Pocket Rotation, Quick Change, Stuff Behind the Sofa, and Hidey Hole can produce a dedicated movement engine.
Quiet Theft, Kleptomaniac, and Inside Job can produce a Contraband engine.
Light Sleeper, Crawlspace, and No Fixed Address support multi turn stealth.
Ceiling Drop, Behind You, and Soft Footfalls create a traditional Ambush package.
Several cards deliberately bridge those packages.
Stolen Momentum converts Pilfer into Pocket movement.
Inside Job converts Contraband into Scurry.
No Fixed Address turns Pocket management into Unseen.
Crawlspace turns Unseen into persistent Pocket value.
That interconnection is intentional.

### Typical strengths
Extremely consistent once established.

Exceptional deck control.

Rather than possessing a fixed answer to everything, Hush steals whatever type of tool the room happens to provide.

Crawlspace becomes reusable.
No Fixed Address provides repeatable access to Unseen.
The House Has Corners makes a prepared Pocket function as automatic concealment.

This archetype nearly always needs another package.
Hallway Phantom plus Ambush is the obvious pairing.
Hallway Phantom plus Pilfer creates a strange control deck where Hush spends long stretches stealing and rearranging things before suddenly attacking.
ADVANCED HYBRID EXAMPLEConsider a deck containing:
Hidey Hole
Hallway Phantom
Quick Change
Quiet Theft
Inside Job
No Fixed Address
Ceiling Drop
Gone Before the Squeak
Every Door Is Mine
Now You Don't
This is not really an Ambush deck, a Pilfer deck, or a Scurry deck.
It is all three.
A late combat turn might function like this:
Quick Change rotates several Tricks through the Shadow Pocket, creating multiple Scurries.
Hallway Phantom converts the first Scurry into Guard.
Quiet Theft Pilfers an enemy.
The generated Contraband is played.
Inside Job moves another Trick from the discard pile into the Shadow Pocket.
Additional movement increases Every Door Is Mine.
Hush becomes Unseen.
Gone Before the Squeak Ambushes from the Pocket.
Gone Before the Squeak restores Unseen.
Ceiling Drop performs a second Ambush.
Subsequent Pocket manipulation empties the Pocket.
Now You Don't restores Unseen, draws two Tricks, and returns Nerve.
Every Door Is Mine converts the accumulated movement into the turn's final attack sequence.
That is the intended high mastery Hush experience.
The player is not merely asking:
What is my strongest card?
They are asking:
Where must every card be located six actions from now for the entire sequence to work?
WHY THE MECHANICS ARE NOT UNIVERSALLY OPTIMALStashing is not automatically goodEvery Trick stored in the Shadow Pocket is removed from ordinary deck cycling.
Stashing a broadly useful defensive Trick may make subsequent turns less safe.
## A full Pocket is both beneficial and dangerous
Several effects reward a full Pocket.
Pilfer requires free space.
Movement effects often require free space.
A player who mindlessly fills the Pocket can disable a significant portion of Hush's deck.
## Scurry consumes tempo
Moving three Tricks can generate excellent synergies, but the movement itself frequently deals no damage.
A Scurry deck without adequate payoff becomes an elaborate sorting machine.
## Unseen does not protect Hush by itself
This is critical to separating Hush from Marmalade.
Marmalade's Ghoststep directly prevents enemy hits.
Hush's Unseen is primarily an offensive state.
If Hush wants to preserve it through an enemy turn, he must still solve the incoming damage.
## Ambush naturally limits itself
The first normal Attack Ambush makes Hush Seen.
A deck cannot simply put five Ambush Attacks in the Pocket and automatically receive five bonuses.
Rare effects can bend this limitation, but doing so requires substantial deck investment.
## Pilfer is contextual
Pilfer against an attacking enemy provides offense.
Pilfer against a defending enemy provides defense.
The player does not receive whichever Contraband they would prefer.
Enemy intent therefore matters.
RARE DESIGN GOALSThe Rare pool deliberately contains several Tricks that alter Hush's fundamental rules.
Bigger on the Inside changes the basic Pocket constraint.
Sticky Little Legend changes temporary Contraband into reusable resources with escalating costs.
Now You See Me changes the one Ambush per stealth cycle rule.
Now You Don't rewards deliberately emptying a zone that many other cards reward filling.
Switcheroo temporarily breaks Shadow Pocket capacity.
Three Places at Once turns card movement into temporary duplication.
The House Has Corners changes Unseen from an activated state into a consequence of maintaining a hidden inventory.
These should create decks that genuinely operate differently after acquisition rather than simply making existing numbers larger.
PLAYER EXPERIENCE TARGETA new player thinks:“I can hide my good Attack in my pocket, disappear, and jump out with it later.”
That is enough to play Hush competently.
### An experienced player thinks:
“I need Ceiling Drop in the Pocket, Crawlspace available after the Ambush, one open Pocket slot for Pilfer, two Scurries before I play Every Door Is Mine, and I need to empty the Pocket afterward so Now You Don't puts me Unseen again before the next sequence.”
That is Hush's mastery layer.
The fantasy is not simply being sneaky.
It is the increasingly alarming realization that Hush knows where everything in the mansion is because he personally put half of it there.
