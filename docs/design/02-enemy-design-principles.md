# Enemy design principles across all regions

_Source: Midnight Menagerie Design.docx, lines 11870-12425_

## 1. Overall enemy design structure
Within each region, I would divide the six ordinary enemies conceptually into:
Two introductory enemies Simple patterns that establish the region's combat language.
Two interaction enemies Support units or enemies whose behavior becomes more dangerous when paired with something else.
Two pressure enemies More threatening opponents used primarily in later Scuffles and difficult combinations.
Then each region receives three Big Scares with very different deck tests.
One might test burst damage.
One might test defense and endurance.
One might test adaptability, deck manipulation, targeting, or another unusual mechanic.
The region boss then combines several ideas without simply being a gigantic version of one normal enemy.
Importantly, the region's associated Companion should have some thematic resonance with these mechanics, but should not simply hard counter them.
For example, Marmalade might be particularly good at elegantly handling one of the Butler's attack patterns, but bringing Marmalade should not make the Forgotten Foyer trivial.
2. The Forgotten FoyerCompanion: Marmalade Boss: The Butler
### Combat identity
The house is trying to determine whether you are a guest, an intruder, or something that needs to be put back where it belongs.
Enemies emphasize interruptions, reactive attacks, opening turns, evasion, and changing intentions.
### Scuffle enemies
Dust Bunny
A ridiculous animated clump of dust that grows whenever ignored. Individually harmless, but gains strength each turn it remains alive.
Coatrack Crawler
An animated coat stand with too many wooden legs. Alternates between Guard and lunging attacks.
Lost Luggage
Possessed suitcase that periodically stuffs Junk Tricks into the draw pile before snapping shut behind heavy Guard.
Calling Bell
Tiny support enemy. Rings to strengthen other enemies, change their intentions, or summon household assistance.
Red Carpet Runner
The carpet itself rolls violently across the room. Telegraphs enormous attacks but takes time to prepare them.
Door Greeter
Animated front door that can temporarily prevent certain Tricks from being played twice in succession, representing the house insisting on proper behavior.
### Big Scares
The Grand Coatcheck
A mass of coats, hats, umbrellas, and hangers. Constantly changes what defensive property it is wearing.
The Unwelcome Guest
A faceless apparition that becomes more dangerous when the player repeats the same type of action turn after turn.
The House Bell
A massive brass bell. Periodically calls lesser Foyer enemies into combat and must be managed before the room becomes overwhelmed.
### Boss: The Butler
Immaculate supernatural servant who genuinely believes he is keeping the mansion orderly.
His battle revolves around house rules.
He announces temporary rules such as:
“Guests do not rush.”
“Guests wait their turn.”
“Guests wipe their feet.”
Violating a rule has consequences, but sometimes deliberately breaking one is strategically correct.
This gives him personality and creates a reactive battle appropriate to Marmalade.
3. The Forgotten NurseryCompanion: Mopsy Boss: The Governess
### Combat identity
Repair, damage transfer, toys, stuffing, attachment, and enemies that alter each other.
### Scuffle enemies
Button Baby
Small stuffed creature that attaches buttons to allies, granting temporary buffs.
Jack in the Box
Alternates between being harmlessly closed and exploding into a dangerous attack.
Patchwork Soldier
Repairs damaged allies using scraps of its own Courage.
Rocking Horse
Builds momentum every turn until interrupted.
Blanket Blob
Covers another enemy, granting it Guard while becoming vulnerable itself.
Porcelain Doll
Cracks when damaged. Each crack changes its attack pattern, making it more dangerous but increasingly fragile.
### Big Scares
The Toy Chest
Starts closed and gradually releases forgotten toys unless destroyed or controlled.
The Patchwork Giant
A huge stuffed creature composed of mismatched toys. When sections are damaged, it changes abilities.
The Porcelain Twins
Two dolls that continually transfer buffs, Guard, and harmful effects between themselves.
### Boss: The Governess
A stern supernatural caretaker obsessed with keeping everything pristine.
She repeatedly repairs enemies, repairs herself, patches over negative effects, and attempts to "fix" the player's Tricks.
Her encounter explores modification and repair in a way that echoes Mopsy without requiring Mopsy.
4. The Sleeping QuartersCompanion: Boggle Boss: The Bedframe Beast
### Combat identity
Fear, hiding, ambushes, uncertainty, large telegraphed attacks, and enemies disappearing from danger.
### Scuffle enemies
Pillow Puff
Weak enemy that inflicts Drowsy style temporary status Tricks.
Slipper Skitter
Tiny animated slippers that attack repeatedly and retreat behind stronger enemies.
Wardrobe Guest
Hides inside a wardrobe every few turns, becoming temporarily difficult to target.
Blanket Creeper
Covers itself in layers of Guard that must be removed.
Nightlight Snuffer
Support enemy that changes enemy behavior whenever the room becomes "dark."
Thing Beneath
Rarely attacks, but builds toward one enormous scare.
### Big Scares
The Night Terror
Changes intentions after the player commits resources, making prediction difficult.
The Blanket Hydra
Every damaged blanket lump begins acting independently until properly dealt with.
The Wardrobe
An enormous wardrobe containing several possible enemies. The player does not initially know what is inside.
### Boss: The Bedframe Beast
The entire bed twists into a massive creature.
It hides portions of itself, retreats beneath blankets, launches enormous ambush attacks, and forces the player to decide when to attack and when to prepare.
5. The Kitchens and CellarsCompanion: Taffy Boss: The Confectioner
### Combat identity
Enemies combine, divide, transform, consume each other, and create strange temporary substances.
### Scuffle enemies
Dough Blob
Expands when not damaged and can divide into smaller Doughlings.
Jam Jar
Sticky support creature that makes Tricks temporarily harder to cycle.
Kitchen Imp
Tiny animated utensil creature with fast repeated attacks.
Candy Clump
Absorbs buffs from defeated allies.
Pantry Mimic
Disguises itself as a harmless reward before revealing itself in combat encounters.
Rising Batter
Starts weak but grows rapidly every turn.
### Big Scares
The Oven Maw
Periodically "bakes" enemies or statuses, transforming them into stronger forms.
The Sugar Golem
Breaks into several dangerous pieces rather than simply dying.
The Pantry Poltergeist
Animates multiple kitchen objects at once and continuously changes the composition of the battle.
### Boss: The Confectioner
Creates edible supernatural minions, melts them back together, divides them again, and feeds failed creations into stronger ones.
Very appropriate to Taffy's transformation identity.
6. The Impossible GreenhouseCompanion: Brambleboo Boss: The Head Gardener
### Combat identity
Growth, battlefield accumulation, roots, spreading effects, and escalating enemies.
### Scuffle enemies
Potling
Tiny walking flowerpot that plants Seeds.
Snapping Blossom
Telegraphs powerful attacks while gradually opening its enormous flower.
Creeping Ivy
Attaches itself to another enemy and strengthens it.
Spore Puff
Creates delayed effects that trigger several turns later.
Topiary Beast
Changes shape between offensive and defensive forms.
Glassvine
Fragile but dangerous plant that retaliates when attacked.
### Big Scares
The Compost Colossus
Regenerates unless particular growths are removed.
The Carnivorous Conservatory
A stationary mass of plants that steadily takes over the battlefield.
The Ancient Topiary
Cycles between several animal shaped combat forms.
### Boss: The Head Gardener
Treats combat as gardening.
Plants enemies.
Prunes enemies.
Transplants buffs.
Roots the player.
Accelerates growth.
At later stages, his carefully controlled garden begins growing beyond even his control.
7. The Mansion GraveyardCompanion: Mossbit Boss: The Groundskeeper of Names
### Combat identity
Patience, countdowns, delayed retaliation, inevitability, memory, and enemies that become threatening if rushed or ignored.
### Scuffle enemies
Grave Moth
Marks the player with effects that trigger several turns later.
Headstone Hopper
Extremely defensive enemy that retaliates after accumulating damage.
Mourning Candle
Support enemy that grows stronger whenever another enemy disappears.
Name Gnawer
Removes temporary buffs or manipulates Trick costs.
Forget Me Not
Tiny flower spirit that returns once after being defeated unless certain conditions are met.
Epitaph Spirit
Announces what it will do several turns ahead.
### Big Scares
The Mourning Angel
Alternates long periods of stillness with devastating turns.
The Epitaph Choir
Several gravestones whose delayed effects resolve in sequence.
The Mausoleum Mouth
Slowly opens over several turns, producing increasingly dangerous consequences.
### Boss: The Groundskeeper of Names
Maintains the cemetery's records and believes forgotten creatures belong to the mansion.
He places Epitaphs that predict future events.
The fight becomes a planning puzzle several turns into the future.
8. The Grand Study and LibraryCompanion: Crinkle Boss: The Archivist
### Combat identity
Trick manipulation, copying, alternate forms, information, and rules written directly onto cards.
### Scuffle enemies
Book Bat
Flying animated book that repeatedly cycles back into action.
Inkblot
Copies the last offensive effect used against it in simplified form.
Paper Knight
Changes between folded offensive and defensive forms.
Bookmark Imp
Marks Tricks and reacts when those Tricks are played.
Quill Clerk
Writes temporary status Tricks into the deck.
Index Beast
Changes behavior according to which Trick type the player used most recently.
### Big Scares
The Bookwyrm
Consumes Tricks temporarily and gains abilities based on what it swallowed.
The Living Index
Categorizes the player's actions and rewards or punishes patterns.
The Inkblot Oracle
Creates distorted copies of the player's own Tricks as enemy effects.
### Boss: The Archivist
Treats everything as information to be catalogued.
He copies, annotates, seals, revises, and occasionally "corrects" Tricks.
9. The Moonlit Attic and ObservatoryCompanion: Wink Boss: The Watcher in the Rafters
### Combat identity
Prediction, intent manipulation, traps, preparation, and hidden information.
### Scuffle enemies
Rafter Peeker
Its intended action changes if attacked.
Star Chart
Support enemy that predicts and strengthens future enemy actions.
Telescope Eye
Studies the player for several turns before exploiting repeated behavior.
Cobweb Bundle
Creates sticky temporary effects that slow Trick cycling.
Moon Moth
Flutters between harmless and dangerous states based on the current turn.
Orrery Imp
Rotates enemy intentions around the group.
### Big Scares
The Great Orrery
Runs through a predictable sequence of celestial phases with powerful consequences.
The Rafter Seer
Displays several possible intentions, only revealing the true one later.
The Moon Lens
Magnifies one enemy at a time, making that enemy dramatically stronger.
### Boss: The Watcher in the Rafters
Knows what the player is planning.
His encounter revolves around revealed future actions, false predictions, and manipulating intent information.
10. The LampworksCompanion: Wisp Boss: The Lamplighter
### Combat identity
Stored power, ignition, delayed explosions, charge states, light and darkness.
### Scuffle enemies
Waxling
Slowly melts, becoming stronger offensively but less durable.
Lamp Moth
Consumes light from allies to empower itself.
Spark Sprite
Stores charge before releasing a large attack.
Gaslight Ghost
Alternates between illuminated and hidden forms.
Candle Cluster
Multiple flames function as individual charges on one enemy.
Blackout Blob
Suppresses beneficial temporary effects while active.
### Big Scares
The Chandelier
Several independently acting lights share one body.
The Blackout Beast
Changes dramatically when the room loses illumination.
The Great Lantern
Stores enormous amounts of energy before releasing it.
### Boss: The Lamplighter
Controls which parts of the battlefield are illuminated and stores supernatural flame inside lamps around the arena.
Very strong thematic parallel to Wisp's delayed power mechanics.
11. The Ballroom and Velvet SuitesCompanion: Count Crumbula Boss: The Master of Revels
### Combat identity
Temptation, exchange, Courage manipulation, buffs with costs, and increasingly extravagant combat.
### Scuffle enemies
Dancing Shoe
Gets faster every turn it remains alive.
Masquerade Mask
Copies beneficial effects from allies.
Velvet Curtain
Protects another enemy until opened.
Party Phantom
Offers tempting combat choices with consequences.
Goblet Geist
Steals Courage and redistributes it among enemies.
Waltzing Armor
Alternates between attacking and forcing another enemy to act.
### Big Scares
The Grand Masque
Changes identity and mechanics several times.
The Eternal Dance
A pair of enchanted suits that become stronger if their rhythm is not disrupted.
The Velvet Host
Invites the player into increasingly dangerous bargains.
### Boss: The Master of Revels
Treats the battle as a party the player is refusing to enjoy.
Offers tempting power at increasingly dangerous prices.
This provides a natural thematic connection to Crumbula's temptation mechanics.
12. The Crypt and OssuaryCompanion: Bones Boss: The Bone Curator

Discard, rebuilding, recursion, pieces moving between enemies, and things refusing to stay gone.
### Scuffle enemies

Tiny bone construct that returns if another enemy retrieves it.
Skull Roller
Repeatedly attacks and then becomes inactive until recovered.
Urn Spirit
Stores defeated enemy effects.
Ribcage Guard
Protects enemies behind itself.
Bone Heap

Retrieves discarded enemy effects.
### Big Scares

The Walking Ossuary
Constructs new attacks from defeated bone enemies.
The Coffin Collector
Temporarily removes portions of the player's deck and stores them.
### Boss: The Bone Curator
Obsessively collects and rearranges bones.
What gets discarded rarely stays discarded.
The battle becomes an elaborate recursion puzzle.

16. The Kennels and Animal WardCompanion: Pudding Boss: The Kennelmaster
This region requires special treatment.
The captive animals should generally not be enemies.
The antagonists here should primarily be the mansion's containment systems.
That reinforces the story instead of making the player beat up frightened animals they are supposedly rescuing.
### Combat identity
Protection, interception, restraint, targeting, guarding, and forcibly keeping creatures where they "belong."
### Scuffle enemies
Walking Cage
Protects another enemy by enclosing it.
Collar Keeper
Marks targets and manipulates who enemies attack.
Leash Hand
Restrains resources or limits actions temporarily.
Feeding Cart
Heals and strengthens containment enemies.
Ward Orderly
Animated uniform that repairs cages and equipment.
Comfort Blanket
A deceptively gentle looking construct that applies increasingly restrictive protective effects.
### Big Scares
The Collar Collector
Places different supernatural collars with different restrictions.
The Rolling Ward
A huge mobile collection of cages, blankets, bowls, and medical equipment.
The Perfect Pen
The room itself becomes a containment mechanism that progressively limits options.
### Boss: The Kennelmaster
One of the most narratively important regional bosses.
He genuinely believes no animal is safe unless it is contained.
His mechanics revolve around protecting things so aggressively that protection becomes imprisonment.
That makes him a smaller thematic reflection of the mansion itself.

18. The Heart of the HouseCompanion: None Final boss: Still provisional
This region should feel mechanically different from all sixteen home regions.
Instead of introducing an entirely unrelated enemy ecosystem, it should feel like the mansion has been watching everything the player has learned.
### Combat identity
Memory, protection, possession, echoes of previous regions, and the house directly intervening.
### Scuffle enemies
Housekeeper
A polished supernatural construct that combines simplified behaviors from several servant enemies.
Memory Animal
Not literally the original animal. It is a magical reconstruction created from the house's memories. Changes form during combat.
Sanctuary Warden
Grants enormous defensive effects to allies because it believes nothing should ever be harmed.
Namekeeper
Marks characters and Tricks as belonging to the house.
Quiet Room
An architectural enemy that suppresses particular actions while active.
House Pulse
A manifestation of the building itself that periodically modifies the entire combat.
Old Welcome
A strange friendly looking entity that continually offers increasingly restrictive "help."
Perfect Keeper
A late Heart enemy that protects another creature so aggressively that defeating it becomes a puzzle.
### Big Scares
The Hall of Names
The room itself attacks through names collected from previous inhabitants.
The House Remembers
Creates echoes of previously defeated bosses and enemies without recreating the complete encounters.
The Perfect Sanctuary
A horrifyingly pleasant room that attempts to prevent anyone inside from attacking, leaving, changing, or taking risks.
The Door That Says Stay
A major Heart encounter centered on the house physically refusing to permit departure.
### Final boss direction
I would not make the final boss simply "The Evil Heart."
The strongest current direction is that the final opponent is effectively the mansion's protective instinct given form.
Possible identities include:
The Caretaker
The intelligence through which the mansion expresses itself.
The Housemother
A supernatural manifestation of the mansion's belief that everyone is safer inside.
The Keeper
The force responsible for finding, sheltering, and eventually refusing to release lost creatures.
The Heart of the House
Not an anatomical heart, but a huge impossible sanctuary room whose architecture itself becomes the boss.
My preference conceptually is The Keeper, with the mansion itself becoming increasingly involved in the battle.
The final confrontation should ultimately ask:
Can you prove that caring for something does not require owning it?
That can influence both mechanics and story resolution.
# 19. The important part: encounters, not just enemies
The next design layer should not be another hundred enemy names.
The individual enemies above are the pieces.
What makes this style of deckbuilder work is how those pieces are assembled into encounters.
For example, Forgotten Foyer could have:
Early Scuffles
Dust Bunny
Coatrack Crawler
Two Dust Bunnies
Dust Bunny plus Calling Bell
Middle Scuffles
Lost Luggage plus Calling Bell
Coatrack Crawler plus Red Carpet Runner
Two Door Greeters
Dangerous Scuffles
Calling Bell plus Red Carpet Runner plus Dust Bunny
Lost Luggage plus Door Greeter
Two Coatrack Crawlers plus Calling Bell
The same six enemy designs therefore produce a much larger combat pool.
I would ultimately give every region roughly 10 to 14 authored Scuffle formations, divided between introductory and advanced pools.
