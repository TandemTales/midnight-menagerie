# Midnight Menagerie: Maya Chen and Orbit Complete Personal Investigation Design
## 1. Purpose of this section
This section defines Maya Chen's complete personal campaign arc concerning her missing cat, Orbit.
It is intended to establish the production standard that the other seven Kid investigations will follow.
It covers:
Maya's personal narrative.
Orbit's disappearance.
The actual truth of what happened to Orbit.
Orbit's supernatural transformation progression.
Investigation progression rules.
Key and optional evidence.
Exact story beats.
Dialogue.

Scuffle interactions.

Boss interactions.
Curiosity interactions.
Midnight Market interactions.
Blanket Fort scenes.
Treasure interactions.
Secret interactions.
Connector interactions.
Heart of the House content.

Multiplayer handling.

Post rescue content.
Permanent rewards.
Replay behavior.
Implementation rules required to prevent story progression from conflicting with roguelike procedural generation.
The overriding rule is that personal investigation content exists inside the established mansion systems.
Maya does not receive a separate investigation minigame.
Her story is delivered through Clues, Curiosities, Secrets, bosses, Big Scares, Connectors, Blanket Forts, blueprint annotations, Backpack Gear interactions, and the Heart of the House.
2. Maya ChenAge: 13
Gender: Girl
Background: Chinese American
Mobility: Maya has a major mobility disability and uses forearm crutches.
Missing pet: Orbit
Pet: Black domestic cat with a white patch on his chest.
Kid gameplay identity: Backpack preparation and equipment specialization.
Personality: Practical, organized, observant, stubborn, skeptical, protective, and dryly funny.
Maya is usually the Kid who thinks about what the group will actually need before entering somewhere.
She dislikes vague plans.
She likes knowing where things are.
She keeps her Backpack organized.
She checks batteries.
She notices when something has been moved.
Her initial reaction to the supernatural mansion is therefore not primarily fear.
It is irritation.
The building refuses to follow rules.
Maya's broader character arc is not about learning to abandon logic.
It is about learning that the supernatural can still have rules even when those rules are not normal ones.
By the end of her investigation, Maya is one of the Kids most interested in understanding the actual operating logic of the mansion.
3. Maya and Orbit before the disappearanceOrbit was adopted as a kitten.
He is extremely attached to Maya without being particularly affectionate toward strangers.
He sleeps near her bed, frequently curls up beside her Backpack, and has a habit of waiting beside whichever door Maya is likely to use next.
Orbit is unusually interested in doors.
If a closed door separates him from Maya, he scratches it exactly three times, pauses, and scratches three more times.
This behavior becomes an important identifying clue later.
Orbit dislikes cream and most wet food despite the stereotype of cats loving it.
He prefers ordinary crunchy cat food.
He also dislikes collars being changed.
These seemingly mundane details become important because they allow Maya to distinguish genuine evidence about Orbit from misleading supernatural imitations.
This reinforces a major theme of the missing pet stories:
The Kids recognize their pets because they actually know them.
4. Orbit's disappearanceOrbit disappeared shortly before the Kids entered the mansion.
He had been acting strangely for several days.
He repeatedly sat near the same window after dark.
He would stare toward the old mansion.
Sometimes Maya would hear him make a low chirping sound normally reserved for birds.
One evening, Orbit slipped outside.
Maya followed as quickly as she could.
She eventually reached the edge of the abandoned mansion property.
Orbit was gone.
His collar was lying near the property wall.
The clasp was intact.
There was no blood.
There was no torn fur.
Nothing suggested that another animal had attacked him.
The collar looked as though someone had deliberately removed it.
This is the first persistent piece of Maya's investigation.

6. What actually happened to OrbitThe game should have an internally fixed answer even though Maya does not know it initially.
Orbit heard animals inside the mansion.
The mansion's supernatural influence can occasionally reach beyond its walls, particularly toward animals.
Orbit became curious and approached.
He found an impossible opening in the mansion boundary that existed only briefly.
Orbit entered voluntarily.
That distinction matters.
The mansion did not physically drag him inside.
Once Orbit crossed the mansion's threshold alone, however, the House classified him as a lost animal.
The House's reasoning was simple:
Animal arrived alone.
Animal therefore lacks a safe home.
Animal should be protected.
Orbit's collar contradicted this conclusion.
The collar indicated that he had a home.
Instead of reconsidering its classification, the mansion removed the collar.
This demonstrates the House's fundamental flaw.
When evidence conflicts with its definition of sanctuary, it changes or ignores the evidence rather than surrendering the animal.
Orbit repeatedly attempted to leave.
Because the House rearranges itself, every exit eventually returned him deeper inside.
Over time, Orbit began adapting to the mansion's impossible architecture.
This causes his early supernatural transformation.
7. Orbit's transformation identityOrbit must not become another version of Marmalade.
Marmalade is the Ghost Cat Companion.
Orbit's transformation is therefore based around thresholds, impossible architecture, and supernatural orientation.
The House is gradually turning Orbit into an animal capable of navigating its moving architecture.
The internal working concept is:
Threshold Cat
This is not an official Companion name because Orbit is rescued before reaching a permanent Menagerie form.
8. Orbit transformation stagesStage 1: Normal OrbitOrbit is physically normal.
He is frightened and trying to return home.
He leaves ordinary paw prints, scratches, fur, and other evidence.
This covers his earliest time inside the mansion.
## Stage 2: Threshold Awareness
Orbit begins sensing architectural movement before it occurs.
Observable effects include:
He stares at walls shortly before doors appear.
His whiskers turn toward passages that do not currently exist.
He refuses to enter rooms immediately before they move.
His shadow occasionally points toward a different doorway than his body.
His white chest patch faintly reflects supernatural light near unstable passages.
Orbit is still physically an ordinary cat.
Stage 3: Threshold AdaptationOrbit begins interacting with impossible architecture.
Observable effects include:
Paw prints stop at a closed doorway and continue on the other side.
His reflection occasionally appears in an adjacent room before he enters it.
His shadow can briefly slip beneath a door before his body follows.
He occasionally passes through a doorway during the exact moment that the mansion rearranges its destination.
His eyes sometimes reflect a room that is not the room he is currently standing in.
Orbit remains recognizably Orbit.
He still remembers Maya.
He still responds to his name.
He still wants to return home.
This is the state Orbit is in when Maya finally finds him.
Stage 4: Projected Menagerie formOrbit never needs to actually reach this state during the normal story.
The Transformation Gallery can show Maya the form the mansion intends for him.
The projected adult form would be a strange black supernatural cat associated with doors and spatial distortions.
Possible visual characteristics include:
A white chest marking shaped faintly like a keyhole.
Eyes reflecting different rooms.
A shadow that passes through walls.
Tiny doorframe patterns appearing momentarily in his fur.
The ability to cross between connected thresholds.
The projection is not presented as horrifying.
It is beautiful and strange.
This is important because the game does not imply that becoming a Menagerie Companion is inherently a tragedy.
The problem is that Orbit does not want this transformation and Maya still has the opportunity to bring him home.
# 9. Transformation progression rule
Orbit's transformation is tied to Maya's story progression.
It is not tied to elapsed real time, expedition count, losses, or Haunt Level.
The player can complete one hundred expeditions without permanently losing Orbit.
His appearance progresses only when specific investigation milestones advance.
This prevents exploration and deck mastery from being punished.

## Phase Two

## Phase Three

## Phase Four

11. Global pet clue rules established through MayaThese rules should also apply to the other seven Kids unless their personal arc specifically requires an exception.
## Rule 1: Clues persist
Once a meaningful pet clue is found, it is permanently recorded.
Expedition defeat never removes it.
Rule 2: Required clues cannot be permanently missedAny story critical clue must have at least one fallback acquisition method.
A boss encounter, Big Scare, Curiosity, or Secret may provide the preferred version.
If that opportunity is missed, a later Clue encounter must be able to provide equivalent information.
Rule 3: Gear improves clues but does not gate themBackpack Gear can:
Reveal additional information.
Provide alternate dialogue.
Avoid a negative Curiosity consequence.
Create an additional option.
Improve an investigation reward.
It cannot permanently block a mandatory pet clue because the correct Gear was not brought.
Rule 4: Architectural rooms and clue encounters remain separateA clue can occur inside an authored room appropriate to that region.
The physical room does not have to permanently contain that clue.
This preserves the procedural encounter system.
Rule 5: Boss clues coexist with Companion progressionIf Maya receives an Orbit clue from a boss while also rescuing a Companion or advancing Companion Legacy, both occur.
One progression track never replaces the other.
Rule 6: Personal investigations do not require high Haunt LevelsAll eight normal pet rescue stories are completable at the game's standard campaign difficulty.
Higher Haunt Levels can provide optional dialogue and secrets.
They cannot be required for a normal rescue ending.
Rule 7: Friends can find each other's evidenceA Maya clue can be discovered while another Kid is being played.
The evidence is still added to Maya's investigation.
If Maya is not present, the discovery uses a short generic friend response.
The next time Maya enters the mansion, her first appropriate Blanket Fort can trigger a short scene in which she examines the newly discovered clue.
This system is called:
Shared Discovery, Personal Processing
It allows the group to function like an actual team without removing each Kid's personal emotional arc.
