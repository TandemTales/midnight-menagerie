# Midnight Menagerie: Eli Rosen and Sprocket Complete Personal Investigation Design
## 1. Purpose of Eli's investigation
Eli Rosen's missing pet story should reveal another major flaw in the House's idea of protection.
Maya's story shows that the House ignores choice.
Mateo's story shows that the House gathers information without understanding it.
Amina's story shows that the House confuses temporary safety with permanent avoidance.
Eli's story shows that the House confuses:
Having choices
with
having freedom.
Sprocket is intelligent, curious, persistent, and extremely good at solving physical problems.
The House initially considers these traits dangerous.
Then it discovers that Sprocket loves puzzles.
Its solution is to build increasingly elaborate controlled environments for him.
Doors.
Latches.
Tunnels.
Puzzle feeders.
Mechanical routes.
Hidden compartments.
Sprocket is allowed to choose constantly.
But every possible route still leads somewhere the House selected.
The mansion concludes that Sprocket is happy because he is making decisions.
Eli eventually identifies the flaw:
A maze does not become freedom just because you can choose left or right.
Sprocket's investigation therefore centers on:
Mechanical Curiosities
Connectors
Secrets
Vents
Dumbwaiters
Locks
Latches
Mechanical observation rooms
Sprocket's problem solving behavior
Small stolen objects
The Lost Things Vault
Controlled environments
The difference between enrichment and autonomy
The House gradually transforming Sprocket into an animal able to understand and manipulate its mechanisms
No new mechanical subsystem is required.

### Personality
Eli is inventive, impatient, funny, curious, and happiest when something is not working properly because that means they get to open it.
They are not particularly interested in following instructions that begin with:
"Do not open."
Eli likes understanding how things work.
They dislike explanations that amount to:
"Because that's the rule."
Sprocket shares this quality.
This is part of why Eli is so attached to him.
Eli's character arc involves learning an important distinction.
Understanding a system does not necessarily mean the system is fair.
Sometimes the cleverest solution is not figuring out how to win inside the rules.
Sometimes it is recognizing that the rules themselves are the problem.

7. What actually happenedSprocket discovered an impossible maintenance vent opened by the House.
Unlike Mochi, he was not frightened into entering.
Unlike Pepper, he was not lured with a familiar voice.
Sprocket entered because he was curious.
The House noticed an unaccompanied small animal entering one of its service spaces.
It attempted to redirect him toward a safer animal intake route.
Sprocket ignored the intended route.
He opened a maintenance panel.
Then a latch.
Then a secondary gate.
This fascinated the House.
Instead of placing him immediately into ordinary animal housing, it began presenting him with mechanical challenges.
The House interpreted Sprocket's repeated participation as evidence that he enjoyed the mansion.
In one sense, it was correct.
Sprocket did enjoy solving the puzzles.
But he also repeatedly used those puzzles to search for an actual exit.
The House refused to distinguish between:
Choosing an activity
and
choosing the environment containing the activity.

12. Transformation Stage Three: Tinker AdaptationSprocket begins interacting supernaturally with mechanisms.
Possible manifestations include:
Small metal objects subtly move toward his paws.
His whiskers emit faint sparks near hidden mechanisms.
Tiny gear like patterns briefly appear in the black patches of his fur.
Loose screws rotate when he sniffs them.
Latches click open around him.
He can sense hidden mechanical Secrets.
He can assemble several small objects into a crude useful arrangement without training.
His tail occasionally curls around an object and manipulates it with unusual precision.
Sprocket remains himself.
He still steals screws for no useful reason.
This should remain important.
His supernatural intelligence does not turn him into a tiny engineer with human cognition.
He remains recognizably ratlike.

48. The House's control logicThe player learns that the House believes autonomy can be simulated safely.
Its reasoning is:
Unrestricted freedom can cause harm.
Therefore:
Provide many controlled alternatives.
If an animal can choose food, room, route, puzzle, toy, sleeping place, and activity, the House believes it has preserved agency while eliminating danger.
The flaw is that some decisions remain categorically forbidden.
Most importantly:
Leave.
