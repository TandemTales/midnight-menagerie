Stranger
then
Guest

Resident

The House believes repeated acceptance of care represents increasing belonging.

Sleeping in a prepared room
Returning to a familiar caretaker
Forming friendships

The House does not consider this malicious.

Once an animal belongs to the House strongly enough, the House begins treating previous relationships as obsolete.

161. What Samir's story teaches about the mansionCompleting Bean's investigation reveals:
The House can send small supernatural attendants beyond ordinary mansion rooms.
The Red Page performs hospitality and escort functions.
The House interprets accepting food and care as evidence of increasing guest status.
Guest status can automatically progress toward residency.
The House uses behavioral criteria rather than explicit consent.
It considers forming local friendships evidence that an animal has successfully transferred belonging.
Some animals genuinely develop meaningful relationships inside the mansion.
Those relationships should not be dismissed as false.
The House assumes new social bonds should eventually replace exterior bonds.
It deliberately reduces exterior reminders when that replacement does not occur.
Bean develops supernatural sensitivity to specific relationships.
Bondlight can show that multiple relationships remain simultaneously meaningful.
Bean's exterior bond with Samir remains strong even while local friendships grow.
The House labels this stable dual belonging an anomaly.
It attempts to intervene when relationships do not fit its single residence model.
The House treats hospitality as potentially creating permanent obligation.
Accepting care does not create ownership.
Being welcomed somewhere does not erase another home.
New family can be real without replacing old family.
A true sanctuary must allow an animal to stay voluntarily as well as leave voluntarily.

Feed hungry animals
Heal injured animals
Give companionship to lonely animals
Protect vulnerable animals
Preserve lost things

Release.

A place that keeps animals

A place animals can come back to.

Protection without freedom becomes imprisonment.

Protection with freedom becomes sanctuary.
