# Midnight Menagerie: Amina Okafor and Mochi Complete Personal Investigation Design
## 1. Purpose of Amina's investigation
Amina Okafor's investigation should reveal another major flaw in the House's philosophy.
Maya's story shows that the House does not respect an animal's choice.
Mateo's story shows that the House hears animals without truly listening to them.
Amina's story shows that the House cannot distinguish:
Being frightened
from
Being unsafe forever.
The House sees fear as evidence that the outside world has failed an animal.
Its solution is to remove every source of danger permanently.
Amina understands something the House does not:
Feeling afraid does not mean someone wants their whole life decided for them.
Mochi's story therefore focuses on:
Fear
Animal defensive behavior
Injury and recovery
Safe places
Courage
Blanket Forts
Animal focused Curiosities
The Kennels and Animal Ward
The difference between temporary shelter and permanent confinement
Recognizing when frightened behavior is actually healthy
The House gradually teaching animals to avoid the outside world
Amina's investigation should contain some of the gentlest scenes in the game, but also some of the most unsettling revelations about the House.
2. Amina OkaforAge: 13
Gender: Girl
Background: Nigerian American
Missing pet: Mochi
Pet: Lop eared rabbit
Kid gameplay identity: Courage recovery, long expedition survival, Blanket Fort efficiency, and animal focused Curiosities
### Personality
Amina is patient, composed, observant, and emotionally perceptive.
She is not fearless.
She simply does not treat fear as a failure.
She tends to watch before reacting.
When an animal growls, hisses, freezes, hides, scratches, or runs, Amina's first question is usually:
"What is it scared of?"
rather than:
"How do we make it stop?"
This becomes central to her investigation.
Amina is especially frustrated by adults or supernatural creatures who describe control as being "for someone's own good."
She values safety.
She also understands that safety is contextual.
A locked room can protect you during a storm.
The same locked room becomes imprisonment if you are never permitted to leave it.
3. Mochi before the disappearanceMochi is a cream colored lop eared rabbit with gray markings around his nose and one gray front paw.
He is gentle but cautious.
He dislikes being picked up unexpectedly.
He prefers sitting beside Amina rather than directly on her lap.
He becomes nervous around:
Loud dogs
Vacuum cleaners
Sudden shadows overhead
Unfamiliar people reaching toward him
Thunder
When frightened, Mochi has several recognizable behaviors.
First, he freezes.
Then his nose begins moving rapidly.
If he determines there is an escape route, he runs toward cover.
If he cannot escape, he thumps.
Mochi's thump pattern becomes important.
When mildly concerned, he usually thumps once.
When extremely frightened, he often gives:
Two rapid thumps
followed by
one slower thump.
Amina recognizes this immediately.
Mochi also has one highly specific comfort behavior.
When he finally feels safe after being frightened, he stretches one back leg completely behind himself and lies partially on his side.
Amina calls this:
"The dramatic leg."
This tiny behavior later becomes some of the strongest evidence that Mochi is genuinely relaxed rather than merely subdued.
4. The day Mochi disappearedMochi was spending supervised time in an outdoor exercise pen.
The pen was covered.
Its latch was intact.
Amina briefly went inside.
During that time, a large shadow passed across the yard.
The exact animal is never confirmed.
It may have been a hawk.
Mochi panicked.
Amina heard thumping from inside the house.
By the time she reached the enclosure, Mochi was gone.
The pen was still latched.
There was no hole underneath it.
There was no blood.
There was no sign of a predator.
One thing was different.
Inside the pen, beneath the small shelter box Mochi used when nervous, Amina found a patch of unfamiliar soft moss.
The moss did not grow anywhere in their yard.

6. What actually happened to MochiThe House sensed Mochi's panic.
The mansion is particularly responsive to frightened animals.
It interprets intense fear as a signal that an animal requires sanctuary.
During Mochi's panic, an impossible burrow briefly opened beneath his shelter box.
The tunnel smelled like moss, hay, and cool earth.
Mochi did exactly what a frightened rabbit naturally does.
He ran toward cover.
The impossible burrow closed behind him.
The House interpreted this as voluntary entry.
From the House's perspective:
Animal experienced danger.
Animal sought shelter.
Shelter was accepted.
Outside environment therefore unsafe.
This becomes the fundamental error at the center of Amina's story.
Mochi sought temporary safety from a frightening moment.
The House interpreted that as choosing permanent sanctuary.
7. Why Mochi remains insideInitially, Mochi repeatedly attempts to return through the burrow.
The House prevents this because it believes returning him to the environment that frightened him would be irresponsible.
The mansion then begins a gradual conditioning process.
It provides:
Quiet rooms
Soft bedding
Food
Hiding spaces
Predictable lighting
No predators
No loud weather
No unfamiliar handling
On the surface, these conditions seem kind.
That is intentional.
The House genuinely makes Mochi comfortable.
The problem is that it also begins treating any discomfort as something that should never be experienced.
The House increasingly isolates Mochi from anything frightening.
Over time, it starts to confuse:
calm
with
health
and
avoidance
with
safety.
8. Mochi's transformation conceptMochi's emerging supernatural identity centers on:
Shelter, burrows, protective spaces, calm, and retreat.
The internal working concept is:
Sanctuary Rabbit
This should remain clearly distinct from Mopsy, the Rag Doll Bunny Companion.
Mochi's visual transformation is organic and supernatural rather than fabric based.
9. Mochi transformation stagesStage One: Normal MochiMochi is physically normal.
He is frightened and hides frequently.
He attempts to find a route home.
Stage Two: Shelter SenseMochi begins sensing locations of safety.
He can identify:
Hidden alcoves
Stable rooms
Protected corners
Rooms with no immediate enemies
Temporary Blanket Fort like spaces
Burrows and passages
His ears occasionally turn toward a safe location before it becomes visible.
Small patches of moss sometimes grow where he sleeps.
Stage Three: Sanctuary AdaptationMochi begins creating supernatural safe spaces unintentionally.
Possible manifestations include:
Moss grows beneath him while resting.
Small burrow openings occasionally appear near him.
Frightened minor animals become calmer around him.
Nearby hostile sounds become muffled when he hides.
His fur becomes faintly luminous in protected spaces.
His footprints sometimes leave tiny sprouts of soft moss.
He can disappear into a small burrow and emerge from another protected space nearby.
He remains recognizably Mochi.
He still recognizes Amina.
He still experiences fear.
Most importantly:
He still chooses when to leave his hiding place.
10. Projected Stage FourThe Transformation Gallery can show the House's intended mature form for Mochi.
He would become a supernatural guardian of refuge spaces.
Possible appearance:
Long soft ears edged with mosslike light
Fur resembling moonlit cream
A gray paw leaving tiny glowing prints

A calm supernatural aura

The form should be beautiful and comforting.

Her objection is not:
"I don't want Mochi to become strange."
Her objection is:
"Mochi should decide whether he wants this life."
11. Transformation progression ruleAs with Orbit and Pepper, Mochi's transformation is story gated.
It does not advance because:
Amina loses expeditions
The player spends many runs exploring
The player uses another Kid
A high Haunt Level is selected
Amina takes too long in real time
The rescue remains available through ordinary campaign play.
12. Amina's investigation phasesAmina's story contains five major phases.
## Phase One
He Ran Somewhere Safe
Amina proves Mochi entered the mansion through an impossible shelter route.
## Phase Two
They Wouldn't Let Him Come Back
Amina learns the House intentionally prevented Mochi from returning after the immediate danger passed.
## Phase Three
Quiet Isn't the Same as Okay
Amina discovers the House has been trying to eliminate every source of fear from Mochi's environment.
## Phase Four
He Started Making Safe Places
Amina learns about Mochi's supernatural transformation and follows his sanctuary signs toward the Heart.
## Phase Five
Being Safe Enough to Choose
Amina finds Mochi and proves that genuine safety must include the option to leave the shelter.

## Principle Three

Her strongest dialogue often accepts part of the antagonist's argument before identifying the flaw.

She does not romanticize danger.

133. What Amina's story teaches about the mansionBy completing Mochi's investigation, the player learns:
The House can respond directly to animal distress.
It can create temporary impossible shelters outside its main structure.
Some animals enter those shelters voluntarily during moments of fear.
The House interprets temporary refuge as permanent consent.
The House deliberately prevents some animals from returning to environments it considers dangerous.
The House provides real comfort and care.
Its care can nevertheless become controlling.
It attempts to remove all sources of fear.
This can increase avoidance rather than resilience.
The House interprets avoidance as successful protection.
Menagerie transformation can emerge from the kind of environment an animal inhabits.
Mochi develops the ability to create temporary sanctuary rather than remaining permanently sheltered.
Recently transforming animals can adapt in ways the House did not intend.
The House treats voluntary departure from safety as a containment problem.
Genuine sanctuary requires the ability to leave.
134. Relationship to Maya and MateoThe first three Kid investigations now expose three distinct errors.
## Maya and Orbit
The House knows Orbit has a home and ignores his attempts to leave.
Failure: It does not respect choice.
## Mateo and Pepper
The House records Pepper accurately but forces his communication into its preferred interpretation.
Failure: It hears without listening.
## Amina and Mochi
The House correctly recognizes genuine fear and provides genuine safety, but cannot accept that temporary protection should eventually end.
Failure: It confuses protection with permanent avoidance.
These stories should gradually make the antagonist more complex.
The House is not wrong about everything.
It is wrong about what its correct observations entitle it to do.
