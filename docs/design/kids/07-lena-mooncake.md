# Midnight Menagerie: Lena Yazzie and Mooncake Complete Personal Investigation Design
## 1. Purpose of Lena's investigation
Lena Yazzie's missing pet story should reveal another distinct failure in the House's philosophy of protection.
Maya discovers that the House disregards choice.
Mateo discovers that the House hears without truly listening.
Amina discovers that the House turns temporary shelter into permanent confinement.
Eli discovers that controlled options are not the same as freedom.
Priya discovers that optimized care does not outweigh individual preference.
Jordan discovers that being useful does not create a permanent obligation.
Lena discovers that the House mistakes:
Wanting somewhere private
for
wanting to disappear.
Mooncake is a hamster.
Hiding, burrowing, nesting, storing food, and spending time in concealed spaces are completely ordinary behaviors for him.
The House observes this and initially responds appropriately.
It gives him:
Quiet spaces
Tunnels
Burrows
Covered nests
Places to store food
Rooms away from frightening activity
Then it takes the idea too far.
If Mooncake feels safest when hidden, the House concludes that he should be hidden permanently.
If being searched for disturbs him, the House concludes that others should no longer be permitted to find him.
If reminders of the outside make him restless, the House stores those reminders somewhere he cannot see them.
Eventually the House begins treating being unseen as evidence of successful protection.
Lena understands the difference.
Privacy means deciding when and how you are seen.
Erasure means somebody else deciding you should not be found.
Mooncake's investigation therefore centers on:
Secrets
Clues
Photographic evidence
Small hidden spaces
Hoarded objects
Secret rooms
Hidden Connectors
Treasure
Blueprint annotations
The Lost Things Vault
The Impossible Door
Evidence intentionally removed from view
The difference between hiding and being hidden
The difference between preserving something and burying it where nobody can reach it
No separate photography minigame, stealth meter, or hidden map layer is introduced.

### Personality
Lena is quiet, observant, patient, mischievous, and difficult to fool twice.
She frequently notices details before anyone else.
She does not always announce them immediately.
Lena prefers seeing the whole situation before explaining what she thinks happened.
She dislikes people assuming that being quiet means she has nothing to say.
That connects naturally to her personal story.
Mooncake is also easy to overlook.
A hamster can be in a room while almost nobody notices him.
Lena understands that being unobtrusive is not the same as being unimportant.
Her story gradually becomes about evidence itself.
Something being:
Hidden
does not mean:
Gone.
Something being:
Unrecorded
does not mean:
It did not happen.
And somebody choosing not to be noticed for a little while does not mean they have surrendered the right to be found by the people they love.

He is round even by hamster standards.

4. Mooncake's identifying behaviorMooncake has a highly specific hoarding habit.
He sorts some objects.
Food usually goes into one cache.
Soft nesting material goes into another.

This becomes critical during the investigation.

7. What actually happenedA tiny impossible pocket opened inside Mooncake's enclosure during the night.
It appeared behind part of his nesting material.
The space looked like a narrow burrow.
Inside was the mansion wallpaper.
Mooncake entered because:
It was dark.
It was enclosed.
It smelled unfamiliar.
It contained interesting paper.
Mooncake began collecting scraps from the space.
Eventually he entered completely.
The pocket closed.
The House detected an animal that strongly preferred concealed spaces.
It classified Mooncake as:
Privacy seeking
and
Concealment oriented.
Initially it moved him to a quiet animal room.
Mooncake repeatedly escaped into smaller spaces.
The House interpreted those escapes differently from Sprocket's.
Sprocket sought mechanisms and exits.
Mooncake sought small places where nothing could disturb him.
The House therefore decided to give him better hiding spaces.
Then better ones.
Then spaces so hidden that almost nobody could locate him.
8. The House's central mistakeMooncake regularly comes out of his hiding places.
He eats.
He explores.
He collects objects.
He investigates new spaces.
He interacts with Lena.
His hiding behavior is temporary.
The House records the part it understands:
Mooncake seeks concealment.
It gradually ignores the second part:
Mooncake leaves concealment when he chooses.
Eventually the House concludes:
Maximum concealment equals maximum comfort.
That is the fundamental error of Lena's story.
9. Why the House becomes interested in MooncakeMooncake begins finding tiny gaps in supernatural architecture.
He fits into spaces other animals cannot use.
He discovers:

Unused ventilation spaces
Gaps behind cabinets

The House realizes Mooncake is developing a relationship with its hidden storage spaces.
It starts using those spaces around him.
This accelerates his transformation.
10. Mooncake's transformation conceptMooncake's developing supernatural identity centers on:
Caches, tiny impossible spaces, stored objects, and private refuges.
Internal working concept:
Pocket Burrow Hamster
This is not an additional Companion.
Mooncake is rescued before reaching a permanent Menagerie state.
11. Distinction from HushHush owns:
Shadows
Stealth
Disappearing
Surprise
Moving Tricks between zones
Concealment
Mooncake must not simply become another stealth animal.
Mooncake does not turn invisible.
He does not become a shadow.
His supernatural ability is spatial storage and tiny refuge creation.
He physically enters impossible little spaces.
He can also store small objects inside them.
His transformation is about where things are kept, not whether something can be seen.
12. Distinction from OrbitOrbit develops supernatural control over thresholds between ordinary rooms.
Mooncake develops tiny pocket spaces.
Orbit might pass through a doorway and emerge somewhere impossible.
Mooncake might crawl behind a book and discover a space large enough to contain an entire nest.
His power is localized, enclosed, and cache oriented.
13. Transformation Stage OneMooncake is physically normal.
He hides frequently.
He collects pieces of mansion material in small caches.
He occasionally escapes his assigned rooms.
14. Transformation Stage Two: Pocket SenseMooncake begins detecting impossible spaces.
Observable signs include:
Staring at blank sections of wall
Pawing at furniture with hollow supernatural interiors
Entering gaps that appear too small
Locating hidden storage spaces
Finding objects that had been placed behind sealed walls
Building nests in spaces that should not exist
His appearance remains normal.
15. Transformation Stage Three: Pocket Burrow AdaptationMooncake begins creating or expanding small impossible refuges.
Possible manifestations include:
A tiny hole opens behind him where no opening existed.
His cheek pouches briefly appear larger inside than physically possible.
Small objects disappear into one hiding place and later reappear in another nearby cache.
His fur develops faint wallpaper like patterns when he is inside a pocket space.
A soft warm glow appears inside supernatural burrows.
Objects stored in his caches remain unusually well preserved.
Tiny doorlike seams appear on furniture after he investigates it.
He can enter a small hiding place and emerge from another nearby private space.
The effects remain cute and uncanny rather than frightening.
16. Projected Stage FourThe Transformation Gallery can show Mooncake's possible mature form.
He becomes a supernatural keeper of tiny lost spaces.
Possible features include:
Soft glowing patterns in his fur resembling wallpaper
Cheeks capable of opening tiny impossible storage spaces
Small arch shaped burrows appearing around him
Floating bits of paper and forgotten objects
A nest containing more space than physically possible
The ability to preserve small important objects safely
Doorlike markings briefly appearing in walls wherever he rests
The mature form could eventually become a wonderful supernatural keeper of treasured objects.
The problem is that Mooncake never chose the role.
17. Transformation progression ruleMooncake's transformation advances only through story milestones.
It does not advance because:
Lena loses expeditions
The player spends time with another Kid
Haunt Level increases
Too many runs occur
The campaign is explored slowly
Optional Secrets are missed
Kid level increases
Mooncake remains rescuable through standard campaign progression.
18. Lena's five investigation phasesPhase One: He Found a Hiding PlaceLena proves Mooncake entered through a tiny impossible space.
## Phase Two: The House Hid Him
Lena discovers that the House deliberately made Mooncake increasingly difficult to locate because it believed concealment made him comfortable.
## Phase Three: He Started Making Pockets
Mooncake develops Pocket Burrow Adaptation and begins creating hidden caches of his own.
## Phase Four: He Wants to Be Found
Lena discovers that Mooncake has been deliberately leaving recognizable objects and evidence outside his hidden spaces.
## Phase Five: Private Is Not Gone
Lena reaches Mooncake and confronts the House over the difference between respecting privacy and erasing someone from everyone who cares about them.

It may intentionally make an animal more difficult to find.
It sometimes suppresses searches in the name of privacy.
It can reduce observation so dramatically that it stops reassessing what the animal wants.

The House often treats something safely stored as though its relationship to the outside no longer matters.
Privacy without ongoing choice can become erasure.
A creature can want solitude sometimes and connection at other times.
Respecting a hiding place requires letting the creature decide when to emerge.
