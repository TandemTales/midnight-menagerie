# Midnight Menagerie: Jordan Brooks and Scout Complete Personal Investigation Design
## 1. Purpose of Jordan's investigation
Jordan Brooks's missing pet story should reveal a different failure in the House's philosophy.
Maya discovers that the House disregards choice.
Mateo discovers that the House hears without truly listening.
Amina discovers that the House turns temporary protection into permanent confinement.
Eli discovers that controlled options are not genuine freedom.
Priya discovers that optimized care does not outweigh individual preference.
Jordan discovers that the House can turn a good quality into an obligation.
Scout is loyal.
Scout is protective.
Scout follows frightened animals.
Scout helps creatures in trouble.
The House sees these behaviors and reaches a conclusion:
Scout is useful here.
It begins giving him animals to find.
Animals to lead.
Animals to comfort.
Animals to protect.
Every time Scout finishes helping one creature, the House gives him another.
Eventually the House decides that Scout's loyalty proves he belongs in the mansion because so many animals need him.
Jordan recognizes the trap before the House does.
Choosing to help someone does not mean they own the rest of your life.
Scout's investigation therefore centers on:
Trails

The difference between loyalty and obligation
The difference between helping because you choose to and being told that others need you too much to leave
No new mechanical subsystem is introduced.

### Personality
Jordan is energetic, impulsive, funny, generous, social, and extremely good at acting when everyone else is still deciding what to do.
He is frequently the first person to say:
"I'll do it."
That is both a strength and a weakness.
Jordan tends to assume responsibility quickly.
If someone needs help, he wants to help.
If something goes wrong while he is responsible for it, he takes that personally.
Scout's disappearance affects him especially strongly because Jordan was physically holding Scout's leash when Scout ran.
Jordan's private conclusion is simple:
I should have held on.
Much of his investigation begins from that guilt.
His personal arc is not about learning to stop helping.
It is about understanding that responsibility has limits.
You can care about someone without being responsible for controlling every decision they make.
You can help someone without becoming permanently responsible for everything that happens afterward.

9. Scout's transformation conceptScout's supernatural transformation should not duplicate Pudding.

He then begins leaving supernatural trails of his own.

The House notices when animals assist one another.
It actively assigns roles based on observed traits.
Those roles can begin as genuinely useful work.
The House may reward behavior an animal genuinely enjoys.
It interprets repeated helpful behavior as evidence of permanent suitability.
It can deliberately direct one animal toward another animal in distress.
It may repeatedly postpone departure by presenting another apparent need.
It formalizes animal roles through records and assignments.
It considers usefulness to the Menagerie relevant when deciding whether an animal should leave.
It can transform naturally helpful behavior into compulsory duty.
Scout's transformation lets him perceive lost or separated creatures.
Scout can leave supernatural routes others can follow.
Those routes eventually threaten the House's containment structure.
The House approves guidance toward sanctuary but suppresses guidance toward exits.
Scout consistently remains oriented toward home.
Being useful inside the mansion is not equivalent to choosing the mansion.
Loyalty retains moral meaning only when the loyal creature can choose when and whom to help.
