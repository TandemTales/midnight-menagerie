# Midnight Menagerie: Revised 20 Room Mansion Structure
## Core structural revision
The mansion now contains exactly 17 regions with 20 named architectural rooms in every region, for a total of 340 permanent named rooms.
This does not mean the regions should be equal in physical size. A huge greenhouse chamber may occupy several times the floor area of a nursery closet. A narrow secret passage may be only a few feet wide. The 20 room rule standardizes the amount of authored content and procedural routing potential without standardizing physical scale.
Every region should provide several meaningful route choices during an expedition. The important procedural variable is not which rooms exist. All 340 rooms exist in the permanent architecture. What changes is how the player can currently move between them and what is happening inside them.
## The three connection layers
Every region should be designed using three separate circulation layers.
### 1. Permanent architectural circulation
These are the ordinary connections printed on the clean blueprint.
They include doors, hallways, stairs, balconies, exterior paths, galleries, bridges, service corridors, and similar architectural features.
These connections establish the believable underlying mansion.
### 2. Current house state
The mansion can alter whether a permanent route is presently usable.
A door might be locked.
A corridor might have stretched beyond reach.
A staircase might currently terminate against a wall.
A flooded passage might be inaccessible.
A hedge might have grown across a path.
A room might rotate so that one doorway no longer reaches the same corridor.
The architectural connection still exists on the blueprint, but the investigation layer shows that it cannot currently be used.
### 3. Manifested connections
These are supernatural links that are not part of the dependable architectural blueprint.
They can include secret doors, fireplace passages, mirrors, wardrobes, dumbwaiters, crawlspaces, holes beneath beds, trapdoors, moving bookshelves, root tunnels, drains, chimneys, animal burrows, paintings, and doors that simply appear where no door should exist.
These are excellent procedural route modifiers because they let a familiar region play differently without turning the underlying architecture into meaningless randomization.
A manifested connection might therefore allow:
Music Room to Butler’s Passage
Day Nursery to Underbed Hollow
Cold Larder to Root Cellar
Fireplace Study to Hidden Stair
Moon Dome to Glass Roof Walk
Pet Crypt to Pet Memorial Row
Pump Room to Lampworks
Collar Room to Hall of Collars
These connections should make thematic and spatial sense even when they violate literal geometry.
## Recommended regional routing structure
Each 20 room region should usually contain:
Two or three entry possibilities
Several early route branches
At least three substantial middle route clusters
Two or more opportunities for routes to reconnect
Several optional side rooms
At least two plausible locations for manifested shortcuts
One or two late approach routes
One fixed Boss room
A normal expedition does not need to send the player through all 20 rooms.
A typical route might pass through roughly 8 to 12 rooms before reaching the Boss, depending on detours, Secrets, Curiosities, investigation choices, and manifested connections.
This means the same region can support many expeditions without requiring the architecture itself to be regenerated.
# Mansion wide architectural organization
The mansion should feel as though it began as a very large estate and was subsequently expanded, remodeled, distorted, and partially swallowed by supernatural additions.
The Forgotten Foyer forms the oldest central entrance block.
The Ballroom occupies one formal side of the ground floor.
The Study and Library occupy the other.
The Nursery and Sleeping Quarters sit farther into the residential portions of the mansion.
The Kitchens occupy the rear service wing and descend into Cellars.
The Greenhouse extends beyond the rear structure.
The Courtyard and Pumpkin Grounds occupy a partially enclosed exterior area beyond the house.
The Bathhouse occupies a later luxury addition.
The Lampworks rise vertically through several floors near the service infrastructure.
The Attic and Observatory occupy the roofline.
The Kennels sit near a service courtyard and exterior grounds.
The Graveyard lies beyond the older portion of the estate.
The Hedge Maze fills another large exterior tract.
The Crypt lies beneath the Graveyard and older mansion foundations.
The Secret Passages thread through nearly all of these areas.
The Heart occupies impossible interior space deep inside and beneath the mansion. Parts of its architecture should physically overlap spaces that should already be occupied by other rooms.
# 1. The Forgotten Foyer
Companion: Marmalade, the Ghost Cat
Boss: The Butler
Architectural form: Broad, formal, nearly symmetrical central entrance block.
The Foyer remains the most believable portion of the mansion. It establishes doors, halls, stairs, and formal circulation before later regions become increasingly impossible.
Its floor plan should have a strong central axis with east and west social routes wrapping around it. This gives the player an immediate understanding of branching paths without making the tutorial region confusing.
Marmalade appears throughout this region as faint paw prints, movements behind curtains, objects knocked from tables, and ghostly reflections.
## Route structure
The central route moves from the Front Vestibule toward the Grand Staircase and Receiving Chamber.
The west route passes through the Parlor, Music Room, and Dining Room.
The east route passes through the Receiving Room, Drawing Room, and Marble Gallery.
Upper balconies reconnect both sides near the Boss approach.
Butler’s Passage provides a concealed service route capable of connecting several normally separate spaces.

# 17. The Heart of the House
Companion: None
Final Boss: Unlocked later
Architectural form: Unnaturally symmetrical interior structure resembling both the formal plan of a mansion and an anatomical heart.
The Heart should feel fundamentally different from every previous region.
The player has spent the game learning that the mansion rearranges architecture.
The Heart reveals that some of those rearrangements are not accidental. The mansion has circulation systems, intake areas, records, holding areas, observation spaces, and transformation facilities.
The architecture begins resembling an organism performing a process.
## Route structure
The Heart begins deceptively linear.
After Menagerie Hall, it divides into two nearly symmetrical wings.
One wing emphasizes records, memory, and previous animals.
The other emphasizes intake, observation, and transformation.
Both surround the central Pet Holding Ward.
The routes eventually reconverge through the Pulse Passage and Inner Sanctuary.
The Heart Chamber is always the final destination.

## Interior hidden structure
Secret Passages can potentially connect to almost every region.
The Heart of the House should have very few ordinary architectural entrances.
Instead, the Menagerie gradually discovers that many strange dead ends, sealed stairs, impossible doors, and unexplained shafts throughout the mansion are fragments of routes toward the Heart.
# Procedural route generation within the 20 room structure
The room count should support procedural exploration without making the mansion feel arbitrary.
At the start of an expedition, the game selects a current circulation state for each region.
For example, the Foyer might normally have:
Entry Hall to Parlor
Entry Hall to Receiving Room
Parlor to Music Room
Receiving Room to Drawing Room
Music Room to Dining Room
Drawing Room to Marble Gallery
Both sides eventually reaching the upper landings and Boss approach.
During another expedition:
The Music Room fireplace opens into Butler’s Passage.
The Drawing Room door to Marble Gallery is sealed.
The Visitor Cloakroom connects directly to West Landing.
The Bell Pull Gallery opens a route into Secret Passages.
The physical region remains recognizable, but route strategy changes significantly.
# Recommended room accessibility model
The player should usually be able to identify several rooms ahead rather than only the immediate next room.
This preserves the route planning appeal of a deckbuilding roguelike.
A region might visually present:
Three currently reachable rooms.
Several rooms one step farther away.
Known encounter information on some of them.
Unknown information on others.
A visible but currently inaccessible Treasure room.
A suspected Secret route based on previous expeditions.
A manifested fireplace route discovered by the current Companion.
The player therefore makes genuine spatial decisions rather than selecting abstract nodes.
# Companion specific route knowledge
Rescuing Companions should gradually change how players understand the mansion.
The Companions do not merely provide combat decks. Each has intimate knowledge of a particular type of supernatural architecture.
Examples:
Marmalade: Detects ghost routes and places animals repeatedly traveled.
Mopsy: Notices fabric seams, toy spaces, wardrobes, and stitched openings.
Boggle: Reveals under bed routes and sleeping room distortions.
Taffy: Finds food storage routes, servant access, and sticky residue.
Brambleboo: Detects roots, cracks, soil routes, and plant controlled doors.
Mossbit: Reads grave names, memorial patterns, and buried paths.
Crinkle: Interprets maps, written clues, hidden documents, and paper mechanisms.
Wink: Detects observation points, webs, rafters, and sight lines.
Wisp: Reveals flame passages, chimney routes, and light activated mechanisms.
Count Crumbula: Understands guest etiquette, formal shortcuts, mirrors, and nocturnal mansion behavior.
Bones: Finds buried objects, bone caches, crypt routes, and animal remains.
Truffle: Reveals burrows, soil routes, compost tunnels, and decayed barriers.
Hush: Is the master of secret doors, wall passages, shadows, and hidden circulation.
Drizzle: Understands drains, pipes, water levels, rain routes, and flooded passages.
Pudding: Detects animal trails, kennels, scent routes, and places frightened pets have traveled.
Pipkin: Reveals pond routes, garden openings, moon activated paths, and seasonal changes.
These abilities can affect the investigation layer without forcing every Companion to have a completely separate map system.
# Missing pet trail integration
The missing pets should move conceptually through this same architecture.
Each Kid’s investigation color can begin with scattered clues near exterior and entrance regions.
Later expeditions reveal a more coherent trail.
For example:
A paw print near the Front Vestibule.
A scratched door in the Kitchens.
A matching hair in the Kennels.
A collar fragment in the Graveyard.
A name written incorrectly in the Archivist’s records.
A recently added tag in the Heart’s Hall of Collars.
Finally, evidence in the New Arrival Ward and Pet Holding Ward.
Because every clue is attached to a stable architectural room, the investigation feels cumulative even while the routes between those rooms continue changing.
# Final mansion rule
The mansion should never behave like a randomly generated dungeon disguised with mansion graphics.
It is a specific enormous haunted mansion containing 340 authored places.
Players gradually learn those places.
They remember that the Music Room is near the Parlor.
They learn that the Cold Larder has a cellar stair.
They recognize that the Kennels back onto the grounds.
They know that the Library fireplace is suspicious.
They understand that the Moon Pond sometimes connects somewhere impossible.
What changes is not the identity of the mansion.
What changes is what the mansion is willing to let them reach.
That distinction should be the foundation of the entire Midnight Menagerie map system.
I think a strong starting target is 6 core enemy types and 3 Big Scares per region, plus the established boss. Those six enemies can then appear alone or in authored combinations, producing considerably more than six actual Scuffle encounters. This gives the base mansion roughly 100 ordinary enemy designs, about 50 Big Scares, and 17 bosses without requiring hundreds of completely independent enemy move sets.
