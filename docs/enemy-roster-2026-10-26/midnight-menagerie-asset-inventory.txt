VISUAL TONE AND ANIMATION FEASIBILITY
Observed art is polished painterly/3D-render-like 2D cutout art with tactile fabric, crochet, patchwork, worn wood, carved brass, glass, candy and foliage; large round expressive eyes and compact toy-like proportions; warm plum, muted purple, brown, gold and green accented with cyan/violet/orange magical glows. The cast often resembles cherished household objects misbehaving. Characters are isolated on transparency and use readable whole-body silhouettes.
Preserved animations already include toothy suitcases/tins, an oven mouth, root claws, sewing scissors and carnivorous glasshouse imagery. Keep the real asset identity and reframe behavior as theatrical surprise, harmless mess, blocking, bells, puffing, rattling, hiding or demanding attention; do not pretend teeth are absent or replace mandatory assets. New designs should lean toward the gentler plush/household end and avoid adding predatory chase/eating concepts.
Useful motion vocabulary: bobs, flops, rocking doors/lids, twirls, ripples, spring pops, soft squash/stretch, expressive eyes, small glow bursts and a collapse/dissolve into ordinary household material. Prefer one distinctive readable object/body and one signature motion; complex independently moving mobs and scene-sized architecture increase 2D production cost.
Existing repetition to distinguish carefully: Blanket Blob versus Blanket Creeper versus Blanket Hydra; Wardrobe Guest versus The Wardrobe; Candy Clump versus Sugar Golem; Topiary Beast (still) versus Ancient Topiary (animated). Preserve separate existing identities while avoiding more variants elsewhere.
