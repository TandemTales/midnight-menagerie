# MM101 — fresh family co-viewing editorial simulation

**Script reviewed:** `MM101-v3.fountain`, complete, scenes S01–S15. No older drafts, reviews, scores, source ledger, or defenses consulted.

**Result: 85/100.** I would keep watching and would expect many families to want episode two. The cast is appealing, the house produces repeatable comedy, and the closing bowl image gives the search emotional purpose. This is a promising pilot with a concentrated revision need in S09–S11, rather than a pilot that needs a new premise. It does not yet clear a 90-point editorial target on this read.

This is a fresh editorial simulation, **not an actual family test**. The approximately 22-minute target is unmeasured. Scores diagnose this script; they do not prove audience response or superiority to another show.

## Would a family keep watching?

Probably yes. S01 makes the shared loss tangible before the mansion performs any tricks. The comic poster problem introduces cooperation rather than eight biographies. Pepper's recording and the collar give the visit a reason, and the answering hatch gives the audience an early supernatural payoff.

The strongest continuous stretch is S03–S09. The house's service instinct causes an actual problem, the children discover a playable rule, the ledger rewards their search, and Mopsy supplies a person worth caring about. The audience gets comedy, evidence, and attachment rather than atmosphere alone.

Attention is most vulnerable in S10–S11. A younger viewer can understand “the trolley is in the way,” but the sequence asks them to keep updating brake states, approach routes, bed placement, wheel paths, the Governess's position, and who can see which corner. The tiny-bed gag is good enough to retain; the navigation around it is more elaborate than the underlying escape.

S13–S15 recovers the emotional focus. The scratch recording is a modest lead rather than a reunion substitute. Maya eating while keeping Orbit's bowl available allows ordinary life and hope to coexist. The ending made me want the next search, rather than feel that the script had demanded a sad response.

## Findings and repairs

### 1. High priority — the children knowingly pass up the return opening

**Scenes:** S09–S10.

Mopsy reveals a floor-level doorway onto the same landing and lift the children already used. They have explicitly asked for a door that is not locked. Nevertheless, all eight return to the Story Nook so Maya can make another clear request to the Governess. The script does not establish a practical reason to postpone leaving or a conscious reason to remain for Mopsy. The subsequent confinement therefore feels partly manufactured by the children ignoring their successful discovery.

Maya's insistence on clarity is a useful flaw, but it needs to be legible as a choice with a cost. Currently the audience may simply think, “Why didn't they go through that door?”

**Repair:** Put an immediate, visible obstacle between discovery and departure, or have the children begin leaving while a specific unfinished obligation keeps Maya and Mateo with Mopsy. Alternatively, make Maya's decision to seek permission explicit and let Mateo challenge it. Preserve her imperfect judgment, but do not rely on the audience overlooking an available exit.

**Classification:** Actual causal weakness on the page; not a runtime speculation.

### 2. High priority — the escape changes the children's side of the obstruction

**Scenes:** end of S10 into S11.

S10 says the children return into the Story Nook. The Giant then blocks the Story Nook arch and closet doorway with its trolley. S11 locates the Governess in the Story Nook and the children on the closet side of the folded frames, without showing how the children crossed. The very obstacle defining the action makes that missing transition consequential.

**Repair:** Establish the children's final positions before the trolley stops: for example, show them moving into Bedtime Hall toward the closet while the Governess stays behind. Then describe the trolley blocking one clear doorway and the Governess's single route around it. A simple overhead layout should be possible from the words alone.

**Classification:** Actual action-continuity ambiguity. This needs resolution before boards are asked to preserve the sequence.

### 3. Medium priority — trolley procedure dilutes the climax

**Scene:** S11, with setup in S10.

The stop/release test, first move, tiny-bed interruption, second proposed move, refusal, alternate turn, corner spotting, pennant clearance, final brake, and bag redistribution create many small logistical beats. Most do not change the emotional problem. Maya and Mateo's collaborative solution is there, but the operational explanation obscures its shape.

**Repair:** Keep three clear changes: Mateo frees the Giant's attention; the tiny bed unexpectedly occupies the intended parking spot; Maya trusts Mateo to guide one alternate maneuver while Mopsy opens the seam. Consolidate the brake operation and bag management into concise action. Keep Maya's stable support and the clear floor route, without explaining each absent hazard.

**Classification:** Actual pacing drag in the read. Its exact screen duration remains an unmeasured production question.

### 4. Medium priority — the shared hospitality mechanism needs sharper escalation

**Scenes:** S03–S10.

The Crawler supplies unwanted coats, the Coatcheck retains unwanted bags, the Butler assigns unwanted accommodation, and the Governess supplies unwanted bedtime. These are distinct designs but repeatedly make the same social mistake: service continues after a refusal. The nursery does escalate from inconvenience to confinement, which is useful; the transition could be faster and more decisive.

**Repair:** Preserve the coat-room comic set piece and the Governess's locked-door escalation. Trim repeated requests for an animal answer during the Butler/nursery transition, or make the Butler furnish one genuinely useful fact before redirecting them. Let the audience feel a new problem when they enter the nursery, rather than another attempt to correct a literal-minded employee.

**Classification:** Repetition concern, not a premise blocker. This recurring trait can remain the house's organizing comic idea.

### 5. Medium priority — practical repair between Maya and Mateo is stronger than emotional repair

**Scenes:** S04, S06, S10–S11, S15.

S04 already allows Maya to accept Mateo's attention to Marmalade. She subsequently dismisses their bell question and finally says another question will get them another bed. Mateo closing the notebook supplies a clear hurt reaction. “Ask her” and the blind-corner trust do repair the working relationship, but Maya never quite owns the personal dismissal. The closing shared question mark is lovely; it has slightly more thematic work to do than the preceding exchange has earned.

**Repair:** Add one brief, character-specific acknowledgment near “What were you going to ask?” or after the escape: Maya admits she cut them off or recognizes that their question found the way out. No speech or prescribed apology ritual is needed. Preserve Mateo allowing her to listen to Orbit's possible scratches in S13; that is an excellent reciprocal adjustment.

**Classification:** Character refinement, not an absent arc. The repair currently exists through behavior.

### 6. Low–medium priority — Orbit's identifying scratch habit is revealed at payoff

**Scenes:** S07 and S13.

The recorded scratches are fairly planted in the new recording, and the script carefully separates it from Pepper's old clip. However, the viewer cannot recognize their particular relevance until Maya explains Orbit's three-scratch habit in S13. This is a fair discovery for Maya, but only partly a clue the audience can solve alongside her. Three scratches also cannot establish which animal made them; the script wisely does not claim certainty.

**Repair:** If audience inference is desired, seed the habit briefly in S01 through an incidental remembered detail. Otherwise retain the current uncertainty and do not market this beat as a solved mystery. The ledger is already a stronger concrete discovery than the sound.

**Classification:** Mystery-participation opportunity, not cheating or a continuity error.

### 7. Low priority — the ensemble is recognizable before it is equally voiced

**Scenes:** throughout, especially S01 and S11.

Samir's relentless courtesy, Priya's ordering instinct, and Jordan's practical physical help are distinct. Eli's treasured screw and Lena's button/sightline vigilance provide good visual signatures. Amina is considerate but less individually audible. Eight missing pets and eight owners are a considerable initial memory demand, even though the script sensibly avoids a spoken roll call.

**Repair:** Do not add introductions. Reassign one existing observation or decision to Amina that exposes how she thinks, and let one quiet supporting child cause a small consequential turn rather than only assist with clearance. Longer-term ensemble distribution is a season question; this pilot need not provide eight equal arcs.

**Classification:** Ensemble-development opportunity, not a requirement to enlarge this episode.

## Elements to protect

- **Mopsy's choice is earned.** She repairs a chair before receiving attention, asks before working on the bag, enjoys a mismatched contribution, and names a specific destination. Refusing to close the seam grows from that established personhood. “Here first” prevents the children from replacing one imposed plan with another.
- **The Governess's hesitation is concrete.** She values Mopsy's repair and chooses to preserve it. This does not suddenly make her understand freedom or resolve her controlling habits. The borrowed blanket leaves the relationship open.
- **Several comedy mechanisms have their own character.** The Coatrack dresses the bench; the Button Baby offers an even larger button; the Giant's pillow erases its tiny bed. Those are stronger than generic spooky pratfalls.
- **Maya's crutches are ordinary equipment.** They inform routes and staging without causing the joke, becoming an obstacle to personhood, or disappearing through a magical cure. Editorial compression should preserve that physical specificity.
- **Missing pets remain distinct from supernatural Companions.** Marmalade is visibly different from Orbit, and Mopsy's material existence is distinct from Marmalade's ghostly passage. No pet is declared dead, transformed, or replaced.
- **The ending is poignant rather than manipulative.** The parent neither falsely reassures nor extracts a speech. Maya eats, shares evidence, accepts uncertainty, and packs a familiar object. The bowl earns the hold.

## Release judgment and limits

There is no content, pronoun, disability-treatment, or tonal blocker identified in this read. The two concrete storyboarding concerns are the unused exit and the unshown side-switch around the trolley. Address those and streamline the same sequence before treating the script as locked.

Runtime, laugh timing, the readability of eight children in wide shots, and whether the closing hold feels quiet or prolonged require a timed read or animatic. This review cannot validate 22 minutes. Likewise, fidelity to the original game/source ledger and real children's comprehension were not tested. The present recommendation is **targeted revision, then timed/visual validation**, with the premise, Mopsy arc, strongest gags, and kitchen ending intact.
