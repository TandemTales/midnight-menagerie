## MM101-v2 findings

### V1-101-01 — High, S08/S11/S12: why is the original entrance still unusable?

S08 blocks the Playroom entrance with a curtain and the bed trolley. In S11 the Giant moves that trolley into Bedtime Hall; after the kids move it aside, the original entrance appears clear. The elaborate Mending Closet escape can therefore feel unnecessary. A curtain alone is not an established locked barrier.

**Actual revision direction:** In S08 show the Governess fastening the original entrance's visible catch as part of her routine, and make its operating side inaccessible from the bedtime suite; or leave a separate, clearly established empty bedframe blocking that route. Do not invent a fresh barrier during S11. In the escape wide shot, show original closed door, Giant's movable obstruction and Mending Closet opening together so the audience understands why this route matters.

### V1-101-02 — Medium, S09/S10/S12: the saved blanket repair has not been seen

Mopsy visibly repairs a rocker strap and Mateo's bag. S10 then calls a long blanket “newly repaired,” and S12 rests the Governess's difficult choice on sparing Mopsy's specific work on that blanket. The beat is promising but currently relies on an unshown repair.

**Actual revision direction:** Show the blanket's distinctive repaired corner in the initial care/mending scene, preferably as a brief action replacing redundant repair business. Use the same stitch/color in S12 when it catches. The repaired bag still matters because Mateo grants permission; the third unrelated repair need not become a sequence.

### V1-101-03 — Medium, S10/S11: locate Mopsy before the trolley blocks the closet

S11 says Mopsy unpicks the fixed return seam although the bed trolley prevents passage into the closet. This can work if she is already inside, but her position is implied rather than shown.

**Actual revision direction:** End S10 with Mopsy inside the Mending Closet, visible at the return seam, and keep that sightline in the next wide shot. She can hear and answer the children through the blocked doorway; do not require her to teleport past the trolley.

### V1-101-04 — Low, S07: specify the bell that is answering Samir

The scene establishes a distant Nursery receiver and local Foyer bell, then says he is supplying both sides of a greeting through the directly linked bell above him. Exact cause and sound direction are not quite clear.

**Actual revision direction:** Show the local striker move only when his elbow/hand moves the linked return cord, or choose one unambiguous two-end demonstration. It should read as an innocent physical misunderstanding rather than a conversation with a mysterious bell intelligence. This matters because the finale later depends on limited, traceable signals.

### V1-101-05 — Medium, S04/S10/S15: preserve some imperfect child friction

Many beats explicitly say Maya asks rather than orders, recognizes her mistake, lets Mateo ask, and adds their question to the map. The progression is sensible, but scripting every beat as immediate courteous self-correction would produce very polished children.

**Actual revision direction:** Let the same interruption or misplaced certainty cost them one small practical opportunity before the repair. Maya's S10 snap already does this: keep the awkward beat of Mateo actually stopping, then have her notice the lost useful question. Do not add an apology speech. Preserve the quiet S15 difference between “found his name” and “found him”; the parent's restrained response gives the pilot its strongest emotional ending.

**Keep:** the ghost-cat coat distraction, Giant's tiny favorite bed, Mopsy's independent exit choice, Marmalade's unprompted return, two separately dated recordings, and the final bowl. No new major age/tone concern found.

## MM106-v1 findings

### V1-106-01 — High, S09–S10 with MM107-S08: repeated escape solution

Maya holds a low release; another character holds or reaches a high control; an empty trolley exposes a built-in safety stop; the group crosses after it latches. MM107-S08 repeats nearly the same solution. MM101 already uses an empty bed's brake and MM104 a walkway catch. The exact choreography is accessible, but accessibility has become the same story job for Maya and the same audience experience for every obstacle.

**Actual revision direction:** Change one whole set-piece mechanism, not only the operator. For example, keep the Warden's five visible latches but have the fixed floor dots reveal a **shared route the Warden's movable wall cannot occupy**, allowing the kids to redirect its false-arrow trap rather than wedge another sliding barrier. Give Maya the decisive route observation or timing choice, not another compulsory lever hold. Preserve a visible fair setup, a failed attempt, Warden opposition and everyone crossing together. The subsequent kennel delivery mechanism can then keep its distinct cart behavior.

### V1-106-02 — Medium, S08/S12: a second protected sightline weakens the escape

S08 establishes one fixed glass view. The Warden covers its approach with movable panels, and the children escape. S12 immediately produces another angled view that the Warden “cannot operate” because it is sealed. But the first glass was also sealed; the Warden covered the access rather than operating the pane. This new view appears exactly when an emotional tag needs it and makes the escape geography harder to understand.

**Actual revision direction:** Either plant the second, physically separate sightline before the Warden appears and explain its stationary passage boundaries, or keep the closing beat on the dry Pipe Gallery landing with Lena completing her Museum drawing for the next meeting. She can put her button beside it and commit to going around; actual reciprocal pet contact can wait for the already-planned ward view in 107. Do not claim sealed glass itself prevents a wall covering it.

### V1-106-03 — Medium, S11: unestablished direct Crinkle readiness link

The scene says a speaking-tube label leads to a single matching bell answered by Crinkle at a “known service station.” MM104 establishes Crinkle at the Map Room with specific Foyer and Astronomy Library pairs. MM106 introduces this Pipe Gallery contact without showing the second endpoint or Crinkle's relocation.

**Actual revision direction:** Use a named, previously recorded pair and demonstrate its two endpoints, or let Crinkle's delivered route strips specify one newly shown service receiver before the children depend on it. A return ring means “heard,” not proof all stations are ready. Do not smuggle in a direct estate-wide intercom after making limited bells a mystery rule.

### V1-106-04 — Medium, S01–S07: Lena corrects more than she risks

Eli repeatedly repairs the wrong thing; Lena repeatedly points to the useful destination. Their cooperation is warm, but the relationship risks making her his error detector. The shared privacy scene in S07 is the closest to a genuine contested value.

**Actual revision direction:** Give Lena one specific reason to want a particular route checked despite Eli's apparently sensible repair priority, and let that preference have a consequence. Her Mooncake concern can make her too ready to interpret a collection clue, then Eli's precise observation helps her locate the actual ward-side barrier. Do not convert her into an infallible silent expert or make withholding urgent information the gag.

### V1-106-05 — Low, S08 and exit ledger: ordinary Peephole is not actually staged

The exit ledger claims the ordinary Peephole performs specific work, but the scenes describe a glass pane and no observable Peephole actor.

**Actual revision direction:** Give the sourced creature a small useful obstruction/reveal in S08—its shutter frames the view, then it moves away to disclose the fixed glass—or remove the coverage claim. No new fight is required.

**Keep:** single-handle custody trail; Curator keeps his standards; rotten internal panel versus sound stone arch; proof of Mooncake alive before any fight; original button never passes through glass; no scary pet remains. The Curator/Minotaur sorting business is clear and original enough when not overexplained.

## MM107-v1 findings

### V1-107-01 — High, S11: the signal montage changes established residents' locations

Mopsy has chosen Umbrella Gallery, Wisp Glow Chamber, and Crinkle Map Room. S11 places them at Musicians' Gallery and the roof/Attic station with no visible request, choice or travel. It labels those places recognizably established, but the **people at those places** are newly arranged. The three remote trios also alter MM104/MM105's explicit limited signal chain. A readiness pattern cannot itself explain relocation.

**Actual revision direction:** Prefer preserving established posts and handoffs: Foyer to Crinkle's Map Room receiver; Crinkle to Wink's Astronomy Library receiver; Foyer to Crumbula's Musicians' Gallery receiver; Crumbula to Wisp's Glow Chamber receiver. If the finale needs physical trios, add their voluntary relocation along known joins in MM106's courier setup, then use recognizable travel strips/room labels at the first new station shot. Do not call the new grouping an already-shown fact.

### V1-107-02 — High, S11: five seconds per trio cannot establish the stated action

Each five-second response shot must orient a region, identify three creatures, show separate affirmative decisions, demonstrate individual practical jobs and communicate readiness. Mopsy's knot, Taffy's adhesive and Crumbula's aisle alone are three actions. These will be unreadable if compressed into one crowded shot; expanding them silently will consume the emotional threshold beat.

**Actual revision direction:** Reduce readiness responses to one unmistakable signal per known chain here, and stage the actual assistance when it matters in 108. Alternatively allocate real time and shorten another scene. The montage should not be a sixteen-character roll call pretending to be consent and mechanism explanation.

### V1-107-03 — Medium, S08/S10/S11: Maya's agency is again a low control

S08 gives her the low gate lever and S10–S11 another low frame pin, after 106's lever escape. The repeated assigned role is not made rich by being reachable.

**Actual revision direction:** Give the frame pin to another child. Let Maya notice that the permanent stone arch already fits the pumpkin while the movable ceremonial hoop does not; then Jordan makes his bounded strength offer. Her contribution becomes the consequential spatial judgment. More broadly vary her role across observation, humor, negotiation, initiative and mistaken judgment, not only access-control competence.

### V1-107-04 — Medium, S06: Amina's question is adult thematic coaching

“Has the closed gate helped Scout answer them?” is hard to parse and sounds crafted to lead Jordan toward a moral answer. The audience has just heard a bark, so the question also tangles the physical fact: Scout can answer through the grille even though the gate is closed.

**Actual revision direction:** Make the question practical and child-sized: which side did the sound come from, and will another counted bowl get them any closer? Let Jordan notice the mismatch himself. Preserve Amina's selfish warm-towel mistake, which usefully prevents her becoming only the wise counselor.

### V1-107-05 — Medium, S05–S09: too many latch demonstrations obscure the central contrast

Perfect Pen manual release, courtyard lever, cart's tidying arm, wooden wedge, metal delivery stop, normal close cord, inspection and later stop acknowledgment occupy much of the Kennels segment. The underlying story is clear—care works, an animal still needs a way out—but six control terms could drown it.

**Actual revision direction:** Keep the Perfect Pen's soft-wall refusal as the emotional demonstration and the cart's collection of a loose wedge as the comic failed attempt. Limit the final gate operation to one visible existing delivery catch with a clear before/after state. Avoid separately explaining every handle and safety feature. The caretaker handoff must stay visible; do not simplify by abandoning the frightened resident.

**Keep:** Amina's towel tag causes her own problem; real veterinary care is shown; Scout's sound comes from the inner grille; Jordan declines a redundant task only after handoff; King wants the intact pumpkin for himself; the seven onsite/nine remote arithmetic correctly accounts for sixteen Companions.

## MM108-v1 findings

### V1-108-01 — High, S12 and decision list: the pictured friend changes identity

MM105-S09 and architecture v4 explicitly show Bean with a Button Baby identified by one blue button. This break calls the friend Mopsy, identified by a patch pattern, then tells the earlier episode to match. That is a substantive upstream rewrite, not a continuity fact already established. The warm reunion beat does not require substituting a Companion for the existing resident.

**Actual revision direction:** Keep the Button Baby and blue button if the greeting remains, showing the resident arriving through an already clear service route. It can be a silent brief visit without a new arc. Alternatively omit this greeting and let Samir preserve the drawing through the home shot. Only change the earlier picture if the showrunner explicitly adopts and records that replacement across all related files; do not leave two identities for the same picture.

### V1-108-02 — Medium, S12/S14: departure register location and one-minute overload

S12 contains six children returning to their animals, all eight preparing carriers/leads/perches, the friend arrival and recognition, a social greeting, all eight pairs crossing, the House's hesitation, and Mateo marking the old departure entry. One minute is extremely tight for the promised unhurried agency. The established old guest register is at House Register Alcove in the Foyer, not at this exterior porch.

**Actual revision direction:** Move the register action to the later Foyer supply visit in S14, when its actual location is accessible. In S05 establish the familiar carriers already open beside the resting animals so S12 need not unpack eight systems. Let two or three grouped preparations represent the work, with one wide shot accounting for all eight pairs. If the friend greeting remains, budget its own visible beat rather than layering it over departure logistics.

### V1-108-03 — Medium, S06: four lock names appear on a plan without a prior plant

The earlier copied plan shows an ordinary release control and route; this scene says the four named Keeper locks were already drawn there. That adds detailed supernatural information to an old architectural document at the moment it becomes useful. It also risks making the finale a four-term memorization exercise.

**Actual revision direction:** Let the names be visibly engraved on the actual Keeper/control structures as they come into view, using only the one or two terms necessary for immediate dialogue. The copied drawing supplies the spindle match; the present structure supplies present facts. Do not needlessly establish a history explaining why the Keeper's manifestation was named on an old plan.

### V1-108-04 — Medium, S06–S10: too much operative exposition for one emotional confrontation

Handle/socket, four lock names, linked shutters, three station circuits, response lamps, floor drainage, wheel brakes, chock, padded contact, flag, held spindle and exterior view are all individually legible. Together they ask a young viewer to process a machine manual while watching the Keeper learn from a cat. The geographical axis is much clearer than architecture v1, but the control density has migrated into the scene descriptions.

**Actual revision direction:** Retain three broad observable states: door closes when they let go; furniture and friends make it stay open temporarily; Keeper stops fighting the opening so everyone can leave. Every detail must serve one of those states. Where a remote action merely confirms another confirmation, remove a signal cut or name. The audience does not need to know every lamp circuit if it can see the affected passage become usable. Preserve the exact background continuity for production while cutting explanatory dialogue.

### V1-108-05 — Medium, S07–S11: vary Maya's active contribution

Maya operates the low latch in S02, sits at the low spindle in S06, turns it in S07 and S10, then visibly releases it in S11. This repeats 101/104/106/107's controlling-lever role. The participation is practical and safe, but it limits her focal role to machine operation plus the moral request.

**Actual revision direction:** Her S02 empty-bag hypothesis is promising active thought: make that her decisive practical move, and let Eli/Jordan take primary spindle labor later. Give Maya the consequential observation or choice that preserves the returning cat's freedom—without ordering the demonstration or announcing its moral—while the others handle the mechanism. She should have a specific personal response to Orbit's changed choice, not only oversee everyone's successful choice.

### V1-108-06 — Medium, S09: preserve ongoing opposition during remote help

The ninety-second remote sequence is more credible than 107's five-second trios, but the Keeper's present resistance may appear suspended while nine residents finish neatly sequenced tasks. Readiness diagrams alone are not dramatic pressure.

**Actual revision direction:** Intercut each useful remote effect with a simple change at the foreground doorway: bedding stops arriving, a shutter clears, exterior light becomes visible. Keep the Keeper's closing pressure visible in the same repeated axis. Do not add injury peril or threaten the resting pets. This makes the help part of the current struggle instead of a roll call of ability demonstrations.

**Keep:** five protected minutes of physical pet recognition; all eight pets with two clearly assigned children in view; no silent caregiver rotation; Pudding's freely chosen rest; source-compatible household Keeper form; single handle restored to its actual control; Marmalade's continuous out-and-back crossing; hands-off test proving release is stable; ordinary home habits without eight speeches. These improvements earn the full warmth/tone marks at break level.
