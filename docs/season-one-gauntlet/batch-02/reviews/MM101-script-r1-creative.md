## Structural and character revisions

### S1 — Medium — MM101-S04: Mateo's sixteen-arms question weakens their investigative identity

During a practical bag emergency, Mateo asks whether sixteen hooks mean the rack once had sixteen arms. The idea is neither tested nor later useful. They become randomly distractible, and their useful cat observation then feels accidental. Their source-based flaw is premature pattern inference, which is richer than unrelated chatter. Direction: replace this with a specific mistaken hypothesis about how the Coatcheck chooses or counts bags. Let Mateo try a harmless test that fails visibly, then notice that the rack tracks the coat instead of the number. Their curiosity remains funny and produces the successful correction. This is a substantive scene revision, not merely a sharper quip.

### S2 — Medium — MM101-S11: the principal physical escape needs a developed complication

Once the Giant selects its own bed, the brake release, trolley movement and seam opening all succeed in one uninterrupted chain. This is clear, but the script promises a sizable climax after the long escalation of confinement. It currently offers one plan with little resistance beyond a single distraction. Direction: add one **harmless, visible** complication created by the Giant's sincere choice—for example, its tiny chosen bed temporarily occupies the intended trolley parking bay. The kids must modify the plan while preserving the Giant's little comfort rather than shove or damage it. Maya should entrust a precise part of that revised plan to Mateo or another child. Keep the geography and original locked door unchanged; do not introduce a new key, danger to a body, or another moral test. This can add useful action time and a different comic rhythm without another multiplying-object sequence.

### S3 — Medium — MM101-S07: upper and lower Nursery sightlines contradict the lift action

The script shows the Nursery bell 'across the landing' through its open door while the children are still below the lift. They subsequently rise a level to that Nursery door. Direction: explicitly establish the upper landing, its open door and visible bell **above** the gallery, with the children on the lower floor. The wire and answering hand can still be seen across the stairwell. Then follow their actual ascent. This matters because the same landing/return opening later carries the escape.

### S4 — Medium — MM101-S11: trolley load is not empty

The trolley begins piled with eight frames and mattresses. The Giant lifts one tiny bed, after which the script calls the moving trolley empty. It still carries the other furniture unless that unloading is shown. Direction: call it an **unoccupied loaded trolley** and preserve the load during movement, or explicitly unfold/set aside the other beds before the push. Avoid making the animation team invent the change. The brake action also needs one consistent visible release/set position; keep this as production clarity rather than dialogue exposition.

### S5 — Low — MM101-S09/S13: Mopsy's voiced boundaries occasionally sound like the production thesis

'I fix the broken bit' fits her practical economy and should stay. 'If I want to help' followed by 'Fair' is accurate but sounds like the writers checking a consent requirement. Direction: retain choice through a warmer specific answer or action—she can name the sort of help she wants to give, or indicate that a ring is an invitation she will answer when able. Do not remove the actual permission beat; let the dialogue express a particular resident rather than a general policy.

## Comedy and dialogue pass

### J1 — Low — MM101-S07: the self-greeting bell gag risks making Samir implausibly oblivious

The short LOCAL TEST cord is plainly labeled and in his hand; he pulls it, hears the direct result, then greets the bell twice. At twelve, his sociability is a stronger comic flaw than failing to understand a simple cord. Direction: let him initially mistake a **delayed** return ring for a reply, or know that he is rehearsing the proper greeting and become too ceremonious. Lena's pointing then exposes a social performance rather than basic cause-and-effect confusion. Keep the two actual room endpoints unambiguous.

### J2 — Low — MM101-S05: preserve Mateo's dignity in the blank-page gag

The performed 'Yes. I was saving that' is funnier and more characterful than the action line treating a blank page as astonishing. Direction: play it as someone stubbornly trying to fit related evidence together on one page, not someone forgetting notebooks have more pages. Their choice has a reason, even if it is an inconvenient one.

### J3 — Low — MM101-S12: 'Show-off' is the most generic quip in an otherwise specific scene

It names the obvious cat trick but adds little to Mateo's relation with Marmalade or the escape. It also arrives at the start of an emotionally significant separation. Direction: cut it or replace it with a short response linked to a question Mateo actually asked the cat earlier. The silent imperious cat behavior may be sufficient. Do not add a joke while the repaired blanket is under tension.

### J4 — Low — MM101-S05: the painted cat is a second supernatural cat introduction

The visual joke is pleasant, but a separate cat stepping bodily out of a portrait immediately after Marmalade's introduction could make younger viewers wonder whether it is another Companion to remember. Direction: keep it visibly an animated painted image leaning to the frame edge, with no independent floor travel, or make the body clearly flat painted material. This is not a demand to remove all background magic; it prevents the first Companion's important identity being blurred.

## Runtime and performance assessment

A simple whitespace count found approximately **5,414 total tokens/words-as-whitespace-items**, including headings, character cues and action. A cue-based count found approximately **949 spoken words**. Counts are diagnostic and approximate; they are not industry page counts. Spoken dialogue alone could occupy roughly seven to nine minutes depending on delivery and pauses. A 22-minute episode would therefore need substantial motivated visual action, travel, reactions and emotional holds.

That is possible for animation. It is not proven merely because the preceding break totals 22:00. The strongest places for real nonverbal time are the noticeboard, coat retrieval, competing bedside preparations, trolley escape, and Mopsy's choice. The weakest way to reach length would be to stretch every silent look or repeat the same welcome gesture. S11's proposed new complication should earn time through changing action.

Before claiming the target has been reached, make a scene-duration estimate from **this revised script**, separating spoken delivery from explicit visual beats, or perform a timed read with shot/action allowances. Pay particular attention to S01's eight-character introduction, S04's repeated rack behavior, S09's two repairs and route explanation, and S13–S15's three distinct emotional releases. Exact runtime remains unmeasured until performance/animatic work.

Dialogue generally fits mouths: short requests, interruptions and incomplete thoughts are usable. The Butler's 'How fortunate. Then you know what to do with them' is a good dry dodge; the Governess's whiskered cushion is a clear failed kindness; Mopsy's 'Then I can bring it back' earns the sanctuary idea through a concrete object. Do not replace these with explanatory speeches.

## Ending and tone audit

The ending is **not empty**. The children retain an arrival record, the new scratch lead, an accessible route, and a relationship with a newly mobile resident. Maya changes her map to include Mateo's question. The bowl is an active decision to search again, not only an image of sadness.

Keep the protected beats: Maya correcting 'He's here' to 'His name is here'; Mateo listening rather than filling the scratch pause; Mopsy choosing the doorway while the Governess chooses to spare a repair; the parent accepting the distinction between finding a name and finding a pet. No new joke should intrude into those exact decisions.

The House's real care is visible through the repaired rocker, carefully made cushion and mending. Its wrongness is concrete—the locked Nursery door and refused departure—not an explanation that all comfort is suspicious. No pet is used as bait or a reward, no cure is offered, and the children's identities are not comic targets. Maya has real practical agency without a mobility miracle.
