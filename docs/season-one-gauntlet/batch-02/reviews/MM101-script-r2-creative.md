## Remaining fixes

### R2-1 — Low, required production clarification — MM101-S08 through MM101-S11: establish one bed assembly and load state

S08 calls the arriving object an 'empty bed trolley piled high with folded frames and mattresses.' S09 says the Giant is busy unfolding frames. S10 places cards beside empty beds. S11 specifies seven folded frames and mattresses still aboard after one tiny bed is removed. 'Empty' can sometimes mean unoccupied, but that does not account for the unfolding and apparent separate beds. A board artist must currently decide which furniture exists on the floor and which remains on the trolley; that decision controls the escape space.

Direction: explicitly keep seven frames folded aboard throughout, with the Giant testing and refolding one sample or only preparing bedding before selecting its tiny bed. Alternatively, establish a different consistent arrangement and amend the S11 count. Replace S08 'empty' with 'unoccupied' or remove the adjective. Keep the cards on a defined stand or on the still-stacked beds. No added dialogue is necessary. Preserve the successful brake, bag and human-width passage states already present.

### R2-2 — Low to medium staging risk — MM101-S11: anchor the Governess during the longer maneuver

The Governess lifts the blanket and approaches before the children test the brake, divert the Giant, attempt the first route, stop, negotiate the tiny bed, plan another route, pivot and distribute bags. She finally rounds the trolley after the group crosses into the closet. The trolley apparently blocks her as well as the kids, which is a sensible answer, but the text does not explicitly place her on the other side of the blocked arch. If she begins on their side, the extra action makes her wait conspicuously.

Direction: one concrete staging sentence can place her beyond the trolley and preserve a readable barrier between her and the children. Her ongoing care could also involve keeping the large blanket out of the wheels while she waits for a path. Do not add a second distraction puzzle or make her inept. During boarding, retain her active desire to settle the visitors rather than cutting her out for the entire maneuver. This is a clarity repair to a successful substantive revision, not a request for another complication.

### R2-3 — Low, optional comedy polish — MM101-S12: generic cat tag

'Show-off' still contributes less than the surrounding specific physical comedy. A silent look from Mateo followed by using the actual doorway can carry the difference without another line. Cut unless the performed reaction earns its space. Do not replace it with a larger joke during the blanket decision.

## Comedy and character judgment

The opening still clusters misunderstandings of hospitality: unsuitable coats, unwanted bag storage, unsuitable rooms and unsuitable bedding. That repetition is thematic and currently changes function sufficiently: the coat business creates a debt and repaired strap; Butler redirects the search; Governess removes the exit; Giant's personal comfort reshapes the escape. There is no need to manufacture a different theme for each. The danger would be extending every encounter with an additional oversized-object joke merely to reach time.

Maya's and Mateo's new S11 exchange is the strongest relationship improvement. 'You don't need to. Tell me when it's clear' expresses trust through a task with necessary information on both sides. It does not require an apology speech or make Maya abandon planning. Mateo also learns to stop a failed physical test in S04, then later stops talking during the recorded scratch. These are different forms of attention, not repeated moral statements.

Samir's bag-passing loop is still broader than his bell routine, but it can work as divided attention while watching Mateo's predicament; Priya's gentle correction prevents the joke from becoming humiliation. Do not extend either loop beyond the written beats. Mopsy's umbrella appointment adds welcome pleasure to her agency. The Giant's insistence that its tiny bed stay put is funny because its choice matters physically to the protagonists, not because the show announces a lesson about choice.

The principal voices remain performable. Short interrupted requests and visual answers do most of the work. The Butler's room fold, Governess's whiskered cushion and Mopsy's returning-blanket exchange need breathing room; hurried line delivery would lose the contrast between real care and the wrong answer. The smaller ensemble members have recognizable behaviors here, but this pilot alone cannot certify their season-long voice differentiation.

## Runtime and ending

The revised escape supplies motivated visual duration through stopping, inspecting, changing direction, coordinating an unseen corner and crossing with possessions. This is a better use of nonverbal time than added conversational filler. It does not establish that the whole episode runs 22 minutes. No performance or animatic has been timed in this review, and the earlier break's timing cannot substitute for the revised script.

Next validation should assign an action-aware duration to each actual scene, then test the estimate with performance/boards. Watch S01's eight introductions, S04's hook retrieval, S09's repairs and S11's maneuver for readable rather than prolonged business. S13–S15 perform distinct releases—new choice, outside access, then private resolve—and should not all be played at the same solemn speed.

The ending remains satisfying for a first installment: a willing resident reaches her chosen refuge; the group physically gets out; they retain names and a fair new clue; Maya has changed how she represents uncertainty and prepares for another visit. The bowl is a decision, not an empty substitute for a climax. Keep the protected register correction, the scratch-listening silence, the Governess sparing Mopsy's stitches and the parent's acceptance of partial progress.

Retain the substantive v2 character and escape revisions. Make R2-1 and R2-2 precise in the next text pass; decide R2-3 by performance value.
