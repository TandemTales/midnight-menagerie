## Remaining concrete concerns

### Medium — episode 3, Priya/Jordan relationship

Priya's habitat/stone decision and Jordan's ceremonial parade each serve the theme, but they do not yet strongly affect each other's choices. This is a relative weakness, not a fatal missing story: the episode has a clear objective, comic escalation, discovery and ending. At the break, let one person's attempted help impose a cost on the other inside the existing parade or habitat action, then require a small reciprocal adjustment. Do not add a separate argument scene or reduce either boss to a cameo. The episode need not repeat the verbal argument shape of episodes 2 and 4.

### Medium — A106-02 through A106-07 and A107-10 through A107-11, pace versus absorption

The changes align objectives; they do not demonstrate how much time the reader will need to recognize each mechanism. Episode 6 still introduces Curator custody, the reconstruction joke, rot/compost mechanics, the private-pocket choice and a route transition before Mooncake contact. Episode 7 still needs the audience to understand why a ceremonial frame is removable and why moving the intact pumpkin reveals the marker. These are now script-stage risks. In the break, count actual visual discoveries and allow reaction beats; trim repeat display/measuring business first. Preserve every boss's refusal and material consequence. If the timing fails, disclose a revised duration rather than accelerate the emotional beats.

### Low — episode 5 Red Page picture and episode 8 friend recognition

“A small drawing Bean has chewed while sitting beside a friendly resident” identifies the circumstances of chewing more clearly than the contents of the picture. Episode 8 requires Samir to recognize that resident from the picture. Make the image visibly depict Bean beside the same identifiable resident, with familiar chew marks corroborating Bean's contact with the paper. This is a wording/prop specification, not a need for a new clue sequence. Avoid requiring the Red Page to explain that a chew mark proves Bean enjoyed the company; Samir is responding to the possibility, not obtaining forensic proof of happiness.

### Low — final beat ordering and repair paragraphs

Episode 1 still says “End on” the welcome mat before appending the home beat within the same tag. Episodes 2, 4 and 5 also describe events and then add repair paragraphs explaining where more actions belong. Their intended integration is understandable, but a scene-break writer should not accidentally stage both the original sequence and a second version of it. Consolidate those paragraphs into one chronological beat order while breaking episodes. Choose whether the bowl or mat is the final image; both can fit if their positions are explicit.

## Execution gates, not invented architecture blockers

Visual comedy remains stronger evidence than dialogue comedy. The welcome mat, coat passing through Marmalade, towel trolley and tiny sandwich are readable gags. Distinctive dialogue cannot be certified from a few samples. Test actual conversations for interchangeable dry lines and excessive explanation of what the audience already sees.

The emotional additions should be played briefly, with disagreement and generosity both visible. Samir does not need to explain the meaning of turning the picture back over. Eli does not need a speech about privacy. Maya's family moment benefits from the ordinary bowl and extra portion, not an explanation of what they symbolize.
