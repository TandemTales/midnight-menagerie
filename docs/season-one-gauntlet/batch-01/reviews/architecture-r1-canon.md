# Architecture round 1 — independent canon and children's-tone review

Reviewer: canon audit agent. Reviewed `03-season-architecture-v1.md`, `01-source-ledger.md`, `02-mansion-coverage.md`, and targeted source passages/registry searches. No other critique or writer defense was read. Child-oriented observations are an editorial simulation, not actual child testing. Scores assess this architecture only; dialogue, timing, and laugh effectiveness remain provisional until scripts/animatics exist.

## Verdict

**Revise before episode breaks. 76/100.** The warmth, agency thesis, all-region ambition, and Keeper identity are strong. Major canon/logic issues require revision irrespective of score: invented encounters are treated as game elites, Mooncake can apparently be reached but is not rescued, the central door mechanism changes without enough physical rules, and late episodes compress three guardian plots into twenty minutes. Several guardian resolutions are variations of making a correct point and being permitted onwards. The child may follow the moral idea while losing what the children are physically trying to do.

## Required findings

### R1-C01 — Major: game encounter names are invented or misclassified

**Locator:** E3 Bell Mourner/Grave Candles; E4 Paper Moth/Great Web; E5 Furnace Saint/“elite Last Dance”; E7 Cistern Giant/Rescue Cage/Seedlings/Pumpkin Coach.

**Problem:** The coverage matrix does not substantiate these as regional enemies/elites, and targeted searches across `docs/design/regions` and `game/src/data` found no such named entities except Seedling in the Greenhouse. “Last Dance” is a boss phase in `10-ballroom.md:701`, not the elite's name; the actual elite is The Eternal Dance. Door Greeter and Red Carpet Runner are real, despite not being the coverage table's examples. All four named Heart elite encounters are real.

**Fix direction:** Replace invented encounters with actual regional actors whose behavior can drive the comic beat: Epitaph Choir/Mourning Angel; Rafter Seer/Great Orrery/Moon Lens; Chandelier/Blackout Beast/Great Lantern; Eternal Dance; Boiler Bellower/Flooded Reflection/Storm Bath; Collar Collector/Rolling Ward/Perfect Pen; Moon Scarecrow/Gourd Knight/Great Root. Use actual Pumpkin Pip/Moonseed rather than relocating Greenhouse Seedlings invisibly. If an original incidental servant remains, label it as a new show character and do not count it toward game encounter coverage. Correct “Kennels and Hunting Halls” to the current region name. Re-audit specific actions after replacements; renaming alone is insufficient.

### R1-C02 — Major: Mooncake is within reach, then left behind without a constraint

**Locator:** E6 Secret Passages voluntary appearance and ending; E7 all pets visible in Pet Holding Ward.

**Problem:** Mooncake takes Lena's button and receives a card. “Don't scoop him up” protects agency but does not explain why Lena cannot offer a carrier and leave the choice open. A child viewer may reasonably conclude Lena walks away from her found pet to preserve the episode plan. His relocation to the Ward by E7 is also untracked.

**Fix direction:** Make the sighting through an observation seam across the Heart boundary with a visible physical barrier; the button can pass through a small shared aperture but neither pet nor child can. Establish why breaking/opening that seam is impossible or would seal it, then locate Mooncake on the far side in the Ward. Alternatively rescue him here and explicitly track him at home; do not transfer him back into captivity for the finale. The former keeps the proposed finale structure.

### R1-C03 — Major: the door/handle payoff has no stable physical rule

**Locator:** E6 “historically important bone” handle, Gardener lets it go because it cannot grow, GOING HOME door; E8 handle attached to a working opening, Keeper shuts it, final lock released.

**Problem:** The handle appears to be a bone, a substitute, a material that cannot grow, and then a magic key. What does it actually enable? If a working opening already exists, why can the Keeper close it? What effort changes the Keeper's capacity or decision? The architecture has a thematic answer, but lacks a fair practical one.

**Fix direction:** Make the original exit hardware unmistakable and show a limited function before the finale: it lets an occupant operate a door from inside, but does not release the House's return lock. A visible harmless demonstration establishes this. Finale action preserves the inside handle and stops automatic relocking; the Keeper's last choice should be enabled by what it sees, not an unexplained moral spell. Keep a metal/wood material consistent; replace the arbitrary “cannot grow” justification with the Gardener's already demonstrated valuation of reusable/compostable parts.

### R1-C04 — Major: chronology, regrouping, and animal whereabouts are not production-ready

**Locator:** E1 returns outside; E2 next visit; E3–E8 implied sequence; E7 sixteen Companions contribute across estate; E8 all reunited at home.

**Problem:** It is unclear whether the children make eight visits, return to a refuge each episode, or remain overnight for multiple episodes. Eight children and sixteen Companions cannot all simply reappear at the end. This matters both to young viewers and future game cutscene prerequisites.

**Fix direction:** Add a one-line entry/exit/status row to every episode: who physically enters, who remains at the outside hub/inside refuge, which Companions are free and available, where the pets are known versus inferred to be, elapsed story interval, and how the next party reaches its starting room. Include explicit regrouping before the Heart. Do not invent compulsory game pairing rules.

### R1-C05 — Major: later bosses have consequences but insufficient story space

**Locator:** E6 5-minute Crypt, 6-minute Maze, 9-minute Passages; E7 5-minute Bathhouse, 10-minute Kennels, 5-minute Grounds.

**Problem:** Each five-minute block introduces a new location, Companion, ordinary enemy, elite, boss, child mistake and solution. That is likely to feel like a checklist despite the requirement for meaningful encounters. The Harvest King currently accepts an empty-gate photo with little reason; the Bone Curator loans the needed object after a labeling correction with little resistance.

**Fix direction:** Give each multi-region episode one traveling comic problem that crosses its boundaries and causes the next obstacle; make early bosses' decisions complicate the later boss resolution. State what is lost, carried or changed after each encounter. Alternatively use the brief's permission to adjust structure, but do not call expanded duration “approximately 22 minutes.” An action montage does not solve the problem.

### R1-C06 — Moderate: guardian resolutions overuse polite semantic victory

**Locator:** Butler limited pass; Governess preserves repaired blanket; Groundskeeper allows records after patience; Archivist records exception; Watcher permits passage after error; King accepts new picture.

**Problem:** Too many guardians accept a small demonstration and move aside. Repetition also weakens the danger: viewers learn every obstruction is negotiable if the right child says the theme.

**Fix direction:** Differentiate at least three resolutions: a guardian remains hostile and is bypassed using a demonstrated limitation; another trades a practical benefit without adopting the children's ethics; another must be physically opposed; another changes its mind. State these outcomes explicitly. Preserve actual guardian behavior, not simply the correct names.

### R1-C07 — Moderate: school-age clarity needs concrete goals and limits

**Locator:** E4 forecast exchange; E5 assignment multiplication; E7 map alignment; E8 keeping care while removing confinement.

**Problem:** “Predictions cannot substitute for an exchange,” “routes align,” and “systems redirected into a supported passage” are writer concepts. A seven-year-old needs to know what must be moved/opened/held and what happens if it fails.

**Fix direction:** Give each beat a visible objective and a repeatable rule. Example direction, not prescribed story: three sconces must light in a known order; a shutter cannot predict two different voluntary routes until it sees the choice. Keep explanation to one child sentence plus a demonstration. No mechanical-game tutorial required.

### R1-C08 — Moderate: unwanted House arrival at the clubhouse violates the promised safe space

**Locator:** E1 double-sided WELCOME mat at clubhouse door.

**Problem:** Cute in isolation, but immediately after establishing an outside refuge it implies the imprisoning House can reach their home and knows where they live. For ages 7–10, this can erase the safety of the ending. The brief permits spooky; it does not need this unresolved boundary threat.

**Fix direction:** Keep the joke at the estate's own threshold or have a child knowingly bring the mat back. If deliberate home-reaching is desired, label and resolve that new power rather than treating it as an innocuous gag.

### R1-C09 — Moderate: memorial and crypt identification need to happen in the action

**Locator:** E3 first arrival at Pet Memorial Row; E6 historical bone/exhibit.

**Problem:** The draft's note says to establish living arrivals before memorial imagery, but the current beat order saves the important reveal until after the procession. A viewer with a missing-pet stake may think the pets have died for much of the sequence. A door handle misfiled as a bone also invites confusion about whose remains are being handled.

**Fix direction:** On entry, show a current arrival card and a separate old memorial date; have the child directly recognize the separation before the ceremony. In Crypt show the actual handle's manufacture before any bone comparison. Respect the old animal memory without inventing a specific death story.

### R1-C10 — Moderate: agency is slightly contradicted at the proposed cure offer

**Locator:** E8 “Maya refuses the offer to make Orbit's changed threshold paws ordinary.”

**Problem:** An unsolicited full reversal offer establishes a new House power and Maya decides for Orbit immediately before the script insists Orbit should choose. It risks making the thesis “the child knows the correct life for the pet.”

**Fix direction:** Remove the unneeded reversal offer. Show Maya accepting a residual trait and asking/allowing Orbit to approach an exit. If restoration is retained, explicitly develop its source status and meaningful animal choice; do not use a child speaking for the animal as the agency payoff.

### R1-C11 — Moderate: Maya's accessible participation needs staging commitments

**Locator:** E1 “upstairs,” E2 underbed opening, E4 climb to attic, E5 catwalk, E6 passages, E8 moving chamber.

**Problem:** Architecture rightly avoids disability jokes but has not shown how Maya physically joins or remains involved on narrow, climbing and underbed routes. Adding accessible paths after plotting could force her into passive waiting or repeated carrying.

**Fix direction:** In each episode where she enters, specify usable circulation and her active task: broad service ramp, level manifested doorway, room-scale underbed aperture, safe landing. It need not be dialogue or a special lesson. Also name when another focus group goes and Maya remains active at the hub rather than silently omitting her.

### R1-C12 — Moderate: source ledgers disagree on starting Companions

**Locator:** `02-mansion-coverage.md`, conflict item 4, versus `01-source-ledger.md`, starter correction.

**Problem:** Coverage ledger says Marmalade, Bones, Taffy, Pipkin are starters. Current `game/src/state/run.js:439` defines `STARTER_SLUGS = new Set(['marmalade'])`; September opening note agrees. At minimum the claim needs a separate documented subsystem/citation rather than being asserted as current universal fact.

**Fix direction:** Reconcile before package approval. For the TV narrative declare the start state independently as proposal; do not silently use a four-starter claim to skip freedom scenes.

## Child-oriented fresh-eyes simulation

I can readily follow “we want our pets back; this House gives nice things but will not let anybody leave.” The most immediate memorable images are coats nobody asked for, an overfull bed, multiplying dishes, and a dance that will not end. I would want to know whether the children really found Mooncake, why they left him, whether the House can come to their bedroom now, and why a door handle fixes the big problem. The late procession of unfamiliar guardian names would be harder to remember than a single ongoing comic task.

## Strengths to preserve during revision

- Keep the actual room-as-boss Carnivorous Conservatory and the elite Head Gardener distinction.
- Keep ghost hospitality materially generous even when wrong.
- Keep pets' familiar behaviors as recognition evidence; do not solve identity with a mystical declaration.
- Keep no founder reveal, no dead missing pet twist, no timer, no obligatory cure, and no Keeper death.
- Keep the final Butler hook offer. It is a clear, funny change of behavior that children can see without a moral speech.
