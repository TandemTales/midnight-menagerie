# Independent architecture review — round 1, mansion/mystery/animation

Reviewed: `03-season-architecture-v1.md` against the runtime roster and `02-mansion-coverage.md`. Reviewer roles: mystery architect, animation reviewer, visual-comedy reviewer, separate continuity/tone check. I did not read other reviews or writer defenses. This is an editorial judgment of an architecture, not a test with children or a screenplay evaluation.

## Verdict and provisional score

**Revise before episode breaking. 74/100.** The rescue promise and hospitality premise are strong. The actual draft contains major source errors, a delayed rescue without a demonstrated physical reason, and several victories in which guardians simply accept the theme. Those require revision regardless of score.

## Required revisions by moment

### R1-M01 — High: replace invented roster names in actual episode text

E3 “Grave Candles” and “Bell Mourner”; E4 “Paper Moth” and “Great Web”; E5 “Furnace Saint,” “Last Dance,” “Red Page”; E7 “Cistern Giant,” “Rescue Cage,” “Pumpkin Coach,” and pumpkin-region “Seedlings” are not the corresponding audited encounter names. These are not harmless shorthand when the package will become game cutscenes. “Last Dance” also hides the excellent existing two-person mechanism of The Eternal Dance.

Direction: substitute real actors and rewrite each mechanism to suit them, not merely rename. E3 Mourning Candle and Epitaph Choir; E4 Book Bat/Bookmark Imp and Great Orrery or Moon Lens; E5 Blackout Beast/Great Lantern and Eternal Dance; E7 Boiler Bellower, Perfect Pen/Rolling Ward, Pumpkin Pip/Moonseed, Great Root/Gourd Knight. Treat Red Page as an explicit adaptation-only character if retained, but the Master already supplies its function. Actual variants of display region names may be retained only if traced to a known source and reconciled in the ledger.

### R1-M02 — High: Mooncake is apparently recoverable in E6

At the Secret Passages encounter, Mooncake takes Lena's button, yet the kids leave a note and depart. Respect for privacy does not explain abandoning a missing pet within reach, especially after six episodes of pursuit. The later sentence warning against that impression does not fix the scene itself.

Direction: either rescue Mooncake here and let him become an active reunited pet in the finale, or establish a clear two-sided barrier before he appears: a viewing slot into the ward through which the button fits but Mooncake cannot pass, with the real route continuing toward the Heart. Show the attempt to open it and why brute force would trap, rather than endanger, the pet. Do not frame rescue as conditional on a pet performing an emotional lesson.

### R1-M03 — High: causal transitions need observable destinations

E3's discarded Scout marker somehow points to the Library; E4's folded map makes a hole, but E5 abruptly begins in the Lampworks; E5's party ends without establishing why the old departure route starts in the Crypt; E6's Crypt tunnel reaches Burrow Row, then Hush reaches the water system without a named connection. These are geographically possible in this mansion but not yet dramatically earned.

Direction: attach a specific clue to each exit and show the practical transfer. A reference stamp directs the children to the Library; a chimney illuminated in the observatory leads down to Lampworks; a ballroom service record points to the buried departure archive; the Crypt's open earth passage reaches the Hedge Maze; Speaking Tube Junction carries the water/pipe clue. Identify supernatural transfers as manifested connections, with a visual transition cue. Do not imply the authored numbered order is a straight physical corridor.

### R1-M04 — High: “exit once existed” needs a fair, bounded inference

E3 calls a decades-old RETURNED HOME mark proof that leaving was possible. In E4 records are shown to be wrong; E5 exposes false consent. An official mark is precisely the kind of record this story teaches us to distrust. The kids may initially hypothesize it, but the story must then independently confirm it. Otherwise the finale rests on a potentially false ledger.

Direction: corroborate with a concrete resident memory and a physical feature, such as exterior wear on the departure threshold plus a coherent account from a Companion who witnessed a departure. Identify new history as a show proposal. If no departure history is needed, make the old door an unused architectural opportunity rather than evidence of a past golden age.

### R1-M05 — High: most guardian resolutions currently repeat agreement

Butler permits, Governess lets pass, Confectioner permits, Groundskeeper allows, Archivist opens, Watcher permits, Lamplighter redirects, Curator loans, Gardener lets go, Matron lends, Harvest King accepts a picture. The motives vary, but the visible dramatic action repeats: learn a lesson, guardian yields. Bedframe Beast, Conservatory, Warden and Keeper supply the strongest external opposition.

Direction: diversify at least four climaxes while retaining kindness: trick an inflexible procedure, redirect a pursuing object, accept an imperfect negotiated trade, escape through a route the guardian cannot use, help one guardian while leaving its ideology unresolved. The Harvest King needs a tangible mechanism that makes its demand fail; an empty-gate photograph alone is a thematic illustration, not a staged obstacle overcome.

### R1-M06 — High: E6 and E7 need a convincing 22-minute contract

Three bosses, three elites, three Companions, travel, a child conflict and a mystery turn leave roughly five to six minutes per region. The Curator's mistaken handle and Matron's towel problem feel like errands. They technically affect action but can read as name-checks inserted to satisfy a coverage grid. The finale's sudden sixteen-Companion mobilization then adds an unshown logistical achievement.

Direction: make each triple-region episode one continuous escalating objective carried through three different systems. In E6 the exit hardware should be classified, composted, and concealed as parts of one physical pursuit; each guardian's choice changes the next stage. In E7 move one continuous open-door rehearsal through water routing, ward access, and courtyard assembly. Seed voluntary Companion return invitations before E7. Retain the 22-minute budget only if the subsequent timed breaks can actually stage it; otherwise flag an extended episode proposal honestly.

### R1-M07 — Medium: visual deductions rely on presumed ownership

E2 recognizes Sprocket's tooth marks; E3 knows Pixel rejected a warm platform and selected a crooked stone before seeing Pixel; E4 changing words prove Pepper is live without excluding House imitation. The audience needs the discriminating evidence, not a confident child explanation.

Direction: establish characteristic markings/behaviors at the noticeboard or through a short home image. Priya should observe displaced objects, a trail, and an actual repeated choice rather than infer intent from warmth alone. Pepper should respond to a fresh question with an unrehearsed familiar behavior; this supports a present connection, not necessarily current physical location. Preserve a margin of uncertainty until the ward is reached.

### R1-M08 — Medium: finale opening rules appear at payoff

E8 Eli attaches the borrowed handle to “a working opening,” then sixteen Companions hold routes while the Keeper releases an unnamed final lock. Which opening works, what initially prevents it, and what agency the Keeper retains are unclear. This risks magic-tool convenience followed by a speech-induced unlock.

Direction: establish the door's operating rule earlier, reuse the real Keeper's Shelter, Routine, Observation, and Return locks as legible supports for distinct arguments/actions if desired, and separate the children's achievement (door kept operable) from the Keeper's consequential choice (releasing its hold). Do not make Companions into power batteries. Reserve final direct communication and silence for an observable decision.

### R1-M09 — Medium: comic callbacks risk an undifferentiated pile-up

E1 coats, E2 trolley, E3 candle/banner/trolley, E4 sandwich label, E5 ceremonial jobs and party all accumulate charming props. But a sandwich label gag has weak causal continuity: who labeled it, why did the map retain it, why is it in the refuge? Random callbacks can feel like rewards for recognizing a prop rather than comic escalation.

Direction: assign two or three serial props with clear owners and functions. The route labels should become more honest, Samir's accepted jobs should cost time until he declines, and the trolley may evolve from unwanted follower to useful voluntary catering. Cut secondary returns that do not change a choice. Protect silent reunion beats.

### R1-M10 — Medium: production readability and body geography

E1 has eight kids, two Companions, duplicating Greeters, Butler, Governess and Patchwork Giant. E7 promises all sixteen Companions, and E8 adds eight pets. At the climax that is 32 protagonists plus Keeper and household systems. Without lanes of action this is an indistinct crowd.

Direction: script two or three readable foreground action teams; establish other participants' positions with wide shots, then retain screen direction. Specify accessible support surfaces for Maya in stairs, service catwalks and underbed routes without making her mobility the gag or a magical cure. “They climb” is not sufficient staging for that ensemble. Character counting and route maps are needed at the break stage, not just final script polish.

## Independent tone check

- Strength: the final pet opportunity, no rescue timer, no cure requirement and House's genuine comfort remain intact.
- E3 must identify current pets as living arrivals before the memorial procession. Do not make kids temporarily mourn their own pets merely to supply suspense.
- The Carnivorous Conservatory should remain an enclosing, active antagonist, but avoid mouth imagery consuming children or animal distress. Growing furniture, blocked routes and the absurd proliferation of “ideal habitats” can preserve the threat without graphic fear.
- Drowned Matron's supernatural form does not establish an actual drowning biography. Current text rightly avoids inventing one.
- “Maya refuses the offer to make Orbit's changed paws ordinary” needs unmistakably pet-specific staging; no parallel disability-normalization offer to Maya.

## What should survive revision

Keep the eight short reunions, the Butler's final offered hook, the physical closed-exit motif, Mopsy's choice, Brambleboo's ugly crack, and the House stopping itself from reclosing a gate. The ending's last small act earns more than a universal guardian conversion would. Next review should inspect the revised text, not just an issue-response list.
