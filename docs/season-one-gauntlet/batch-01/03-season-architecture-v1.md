# Midnight Menagerie season one architecture

Architecture draft 1 for independent review. Eight episodes of approximately 22 minutes. This is an adaptation proposal, not a replacement game bible. The mansion traversal follows the documented region sequence, with geographic transitions made visible; the current game permits branching expeditions. All 17 principal bosses must materially affect a scene. An appearance in a montage does not satisfy that requirement.

## Series promise

Eight kids go into an impossible house to get their pets back. The House keeps trying to make them comfortable enough to stop asking. Every visit turns an ordinary activity—checking in, going to bed, tending a garden, finding a record, attending a party—into a comic problem with a real animal at its center. The kids and supernatural residents discover that the best home has an exit you can use.

The central question is first where the animals went, then why the House keeps them, then whether care can survive an open door. We answer the season's rescue promise. The larger history remains open; no founder or tragedy is invented as a final explanation.

Working ending: all eight pets get a genuine opportunity to leave with their kids; the sixteen Companions collectively help open the Heart without all needing a full origin or reunion story. The House begins practicing sanctuary rather than becoming perfectly healed after one speech. This ending and its placement in season one are proposals.

## Comedy and tone

Warm spooky adventure for ages 7–10 with protagonists kept at their sourced older ages. Children can feel afraid without becoming incapable. Two or three kids drive any given scene. Ordinary animal behavior interrupts grand supernatural plans. Horror objects have readable jobs and frustrations. Guardians do not all speak in riddles or all accept the same moral argument.

The House's hospitality has a physical cost: tea arrives faster than anyone can drink; a bed follows a child who refuses bedtime; a dance floor pursues guests who sit down. The children make the comic trouble worse through distinct good intentions. When a reunion or grief beat lands, hold the shot instead of immediately adding a joke.

Recurring comic mechanisms: Maya labels unreliable routes, which acquire increasingly specific labels; Samir accepts tiny ceremonial jobs that multiply; Jordan volunteers before learning the size of a task; Lena quietly relocates one crucial object while everyone debates. These mechanisms must change use and consequence. No fixed joke quota and no recurring joke at the expense of mobility, identity or frightened animals.

Companions may speak in short characterful phrases as a TV proposal; recently missing pets remain animals. The House communicates first through arrangements, bells, signs and servants, and directly through the Keeper late in the season. Do not have every creature narrate its ability.

## Episode 1 Please Stay for Breakfast

Regions 1–2: Forgotten Foyer → Forgotten Nursery. Focus: Maya and Mateo; Samir causes the hospitality escalation. Companions: Marmalade and Mopsy.

Cold open: a neighborhood missing-pet noticeboard. Jordan's dog photo keeps flapping over Priya's carefully straightened gecko photo; eight different hands fix the board eight incompatible ways. Maya lays out Orbit's collar. Mateo plays Pepper's strange recorded phrase, “Open the little door.” A little door appears in the mansion gate beyond them.

In the Front Vestibule, a Door Greeter insists on taking coats the children are not wearing. Samir thanks it so generously that it fetches eight ceremonial coats and eight more greeters. Marmalade appears in the wrong reflection and leads them to the House Register Alcove. Their pets have all been checked in. The departure column is blank.

The Butler at the Receiving Chamber requires the children to choose a room before allowing them to inspect the register. Maya attempts a precise short visit; Mateo keeps accepting fascinating extras. The Red Carpet Runner carries their carefully organized luggage in the opposite direction. They win a limited guest pass by helping Marmalade knock the correct bell off a shelf; the Butler lets them follow the recent intake trail upstairs, believing the Governess will settle them.

The Governess mistakes questions about missing pets for bedtime worries. Mopsy repairs a small tear in Mateo's bag, then the room begins supplying increasingly enormous comfort objects. The Patchwork Giant tries to tuck eight beds into one doorway. Maya stops trying to out-organize the schedule; Mateo asks Mopsy what she wants. Mopsy chooses to open the stitched route past the Governess, who must decide whether to pull apart a blanket she has just carefully repaired. She lets the children pass through to the residential landing, preserving the blanket rather than admitting defeat.

Resolution: the kids establish an Umbrella Gallery refuge and an exit back outside with Marmalade's help. They have tangible evidence, a next route, and a reason to return. Mateo listens to their recording again: under Pepper's words are three scratches and a pause. Maya recognizes Orbit. End on hope, then the House places a welcome mat at their clubhouse door; Maya turns it over. It reads WELCOME on both sides.

Mystery advance: all eight arrivals exist; the same household system handles separate disappearances. Emotional turn: Maya cannot make the House obey, but she can share an investigation. Budget: 3 minutes neighborhood/entry, 8 Foyer, 9 Nursery, 2 return/tag.

## Episode 2 Breakfast Is Following Us

Regions 3–4: Sleeping Quarters → Kitchens and Cellars. Focus: Eli and Amina; Jordan supplies kinetic support. Companions: Boggle and Taffy.

The next visit follows the overnight guest route from the residential landing. Eli thinks he can force a door by dismantling its latch; Boggle points out that the door has moved while he worked. Amina hears Mochi's two quick thumps and one slow thump in the walls. Eli decides a convincing performance of sleep will make the bedroom unlock. The Bedframe Beast enthusiastically wheels every sleeper toward the same overfull bed. Amina's refusal to pretend comfort lets Boggle identify a safe underbed opening. The Blanket Hydra tangles itself trying to deliver enough pillows to every head. The children get out by moving the empty bed where the Beast expects a sleeping child to be, freeing Boggle to choose the route.

The service stair leads into the Scullery. The Confectioner offers breakfast while searching for a missing delivery slip. Jordan says they can help clear the dishes. The Dough Blobs split every time he slices a portion; the Sugar Golem keeps grading the symmetry of stacks. Eli builds a conveyor that makes the doubling worse. Amina notices the trays already know where the animals eat. Taffy takes the children along the Cold Larder/Root Cellar route while Eli stops the conveyor and sorts destinations rather than portions. They return the Confectioner's misplaced slip instead of stealing it. He lets them follow the vegetable delivery to the Greenhouse because he wants accurate meals delivered, not praise.

They find a screw with Sprocket's tooth marks in a latch that opens from inside, then another latch bolted over it. Amina explains only what they can see: something escaped; somebody added a lock. Eli keeps the screw rather than the clever mechanism. End: an abandoned breakfast trolley has followed them to the refuge; Jordan opens it to find one very small, exquisitely plated sandwich. He finally sits down to eat it.

Mystery: food and comforts are genuine; repeated containment follows individual animal choices. Emotional turn: helpful action requires watching the actual recipient. Budget 9 residential, 11 kitchen, 2 refuge/tag.

## Episode 3 A Very Small Parade

Regions 5–6: Impossible Greenhouse → Overgrown Graveyard. Focus: Priya and Jordan. Companions: Brambleboo and Mossbit, using authored homes pending the documented Mossbit/Pudding implementation conflict.

Priya follows an unnaturally warm little stone toward the Greenhouse. The Head Gardener tries to prune every route into one impeccable line. Priya accepts the offer of a “best path.” It delivers the group to the center of the Carnivorous Conservatory, whose helpful vines arrange personalized habitats around them. The room is the boss, not a plant monster inside it. Creeping Ivy repeatedly tidies the escape marks. Priya discovers Pixel has rejected a perfectly positioned warm platform in favor of a crooked stone. She lets Brambleboo choose an ugly crack through the growing enclosure while Jordan carries supplies, not animals. She leaves the desired stone accessible instead of using it as bait.

Through the rear grounds they reach Pet Memorial Row. The Groundskeeper of Names is conducting a procession for an old forgotten name and insists that everyone move at the slowest participant's pace. Jordan turns the task into an ever larger ceremonial parade. Grave Candles march in incompatible directions; a name-bearing stone keeps being presented as guest of honor. The elite Bell Mourner rings each time Jordan tries to announce the next item before the previous one finishes. Mossbit finally makes the short walk in silence. The Groundskeeper allows them to inspect the living-arrival records because Jordan has stopped confusing haste with help.

Important reveal: the recent pets are registered as arrivals, not dead animals. A discarded Scout trail marker leads toward the Library; a decades-old RETURNED HOME mark shows that leaving was once possible. End: Priya deliberately lets the crooked stone sit crooked on the clubhouse evidence shelf. Jordan quietly puts the ceremonial banner down. A Grave Candle has followed it and bows to the sandwich trolley.

Mystery: departure used to happen; the House's rule changed. Emotional turn: patience is useful action. Budget 12 Greenhouse, 8 Graveyard, 2 tag. No suggested death of a missing pet; establish living arrival category before any memorial imagery.

## Episode 4 The Wrong Answer Club

Regions 7–8: Grand Study and Library → Moonlit Attic and Observatory. Focus: Mateo and Priya. Companions: Crinkle and Wink.

The Archivist insists a misplaced animal cannot be found until it is classified. Mateo offers increasingly elaborate theories; each theory becomes a pile of forms that buries Crinkle. A Paper Moth eats the punctuation; the elite Living Index points at whatever label was most recently written, including a sandwich. Priya traces the old return mark to the Letter Archive. The kids retrieve a record by describing a behavior, not a category: Pepper answers Mateo's question differently from the mechanical House announcements. The Archivist reluctantly records an exception and opens the Rolling Ladder Gallery.

They climb to the Star Chart Room, where the Watcher in the Rafters predicts their every route. Priya tries to out-plan it and keeps accidentally directing the others into its webbed predictions. Wink is a willing collaborator, not a surveillance tool. The elite Great Web rearranges their careful signposts. Mateo hears a distant live Pepper phrase that includes a new sound from below, proving this is not just an old recording. Priya lets Mateo choose the next question instead of assigning the answer; the Watcher's forecast cannot substitute for that exchange. It permits passage when they expose a wrong prediction without smashing the observatory.

End: the intake map routes every pet toward an unprinted central room. Crinkle folds the map and the blank area becomes a door-shaped hole. Maya labels it “DOOR, PROBABLY.” Priya adds, “ASK FIRST.” The folded map sneezes out the sandwich label.

Mystery: records and predictions can be wrong; pets are active participants leaving evidence. Emotional turn: Priya shares uncertainty; Mateo learns that a clever theory needs a test. Budget 10 Library, 10 Observatory, 2 tag.

## Episode 5 Everybody Has a Job

Regions 9–10: Lampworks → Ballroom and Velvet Suites. Focus: Samir and Lena. Companions: Wisp and Count Crumbula.

The Lamplighter needs someone to carry a tiny light along the service catwalk. Samir accepts. The service bell then assigns him every other task because he has not declined the first. Wisp keeps darting toward blue-flame messages on distant sconces. Lena notices that the lantern goes dark only when it is enclosed. The elite Furnace Saint demands an absolutely steady flame, so Samir holds still while tools, ribbons and invitations pile around him. Lena opens a safe vent; Wisp chooses where to burn; the Lamplighter reluctantly redirects illumination to the pets' actual route instead of the official guest circuit.

They arrive at a ball where the Master of Revels has scheduled a farewell dance that never reaches its final step. Count Crumbula has opinions about table manners and no appetite for surrendering his whole evening. Samir becomes assistant host and tries to keep everyone happy; Lena disappears into the service spaces and returns with Mooncake's chewed wallpaper. Masked guests copy Samir's frantic politeness; the elite Last Dance keeps resetting the final bow. Lena reveals the music has a perfectly usable ending which the Master always interrupts. Samir interrupts his own welcome speech instead, declines the next ceremonial job, and lets Crumbula take the last bow. The Master gives up the stage to applause that was freely offered, not compulsory. He does not convert to the children's whole cause.

The Red Page appears with a tray Bean once ate from and insists accepted food means an accepted invitation. Samir catches his own polite “yes” being written beside a permanent address. Lena crosses it out. End: the two children hold a tiny two-person party in the refuge, deliberately ending it while they are still having fun. Bean's familiar wheek sounds through the service pipes.

Mystery: “voluntary arrival” is an interpretation, not proof. Emotional turn: a host can welcome someone without keeping them. Budget 9 Lampworks, 11 Ballroom, 2 tag.

## Episode 6 Do Not Put That Back

Regions 11–13: Crypt and Ossuary → Withered Hedge Maze → Secret Passages. Focus: Lena and Eli; Jordan supports Bones. Companions: Bones, Truffle, Hush.

Following the old departure route leads down to the Name Vault. The Bone Curator has filed a door handle as a historically important bone. Bones retrieves everything thrown, including the things the children wanted out of reach. The Ribcage Knight keeps assembling its display around the wrong exhibit. Eli solves this short encounter by giving the Curator a properly identified harmless substitute handle and an honest correction to its label; the Curator allows the real exit piece out on loan. No claim the exhibit is a dead pet.

The Crypt's buried route emerges into Burrow Row. The Gardener of Rot wants every discarded object to become useful mulch. Truffle likes the garden and objects to being used as a gardening tool. The Mold Minotaur takes directions literally enough to keep returning its own hedge clippings to the path it just cleared. Eli keeps restoring a gate the garden wants to compost. Lena makes a route from openings instead of rebuilding fences; the Gardener lets the useless old handle go because it cannot grow. They find Mooncake's Museum objects in an impossible pocket, carefully sorted, not abandoned.

Hush leads them into the walls. The Whisper Warden keeps residents safe by making their paths impossible to find, then tries to erase the children's return route. Lena briefly likes being unseen; Eli wants to expose every hidden passage. They argue over whether Mooncake's private refuge should be opened. The elite Moving Wall turns Eli's publicity arrows toward Lena's hiding place. Lena leaves a familiar yellow button at the threshold, then waits without peering. Mooncake voluntarily comes into view long enough to take it, proving he is alive and can choose visibility. Hush refuses the Warden's order to conceal the route, offering the children a passage to the water system instead. They do not drag Mooncake away or reveal other residents' private rooms.

End: the original handle fits a public door whose reverse side reads GOING HOME. The wall behind it is solid. They have found a real lost exit, not completed the rescue. Lena writes “We found you” on a small card and leaves it where Mooncake can choose to retrieve it.

Mystery: departure infrastructure was actively closed; privacy and disappearance are different. Emotional turn: Eli asks before opening; Lena speaks a boundary aloud. Budget 5 Crypt, 6 Maze, 9 Passages, 2 ending.

## Episode 7 The Going Home Practice

Regions 14–16: Bathhouse and Rain Wing → Kennels and Hunting Halls → Moon Courtyard and Pumpkin Grounds. Focus: Amina and Jordan; Samir's Bean thread pays off. Companions: Drizzle, Pudding, Pipkin.

Drizzle brings the children to the Drowned Matron, a water caretaker, never a drowned corpse. Amina needs to move towels; the Matron keeps trying to wash and dry the entire procession in the wrong order. Soap Sprites turn an orderly trolley into a gentle indoor regatta. The elite Cistern Giant blocks the outlet to prevent anyone becoming cold. Amina uses separate warm and dry routes so Drizzle can open the channel without draining anyone's home. The Matron lends the route because the water now has somewhere safe to go.

At Animal Intake, Jordan sees that the House really does treat and feed animals. Pudding anxiously tries to escort everyone simultaneously. The Kennelmaster refuses to open the GOING HOME gate because there is always one more safety check. The elite Rescue Cage offers warm shelter then closes when the occupant changes its mind. Jordan begins promising flawless supervision to get Scout back. Amina refuses to promise no animal will ever be afraid. Scout's signal comes from deeper inside; he is still being sent to help others. Jordan stops taking the next assigned rescue job and helps Pudding leave a gate open instead. Amina creates a quiet waiting area with a usable exit; frightened animals begin moving when ready.

The route opens onto the Pumpkin Grounds. The Harvest King is running a compulsory celebration for a harvest that is never quite finished. Pipkin keeps bouncing out of the ceremonial picture. A march of Seedlings blocks the Moon Gate; the Pumpkin Coach keeps collecting passengers who have already stepped out. The King wants one final photograph with every guest in place. The kids offer a picture of the empty, open gate instead. He keeps celebrating while the procession chooses when to go. The sixteen Companions contribute their different knowledge across the estate; the folded map's routes align through the open courtyard.

End: the Impossible Door opens. Eight living pets are visible across the Pet Holding Ward. The Keeper says, gently, “There you are. I've made room.” No animal is injured and no timer begins. Budget 5 Bathhouse, 10 Kennels, 5 Grounds, 2 Heart threshold.

## Episode 8 You Can Come Back

Region 17: Heart of the House. Ensemble resolution, Maya focal viewpoint. Keeper confrontation unfolds across Hall of Collars, Hall of Names, Pet Holding Ward, Sanctuary Garden, Voice Chamber, Inner Sanctuary and Heart Chamber.

The children can finally reach their pets but the House keeps placing comfortable walls between each pair. A Housekeeper persistently tidies personal objects out of reach. Memory Animals repeat old behaviors; distinguish their loops from living animals changing their choices. The Hall of Names elite makes the old labels loom. The Door That Says Stay offers every excuse at once and opens into the Perfect Sanctuary, which has everything except an exit. The children recognize things they once wanted: faultless plans, invisible privacy, endless interesting puzzles, everybody liking them.

Maya refuses the offer to make Orbit's changed threshold paws ordinary. She lays the collar down and lets him decide. The children show the Keeper the abandoned departure door and ask for an exit. The Keeper gives them a perfect picture of one. Eli takes the handle out of his pocket and attaches it to a working opening. The sixteen Companions hold different routes, volunteering rather than becoming fuel. Each kid must stop an old habit: Priya puts down the schedule; Jordan waits instead of chasing; Samir stops negotiating away Bean's right to leave; Lena leaves space for Mooncake; Amina lets Mochi hesitate; Mateo listens; Eli offers a mechanism without forcing its user; Maya waits at Orbit's pace.

The Keeper panics and closes the route. It has to be opposed physically: the children and Companions keep the door operable while it rearranges the chamber. Nobody attacks a living pet. The House's own helpful systems—light, warm water, movable beds, bell pulls—are redirected into a supported passage. The children do not have to destroy all care to dismantle confinement.

Resolution is action: the Keeper releases the final lock, then watches animals use the door. Bean leaves with Samir and pauses to greet a friend. One established Companion chooses to remain in the Garden with its door open. The House starts to close the gate after a departing animal and stops itself. It has not forgotten anyone. The old departure register gains a new entry written by a child, not an imposed classification.

At home, allow eight distinct short reunions with their familiar behaviors, not eight speeches. Final visit: the kids bring supplies to the House. The Butler offers eight coats. Maya says they brought their own. He thinks, then offers a hook. Marmalade chooses the coat pile. End on a small funny act of real hospitality.

Budget 4 recognition/false sanctuary, 4 pet choices, 8 Keeper/door action, 4 reunions, 2 return/tag. No missing-pet rescue is saved for a sequel in this working ending.

## Setup and payoff controls

The guest register in episode 1 → old return entry in 3 → misclassification in 4 → false consent in 5 → blocked departure door in 6 → unused gate in 7 → actual departure entry in 8. Each discovery changes the children's next physical goal.

Pepper's recording in 1 → recognizable but changing live response in 4 → Mateo listens instead of supplying the answer in 8. Recordings alone never prove where a living pet currently is.

The scratched latch and replacement lock in 2 → counterfeit certainty exposed in 4 → Eli's correction/permission turn in 6 → an opening that an animal controls in 8.

The crooked stone in 3 → Priya gives up a flawless forecast in 4 → offers the preferred object without controlling Pixel's approach in 8.

Mooncake's wallpaper in 5 → sorted Museum and voluntary appearance in 6 → actual reunion at Mooncake's pace in 8. The episode 6 appearance must not suggest the kids can simply scoop him up but deliberately abandon him.

Scout's route marker in 3 → his helping signal and Jordan's overpromising in 7 → reunion that lets Scout stop working in 8.
