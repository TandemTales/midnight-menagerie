# Midnight Menagerie character and comedy bible

Batch 01 working adaptation. Identities and conflicts are controlled by `01-source-ledger.md`. The performance choices, relationships and sample lines below are proposals, not quotations from game canon. Samples demonstrate voices; they are not finished episode dialogue.

## Emotional thesis and performance

Love must remain recognizable when the person or animal you love makes a choice you did not plan. The House provides an exaggerated physical version of a temptation the kids sometimes feel themselves: make everything predictable and nobody can be lost. Their resistance becomes convincing when they relinquish control in their own relationships.

Let children want a sandwich, the last word, a useful photograph, credit for a clever plan, or a turn carrying the interesting lantern. A character's thematic role must not consume their personality. Funny behavior should occur while pursuing those wants. Children need not understand the complete philosophy to recognize a door that will not let their pet out.

Companions speak briefly when they have something to say; use animal action whenever it can carry the moment. Recently missing pets communicate through behavior and their specific adaptations. Pepper's mimicry does not turn him into an omniscient commentator. Household guardians have varied speech rhythms and practical wants. No universal snark voice; no universal computerized hospitality voice.

## Companion ensemble and communication

No more than two Companions carry dialogue in a normal scene. Others may appear visually or through a previously established signal. The finale's broad network should use recognizable actions, not a roll call of sixteen speeches. Each regional encounter changes a resident's capacity to choose or help; characters are not collected into a silent procession.

| Companion | Source-supported form or foundation | Proposed comic behavior and distinct role |
|---|---|---|
| Marmalade | Ghost cat | Chooses the inconvenient perch; refuses ceremony but notices recurring animal routes. First bond with kids; not Orbit's replacement. |
| Mopsy | Rag doll bunny; origin conflict retained | Repairs precisely, then deliberately leaves one seam open. Has preferences beyond caring for others. Do not assert a former living-rabbit biography. |
| Boggle | Monster under the bed | Gives proud directions from a hiding place and thereby reveals it. Local knowledge can coexist with fear. |
| Taffy | Candy slime | Splitting creates helpers with different immediate priorities; rejoining requires a shared task. Nobody eats her. |
| Brambleboo | Haunted houseplant | Tends a helpful opening into an inconvenient garden. Growth has visible direction and scale. |
| Mossbit | Tombstone turtle, alive | Waits for the useful moment; fastest delivery is sometimes a well-timed sentence. No slow/old ridicule. |
| Crinkle | Paper crow; source supports annoyance at Ghost Writing | Protests being treated as stationery while using paper abilities on his own terms. Do not fold his body without permission. |
| Wink | Eyeball spider | Prepares for the next complication and must ask who wants the preparation. Forecasts are limited; not an infallible clue dispenser. |
| Wisp | Baby will o' wisp | A tiny delayed ignition turns out to matter somewhere surprising. Light is a readable route signal, never an unexplained universal unlock. |
| Count Crumbula | Vampire chinchilla; aristocratic appetite | Lavish ceremony for a very small morsel; chooses when the party ends for him. No blood consumption or copied vampire catchphrases. |
| Bones | Skeleton puppy; playful retrieval | Joyful fetch makes a precise sorting task worse. Detachable pieces are visibly painless and reassemble; no suggestion of a recently killed pet. |
| Truffle | Zombie hedgehog; resilient cartoon disarray | Treats a temporary missing tuft as a maintenance inconvenience. No putrefaction close-ups or enjoyment of pain. |
| Hush | Shadow ferret | Quietly relocates an obstacle and one object somebody still needs. Privacy expert who asks before opening another resident's refuge. |
| Drizzle | Raincloud ghost | Tries to place exactly enough weather over one small area. Weather is an ability, not a diagnosis or moral judgment about emotions. |
| Pudding | Graveyard pug; self-appointed protector with numerous jobs | Cannot carry all the job hats he has accepted. Learns to share responsibility without losing his pride in helping. |
| Pipkin | Pumpkin frog; changing size and bouncing | Cannot keep the same formal pose for the duration of a ceremony. Immediate movement distinguishes him from Brambleboo's cultivation. |

Forms and mechanics derive from the sixteen chapters in `docs/design/companions/`; exact comic behavior above is proposed. Authored homes put Mossbit at Graveyard and Pudding at Kennels; implementation drift must remain in the conflict ledger.

## Recurring comedy with development

| Mechanism | First use | Escalation | Payoff and stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labels on an uncooperative house | Maya labels a dependable way out. | Priya adds conditions; the House physically moves the sign but leaves the nail. | A sign eventually asks the traveler where they want to go. Maximum one featured use per episode; do not write eight variants of “that is not normal.” |
| Jobs accepted too quickly | Jordan takes a trolley; Samir accepts a badge. | The task reveals branches or ceremonial accessories; attempts to remain helpful conflict. | They hand over a task with clear boundaries. Their lesson must differ: Jordan obligation, Samir social approval. |
| The overprovided comfort object | A sensible pillow delivery becomes impractical. | A returning object acquires a useful new function. | In the climax comforts support an open exit. Do not repeat a pile-up unchanged in every wing. |
| A resident choosing something mundane | Cat chooses a box; gecko prefers the crooked stone. | Kids stop treating an object's official purpose as its only value. | Final hospitality offers a hook rather than compulsory coats. Avoid explaining the joke's moral afterward. |

## Comparative craft study

The reference shows identify useful broad craft goals, not templates to imitate. This research consulted official descriptions and a creator interview; it is not a claim to have newly watched or closely analyzed their complete seasons.

DreamWorks describes ordinary school life intersecting with an extensive hidden world in *Trollhunters*. The adaptation lesson is to keep each large discovery attached to a small recognizable obligation. Our stakes arise from missing animals and relationships, with no chosen-one artifact or replicated mythology. [DreamWorks series description](https://www.dreamworks.com/shows/trollhunters)

Netflix's *Hilda* description joins friendship, exploration, and encounters with unfamiliar creatures. The useful principle is curiosity before judgment: a strange resident should have a readable perspective before the kids decide how to respond. This is an editorial inference from the description, not a borrowed character or plot. [Netflix Media Center](https://media.netflix.com/en/only-on-netflix/80115346)

The creators of *The Ghost and Molly McGee* describe contrasting temperaments that change through friendship. For this ensemble, comedy should reveal relationships rather than pause them for unrelated jokes. Use the source's general craft insight without copying its central pair, curse, songs, or comic voices. [D23 creator interview](https://d23.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-ghost-and-molly-mcgee/)

## Script and animation checks

Maya's route must be staged, not assumed: show usable floor clearance, places to rest, and access to any elevated route. Friends offer specific assistance; she accepts or declines. Do not invent magical mobility restoration. Creature tunnels need an alternative human route or a clearly established enlargement mechanism.

Use a wide shot to establish danger, a visible escape option, then escalating comic obstacles. A brief uncertain shadow can become a recognizable resident. Never linger on an animal suffering to motivate the kids. Crypt imagery is clean, stylized, and non-graphic. The living pets' whereabouts should not become ambiguous with memorial objects.

For each scene ask who wants what, who changes the situation, what the audience can see before the characters notice, and why this joke belongs to this character. Cut redundant lines explaining visible actions. Reserve stillness and silence for decisions and reunions.
