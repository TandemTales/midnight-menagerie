# Midnight Menagerie continuity and cutscene ledger

Applies to architecture version 4. These are proposed television continuity choices. They do not impose new campaign prerequisites on the branching game.

## Expedition and ensemble continuity

All eight children join each principal expedition in this version. Each episode foregrounds two, permits one or two supporting speakers per scene, and keeps the others visible in establishing shots or working in an adjacent named space. Nobody silently vanishes or travels alone between wings. Do not write every reaction as a group roll call. Maya uses her crutches throughout; the route includes identified floor access, clearance and resting places.

## Companion choices and the final network

Each Companion's initial freedom and movement are adaptation choices, distinct from dynamic game rescues. Only Marmalade is a current game starter; older four-starter comments are stale.

The first two episodes establish that bell pulls connect specific rooms: a pull rings a visible matching bell, not every bell in the estate. Mopsy leaves a recognizable ribbon on the bell for the refuge. In episode 3, Brambleboo agrees to leave a route marker at the Greenhouse junction; Mossbit agrees to relay one signal through the rear grounds. In episode 4, Crinkle records the actual routes the group has used rather than magically inventing them. Wink checks sightlines. In episode 5, Wisp illuminates the next marked connection and Crumbula clears access through the musicians' service corridor. In episode 6, Bones, Truffle and Hush confirm the remaining ground and wall routes. In episode 7, Drizzle connects the water-service approach, Pudding keeps the ward-side approach usable, and Pipkin identifies the Moon Dial alignment.

Every request has an observable affirmative response or alternative offered by that Companion. They are not compelled by a magical bond. The full route is assembled from their different knowledge; no one character could have simply supplied the whole solution earlier. Choose a limited group for each finale shot. A clean sequence of regional actions can show the distributed group holding connections without pretending all sixteen are physically beside the Keeper.

For script breaks, distinguish a resident's introduction, a first chosen act of assistance, removal of a specific constraint, and final Stay/Leave/Return choice. Meeting all sixteen does not certify sixteen fully developed origin stories.

## Geography and scene export

Permanent joins follow the design's broad relationships; specific new service lifts, pipe bypasses and connections above are staging proposals. Show a sign, matching opening, sightline, or movement through the join. Do not cut between unrelated wings and imply ordinary adjacency. Exterior grounds are further along the investigation, not necessarily physically nearer a geometric center.

Every full script scene will receive an ID such as `MM101-S01`. A later extraction row should include: episode and scene; region/room; exact boss/elite IDs where applicable; speakers and pronouns; what the viewer/player must already know; new knowledge; pet state before/after; optional versus core; and game-order dependencies.

An episode's essential plot scene is not automatically mandatory game dialogue. Reusable material should be split into a local encounter core and campaign-aware variants: first visit, prior clue known, pet found, post-sanctuary. Preserve conditional recognition; never let a procedural early Library encounter announce an already completed episode 7 revelation.

Planned coverage is 17 regions, 17 principal bosses, selected actual ordinary enemies and elites in every region. The 52-elite matrix is an option inventory, not a promise to feature all 52 this season. Screenplay coverage remains zero until complete scenes exist.
