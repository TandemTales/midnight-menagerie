## How to read the move tables

**Intent** is the schema.js `Intent` value the silhouette uses. **Damage** is the base;
where a number is written `a→b` the intent shows it live and it changes in front of the
player as board state changes. That live re-read is the point — StS2's rule is that the
number on the intent is always the number you are about to take, after every modifier.

A move marked **dyn** carries a `damageFn`/`hitsFn`/`intentFn` the engine must call for
display rather than using the static value.

### Dust Bunny — 20
| Move | Intent | Numbers | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gather | defend | 5 Guard | +1 Dust if it was not damaged last player turn |
| Tumble | attack / attackBig **dyn** | `7 + 3×Dust` (max 19) | clears all Dust |

**Pattern:** Gather → Tumble, forever.
**Dust:** gains 1 at the start of its turn if the player ended the previous turn without
damaging it. Max 4. Combines with Gather, so an ignored Bunny banks 2 per Gather turn.
**The read:** while untouched, Tumble's intent *projects* the Dust it is about to gain, so
it shows 16 — and drops to 13 the instant you poke it for 3. That is the region's first
lesson made visible: a small amount of damage now prevents a bigger problem later.
Base raised 5 → 7 in round 2 (balance deviation, see below).

### Boss: The Butler — 178, two phases

**House Rules.** At the start of a player turn he announces his rules. Rules never
prohibit; they attach a consequence. He never announces the same rule set twice running.

**Phase one stands up ONE rule. Phase two stands up TWO at once.** That is the phase
change that matters, and it inverts the read rather than swapping a move list:

- *Phase one* — one line on the board, Discomposed at 3 Flustered. Breaking a rule is
  cheap and optional, so Flustered is a resource you **farm on purpose**, timing the
  Discomposed window for your biggest turn.
- *Phase two* — two lines on the board, Discomposed at 2 Flustered. Almost any strong turn
  crosses one of them, so Flustered stops being something you accumulate and becomes
  something you **steer around**. The question flips from "which rule do I break for
  value?" to "which rule can I still afford to respect?" A careless burst turn crosses
  both at once and hands him the recovery on his terms.

| Rule | Broken by | Reprimand (P1 → P2) |
|---|---|---|
| GUESTS DO NOT RUSH | playing a 4th Trick this turn | 6 → **8** damage |
| GUESTS WAIT THEIR TURN | two Tricks of the same type in a row | 8 → **10** Guard to him |
| GUESTS DO NOT CLUTTER THE HALL | ending the turn with 12+ Guard | 10 → **12** Guard to him |
| GUESTS DO NOT ROUGHHOUSE | dealing 15+ damage in a turn | his next attack +5 → **+7** (spread across its hits, not per hit) |

**Flustered / Discomposed.** Each break gives 1 Flustered. At 3 (**2 in phase two**) he
loses all Flustered and becomes Discomposed: takes **+25% damage**, cannot enforce a rule,
and his next action is wasted on Collect Himself (12 Guard, nothing else).
**This is the boss.** Breaking rules is a strategic option, not a failure — the player can
deliberately irritate him into an offensive window.

**Phase 1 (178 → 101)**

| Move | Intent | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Formal Welcome (turn 1 only) | defend | 12 Guard, announce a rule |
| Walking Stick | attack **dyn** | 10 (+ Roughhousing rider), announce next rule |
| Dust Them Off | attack **dyn** | 5×2 (+ rider), announce next rule |
| Service, Please | summon | Dust Bunny at 12 Courage, or 8 Guard to an existing ally |

Sequence: Formal Welcome → Walking Stick → then repeating **Dust Them Off → Service,

**Transition at ≤100:** *This Is Most Irregular* — 16 Guard, dismisses every summoned

**Phase 2 (100 → 0)**

### Button Baby — 21
| Move | Intent | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Sew On | buff | attaches a Button to an ally without one |
| Button Toss | attack | 5 |
| Gather Thread | defend | 7 Guard; its next Sew On also grants 5 Guard |

**Buttons** (one per enemy, breaks if that enemy takes 12+ in a turn):
Brass = +2 damage · Pillow = 4 Guard at the start of its turn · Spring = next attack

**Priority:** Spring if the target is telegraphing an attack, Pillow if it is below half

**Pattern:** Sew On whenever anyone lacks a Button, else Button Toss → Gather Thread → Button Toss.

Haunt 1 is the only Courage bump (ordinary +8%, Big Scares and bosses +6%) and it does not
compound. Everything after it changes **behaviour**.

test §5f, which diffs `hauntScaling(n)` against `hauntScaling(n-1)` and ignores `hpMul`

### GUESTS DO NOT DAWDLE (Haunt 8)

> Ending your turn having played fewer than two Tricks breaks the rule.
> Reprimand: 1 Clutter into your discard pile (2 in phase two).

The other four rules all punish excess — too many Tricks, too much Guard, too much damage,
the same type twice — so slowing down answered all of them at once. This is the first that
punishes doing too little. Beside NO RUNNING in phase two it closes the safe band to exactly
two or three Tricks a turn, the tightest sequencing constraint in the region. Its Reprimand
is Clutter rather than damage or Guard: a fifth flavour of consequence, it ties the boss to
the Foyer's own deck-interference thread, and unlike a heal it makes the fight harder
without making it longer.

# Interpretations and deviations

The doc is exact almost everywhere. These are the places it was silent or ambiguous, and
what was chosen. A critic should check these first.

1. **Grand Coatcheck, Evening Coat: +4 is per hit, not per attack.** The doc says
   "damaging attacks deal 4 additional damage" without saying how multi-hit interacts.
   Per-hit is the standard grammar for every damage modifier in the genre and is what the
   engine's pipeline will do to a Strength-like status. Consequence: Hanger Flurry becomes
   8×3 = 24 under Evening Coat, which is larger than Everything at Once (19). Per-cycle
   damage is 54 over 5 turns against a 96-Courage elite. **If a critic disagrees, this is
   the single number to change**, and it lives in one function (`grandCoatcheck.eveningBonus`).
2. **The Butler's phase-two sequence produces a double Remove the Intruder at the seam.**
   The doc lists "Remove the Intruder. Restore Order. Enough of This. Remove the Intruder.
   Repeat." Implemented literally as a 4-move cycle, so 7×3 lands twice in a row once per
   cycle. It may have been intended as a 3-move cycle. Kept literal.
3. **Emergency Repair's "then check".** The doc says to check, not always to use it. The
   check implemented is: used fewer than twice, not on the previous cycle, and she is
   missing at least 15 Courage — otherwise a boss wastes a turn healing at full health.
4. **The Patchwork Giant and the Blanket Hydra body have no stated move order.** Authored:
   Giant = Stuffed Fist → Sit Down → Wild Flail → Coming Apart (which also defines the
   "first attack each cycle" that Spring Patch keys off). Hydra body = Blanket Crush →
   Tangle → Roll Over.
5. **"The most immediate benefit" Patch choice** is a scoring function: Bear scores 3 per
   hit of the Giant's next move, Pillow a flat 6, Spring 20% of the next attack's total
   when that attack is the cycle's first. Ties break in a fixed order. Deterministic and
   explainable, which matters because the player is watching it choose.
6. **Crack thresholds are stored as fractions of max Courage** (2/3 and 1/3), not the
   absolute 24 and 12, so they survive Haunt and party scaling. At the doc's 36 they land
   exactly on 24 and 12. Same for the Giant's 90/60/30, scaled by maxHp/126 — the doc's own
   multiplayer note calls these 75% / 50% / 25%.
7. **Night Terror's Haunt 9 Power branch** is authored (the doc says only that it "may also
   react to Power as a separate branch"): **Looming**, 10 damage and Frightened.
8. **Courage ranges are collapsed to the doc's exact values** (`hp: [n, n]`). The schema
   supports a roll range and StS uses one; opening these up is a one-line change per enemy
   if the balance pass wants variance.
9. **`Intent.DEFEND_DEBUFF` does not exist in schema.js.** Pack Wrong and Feather Cloud
   both gain Guard *and* pollute the deck. They declare `Intent.DEBUFF` (the meaningful
   read) plus a `block` value the engine can draw as a shield sub-badge. A
   `DEFEND_DEBUFF` intent would be cleaner — that is a request to the lead.

# What the validator checks

`tests/enemies/index.html`, run via `python tests/enemies/run.py`:

1. **Schema conformance** — every EnemyDef and MoveDef against `schema.js`, plus the

3. **nextMove purity and determinism** — it is called twice per turn and must return the

# Round 2 changes (2026-08-20)

## The catastrophic one: every multi-hit attack dealt hits² damage

`hitPlayer` in `data/enemies/_lib.js` looped `hits` times **and** passed `{hits}` into
`ctx.damage`, which loops again (`combat/engine.js:1061`). All 16 multi-hit moves in the
three regions were affected. Claw Ambush telegraphed 21 and dealt 63.

The fix is one line — hand the count to the engine exactly once:

    export function hitPlayer(c, n, hits = 1) { c.damage?.(c.player, n, { hits }); }

**Why the suite was green with it in:** the mock's `damage()` treated `{hits}` as a single
hit, so a caller that also looped looked correct. The harness did not model the engine it
was standing in for. That is now fixed at the source — the mock loops on `opts.hits`
exactly as the engine does — and pinned by three new tests (5b, 5c, below).

Measured effect on win rate (critic's A/B, n=40, marmalade starting deck, greedy AI):
Grand Coatcheck 0% → 75%, Unwelcome Guest 22.5% → 97.5%, foyer-14 (no multi-hit) 100% → 100%.

## Intent truth, measured properly

The critic's audit reported 636 of 2026 enemy turns mismatched. Most of that is the
instrument. Their `dealt` is `(hpBefore - hpAfter) + max(0, blockBefore - blockAfter)`,
sampled across `endTurn()` — but `endTurn()` finishes by calling `_beginPlayerTurn()`,
which zeroes player Guard. So the block term counts the player's **entire unspent Guard**
as damage taken, and every defensive turn scores as a lie. Player HP also floors at 0, so
every killing blow scores as an overstatement.

`tests/enemies/engine-audit.html` (run via `python tests/enemies/audit.py`) measures the
same thing against the same real engine, but takes damage from the engine's own event
stream as `blocked + hpLoss`, which is immune to both artifacts. It also snapshots the
intent **at the commit moment** (after the player's last card), because dynamic intents are
designed to move during the turn — 6.6% of turns have an intent that changes as you play,
and that is the feature.

With a correct instrument the starting number was **103 / 2050**. Those were real, and all
of them are now fixed:

| Count | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 39 | `too-familiar` resolved a **live** Familiar count after `onPlayerTurnEnd` had already rotated the Familiar type, dealing 15 where the intent promised 12 | resolve from the count latched at turn end |
| 20 | Nightlight Snuffer in a **middle** board slot stripped Darkness at its turn start, removing +2 from an ally whose intent already showed it | Snuffer placed last in sq-14 |
| 15 | Calling Bell in a middle slot Roused an ally that had not acted yet, adding +2 to an already-telegraphed attack | Bell placed last in foyer-14 |
| 24 | House Bell / Butler Rousing their own summons, which always act after them | those two branches now grant Guard (deviation, below) |
| 5 | `porcelain-twin-prim`'s **Alone** bonus was latched in `onTurnStart`, i.e. after the intent was read — the survivor telegraphed a stale number for a full turn | Alone derived purely from "no living twin" |

**Result: 2368 scored enemy turns, 0 mismatches.** 83 turns are excluded as unscoreable —
the fight ended mid-phase (remaining hits of a multi-hit never resolve) or an enemy left
the board (Wardrobe summons vanishing with their parent). Neither is the intent lying.

### Board order is turn order

Two formations were reordered so support acts last. This is now a standing rule for this
content: **an enemy that buffs its allies goes in the last slot**, because a buff granted
mid-phase lands on allies whose intent numbers are already on screen.

- `foyer-14`: Door Greeter + **Dust Bunny + Calling Bell** (Bell moved from slot 1 to 2)
- `sq-14`: Slipper Skitter + **Thing Beneath + Nightlight Snuffer** (Snuffer moved from 1 to 2)

## Statuses now announce themselves

`combat/intents.js` reads `move.applies` / `move.appliesFn` to draw status pips. Nothing in
the roster declared either — Roused, Frightened, Smothered, Darkness, Scurry, Hidden,
Buttons and Cover all landed with no warning. **17 moves now declare them**, using
`appliesFn` wherever the status or its size is conditional (which Button gets sewn, how
big Darkness is, which branch The Night Terror takes). Reconciled by a new test that
resolves every move and fails if it applies a status it did not declare.

## The Butler was silently enforcing all four rules at once

`announceRule` only replaces a rule **by id**, so his rules accumulated: by turn four,
phase one had all four House Rules standing simultaneously, which is not what "he enforces
one House Rule at a time" means. He now clears his set before announcing. The mock had
been replacing rules by *source*, which the engine does not do — that is why this never
surfaced. The mock now matches the engine, and the standing-rule count is asserted per
phase (1 in phase one, 2 in phase two).

## Balance: the two early-Scuffle buffs, and what they actually did

Applied as directed — Dust Bunny Tumble `5 + 3×Dust` → `7 + 3×Dust`, Coatrack Crawler Hat
Swipe 6 → 8. Both are deliberate deviations from the design doc.

Re-measured (n=40, same harness): **Scuffle 1 cost 0.18 → 0.25** Courage, **Scuffle 2
2.15 → 2.35**. Essentially unmoved, and it is worth being precise about why rather than
buffing further:

| Encounter | Damage thrown | Absorbed by Guard | Reached Courage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foyer Scuffle 1 | 84 | 80 (**95%**) | 4 |
| Foyer Scuffle 2 | 314 | 285 (**91%**) | 29 |
| Foyer Scuffle 14 | 823 | 404 (49%) | 419 |
| SQ 14 | 1399 | 642 (46%) | 757 |

The greedy AI blocks exactly the number the intent shows, and with 3 Pluck it can cover any
single telegraph an early enemy can produce. **A solo enemy is structurally free against
this AI regardless of its damage value** — Tumble would have to exceed a full turn of Guard
(~13-16) to cost anything, which is not a sane number for the region's first enemy and
would make it a coin flip for a human who draws badly. What actually costs HP is
*simultaneous* threats: the multi-enemy formations already land 46-57% of what they throw.

Recommendation, rather than inflating enemy #1 further: if the opening Scuffle must cost
8-12 Courage, that is an encounter-composition decision (weight the early pool toward
`foyer-3`, two Dust Bunnies) or an argument that the greedy AI over-blocks relative to a
human. Both are outside what a single enemy's damage number can fix. Flagging for the
critic rather than deciding unilaterally.

**Turn counts are not yet meaningful.** `foyer-14` now runs 9.4 turns (up from 7.4, because
moving the Calling Bell last makes the AI kill it later and eat more Ring value). As
already noted for the Butler's 250 Courage, every turn-count figure here is measured
against the 10-card starting deck; a realistic 25-card Act-1 deck kills far faster. No HP
was cut on the strength of a starting-deck measurement.

## Two deviations from the design doc — RESOLVED in round 4

For two rounds the House Bell's Ring for Service and the Butler's Service, Please granted
Guard where the doc says Roused. Both sit permanently in slot 0 with their summons behind
them, so a Roused handed out mid-phase always boosted an attack whose number was already
on screen. Ordering support enemies last fixed that for ordinary supports; it could never
fix a summoner.

The engine's `onEnemyPhaseEnd` hook — fired after every enemy has acted and the decay
buckets have run, but **before** the next intents are chosen — closes the window. All three
Roused sources now *arm* during their action and *apply* at phase end, so the buff always
lands on the intent that carries it rather than behind it:

- **Calling Bell — Ring**: armed, not applied inline. Board order is now irrelevant to
  correctness (support is still authored last because it reads better).
- **House Bell — Ring for Service**: the doc's 1 Roused to every other enemy, restored.
- **The Butler — Service, Please**: 8 Guard **and** 1 Roused, as written.

The same window fixed the mirror-image bug on **Nightlight Snuffer**. Darkness used to
expire at the Snuffer's own turn start, which from any slot but the last stripped the +2
out of an ally that had not swung yet — the player took *less* than the intent promised. It
now expires at `onEnemyPhaseEnd`, one full phase after it is cast, so slot order no longer
matters there either.

Verified against the real engine with Roused live: 2374 scored enemy turns, 0 mismatches.

## Boss Courage ramp flattened (round 4)

Bosses are off the continuous Courage ramp entirely — flat +6% (the design doc's Haunt 1
value) at every level. The ramp bought difficulty in the one currency that also buys
*length*: at Haunt 10 the Butler ran 15.5 turns against an 8–12 band while winning 64.3%,
and boss turns track boss Courage almost linearly.

The difficulty is bought back through `bossDmg` — **+1 damage per hit every third Haunt
level**, applied by each boss's own damage path so it appears in the intent as well as the
hit. It is Courage-neutral: each boss turn hurts more instead of there being more turns.
Ordinary enemies and Big Scares keep their ramp; neither was measured as too long, and
their Courage is what wears the player down on the way to the door.

Measured at Haunt 10, competent bot, n=60, two independent seeds:

| | boss turns | boss win |
|---|---|---|
| before | 15.5 | 64.3% |
| after, seed A | **11.50** | **46.4%** |
| after, seed B | **11.79** | **48.3%** |

Both inside the 8–12 turn and 45–65% win targets, and stable across seeds. Haunt 5 moved
12.18 turns / 61.5% win, so the ladder ramps monotonically in difficulty while getting
*shorter* rather than longer.


## New tests

- **5b — intent number vs damage actually dealt.** Every attack move is resolved against a
  dummy and its damage events reconciled against the intent: total must match *and* the
  number of damage events must equal `move.hits`. 65 attack moves, 16 multi-hit.
- **5c — `hitPlayer` invariant.** Asserts one `ctx.damage` call carrying `{hits:3}`, not
  three calls. Pins the exact bug at the source.
- **5d — declared vs applied statuses.** Fails if any move applies a status it did not
  declare, or declares the wrong stack count.
- **Butler standing-rule count** per phase.
- **`tests/enemies/audit.py`** — the real-engine intent-truth audit described above,
  including the per-encounter Guard-absorption table.
