# Blind style judgment — rubric, round 14: EVERY WING HAS ITS ROOMS

Read `RUBRIC.md`, then `RUBRIC-r9.md`, then `RUBRIC-r11.md` in full: the unit
is still a CONTACT SHEET of three rooms of one wing, and a sheet must win both
questions — are these different rooms, and are they the same wing? — or it
fails. All of it holds; this file wins where they disagree.

**Two more sheets this round**: `sheet-lampworks` (the house's lamp and candle
works: a wax room, a reflector gallery, a boiler walk) and `sheet-bathhouse`
(a steam room, an indoor pool, a pipe gallery). Neither wing has been judged
before, and until this round every room of each was one room rearranged.
Judge them exactly as the other sheets: a pool is not a steam room with the
furniture moved, and a lamp works that turns into a ballroom has broken its
wing.

**`rest` is not judged this round.** `combat` is, as RUBRIC-r9 says, to check
that the fight still reads with the room behind it — the enemies' nameplates
especially.

**Where you stand is part of the room.** Round 11's judges both said the same
thing of its winner: the rooms differed by their back wall, "never by
vantage". A sheet whose three panels are three walls photographed from one
centred tripod has answered half of the first question. Credit a real change
of vantage — a room seen from one end, from a landing, from a corner, from
among the graves — in `composition`, which is still this track's heaviest
field.
