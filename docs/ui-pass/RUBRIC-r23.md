# Blind style judgment — rubric, round 23: FOUR MORE WINGS THAT ARE NOT YET THEIR NAME

Read `RUBRIC.md` first for the 0-10 scale, the eight fields and the blindness
rules; this file wins where they disagree. Standing rulings: a screen's
COLOUR is not held to the samples' hues — readable, accurately drawn,
well-detailed objects are what matter; the darks are where this house keeps
its colour, so a pale, milky or grey dark is a defect; and **a room that is
SOFT, hazy or smeared is a worse defect than one whose lines stair-step** — a
line should be crisp AND smooth, like ink.

**Six screens.** Four contact sheets of empty fight rooms — `sheet-bathhouse`
(the bathhouse, the steam room, the pool), `sheet-lampworks` (the workshop,
the wax room, the reflector gallery), `sheet-attic` (the attic observatory,
the archive under the eaves), `sheet-kennels` (the kennels, the wash room) —
and two fights, `fight-bathhouse` and `fight-lampworks`, each also at
1280x800 (the Steam Deck). Everything was drawn
at the Deck's quality tier.

## WHAT TO LOOK AT FIRST

1. **Is each wing its name?** Would a viewer who was told only "the
   Bathhouse", "the Lampworks", "the Attic Observatory", "the Kennels" point
   at the right room without reading its caption? A bathhouse has its baths
   and their water; a lampworks is full of lamps; an attic observatory has a
   roof of real rafters and a telescope; a kennel has kennels, beds and
   bowls. A lump, a cube of noise or an outline drawn on a flat sheet is
   NOT the object, however it is coloured.
2. **Is every wall BUILT?** Courses, panels, mouldings, leaves and joints with
   relief and light — or a mottled cloud texture with lines drawn on it? The
   second is the defect this round exists to remove.
3. **Are the darks dark?** A milky or cloud-blue wall costs `palette` and
   `background`.
4. **Each room of a wing is a room that wing really has**, with the wing's
   palette and arch, and no two rooms of a wing the same room re-lit.

## `fits_between_samples` and `worst_problem`

`fits`: would a viewer believe this room was painted by the same hand as the
samples? For `worst_problem`, name the sheet, the room, the object and where:
"on sheet-bathhouse's pool room the pool is a flat blue rectangle with
no kerb or reflection" is useful; "the bath is weak" tells a builder nothing.
