### PATTERN
The better versions keep colour inside the darks: far-floor pools that read as blue-violet light rather than a maroon stain, dark walls, corners and ceilings that stay brick-red or umber instead of draining to neutral grey-black, and a dark jug with its own green glaze instead of the room's general maroon. Every difference is small -- invisible at a glance on the full frame in the passages and kitchens, visible without zooming only in the two lampworks rooms -- and none changes drawing, crispness, framing or how objects stand. Two things pull the other way and are worth watching: the coloured-dark versions let the passages' mid-floor boards drift slightly olive/khaki, and the green jug in the kitchen sits very near black so its ribbing is fainter than the red one's. What the better versions most need next to reach the samples: (1) the far thirds of the kitchens and passages floors still sink into one red-brown mass, so far crates, dressers and tables need lit edges and cast shadows to stand as objects; (2) dark props need modelled form -- a lit side, a core shadow and a rim -- not just a hue change, with the kitchen jug and the far plates and jugs the clearest cases; (3) the cold light (windows, shafts, blue pools) should be carried further against the warm lamp light as the samples do with candle against moonlight, since the red rooms remain nearly monochrome.
