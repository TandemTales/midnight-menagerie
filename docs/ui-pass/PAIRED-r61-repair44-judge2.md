### PATTERN
The better versions do two things at once: they keep a dark, tight contact shadow directly under and touching each foot, and they let the candle stands and candle-topped book piles actually light the floor near them. Where a version brightened the floor but moved or loosened the shadow away from the feet (the foyer's A), the furniture floats and that costs far more than the light gains; where the shadow stays attached, the extra candle pool is a small but real gain and the darks did not go milky (the ballroom's dark tiles stay deep purple in both). The differences in the passages and ballroom are slight -- roughly a 10-20% lift in a few floor patches -- and nothing else in the frames changes. Next, the better versions most need: (1) the candle pools shaped by the objects, with a cast shadow thrown away from the flame rather than a soft round lift, (2) the near props (book piles, chairs, tripod stands) painted with modelled light and dark sides so they sit in the same light as the floor, and (3) crisper drawn detail on the near floor (board joints, tile edges, the runner's border), which is still soft and grainy beside the samples' sharp painted stonework.
