## Objects that differ

GRAVEYARD-YARD: only the stonework on the grass differs; sky, chapel, mansion, fence, path, grass and leaves are identical.
- Near-left chest tomb: B better. B's lid has a bright lit top edge and a dark underside so it reads as a slab overhanging the chest; the recessed panels have a firm dark inner edge and the rings a lit rim. A's lid is one flat grey band and the panels are faint outlines under a moss wash.
- Urn/ball finial standing on that tomb: B better. B shades the sphere half-lit, half-shadow so it is round; A's is a flat disc with a wavy line across it.
- The four near headstones: B better. B gives each a lit bevel rim, a framed inscription panel and rows of engraved lines under the small cross; A's faces are mottled moss with only the cross, softer at the edge. A's moss is the richer colour, but it reads as a smear rather than a surface.
- Mid-ground cluster (round-top stones, the two small Celtic crosses, the tall cross's stepped base, the long low kerb): B better. Inscription lines and brighter edges on the stones, the kerb's top face is lit and separates from its side; A's kerb is a green-streaked strip.
- Right-hand pair of chest tombs with the two lanterns: B slightly better. Panels are darker-recessed and crisper, lid edge lit; A's left tomb is covered in a heavier moss blotch that hides the panels. Lanterns themselves the same.
- The lone headstone right of those tombs: B better, it has a legible inscription block; A's is a blank mossy face.
- Left-side tomb, small Celtic cross, round-top stone and far small stones/tombs: B slightly better, same treatment; small at this distance.
Nothing in A is drawn better; A's only advantage is a little more green in the moss.

STUDY-LIBRARY: only two regions differ: the red wing chair, and the desk with the green chair behind it.
- Red wing chair: B much better. B's back is real deep-button tufting: pillowed diamonds with a highlight and a shadow each and brass buttons at the crossings. A's back is a flat panel with a faint printed lattice and dots, plus a pale stair-stepped smear at the top of the back and another across the seat front. B also adds a column of brass nail heads down each arm front and a cleaner seat cushion; A's arm fronts are plain with one odd pale tick.
- Green chair behind the desk: B better. Same change at smaller size: tufted, lit diamonds against A's flat dotted panel.
- Desk: B better. B's drawers have a recessed frame each and brass bail handles, and the centre drawer has a knob; A's drawers are flat fronts with a pale bar for a handle. Desk top, lamp, blotter and book stack are the same.
Nothing in A is drawn better.

### PATTERN
In both rooms the better version is the one that models the surface of an object rather than staining it: a lit edge and a shadowed underside on a tomb lid, engraved lines on a headstone, pillowed diamonds and brass buttons on leather, framed drawers with real handles. The worse versions carry the same silhouettes but fill them with a flat wash (moss blotches, a printed lattice), which reads soft and, on the chair, leaves pale stepped artefacts. The better versions still need three things. First, the graveyard stones have lost most of their moss and now all share one cool grey; they want the moss back as painted patches sitting on top of the new carving, plus some variation stone to stone. Second, both rooms' objects still stand on weak feet: the tombs' bases sink into a black band and the wing chair's legs and the desk's foot fall to near black, so they need contact shadows and a lit ground edge rather than a void. Third, the same modelling has not yet reached the neighbours, so the globes and stands, the book stacks, the lanterns, the angel and the crosses now look flatter beside the improved pieces and should get the same lit-edge and painted-detail treatment.
