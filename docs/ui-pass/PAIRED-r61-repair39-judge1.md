SURVEY-room-crypt-catacomb: A by 1 -- the left wall's burial niches stay a warm, deep brown-black with crisp contrast in A, while B lays a cool blue-grey cast over them that lifts and greys the dark.

SURVEY-room-study-library: A by 1 -- the rug's navy border stays navy against its red field in A, while B's red wash over the darks turns the border maroon and flattens the far floorboards into one hue.

### PATTERN
All four pairs are near-identical in drawing, layout, objects, shadows and crispness; the only difference is a low-level tint in the darkest tones (mean dark level moves by one or two steps out of 255), so every margin is slight. The better versions keep the darks deep and saturated in the room's own colour and keep local colours apart (the navy rug border, the warm stone niches, the aubergine columns), while the worse ones either grey the dark with a cool cast (crypt) or wash one hue over everything (study), or let a recess go to dead black (heart, ballroom). Next the better versions most need: (1) the far floor and the side-wall recesses carried by drawn detail and local light rather than by a tint, since the far third of every floor still sinks to near-black; (2) near and middle-floor objects modelled with painted light and firm cast shadows at the samples' level, as several (ballroom chairs, heart statues, crypt candlesticks) still read as soft cut-outs with stair-stepped edges; (3) a crisper far wall, where the portraits, the orchestra balcony and the upper bookshelves stay slightly hazy and low in resolution next to the samples' sharp painted backgrounds.
