## Per-item notes (B viewed before A)

**graveyard-yard**
(a) Moss: A better. B hangs large dark-green rounded blobs on the stones' sides and a broad dark mound across the lower face of the near stones; they read as stuck-on green cushions and they dirty the faces. A's side moss is thin streaks hugging the edge, its face moss is a smaller scalloped clump, and its shoulder sprigs are lighter and smaller; it reads as growth on the stone. On the tomb lid B has a solid soft green slab, A a row of tiny dashes: neither is good, B's at least reads as a mat, A's as a dotted line.
(b) Stone near the lantern: A better, slightly. The tall Celtic cross between the two lanterns is saturated orange in B (105/73/45) and a warm grey-tan in A (95/75/59). B's reads as terracotta or varnished wood; A's still reads as carved stone with the knotwork visible, though it gives up some of the lantern's warmth.
(c) Foot of the nearest stones: A better, clearly. In A the three near headstones, the buff stone, the low kerb and the near-left tomb all have a fringe of bright drawn blades standing up in front of the stone's base, so the stone is planted in grass. In B the same feet are a soft dark-green smear that merges with the cast shadow. A's fault: the blades are an even comb of spikes, bright green on what is brown leaf-strewn earth, and the near-left tomb's fringe sits on the black shadow band rather than in front of it.

**graveyard-plots**
(a) Moss: B better, clearly. A puts near-perfect dark-green discs on the sides of four stones: they read as green buttons or balls, not moss. B's are small lumpy clumps half-hidden on the edge. On the central Gothic stone's shoulder A has a large flat dark jagged shape like bunting; B a smaller, lighter trailing sprig with leaf detail.
(b) Stone near the lantern: no real difference.
(c) Foot of the nearest stones: B better, clearly. B gives the big left Gothic stone, the central Gothic stone, the slate stone, the pale right stone with its long kerb, and the tomb behind a lit fringe of blades; the kerb now reads as a stone edge with grass against it. In A those feet fall into black. B's fault: on the kerb the fringe alternates spikes with regular scalloped humps, a repeating pattern, and the blades are a brighter green than anything else in the room.

**graveyard-gate**
(a) Moss: B better. A again has the disc on the near stone's left side and a rounded blob on the stone by the path, plus big flat dark shoulder patches and a heavy green mat on the left tomb's lid. B's shoulder sprigs are small and bright, its side moss is a thin streak, and the lid carries only a faint trace. B's lid moss is nearly absent, which is too little; A's amount there is closer to right but its shape is a flat slab.
(b) Stone near the lantern: B better, marginally.
(c) Foot of the nearest stones: B better.

### PATTERN
The better version in all three is the same treatment: small, irregular moss clumps and thin edge streaks instead of large round dark discs, stone near a lantern that stays stone-coloured rather than turning orange, and a fringe of drawn grass blades in front of each near stone's base instead of a dark smear that merges with the shadow. The disc moss is the single worst element in the worse versions (it reads as buttons), and the orange cross in the yard is the second. What the better versions most need next: (1) break the grass fringe's regularity and brightness; it is an even comb of spikes and scallops in a green more saturated than the ground it grows from, and should vary in height, clump, lean and darken to the ground's own colour at its roots, with some blades overlapping the stone's bottom edge and some ground showing; (2) give the ground in front of the largest stones something other than a black shadow band, i.e. a lit lip of earth or turf and a softer contact shadow; (3) make the remaining moss painted rather than stamped: the shoulder sprigs and lid moss are still flat single-tone shapes (the lid moss is a dotted line in yard and almost gone in gate), and want a light edge, a dark underside and a ragged outline that follows the stone's moulding, with a little of the lantern's warmth put back on the lit side of the yard's cross without losing its stone colour.
