### PATTERN
In all four pairs the only difference is the candles on the standing candelabra and side tables: the better version lights them, with a flame at each tip and a warmer, brighter metal or tabletop, and the worse leaves them as unlit grey sticks that fade into the dark. Nothing else moves: framing, floors, walls, crispness and the cut of objects at the frame edge are identical, so the margins are small to clear, never large. The lit version sits closer to the samples because their candles always burn and warm what is near them. Next the better versions need: (1) the flames to actually throw light, a pool on the floor and a warm spill on neighbouring chairs and tables, since at present the flame is a bright tip with almost no glow or cast light; (2) the near-floor objects (ballroom chairs and candelabra, passages book piles, gallery bust) to be modelled with painted light and shade and firmer contact shadows instead of flat, evenly toned cutouts; (3) the dark thirds of each room (gallery mid-floor and left corner, parlor left wall, passages ceiling and side recesses) to hold colour and drawn detail instead of falling to near-black.
