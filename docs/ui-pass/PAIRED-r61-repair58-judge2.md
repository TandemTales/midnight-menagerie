### PATTERN
The better versions keep moss as a drawn, modelled shape (a cap that drips over the shoulder, a patch on a lid) and keep the foot-grass as irregular clumps that stay within the stone's footprint, so the stone face stays crisp and still stands on its shadow. The worse versions swap that for a mechanical, evenly spaced comb of straight blades that climbs the kerb faces as a soft green veil, leaves stray ticks beside the stones, and thins the moss to slivers. The comb version does have one real merit: at 1x its blades are brighter and read more plainly as grass, where the hummocks read as moss or clipped hedge. The differences are small in every pair. What the better versions most need next:
1. Real blades of uneven height, lean and spacing breaking out of the hummocks, brighter where the lamp or moon hits them, so the foot growth reads as grass and not as a scalloped trim.
2. The regular scallop rhythm along the long kerbs broken up with gaps of bare earth and a fallen leaf or two, and the tomb-lid moss given a ragged, modelled edge with a lit top and dark underside in place of flat green blocks.
3. Contact painted in: a darker occlusion line and a little worn earth where each plinth meets the ground, with the moss colour varied (olive and ochre in the warm light, blue-green in the moonlight) so it belongs to the stone and is not one saturated green.
