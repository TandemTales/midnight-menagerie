### PATTERN
The better versions model the surface of each object: tufting that catches light pillow by pillow, drawer fronts with hardware, stone with a lit edge and a cut inscription. The worse versions lay a flat pattern or an even grey over the same silhouette and come out softer. The better versions are also crisper, and neither loses its feet or shadows. They most need three things next:
1. Fix the round forms. The urn finial's hard vertical split, and the half-dark ring bosses on the tomb panels, should be shaded as a sphere and a torus rather than a banded disc.
2. Give the remaining flat props the same treatment. In the study the floor globes' stands, the book stacks and the open books still read as flat cut-outs beside the chairs; in the graveyard the angel statue is unmodelled line-work in both versions.
3. Tie the objects to the room's light. The red armchair's legs and seat base sink into near-black with only a weak contact shadow, and the stonework lacks a warm lantern side against the cold moon side, which is the candle-versus-moonlight contrast the samples have.
