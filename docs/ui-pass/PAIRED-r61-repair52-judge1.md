## Objects that differ

**kitchens-kitchen** -- one object: the small handled keg-jug on the floor at right-centre, in front of the "3" crock.
- A: green glazed body with three raised hoop bands, a lit left-to-right turn of form, an oval gloss spot, a green collar under the cork. Reads as a banded stoneware keg and separates from the brown crock and sacks behind it.
- B: the same silhouette in near-black brown with no bands, one thin vertical streak as its only highlight; the body merges with the crock and its own shadow and reads as a dark hole in the group.
- Floor under and beside it: same in both. A draws it better.

**kitchens-scullery** -- the same single object. A: green, banded, modelled light to dark, oval gloss. B: flat dark brown, bands gone, a vertical streak highlight. A is the readable object.

**passages-library**
- Left writing desk: A is a dark silhouette with a broad black shadow pool under it that swallows the floorboards and the paper sheet beneath. B lights the apron and leg, shows the drawer front and ring pull, replaces the pool with small contact shadows at the feet, and the boards and the paper under it stay readable. B better.
- Right writing desk: same change. B shows the lit apron, drawer and knob and a lit leg edge, with a tight foot shadow; A is a flat dark shape over a black patch. B better.
- Near stacked crates by the left book pile: B catches light on the top rims and has a soft contact shadow on the floor; A is darker and flatter. B slightly better.
- Crates down the right wall and the far-left crate cluster: B's top edges and frames are lit so each crate separates from the bookcase dark; A's sink into it. B better, without going pale.
- Floor: B holds its boards under and beside the furniture; A falls to black there.

**passages-passage** (builds the other way round)
- Left side table with vase and candle: A has a lit top, apron, drawer and ring pull, a lit leg and a tight foot shadow; B is duller, with a wide black pool under the table. A better.
- Right side table with vase: A's leg and apron are lit and the drawer and knob read; B's table is a dark outline fused with the crates behind it. A better.
- Three crates beside the right table: A has lit top rims and frames and a crisp contact shadow under the front crate; B is murkier and its shadow merges with the table's. A better.
- Crates down both walls in the middle distance: A's are brighter-rimmed and separate from the panelling; B's are dimmer. A slightly better.
- Floor: A's boards stay visible under both tables; B loses them to black.

### PATTERN
The better version in every pair is the one where a floor or wall-side object carries its own light: a lit plane, a named detail (hoop bands, a drawer and pull, a crate's top rim) and a small contact shadow at the feet rather than a black pool that eats the floor. The worse versions leave the same silhouette as a dark shape fused with its shadow and the wall behind, so the floor falls to black exactly where the rubric asks it to hold. None of the better versions went pale or milky; the darks still hold colour. Next they most need: (1) painted surface detail on these props -- wood grain and worn edges on desks and crates, glaze variation and a maker's mark on the keg -- because they are still smooth-shaded solids beside the samples' brushwork; (2) cast shadows with direction from the nearest candle or lamp, not only contact blobs, plus a warm candle spill on the table tops and vases; (3) the same lit treatment for the wall furniture still in silhouette (the kitchen's back tables and dresser, the far bookcase feet and lamp posts), and less repetition in the identical tilted crates down the passages.
