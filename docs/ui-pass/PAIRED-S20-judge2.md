### PATTERN
These two builds are almost the same game: 14 of the 17 rooms and every one of the 16 screens match pixel for pixel or differ only by animation phase, flicker or a camera pan. The three real differences are all small near-floor or foliage objects. In the kennels and the hedge the better version keeps the object's hard, drawn edge and surface texture (boxy banded bales, leaf-textured topiary), and the worse one rounds it into a soft, lumpy or rubbery mass. In the attic the better version lights the near-floor corners so the planks don't fall to black. To reach the samples, the better versions most need: (1) near-floor objects that are modelled and textured with a painted, inked edge rather than smooth or inflated volumes; (2) painted surface detail at the samples' density on large flat areas (hedge walls, floors, kennel walls), which are still broad gradients; and (3) candlelit pools that reach the foreground corners in every room, as the attic's B does, so no room's near floor falls off to black.
