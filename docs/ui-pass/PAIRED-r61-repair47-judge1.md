### PATTERN
The better version in all four rooms is B, and the difference is always the same: objects are modelled with a light side, a dark side and drawn detail (turned stems, drapery folds, moulding bands, inset panels) instead of being flat, evenly toned shapes. Nothing else in the frames changed: floors, walls, ceilings and framing are identical, so the gains are small in the two candelabra rooms and clear where a large near object (the gallery bust, the heart urn) is repainted. The one pull the other way is that B's thinner candelabra stems lose a little weight low down against dark floor, and one far gallery pedestal loses A's warm rim-light. Next, to reach the samples: (1) give the near objects painted surface detail and ink line at the samples' level, since the bust's bands and the urn's faces are still coarse and pixel-stepped up close; (2) seat the repainted objects with firmer contact shadows and warm candle rim-light, so the lit stone does not float pale over the floor; (3) carry the same modelling to the objects left untouched (chairs, vitrines, tables, wall busts, far statues), which now look flatter beside the repainted ones.
