### PATTERN
Every pair is one change applied in two directions: one version pushes its darks toward a saturated warm red (dark props, object undersides, far floors, and in the lampworks the walls and ceiling), the other leaves them neutral, greenish or grey and keeps coloured pools nearer blue. Where the dark is a wall, ceiling or floor of masonry the warm version clearly wins, because the neutral one reads grey and slightly milky; where the dark is a prop the warm version wins only narrowly, since it gains modelling but loses the prop's own local colour; and on the passages' far floors the same warming is a loss, because it turns the one cold-blue moonlight pool purple and buries it. The differences are small everywhere except the lampworks wall; no pair changes drawing, crispness or framing. Next the better versions need: (1) the warm darks kept but the far-floor moonlight pools left truly blue, so warm candle and cold moon both read; (2) dark props such as the jug given their own local colour and a lit edge inside the warm dark, so they neither go to a black cut-out nor merge with the crock behind; (3) the far corners, ceilings and deep floor still falling to near-black in all six rooms given drawn, painted detail at the samples' level rather than empty dark.
