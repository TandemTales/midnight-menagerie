### PATTERN
The differences are small and local; no room changes in framing, floor, crispness overall or lighting, and the heart's candle stands and urn do not differ at all. The better versions are the ones where an object's construction is drawn rather than implied: glazing bars on cabinets, a stem that reaches its feet without a break or a dark gap, a bust whose chest is modelled instead of blocky, statues with light and shadow rather than line alone. The two candle-stand verdicts pull in opposite directions by letter (A in the parlor, B in the ballroom) but agree in kind: the unbroken stem wins each time. To reach the samples the better versions most need (1) the glazed cabinets to keep their drawn bars while holding the darker, richer glass of the other version rather than going pale; (2) the near bust's head, still blocky and split down the middle in both versions, modelled like its chest, and the statues given both the volume and the crisp fold lines together; (3) far and mid-distance objects (clock, far busts, back cabinets, far statues) lifted out of the murk with a lit edge and a foot shadow so they read as drawn objects rather than dim shapes.
