# Blind style judgment — rubric, round 8: BACKGROUNDS

Read `RUBRIC.md` first: the eight dimensions, the 0–10 scale and the blindness
rules. 9 is "a viewer could not tell this screen was not painted by the same
hand as the samples". Then read `RUBRIC-r1.md`.

**One track this round, and its subject is the ROOM.** Every earlier round asked
you to judge a screen of which the background was one dimension of eight. This
round the background *is* the work: three of the six captures have no interface
on them at all, only a room.

`refine`: `winner` is the one candidate whose work replaces what is there now.
`screen_winners` are recorded but do not decide the merge.

## What these six captures are

- **`room-foyer`, `room-crypt`, `room-graveyard`** — a room photographed on its
  own, no HUD, no board, no cards, no creature. A panelled hall, a coursed stone
  vault, and a graveyard open to the sky. **Judge these as paintings.** They are
  the round's subject, and they are the captures where `background` and
  `material` mean the most.
- **`combat`, `combat-boss`** — a real fight, so you are judging whether the room
  survives having a board in front of it: whether it still reads as a place, and
  whether it sits behind the fight instead of competing with it.
- **`rest`** — a board, and a different room entirely (the boards' background is
  a separate system from combat's). Judge it as a papered wall behind a plate.

## How to score a room

The samples are four paintings of this house. Two of them — `selectKid.png` and
`selectCompanion.png` — are interiors, and they are the fair comparison for
`room-foyer` and `room-crypt`: near-black grounds, purple scrollwork, cobbled
floors with a dark line round every stone, candles with a small warm pool.
`mainMenu.png` is the fair comparison for `room-graveyard`: a clouded night sky,
a silhouette with lit windows in it, and paving in the foreground.

Ask, in this order:

1. **Is anything DRAWN on it?** A painted room is covered in things a hand put
   there — courses, joints, tracery, ornament, mouldings, the contents of
   furniture. A room whose surfaces are correct but bare is the failure mode this
   round exists to fix, and it should score low on `ornament` however good its
   light is.
2. **Does every form have a line round it?** The samples outline everything.
3. **Is it a place, or a box with textures on it?** Does the floor meet the wall,
   does the light come from something you can see, is there anywhere your eye
   goes?
4. **Does it hold up at 1:1?** Open the capture at full size. Surfaces that read
   at thumbnail size and fall apart up close are the render/painting tell.

## Things that are NOT differences any builder made

- **The prop ARRANGEMENT may differ between candidates.** The furniture is
  placed procedurally per region and a builder who changed a room's proportions
  will get a different arrangement. Judge the props themselves — their
  silhouettes, their surfaces, their contents, whether they read as objects in a
  room — not which corner one landed in, and not whether one candidate happens
  to have a cabinet where another has a chair.
- **The creatures are animated.** A creature in frame on `combat` or
  `combat-boss` will be on a different frame in every candidate. Do not score a
  creature's pose, and do not score the creatures at all: they are the same
  paintings in every candidate.
- **The HUD, the cards, the plates and the type are nobody's this round.** They
  are identical in every candidate on `combat`, `combat-boss` and `rest`. Score
  them only where they interact with the room — whether the room reads behind
  them, whether contrast still lets the board be read.
- **There is no background art in this game and none is coming.** You are not
  being asked whether a painting would be better. You are being asked how close
  procedure got. Score what is in front of you against the samples.

## `fits_between_samples`

For this round it means: **could this ROOM hang between `selectKid.png` and
`mainMenu.png` without looking like it came from a different game?** Not the
interface — the room.

## `worst_problem`

Name the most specific thing you can see, on the capture where you can see it,
and say where on the screen it is. "The background is flat" has been written
seven times and has stopped being useful. "The left wall between the door and
the corner has nothing on it for two metres" is a fix.
