### PATTERN
In all four pairs the better version gives its free-standing objects a lit side and a dark side with a crisp edge, so brass and stone read as round, solid things; the worse version paints the same objects as flat, even-toned silhouettes that go soft or sink into the dark. The gain is real but small at full frame, largest where the object is big and near (the heart's urn and plinth) and nearly invisible on far-wall pieces. The better versions next need: (1) surface detail that follows form rather than bands laid across it -- the gallery bust's diagonal stripes and the hard, stair-stepped fold lines on the heart statues are the places where the new lighting reads as pattern, not modelling; (2) the small dark break in the candelabra stems above the tripod closed, and the same lit-edge treatment carried to the chairs, cabinets and tables, which are now flatter than the objects beside them; (3) painted detail at the samples' level on these near props -- chased ornament on the brass, chips and veining on the stone, warm candle bounce on the lit side -- since they are still smooth primitives next to the samples' hand-drawn objects.
