### PATTERN
The better versions give a prop a material and a light: wood that is warm and banded rather than grey or black, upholstery that pinches into tufts rather than carrying a printed dot grid, and crates that stay lit enough to show their planks instead of falling to black against the wall. Where a version loses, it is because detail was added as hard black marks (the lampworks' dashed slat gaps over the stencil) rather than as modelling. Two of the five pairs (kitchens, foyer) are close to identical at 1280x800 and only separate under zoom. To reach the samples the better versions next need: (1) the far and corner props (kitchen crates, passage crates behind the tables) lifted out of the dark with a rim of candlelight so they read at 1x without zoom; (2) drawn ink contours and painted highlights on the near props, since chairs and crates are still soft textured shapes beside the samples' crisp outlined objects; (3) cleaner surface drawing on the big near crates and chair frames (plank ends, nail heads, carved rails) in place of noise grain and broken line fragments.
