## THE HEADLINE: A PAIRED TEST, BECAUSE THE SCORES DID NOT MOVE

Survey 6's independent scores came out FLAT against survey 5's (boards 5.5-7,
rooms 4-7), several lower, though every round since survey 5 beat its own
baseline blind by +1.28 to +3.58. So two more blind judges were shown survey
5's and survey 6's capture of every screen and room side by side, A and B
assigned at random per pair, the key kept from them (`C:/UILOOP/pairs56/`,
`PROMPT.md`, `judge1.md`, `judge2.md`):

| | survey 6 better | survey 5 better | equal | net margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| paired judge 1 | **24** | 1 | 7 | +46 |
| paired judge 2 | **23** | 2 | 7 | +40 |

**Rounds 34-38 moved the game forward; the independent scores did not see it.**
A fresh panel's absolute scale drifts from the last panel's, so a survey's
scores compare screens WITHIN a survey, never across surveys -- measure
progress between surveys with a paired blind test. The seven "equal" pairs are
exactly what nothing touched: the clubhouse, the opening, and five rooms
byte-identical between the surveys (ballroom, bathhouse, kitchens, lampworks,
passages). The one pair both judges call worse is **gameover** (-1 each): its
paintings now hang "hooked short and the Dowager's label floats beside her
frame" where survey 5's hung on cords with the plate under the frame -- the
10-16 painting-fit fix's regression. Settings is split (J1 prefers survey 5's
gilt palette pills, J2 survey 6's medallions).

What decides a pair, both paired judges: **drawn objects standing on the near
floor beat lit empty space, and objects beat UI boxes**; the better versions
still fall short in their lower thirds and in soft, smeared floors.
