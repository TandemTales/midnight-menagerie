### PATTERN
The better versions treat each stone object as lit and aged: finials are turned round rather than split into two flat halves, lanterns have dark frames and amber glass that throw warm light on nearby stone, sandstone is told apart from grey stone, and tombs are seated in the grass by a moss line instead of ending on a ruled edge. The worse versions keep every stone the same cold grey with faint mottling, pale lantern boxes and bare bases, so objects read as cutouts placed on the lawn. The three things the better versions most need next:
1. The round dark-green moss pads on headstone faces and sides are flat, same-sized, hard-edged discs. They need broken edges, a light top and dark underside, and varied size, like the leafy caps already on the arch tops.
2. The lantern light should reach further: a warm pool on the grass and on the faces of neighbouring headstones, not only the tomb lid directly beneath.
3. The angel and tall crosses need carved detail (feathers, face, drapery folds, a deeper-cut knot on the shafts) and stronger contact shadows; the near-foreground stones still fall to near-black at their feet.
