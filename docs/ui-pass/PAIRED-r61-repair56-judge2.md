### PATTERN
The better version in every pair makes an object opaque and lit as a solid: furniture legs and bases get a light side, a foot and a contact shadow, and the dresser gets its own wooden back, sides and cornice. In the worse versions the brick wall shows through the dresser, and the legs and stands are flat dark silhouettes that neither meet the floor nor separate from it. No pair shows the better version losing crispness, floor or framing; the only costs are a slightly pale, flat dresser wood in the scullery and hard black ovals under the chair feet. What the better versions most need next:
1. Deepen and enrich the scullery dresser's wood so it is not pale or hazy, keep its darks coloured, and bring its plates back to the contrast they have in the kitchen.
2. Soften the contact shadows under the chair feet into the seat shadow rather than separate black pucks, and give the desk, globes and green chairs the same grounded shadow.
3. Add painted detail to these now-solid surfaces -- grain, moulding lines and edge highlights on the dresser cornice, desk drawers and globe stands, and candle-warm against cool modelling -- so they read as painted objects like the samples rather than evenly lit models.
