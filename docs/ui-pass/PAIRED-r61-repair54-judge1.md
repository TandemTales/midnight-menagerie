### PATTERN
The better version in every pair is the one where furniture against the back and side walls is lit enough to show its construction (dresser base and shelves, table aprons, leg joints, stretcher rails) instead of sinking into the wall or floor behind it; where a dark stays dark it still holds form. On the jug, a highlight that follows the form and agrees with the cast shadow beats a flat oval. The two passages rooms are a split: each version lights one table's apron well and leaves the other murky or hot, so neither is right on both sides, and the margins there are small. Next the better versions most need: (1) consistent light on both side tables in the passages, with no hot orange patch at the apron corner and no dead-dark twin opposite; (2) a contact shadow and visible floor under the kitchen tables and dresser, so the legs have feet on the ground rather than fading out, with the far-right legs brought up to match the left; (3) painted surface detail on the lit pieces (wood grain and edge wear on aprons and the dresser, a proper glaze gradient on the jug), since the newly lit dresser in the scullery now shows flat, slightly over-orange planes and stair-stepped plates.
