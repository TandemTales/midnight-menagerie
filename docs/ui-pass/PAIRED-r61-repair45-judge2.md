### PATTERN
The better versions give an object a readable form by light: a lit top plane, a dark side, and separate parts (straw layers, individual skulls, lobed leaves), where the worse versions lay a mottled or smeared texture over a silhouette so that it reads as a flat patch or a hazy lump. The two rooms pull in opposite letters, so neither A nor B is consistently the better painter; the pumpkin difference is large and near the camera, the crypt difference is tiny and at the far wall. To reach the samples the better versions next need: (1) drawn contour and ink detail on near props (the bales still read partly as planked crates, and need loose straw ends and a ragged edge rather than straight bands); (2) the far and middle-distance objects held as crisp as the near ones, since the crypt's back wall and the courtyard's far pumpkins are soft; (3) darks that keep colour and contact shadows under every object, as the crypt's side aisles and the bales' feet still fall to plain black.
