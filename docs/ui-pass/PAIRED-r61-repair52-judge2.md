### PATTERN
The better versions give each object its own light: a lit face and a shadow face, a bright top rail or rim, and a small dark contact shadow directly under the feet. The worse ones leave furniture as dark, half-transparent flats over big soft blobs that black out the floor and hide the papers on it. In the kitchens the better jug is the one modelled round, with ribs and a colour that separates it from its neighbour; the worse one is a silhouette with a streak. The better versions most need three things next:
1. Painted surface detail on these props. Wood grain and edge wear on the table tops and crates, and glaze variation and a soft, shaped highlight on the jug in place of the flat oval sticker, lit from the side the fire or candle is actually on.
2. Solid, consistent tables. The right-hand passage table's apron and the crate standing on or behind it still read as slightly see-through, and the stretcher rails are hairlines; they need thickness.
3. The same treatment for the far crates. The rows down the floor are still small, repeated, identical boxes; they need varied sizes or angles and enough light to read at Deck size, with the far floor under them held out of black.
