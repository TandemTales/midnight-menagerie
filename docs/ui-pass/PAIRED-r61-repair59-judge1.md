### PATTERN
The better versions model the object rather than silhouette it: stone gets mouldings, a carved scroll and a lit face against a shadow face; crates stop being one stamp repeated; chair feet get a contact shadow that pins them to the boards. None of the changes softens or greys anything, and the gains are largest where a near-floor object is big in frame (the heart bench and pedestal) and smallest where they are a few pixels (the study feet, the far crates). Next they most need: (1) the same lit-face/shadow-face modelling on the stone's neighbours, which now look flatter beside it - the urn bowl, the statue plinths and the column bases in the heart rooms; (2) contact shadows and a cast shadow under the crates and under the pedestal's plinth, since the leaning crates and the lit plinth are not yet seated on the floor, and the fireplace chair's right-foot ellipse should be softened to match the library's; (3) painted surface detail on these near props - chips, veining and a worn edge on the stone, plank grain, nail heads and a stencil or rope on the crates - so they approach the samples' hand-painted finish rather than clean geometry.
