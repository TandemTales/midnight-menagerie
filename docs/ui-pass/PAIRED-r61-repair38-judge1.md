### PATTERN
In every pair the better version is the one whose candles and candelabra are actually lit: flames on the wicks, warmer gilt on the stems, and a little light spilling onto the table top or objects beside them, which makes thin props read as drawn objects instead of bare forks dissolving into the dark. The differences are small and local; rooms, floors, framing and crispness are otherwise identical between versions, so no pair is more than a clear win. To reach the samples the better versions next need: (1) the lit candles to cast a visible pool of light and a contact shadow on the floor around their feet, since the flames currently light almost nothing beyond themselves and the stands still float slightly; (2) the far and middle-distance props (far candelabra, crates, cabinets, the dark side tables) to hold colour and edge instead of fading into near-black silhouettes; (3) more painted modelling on the near objects -- chairs, book piles, vases -- with highlights and form shading at the samples' level, so they read as painted by the same hand rather than as flat textured cut-outs.
