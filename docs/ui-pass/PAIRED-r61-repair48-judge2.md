### PATTERN
In every pair one version keeps its darkest surfaces (ceiling, side walls, corners, far floor) a saturated warm dark with the brick, panelling or boards still drawn in it, and the other lets them go to a neutral grey-green or to plain black; the coloured-dark version is the better room each time because the walls and ceiling stay in frame as surfaces. The price of that version is a red wash over near objects in the kitchens (jugs, sacks, plates lose their local colour) and, in the library, a weaker violet pool and less separated book colours. Next, the better versions most need: (1) local colour kept on near and middle-floor objects so the warm dark tints the room without recolouring the props; (2) the cool pools (moonlight on tile, the violet patch on the far floor) held at full strength inside the warm dark so warm-versus-cold reads as in the samples; (3) the far corners and the top band of every room still reaching true black in places, which should become drawn, textured surface with the same painted grain as the near floor.
