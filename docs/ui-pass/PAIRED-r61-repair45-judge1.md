### PATTERN
The better versions give an object a readable top plane, a light side and a dark side, and surface marks that follow its form (straw courses, individual skulls), where the worse ones cover the silhouette in an all-over noise texture or let it fall into one dim mass. The differences are small and local: both rooms are otherwise identical, with floor, walls, feet and shadows already holding. To reach the samples the better versions next need: (1) material that is unmistakable at Deck size -- the bales should bristle with straw ends and a ragged edge so they stop reading as wooden crates, and the far skull stacks need eye sockets, not just balls; (2) painted edge lines and warm rim light on near props, since the pumpkins' stair-stepped rib seams and the bales' soft outlines are still rendered rather than drawn; (3) darks that keep colour -- the shadowed sides of bales, the far crypt corners and the cast shadows go to flat near-black where the samples keep aubergine or umber.
