### PATTERN
In every pair the two versions share identical objects, feet, shadows and near floor; they differ only in how the darkest surfaces are graded, so the better version is the one whose side walls, corners and ceiling stay a saturated dark of the room's own colour rather than going grey or collapsing to black (kitchen B, passage B, scullery A, library A). The trade runs the same way each time: the version with the warmer, richer darks tends to have the muddier or smaller cold light pool, and the version with the clean blue pool tends to have greyer surrounding darks, so neither treatment wins outright and all four margins are slight. What the better versions most need next: (1) both at once, warm saturated darks AND a clean cold blue pool, which no version here manages; (2) the far floor and far third of each room are still soft and low-contrast beside the samples' crisp drawn stonework, most visibly the library's and scullery's far boards and tiles; (3) the extreme upper corners and ceilings still end in flat, featureless dark (scullery and kitchen corners, the passage ceiling's beams barely modelled) where the samples carry painted texture and line right to the frame edge.
