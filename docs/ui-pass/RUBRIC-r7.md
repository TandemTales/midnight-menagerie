# Blind style judgment — rubric, round 7

Read `RUBRIC.md` first: the eight dimensions, the 0–10 scale and the blindness
rules. 9 is "a viewer could not tell this screen was not painted by the same hand
as the samples". Then read `RUBRIC-r1.md`.

- **DIALOGS follows its REFINE section.** `winner` is the one candidate whose work
  replaces the current screens; `screen_winners` are recorded, but do not decide
  the merge.
- **KIDS' PLACES follows its EXPAND section.** `screen_winners` decide what
  merges, one screen at a time; `winner` is only for the record.

Only these two tracks run this round. There is no POLISH or COMBAT judging; those
nine screens keep round 6's winners.

On the DIALOGS track, two screenshots are dialogs opened over a screen: Settings
over the map, and a pile of cards over a fight. The third is the opening's story,
told over the lit room. Judge the dialog and how it sits over what it covers. A
dialog either belongs to the painted world or is a web page laid on top of one,
and a dialog whose sections or buttons do not fit its own frame fails the
readability score, however well it is ornamented. **A control that states its
state twice** — a switch and a separate ON/OFF pill in the same row — is a web
form, and should be scored as one.

On the KIDS' PLACES track, the Lobby is a treehouse the players climb into, the
Clubhouse is a children's cosy headquarters (warmer than the mansion, same painted
hand), and the Atlas is the whole house's floor plan with a side panel. The
Lobby's password is rolled fresh on every load, so its word and number differ
between candidates: judge its presentation, not the word.

**The creatures are animated, and the pile viewer is opened over a fight.** A
creature will be in frame on the `piles` capture and it will be on a different
frame in every candidate's, and in the baseline's. That is not a difference any
builder made and it is not evidence about any candidate. Do not score a creature's
pose.

**The top HUD strip is nobody's this round.** It was rebuilt as a carved rail in
round 6 and it is in frame on the Settings capture. Judge the dialog over it, not
the rail; it is the same rail in every candidate.

**The background dimension.** The room or wall behind every one of these six
screens is still a render, not one of Josh's paintings, and it is the same render
in every candidate on a given screen — Settings and the pile viewer score lowest
on it of anything in the pass. Score it honestly; it is the dimension holding this
pass at 7. But do not let it swing the candidates apart, because no candidate can
fix it this round. Where a candidate has genuinely changed what sits on or in
front of that ground — a painted floor under the opening, a lit window, a dressed
wall, a staged shelf — that IS its work and counts.
