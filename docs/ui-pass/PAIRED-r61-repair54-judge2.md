### PATTERN
The better versions light furniture as furniture: a dresser whose carcass, cornice and base are drawn rather than plates hanging in black, and a jug whose highlight follows the belly from the side the fire is on. The same modelling pass on the passages' side tables is a wash: it helps where a lit edge is added (apron beads, library rails on a bright floor) and hurts where it adds a dark patch that swallows a joint or fuses a rail with its shadow on a dark floor. That is why the passage pair goes to the flat version and the library pair to the modelled one, each only by 1. What the better versions most need next:
1. Rails and apron ends should keep a lit top edge and never go darker than the floor behind them; a readable leg-to-apron joint on every table.
2. The lit scullery dresser needs real planked wood on its lower back instead of the brick-like grid, and the kitchen's upper dresser needs the same lighting as its base, with the tables in front given a contact shadow or rim so they separate from it.
3. Carry the jug's treatment (form-following highlight, glaze foot, light from the room's actual source) to the other floor vessels, crocks and sacks, and give the back-wall tables' feet contact shadows on the floor.
