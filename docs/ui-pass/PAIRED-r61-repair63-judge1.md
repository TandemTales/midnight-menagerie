### PATTERN
The better version is in every case the one that puts drawn, lit architecture or objects into the top fifth of the frame (panels and medallions, prints and sconces, newel posts, ceiling joists) where the worse one lets the upper wall or ceiling fall to an empty dark; none of these additions cost crispness or floor. The pedestal changes are at the limit of visibility and decide nothing. What the better versions most need next: (1) the new upper-wall and ceiling work should take the room's warm colour and painted grain rather than a flat grey-tan (passages joists) or blown white streaks (library side walls); (2) repeated elements need variety and clean relation to what hangs in front of them (the fireplace's identical cream prints, the cornice line running straight through both chandeliers); (3) the same care is still owed to the near floor and far side walls, which remain the darkest, least drawn parts of every room compared with the samples' fully painted grounds.
