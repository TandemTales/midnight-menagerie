### PATTERN
The better versions build furniture as joinery in its own material: the dresser has planks, cornice, sides and panels in the same pale wood as the tables, and the chairs, desk and globes end in feet, casters and plinths that touch the floor with a shadow. The worse versions leave the dresser wearing the wall's brick and colour, and leave legs as dark sticks with no contact. Every difference is small in frame and in the back or middle distance, so the gains are real but modest at Deck size. What the better versions most need next:
1. The dresser's lower back boards and the globe's thin legs sit slightly pale and grey; they need richer, warmer darks with a candle-side highlight and a cast shadow from the dresser onto the wall and floor.
2. The contact shadows and feet should be carried to every other standing object -- tables, chopping block, green chairs, bookcase -- so the grounding is consistent, and the near globe stand given a little more weight.
3. These surfaces need painted detail -- wood grain, worn edges, brass glints, plate patterns that hold at 1280 -- so they read as painted objects like the samples, not clean lit models.
