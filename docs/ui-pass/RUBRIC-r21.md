# Blind style judgment — rubric, round 21: THE FIGHT'S ROOM, AS THE DECK DRAWS IT

Read `RUBRIC.md` first for the 0-10 scale, the eight fields and the blindness
rules; this file wins where they disagree. Two standing rulings hold: a
screen's COLOUR is not held to the samples' hues — readable, accurately drawn,
well-detailed objects are what matter — and the darks are where this house
keeps its colour, so a pale, milky or grey dark is a defect.

**Five fights**, each also at 1280x800: `combat` and `combat-boss` (the
Foyer), `fight-greenhouse`, `fight-graveyard`, `fight-ballroom`. Every capture
was drawn at the Steam Deck's quality tier. **1280x800 is the Steam Deck**, the
platform this game ships on first.

## WHAT TO LOOK AT FIRST

1. **The room's lines.** Zoom into the room behind the fight — stair strings,
   balusters, rails, column flutes, mouldings, window bars, frames. Do any
   show pixel stair-stepping, "an upscaled low-res sprite"? That is a defect
   of `material` and `background`. **But a room that got SOFT to hide its steps
   is a WORSE defect than a stepped one** — a line should be crisp AND smooth,
   like an inked line on the samples. Blur, haze or smear standing in for
   detail costs `background` and `material` more than steps do.
2. **The floor.** Do the Kid, the Companion and the creature stand ON the
   room's floor — one ground plane, a contact shadow where each meets it, lit
   by the room's own light? Or do they hang in front of a wall with the floor
   far below? A flier should cast a shadow on the floor under it. Are they
   scaled believably against one another and the room? This is
   `composition` and `coherence`.
3. **Every wing.** A candidate that fixed the Foyer and left the Greenhouse,
   the Graveyard or the Ballroom floating or stepped has fixed one screen.

## WHAT IS NOT A DIFFERENCE ANY BUILDER MADE — do not score it

- The run rail across the top, the hand of cards, the Nerve dial, the piles,
  END TURN, the Companion's panel — identical in every candidate.
- The creatures' and the Kid's painted ART (their pose, their drawing) and
  which way the creatures face. Their PLACEMENT and SHADOW are in scope.
- The rail reading "The Foyer · Wing 1" on a Greenhouse, Graveyard or
  Ballroom fight: the harness's mock run.
- A "PREVIEW" tag on the rail: a harness screen opened outside a run.

## `fits_between_samples` and `worst_problem`

`fits`: would a viewer believe this room was painted by the same hand as the
samples? Say which object costs a screen its TRUE. For `worst_problem`, name
the screen, the object and where it is: "on fight-graveyard at 1280 the iron
railing behind the Grave Moth stair-steps along its top rail, and the Moth
casts no shadow on the path" is useful; "the room is noisy" tells a builder
nothing.
