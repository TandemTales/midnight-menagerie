# Blind style judgment — rubric, round 30: THE RUN STRIP AND THE PARCHMENT

Read `RUBRIC.md` first for the 0-10 scale, the eight fields and the blindness
rules; this file wins where they disagree. Standing rulings: readable,
accurately drawn objects matter more than matching the samples' hues; the
darks are where this house keeps its colour, so a pale, milky or flat-tan
surface that breaks them is a defect.

**Three screens, each at the default size and at 1280x800 (the Steam Deck).**
`hud-late` is the map with a late-run load in the run strip across the top
(ten Keepsakes, eight Gear, three Snack slots) -- judge the STRIP; `map` is
the wing's blueprint, early in a run; `atlas` is the whole-house floor plan
with one wing surveyed.

## WHAT TO LOOK AT FIRST

1. **hud-late at 1280: can you see every Keepsake and every Gear item as an
   icon?** Each in its own socket, none overlapping, nothing reduced to a
   word like "10 KEEPSAKES"; a "+N" for overflow is fine. Is the strip ONE
   readable row of a few bigger cartouches, or a crushed line of small caps
   with dot separators?
2. **map: does the parchment keep the house's darks** (aged, burnt toward the
   edges) or is it a pale tan sheet? **Can you tell the reachable rooms from
   the visited and the distant at a glance?** The next move should be the
   focal point; a field of identical tan coins is the defect.
3. **atlas: does the surveyed wing stand out in ink while the unknown wings
   recede?** Or are all seventeen at one line weight? Are the plan's walls
   crisp at 1280?

## WHAT IS NOT A DIFFERENCE ANY BUILDER MADE — do not score it

- The rooms behind the boards and the painted art of the Kid and Companions.
- The run's content (which Keepsakes, which rooms): the harness fixes it.

## `fits_between_samples` and `worst_problem`

`fits`: would a viewer believe this screen came from the same game as the
samples? For `worst_problem`, name the screen, the size, the element and
where: "on hud-late-1280 the Gear show as the word '8 GEAR' between SNACKS
and Haunt" is useful; "the strip is cramped" tells a builder nothing.
