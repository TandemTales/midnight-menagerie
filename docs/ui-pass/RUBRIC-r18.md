# Blind style judgment — rubric, round 18: THE WHOLE HOUSE, NOT ITS BEST ROOM

Read `RUBRIC.md`, then `RUBRIC-r9.md`, then `RUBRIC-r11.md` in full, then this.
The unit is a CONTACT SHEET of three rooms of one wing, and a sheet must win
both questions — different rooms, same wing. `RUBRIC-r16.md`'s and
`RUBRIC-r17.md`'s tests still hold and are restated here so you need not read
them: is the object FINISHED inside, does a repeated element VARY, and are the
PEOPLE in the house people.

**Four sheets** — foyer, ballroom, greenhouse, graveyard — plus `combat` at
both sizes. The Bathhouse and the Lampworks are not judged and were not to be
touched.

## THIS ROUND'S QUESTION: is every wing at the same standard?

Round 17's winner peopled the foyer and the ballroom and left the greenhouse
and the graveyard as they were; the best work on those two sheets was on the
candidates that lost. So judge each sheet on its own, and do not let a
candidate's strongest sheet carry its weakest in your mind. A candidate that
is excellent on two sheets and unchanged on two is not a better system than
one that is good on all four — say which it is in `verdict`.

## THE THREE STANDING TESTS

1. **Is the object FINISHED inside?** Pick the three nearest drawn objects on
   each panel and look inside them. A face at the size it is drawn carries a
   brow, a nose line and one dark eye socket — `mainMenu.png` does it at
   60 px. A bust is a head, not an ovoid. A glass roof has glazing bars that
   converge, purlins and a ridge. A proscenium has an arch, imposts and a
   keystone.
2. **Does a repeated element VARY?** Trees on a horizon, vines on a wall,
   plants in troughs, musicians in a gallery, panes in a roof, sitters on a
   wall. One unit stamped at an even pitch is unfinished work, whatever it is.
   A removed picture — an empty frame with an unfaded patch of paper behind
   it — is a drawn thing; an empty frame with nothing behind it is not.
3. **Are the people PEOPLE?** Differing in sex, age, dress, pose, scale and
   direction; not one plate repeated.

## THE TWO WAYS A CANDIDATE GOES BACKWARDS

- **Flattening.** A drawn object that is now a smooth field or a void is a
  REGRESSION — round 16 lost a coffered ceiling that way and round 17 lost a
  glass roof to a starry hole. Say so and score that screen below the
  baseline.
- **Greying.** The darks are where this house keeps its colour. Darkest
  corners gone pale, milky or grey score below darker ones; a creature or
  card that has lost its silhouette against a newly-lit wall is a
  `readability` failure.

## AND A THIRD: MARKS THAT DO NOT BELONG TO ANY OBJECT

Thin stray lines, streaks or diagonals crossing masonry, glass or sky, which
belong to no drawn thing in the room, are rendering leaks, not texture. Round
17 had tree drawing leaking over a mausoleum. Name them where you see them.

## PERSPECTIVE

Round 15 and round 16 judges both reported the mirror hall's parquet as
wrongly drawn, and both times measurement showed it was right: the arcs are
correct perspective across a wide field of view, and the block period shrinks
from 21 px near to 12 px far. **Do not report that floor again unless you can
name a line that does not share its vanishing point.** Other perspective
errors — a room inset in black margins, a cornice that does not converge —
remain correctness, not style.
