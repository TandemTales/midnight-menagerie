## Per-object notes

**graveyard-gate**
- (a) Urn finials / ring bosses: A better. A's urns have a lit side, a shaded side and a small highlight, so they read as turned stone; B's are a flat speckled disc on a stub.
- (b) Moss, lichen, stone colour: A better. A's stones vary (warm sandstone by the lanterns, cream on the near chest tomb, grey-blue slate elsewhere) and carry moss as streaks, a clump on the tomb lid and a fringe at the foot. B's stones are one cool blue-grey with only faint green staining. Weakness in A: the moss "caps" on headstone shoulders and the round patch on the near-right headstone are hard-edged flat green shapes, closer to a leaf or sticker than to growth.
- (c) Lanterns and warm light: A clearly better. A's lantern is an amber glazed box with dark glazing bars, a cap and a flame inside, and the tomb and headstones beside it pick up the warm light. B's is a pale blank block with a white blob.
- (d) Angel: A slightly better. The wings and robe folds have firmer light and dark planes; B's is the same drawing with a mottled grain that flattens it.
- (e) Tall crosses: A better. Domed centre boss and lit edges on the ring.
- (f) Stone meeting grass: A better. Moss band and green fringe at the plinths, plus a dark contact line, seat the tombs.

**graveyard-plots**
- (a) A better. The two urns behind the long chest tomb are modelled spheres with a lit quarter in A; in B they are split down the middle into a flat light half and flat dark half.
- (b) A better overall, with the pair's clearest flaw. A's near-left headstones are warm sandstone against cool slate ones behind, which separates the rows. But A's round moss cushions are flat dark-green discs that overhang the stone's outline and read as stickers or bushes; the shoulder "caps" read as draped green cloth. B has none of these and is cleaner, just barer.
- (c) A clearly better. Both lanterns are amber glass with bars in A, and the tomb under the centre one glows; B's are chalky blocks.
- (d) Angel: A slightly better. (e) Tall crosses: about equal. (f) A better: green tufts and a moss band along the kerb and at headstone feet; in B the feet sink straight into black.

**graveyard-yard**
- (a) A better: the urn on the near-left chest tomb is a shaded sphere in A, a flat pale disc in B; ring bosses bright raised rings in A.
- (b) A better. Same caveat: the green shoulder caps and one round disc on the kerbed stone are flat shapes.
- (c) A clearly better. The two lanterns are amber glazed lamps in A and light the tomb tops and the tall cross orange; in B they are pale tombstone-shaped blocks with a white core.
- (d) Angel: near equal, A marginally.
- (e) A better for the right-hand cross (strongly warm-lit with a visible lattice and boss, though it is close to over-saturated orange) and the small far cross.
- (f) A better.

### PATTERN
In all three pairs the same version wins on the same grounds: its lanterns are drawn objects (amber glass, bars, flame) that light the stone near them, its stones come in more than one colour, its urn finials and ring bosses are shaded as round forms, and moss at the foot seats tombs on the grass, where the other version is a uniform cool grey with blank pale lanterns and bases that fade into black. The worse version wins nothing outright; its only advantage is the absence of the flat moss shapes. The three things the better versions most need next: (1) redraw the round moss cushions and shoulder caps, which are flat hard-edged dark-green discs and drapes that overhang the stone's silhouette, as broken, lit, textured growth that stays inside the outline; (2) carry the lantern light further, as a visible warm pool on the grass and a warm edge on every neighbouring stone, while pulling back the nearly over-saturated orange on the yard's right-hand cross; (3) give the near, large stones the same modelling the small ones now have: a lit edge and shadowed return on the angel, cross arms and headstone thickness, and cast shadows at the foot of the nearest stones, which still fall into black at the bottom of the gate and plots frames.
