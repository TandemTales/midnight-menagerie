# Blind style judgment — rubric, round 11: DOES A WING FEEL STALE?

Read `RUBRIC.md` first for the 0–10 scale and the blindness rules, then
`RUBRIC-r9.md`, whose re-definitions still hold: colour is not a target, objects
must be nameable and accurately built, and `fits_between_samples` asks whether
this is a careful, accurate drawing of a real place. **This file changes the
UNIT you are judging.** Where it disagrees with an earlier rubric, this one wins.

## WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Josh, 2026-09-18: *"i want variation between backgrounds within sections of the
mansion as well, multiple different foyer, ballroom, greenhouse, etc. rooms so
that different encounters within the same section wont feel stale."*

A wing of this house contains many encounters. Every one of them draws the same
region, re-seeded by the room's name. **Most of your screens this round are
CONTACT SHEETS: three rooms of one wing, stacked, each labelled with the room it
is.** They are one image because the question is not "is this room good" but
"are these three different rooms in the same house".

## THE TWO QUESTIONS, and a candidate must win BOTH

A sheet fails if it misses either, and the failure modes are opposite:

1. **ARE THESE DIFFERENT ROOMS?** Not "has something moved" — three
   arrangements of one room is what this round exists to fix. Cover the labels
   and ask whether you would believe you had walked into a different room. What
   makes that true is the thing your eye reaches first: the feature on the wall,
   where you are standing, what the room is shaped like. Furniture sliding along
   a wall does not.
2. **ARE THEY THE SAME WING?** A Foyer that becomes a crypt is not variation, it
   is a bug. The wing is its colour, its materials, its lamps and its
   architecture; those should hold across all three while the room changes.
   **A candidate that achieves variety by breaking a region's identity should
   score BELOW the baseline**, because the baseline at least knows what the
   Foyer is.

The best candidate is the one where three panels are obviously the same wing and
obviously not the same room.

## HOW THE EIGHT FIELDS ARE SCORED ON A SHEET

Score the SET, not the best panel and not the average of three.

- **`composition`** — the heaviest field this round. Do the three panels frame
  differently: a different vantage, a different centre of interest, a different
  shape of space? Three photographs from one tripod position score low here
  however much has been rearranged in front of it.
- **`ornament`** — does each room carry its own drawn feature, or is it the same
  wall feature three times with the furniture moved?
- **`coherence`** — the SAME-WING half. One house, one set of materials, one
  lighting logic, three rooms.
- **`background`** — are all three rooms places you could describe, or has
  variety been bought by making them vaguer?
- **`material`** — stone as stone, timber as timber, consistently across the set.
- **`palette`** — does the colour suit the setting, and is it consistent across
  the three? **Not** whether it matches `UI/*.png`.
- **`readability`** — on `combat` and `rest` only, whether the board still reads.
  On a sheet, whether each room's objects are legible.
- **`typography`** — not this round's work; score it the same for everyone.

## `fits_between_samples`

On a sheet, read it as: **would a player moving through this wing believe it was
a real house with several rooms in it, rather than one set redressed?**

## `worst_problem`

Name the panel, say what is identical across the three that should not be, or
what broke the wing's identity. "All three Foyers put the same staircase on the
same wall from the same camera height, so the sheet reads as one hall with the
chairs moved" is the shape of a useful answer. "The background is flat" has been
written for eleven rounds.

## What is NOT a difference any builder made

- **Prop ARRANGEMENT within a panel** is re-rolled per room by machinery that
  already existed before this round. Arrangement alone is not variety — that is
  the point of the round — so do not credit it as one.
- **The creatures, the HUD, the cards, the plates and the type** are identical
  in every candidate.
- **Which three room names a sheet uses** is fixed for all candidates.

## The screens
