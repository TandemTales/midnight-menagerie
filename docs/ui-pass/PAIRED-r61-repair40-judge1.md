### PATTERN
The better versions are the ones where an object keeps a warm, saturated local colour and continuous drawn lines (gilt chair frames, whole plank seams) or gains real modelling (tufted upholstery, crate faces that catch light); the worse ones either desaturate the object toward a pale grey, break its lines into dashes, or let it fall to a black silhouette. The differences are confined to props; floors, walls, ceilings and framing are identical in every pair, and the foyer and kitchen pairs are close to indistinguishable at normal viewing distance. To reach the samples the better versions most need: (1) painted modelling on every prop with a warm lit side, a coloured shadow side and a rim, never a grey or chalky wash; (2) mid-distance and far objects such as the crates in the passages and kitchen lifted out of black with hue-holding darks and unbroken, un-stair-stepped line work; (3) surface detail at the samples' level on near props, such as wood grain, carved ornament and gilt highlights on chair frames and crate boards, so they read as painted objects rather than textured solids.
