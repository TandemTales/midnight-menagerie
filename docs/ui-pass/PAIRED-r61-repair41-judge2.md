### PATTERN
In all three rooms the better version (A each time) lights the floor around a candle-bearing object and puts a contact shadow under its feet, so the object stands on the floor instead of floating over it; the lift stays warm and saturated (measured saturation is essentially unchanged, 0.82 vs 0.82 in the passages and foyer), so it does not read as a milky or grey dark. The differences are small and local: the passages pair is near-identical at full frame, and the foyer is the only one visible without zooming. What the better versions need next: (1) the pools must not bleach what lies in them -- the ballroom's rose petals lose their red and the passages' boards flatten toward a plain tan under the light; (2) the same grounding for the objects that still have none, the foyer's far chairs and right-hand furniture and the ballroom's right-hand chairs, whose feet still fade into the dark; (3) painted detail and crisper drawing on the near props and the floor itself (book spines, tripod feet, board and tile edges are still soft, smooth models beside the samples' drawn, ink-edged objects).
